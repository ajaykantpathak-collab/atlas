## DEFINITION
Roman Numerals are a non-place-value number system developed by ancient Romans that uses seven specific Latin letters (I, V, X, L, C, D, M) to represent values. Unlike our standard system, the value of a symbol depends on its position relative to other symbols: placing a smaller value before a larger one subtracts it, while placing it after adds it.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are looking at the face of an old clock or the copyright date on a book, and you see "MCMXCV." How do you read that? To start, remember that unlike our normal numbers, these are just letters with assigned values. First, list out the values: I=1, V=5, X=10, L=50, C=100, D=500, M=1000. When you see letters side-by-side, check: is the one on the left smaller than the one on the right? If yes, subtract it. If it is larger or equal, add it up. Break the number into pieces based on these pairs, and you will find your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the seven basic symbols used in the Roman Numeral system and their corresponding values?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The symbols are I=1, V=5, X=10, L=50, C=100, D=500, and M=1000. A wrong answer here suggests the student has not memorized the base values, making any further conversion impossible.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is "IV" equal to 4, but "VI" equal to 6?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
In "IV", I (1) is before V (5), so we subtract 1 from 5 (5-1=4); in "VI", I (1) is after V (5), so we add 1 to 5 (5+1=6). This reveals if the student understands the position-based rule of addition versus subtraction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Convert the Hindu-Arabic number 49 into Roman Numerals.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The answer is XLIX. To solve: 40 is XL (50-10) and 9 is IX (10-1). A student who answers "IL" fails to realize that Roman numerals must be broken down by place value (tens and ones separately).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the Roman Numeral "XCI". Why can't we simply add the values from left to right?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Adding left to right (10+100+1) gives 111, which is incorrect because the "XC" is a subtractive pair (100-10 = 90). The student must analyze the sequence in groups rather than treating each character as an independent addition.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the claim: "You can repeat the symbol 'V' three times to write 15." Is this true or false? Justify your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
False. The rules of Roman numerals state that symbols V, L, and D cannot be repeated; only I, X, C, and M can be repeated (and usually only up to three times). A student saying "True" has not mastered the structural constraints of the system.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a Roman Numeral for the number 384 and explain how you constructed it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The answer is CCCLXXXIV. I constructed it by splitting 384 into 300 (CCC), 80 (LXXX), and 4 (IV). A poor answer shows a failure to group the number by its place-value components (hundreds, tens, and ones).
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to subtract any smaller number from any larger number, such as writing 95 as "VX" (100-5) or 99 as "IC" (100-1). This is incorrect because Roman numerals only allow specific subtractive pairs (like IV, IX, XL, XC, CD, CM) based on powers of ten.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student uses an invalid subtractive pair, ask them to "deconstruct" the number into its tens and ones place first (e.g., 95 is 90 + 5). Show them that 90 must be written as XC (100-10) and 5 as V, resulting in XCV. Remind them that they can never skip a place value when subtracting.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Convert the number 76 into Roman Numerals.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Split 76 into its parts: 70 and 6.
- Step 2: Represent 70 as (50 + 10 + 10) = LXX.
- Step 3: Represent 6 as (5 + 1) = VI.
- Step 4: Combine the parts: LXX + VI = LXXVI.
REASONING: By partitioning the number, we ensure that we represent each part correctly according to the rules of addition, preventing confusion between place values.
ANSWER: LXXVI
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Convert the Roman Numeral "CDXL" into a Hindu-Arabic number.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the pairs: CD and XL.
- Step 2: Calculate CD (500 - 100) = 400.
- Step 3: Calculate XL (50 - 10) = 40.
- Step 4: Add the results: 400 + 40 = 440.
REASONING: We treat the pairs separately because the subtraction rule applies specifically when a smaller letter precedes a larger one within a logical group.
ANSWER: 440
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: You have 345 rupees. Write this amount using Roman Numerals.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the place values: 300, 40, and 5.
- Step 2: Write 300 as CCC (100+100+100).
- Step 3: Write 40 as XL (50-10).
- Step 4: Write 5 as V.
- Step 5: Combine them in order: CCC + XL + V = CCCXLV.
REASONING: Following the standard structure (hundreds, then tens, then ones) ensures the number is written according to the specific Roman system constraints.
ANSWER: CCCXLV rupees
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student claims that the Roman Numeral for 99 is "IC". Is this correct? Explain why or why not, and provide the correct representation.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the claim against Roman rules (only 1 can be subtracted from 5 or 10).
- Step 2: Identify the error: 1 cannot be subtracted from 100.
- Step 3: Partition 99 into 90 + 9.
- Step 4: Convert 90 to XC and 9 to IX.
ANSWER: No, it is incorrect. In Roman numerals, you can only subtract a power of 10 from the next two higher symbols (e.g., 1 from 5/10, 10 from 50/100). The correct representation is XCIX.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why does the Roman system make it harder to perform complex addition compared to our Hindu-Arabic system? Provide an example of an addition that is easier in our system.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that our system is base-10 with place value.
- Step 2: Identify that Roman system lacks place value and requires symbols to be broken apart.
- Step 3: Give an example, such as 15 + 17 = 32 (Hindu-Arabic) vs XV + XVII (Roman).
ANSWER: The Hindu-Arabic system uses place value, allowing us to align digits (units, tens) and carry over easily. In the Roman system, you must convert everything to individual symbols, combine them, and then re-simplify the result, which is prone to error. For example, 15+17 is a simple carry-over in our system, but XV + XVII requires grouping all letters (XXVVII) and then simplifying VV to X to get XXXII.
DIFFICULTY: hard
