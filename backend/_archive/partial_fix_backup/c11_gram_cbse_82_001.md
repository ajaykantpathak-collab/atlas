## DEFINITION
The three conditionals encode reality-distance: Type 1 (if + present, will) = real possibility; Type 2 (if + past, would) = present unreal; Type 3 (if + past perfect, would have + V3) = past unreal, gone forever. 'Were' serves all persons in Type 2 — 'If I were you'.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student selects the correct conditional type by reality-status, forms all three including Type 3 regrets, and uses inversion forms (Had I known, Were I you).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers types 0-3 and formal inversions. Does NOT cover mixed conditionals in depth or wish-constructions.

## WALKTHROUGH
The reality ladder: 'If it rains, I will stay' — could happen. 'If it rained, I would stay' — it is not raining now. 'If it had rained, I would have stayed' — it did not rain; the chance is gone. Formal flip: drop 'if' and invert — 'Had I known, I would have come' = 'If I had known'.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students mix halves across types ('if I would have known'), use 'will' in the if-clause ('if it will rain'), and never learn that Type 2's 'were' covers all persons.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Ask reality-status — possible, present-unreal, or past-gone? Step 2: Match the type's two halves as a SET — never mix. Step 3: 'if' clauses never take 'will/would' directly after 'if' in standard types. Step 4: Inversion = drop 'if', front the auxiliary.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'If I ___ the lottery, I would travel the world.' win/won/would win?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'won' — Type 2 (unreal present): if + past, would + base. A wrong answer 'would win' puts 'would' inside the if-clause — the classic half-mix error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'If she had studied, she ___ passed.' would have / will have?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'would have passed' — Type 3 halves go together: had + V3, would have + V3. A wrong answer 'will have' mixes a Type-1 result into a Type-3 frame.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Rewrite without 'if': 'If I had known, I would have helped.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'Had I known, I would have helped.' — formal inversion fronts the auxiliary and drops 'if'. A wrong answer keeping 'if' plus inversion shows the construction isn't understood as a replacement.
