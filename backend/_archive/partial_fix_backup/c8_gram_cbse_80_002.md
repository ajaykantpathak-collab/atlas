## DEFINITION
Sequence of tenses: when a sentence has two clauses, the tense of the second is constrained by the first. A past main clause drags subordinate verbs back — 'He said he WAS tired' — while a present main clause leaves the second free — 'He says he IS tired.'

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student aligns subordinate-clause tense with the main-clause tense in object clauses, time clauses, and purpose clauses, including the universal-truth exemption.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers tense harmony in reported-style and dependent clauses. Does NOT cover conditional sequences (class 11) or full reported speech (ch 82).

## WALKTHROUGH
The anchor rule: the main verb sets the time-zone; subordinate verbs must live inside it. 'I think she IS right' / 'I thought she WAS right'. Exemptions: timeless truths and habitual facts can stay present — 'He said the earth IS round.'

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students shift every verb blindly after a past main clause — including facts that are still true — or fail to shift at all, writing 'He said he is busy'.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Fix the main clause's time-zone. Step 2: Past zone → subordinate verbs step back one tense. Step 3: Apply the exemption — still-true facts may keep present tense.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'I did not know that she ___ a doctor.' is or was?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'was' — the main clause is past, so the fact-about-her steps back. A wrong answer 'is' shows the student missed the time-zone rule — though 'is' can defend if she still is one, boards expect 'was'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'The teacher explained that the sun ___ in the east.' rise/rises/rose?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'rises' — universal truth exempt from backshift. A wrong answer 'rose' shows mechanical shifting without the exemption — the competent-but-rigid error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'When I was young, I believed that stars ___ tiny lamps.' are/were?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'were' — the belief was false, so no truth-exemption applies; the past zone governs. A wrong answer 'are' misapplies the exemption to a mistaken belief.
