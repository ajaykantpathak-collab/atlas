## DEFINITION
A school-magazine article balances personality and structure: catchy title + byline → engaging intro (question/anecdote) → 2-3 body paragraphs developing the idea → conclusion with a takeaway or suggestion. Unlike reports, articles may have a voice — but register stays semi-formal.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
create

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student writes an article with title+byline, an engaging (not announcing) opening, developed body paragraphs, and a takeaway conclusion — distinguishing article register from both report and essay.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers magazine/newspaper articles at class 11 level. Does NOT cover editorials or feature formats.

## WALKTHROUGH
The genre sits between report and essay: a report forbids voice; an article invites it; an essay argues a thesis. 'Social media: friend or foe?' can carry opinion-flavored analysis — but slang and ranting break the semi-formal contract. Title + byline ('By Ananya, XI-B') are format marks.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students write articles as essays (thesis-argument-conclusion, no title/byline) or as reports (dry facts, no engagement) — missing the middle register that defines the genre.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Title (catchy, short) + byline. Step 2: Open with a question, anecdote, or startling fact — never 'I am going to write about'. Step 3: Body develops 2-3 angles with examples. Step 4: Close with a takeaway/suggestion, not a summary.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Article vs report — which opener is article-register: 'The fest was held on 5 May' or 'Five hundred students, zero phones, one unforgettable evening — this year's fest rewrote the rules'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The second — articles may perform voice and scene; reports must front-load plain facts. A wrong answer shows genre-boundary confusion.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
What format elements does an article need that an essay does not?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
A catchy title and a byline ('By Name, Class'). Essays carry no byline. A wrong answer 'subject line' imports the letter format.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Is 'Yo, phones are literally destroying our brains fr' acceptable in a school-magazine article?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
No — articles allow voice but hold semi-formal register; slang breaks the contract. A wrong answer shows the student over-corrected from formal to casual.
