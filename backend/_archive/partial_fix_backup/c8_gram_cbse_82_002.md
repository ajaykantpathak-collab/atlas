## DEFINITION
Reported questions and commands change shape, not just tense: questions lose their question-order and question mark ('Where do you live?' → 'He asked where I lived'); commands become 'told + object + to-infinitive' ('Sit down' → 'He told me to sit down').

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student reports yes/no questions with if/whether, wh-questions with the wh-word kept, and commands with the told+object+to-infinitive frame — including negatives ('not to').

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the two non-statement report frames. Does NOT cover exclamations or reporting-verb nuance beyond told/asked/ordered/requested.

## WALKTHROUGH
De-interrogate: 'Did she come?' → 'He asked IF she had come.' The question dies — no do/does/did, no question mark, statement order returns. Commands: the frame is [reporting verb] + [object] + [to + base]; 'Don't run' → 'told me not to run' — 'not' sits before 'to'.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students keep question grammar inside reports ('asked where did I go'), glue commands with 'that' ('told that sit down'), and drop the object ('told to sit' — told WHOM?).

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Type-check — yes/no → if/whether; wh- → keep the wh-word; command → to-infinitive. Step 2: Kill question order and do-support. Step 3: Commands need an object — who is being told.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Report: 'Do you like cricket?' he asked me.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'He asked me if/whether I liked cricket.' A wrong answer 'he asked did I like' keeps question-order inside the report — the core error this node targets.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Report: 'Where have you been?' mother asked.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'Mother asked where I had been.' wh-word kept, order restored, have→had. A wrong answer 'that where I had been' glues 'that' onto a wh-question — illegal.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Report: 'Don't touch the fence,' the guard said to us.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'The guard told/ordered us not to touch the fence.' A wrong answer 'told to not touch' misses the object; 'not to touch' is the preferred negative placement.
