## DEFINITION
Formal register bans contractions (can't→cannot), slang, exclamation marks, first-person emotive phrasing, and vague fillers; it prefers precise nouns, passive constructions for objectivity, and courteous fixed phrases (I am writing to…, I would be grateful, kindly).

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Given an informal draft, the student rewrites every informal marker — contractions, slang, emoticons of tone, vague quantities — into formal equivalents while preserving content, and can name the register rule each edit satisfies.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers lexical and tonal register rules. Does NOT cover letter skeleton (90_001) or essay register (chapter 93).

## WALKTHROUGH
Contrast two openings: 'I wanna complain about the garbage — it's really bad and smells gross!!' vs 'I wish to draw your attention to the non-collection of garbage in Ward 12.' Same complaint, different social costume. List the translation table: wanna→wish to, really bad→a matter of serious concern, gross→unhygienic, !!→full stop.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students think formal = long sentences and fancy words, producing stiff-but-wrong prose with slang intact ('the issue is like really serious'). The real rules are deletions: no contractions, no slang, no exclamations, no vagueness.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Hunt the banned list — contractions, slang, !!, 'really/very/so' intensifiers, vague words (stuff, things, a lot). Step 2: Replace each with the formal equivalent. Step 3: Convert emotional statements to factual claims ('smells gross' → 'poses a health hazard'). Step 4: Keep the facts, lose the performance.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Fix the register: 'I'm writing coz the streetlight near my house hasn't worked for like two weeks and it's super annoying.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'I am writing to report that the streetlight near my residence has been non-functional for two weeks.' Every marker fixed: I'm→I am, coz→to report, like→(deleted), super annoying→non-functional. A wrong answer keeping contractions or 'super' shows the student treats register as optional polish, not as scored content.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Which opener for a letter to the editor: 'You won't believe what's happening in our park!' or 'Through the columns of your esteemed daily, I wish to highlight…'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The second — formal public register, no exclamation, no direct tease. A wrong answer preferring the first reveals the student confuses attention-grabbing (informal) with addressing (formal) — the genre confusion this node targets.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Is 'I would be grateful if you could look into this' appropriate as a letter's request line?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Yes — courteous fixed phrase, polite indirection, the standard closing move of a formal request. A wrong answer calling it 'too weak' or replacing it with 'You must fix this' reveals the student thinks formality means assertiveness — it's the opposite: formal register commands through courtesy.
