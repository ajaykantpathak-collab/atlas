## DEFINITION
Some verbs insist on gerunds (enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest), some insist on infinitives (want, decide, promise, learn, agree), and a few take both with meaning shifts (stop smoking ≠ stop to smoke; remember doing ≠ remember to do).

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student supplies the correct non-finite after common verbs, applies the preference lists, and explains meaning-flips in stop/remember/try + gerund-vs-infinitive pairs.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the high-frequency preference lists and the three meaning-flip verbs. Does NOT cover infinitive of purpose or causative structures.

## WALKTHROUGH
The meaning-flips are the treasure: 'He stopped smoking' = quit the habit; 'He stopped to smoke' = paused in order to smoke — opposite actions. 'Remember to lock' = don't forget (future task); 'remember locking' = recall the past act. The non-finite is a time-direction switch.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students use infinitives everywhere ('enjoy to swim'), never learn the preference lists, and miss the time-direction logic in flip-verbs.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Learn the two lists — enjoy/avoid/finish/mind/suggest + gerund; want/decide/promise/learn/agree + infinitive. Step 2: For stop/remember/try, ask time-direction — gerund looks back/at the act, infinitive looks forward to purpose.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'I enjoy ___ (swim) in the morning.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'swimming' — 'enjoy' takes gerunds only. A wrong answer 'to swim' shows the universal-infinitive habit.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'She stopped ___ (smoke).' = she quit the habit. Which form?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'smoking' — gerund = the stopped activity. 'to smoke' would mean she paused in order to smoke — the opposite meaning. The flip is the whole lesson.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'Please remember ___ (lock) the door.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'to lock' — a future task needs the infinitive ('don't forget to'). 'Locking' would mean recalling a past act of locking.
