## DEFINITION
Reporting speech moves every tense one step back: present→past, past→past perfect, will→would, can→could, am/is→was, have→had. The shift exists because the reporter stands in a later now; the original speaker's 'now' becomes 'then', 'today' becomes 'that day', 'here' becomes 'there'.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student converts statements, questions, and commands between direct and indirect speech with correct backshift, pronoun re-anchoring, and time/place expression changes, in both directions.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the mechanical backshift table and deictic changes for all three sentence types. Does NOT cover reporting questions' word-order restoration (93_002) or command structures (93_003).

## WALKTHROUGH
Build the machine visibly: Direct 'I am tired today' → Reported 'She said (that) she was tired that day.' Highlight every change: I→she (speaker re-anchor), am→was (backshift), today→that day (deictic shift). Then the rule of thumb: walk the sentence back in time and re-anchor every pointing word to the reporter's position.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students backshift the verb but forget pronouns and time words ('He said he is tired today'), or over-shift when the fact is still true — 'The teacher said the earth revolves around the sun' keeps 'revolves' because universal truths resist backshift.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Identify speaker, listener, content. Step 2: Re-anchor pronouns to the reporter's view. Step 3: Backshift every tense one step (unless universal truth). Step 4: Swap deictics — now→then, today→that day, here→there, this→that, ago→before. Step 5: Read the result as the reporter would hear it.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Report: She said, 'I will finish the project tomorrow.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'She said (that) she would finish the project the next day.' will→would, I→she, tomorrow→the next day. A wrong answer keeping 'will' or 'tomorrow' reveals the student did a partial transform — verb only — missing that reported speech re-anchors ALL pointing words, not just the verb.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Report: 'The earth is round,' the teacher said.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'The teacher said (that) the earth is round' — universal truths keep present tense. A wrong answer 'was round' reveals over-shifting: the student applied the table mechanically without the truth-exemption — the classic competent-but-rigid error boards love to test.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Report: He said, 'I saw this film here yesterday.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'He said he had seen that film there the day before.' saw→had seen (past→past perfect), this→that, here→there, yesterday→the day before. A wrong answer 'he saw that film here yesterday' shows the student backshifted nothing but pronouns — the deictic field is invisible to them.
