## DEFINITION
Non-finite verbs don't carry tense or agree with subjects — they are verb-forms doing other jobs: infinitives (to go) act as nouns, gerunds (going) act as nouns, participles (going/gone) act as adjectives. 'To err is human' — 'to err' is the subject, a thing, not an action-in-time.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student identifies infinitive, gerund, and participle forms in sentences, names the job each is doing (noun vs adjective), and distinguishes gerunds from present participles.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers identification and function of the three non-finites. Does NOT cover the verb-preference lists (enjoy + gerund, want + infinitive — 84_002).

## WALKTHROUGH
Same word, different job: 'Swimming is fun' — 'swimming' is the subject, a thing = gerund. 'The swimming champion smiled' — 'swimming' describes champion = participle. 'She loves to swim' — 'to swim' is what she loves = infinitive. The job in the sentence names the form, not its shape.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students classify by word-shape — every -ing becomes 'present participle' to them — missing that gerund vs participle is decided by FUNCTION (noun-job vs adjective-job), not spelling.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Spot the verb-form (to+V, V-ing, V3/en). Step 2: Ask its job — naming a thing/idea → gerund or infinitive; describing a noun → participle. Step 3: 'to' + base = infinitive; V-ing as noun = gerund.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'Walking is good exercise.' — gerund or participle, and why?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Gerund — 'walking' is the subject, naming an activity as a thing. A wrong answer 'participle' shows shape-classification instead of job-classification.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'She wants to learn French.' — what is 'to learn' doing?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Infinitive acting as the object of 'wants' — what she wants is to-learn-French. A wrong answer calling it a 'helping verb' misses that infinitives don't help, they name.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'The broken window frightened the children.' — what is 'broken'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Past participle used as an adjective describing 'window'. A wrong answer 'verb' misses that 'frightened' is the sentence's verb; 'broken' only describes.
