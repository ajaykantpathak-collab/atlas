## DEFINITION
Passive voice flips the spotlight: the object becomes the subject, the verb becomes be + V3, and the doer moves to 'by…' or disappears. 'The gardener planted the roses' → 'The roses were planted by the gardener.' The 'be' helper carries the tense.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student converts active↔passive in all four basic tenses, keeps tense inside the be-auxiliary, and drops vague agents (someone, people) instead of keeping them.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers standard transitive conversion in simple, continuous, perfect tenses. Does NOT cover passive with modals in depth or stative passives.

## WALKTHROUGH
The surgery in three cuts: (1) object walks to subject position; (2) verb → be + V3 where 'be' carries BOTH the original tense AND the new subject's number; (3) old subject → 'by' phrase, or delete it when it is vague ('someone stole' → 'was stolen', not 'by someone').

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students convert the participle but forget 'be' ('the letter written'), let the tense drift ('was stolen' becomes 'is stolen'), or keep junk agents ('by someone').

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Object → subject. Step 2: Verb → be(tense, matching new subject) + V3. Step 3: Doer → 'by + agent' only if it adds information — vague agents delete. Step 4: Sanity check — is the receiver the point?

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Passive: 'The students clean the classroom every day.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'The classroom is cleaned by the students every day.' A wrong answer 'is clean' or 'was cleaned' shows tense drift — 'be' must keep the original present tense.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Passive: 'Someone has broken the window.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'The window has been broken' — vague agent 'someone' deletes, not 'by someone'. A wrong answer keeping 'by someone' shows the junk-agent error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Active: 'The novel was written by Premchand.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'Premchand wrote the novel.' A wrong answer 'Premchand was written' reverses the surgery — object and subject swapped wrongly.
