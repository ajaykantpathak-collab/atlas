## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you're about to watch a play but the programme notes are in a language you barely understand—you'd miss half the meaning. That's exactly what happens when students jump into Agyeya's essay without knowing the theatre history he's referencing. Start by asking yourself: "What makes a play 'literary' versus 'performative'?" Then trace how Indian theatre moved from Sanskrit classics to Parsi theatre to the modern realism of Mohan Rakesh. ## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
According to the background context, what is the fundamental difference between साहित्यिक नाट्य (literary drama) and रंगमंचीय नाट्य (stage/visual drama) in terms of their primary medium and reception?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
साहित्यिक नाट्य is written primarily for reading—its medium is language on the page, and its reception is individual, silent, and paced by the reader. रंगमंचीय नाट्य is written for performance—its medium includes sound, light, silence, movement, and spatial design, and its reception is collective, immediate, and time-bound. A wrong answer (e.g., "one is old, one is new" or "one is better than the other") reveals the student is confusing historical period with aesthetic category, missing Agyeya's central argument that these are two distinct art forms with different grammars.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Agyeya states: "चित्रकाव्य नाट्य के कारण लेखिक नाट्य की परम्परा उपेक्षित नहीं हुई, बल्कि दर्शकों का साहित्यिक नाट्य को देखने का दृष्टिकोण भी बदल गया।" Explain in your own words what this means for the relationship between the two forms.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Visual drama did not replace or diminish literary drama; instead, it transformed how audiences approach literary drama—viewers now bring performance-aware expectations (visualization, pacing, sensory imagination) to their reading of plays. A wrong answer (e.g., "visual drama killed literary drama" or "they have nothing to do with each other") shows the student hasn't grasped Agyeya's nuanced view: the forms are in dialogue, not competition, and the modern reader of drama is inevitably shaped by stage conventions.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
The text mentions three historical phases: classical Sanskrit theatre (with its निर्दिष्ट नियम), Parsi/folk theatre (लोक-रंजक), and modern realistic theatre (Mohan Rakesh). Apply this framework: if a contemporary Hindi play uses प्राकृतिक संवाद (naturalistic dialogue), मनोवैज्ञानिक गहराई (psychological depth), and इकाई स्थान-काल (unity of place-time), which phase does it inherit from primarily, and why?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
It inherits primarily from the modern realistic phase (Mohan Rakesh). Classical Sanskrit theatre follows codified rules (नाट्यशैली) with stylized speech and symbolic time/space. Parsi/folk theatre prioritizes entertainment (लोक-रंजक) with melodrama and music. Mohan Rakesh introduced Ibsen-inspired realism: everyday speech, psychological interiority, and concentrated temporal-spatial settings. A student who answers "classical" or "Parsi" is conflating historical sequence with aesthetic genealogy—missing that realism is a specific break from both earlier traditions.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze this claim from the text: "रंगमंचीय नाट्य का रचयिता ... दृश्य और श्रव्य दोनों माध्यमों का उपयोग करता है।" Compare the 'authorial control' in literary drama versus visual drama. Who decides the pace, the tone, the imagery—the writer or the director/actor?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
In literary drama, the writer controls pace (through sentence length, punctuation), tone (through word choice), and imagery (through description)—the reader realizes these internally. In visual drama, the writer provides a blueprint; the director, actors, designers, and technicians collectively realize pace (through timing, pauses), tone (through voice, lighting, music), and imagery (through sets, costumes, movement). The playwright surrenders final control to the collaborative event. A student who says "the writer controls both" fails to see that visual drama is inherently collaborative and ephemeral—its 'text' is the performance, not the script alone.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate Agyeya's assertion: "चित्रकाव्य नाट्य ने लोगों के 'दक़ देखने की दृष्टि' बदल दी।" Is this an overstatement? Defend your position with evidence from the text's description of how modern audiences experience theatre versus earlier forms.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Not an overstatement. The text details concrete shifts: audiences now perceive sound as meaningful (not just noise), silence as dramatic (not empty), lighting as narrative (not illumination), and psychological realism as expected (not melodramatic). Earlier, theatre was घटना-प्रधान (event-driven) and लोक-रंजक; now it's चरित्र-प्रधान and मनोवैज्ञानिक. The 'gaze' has moved from "what happens next?" to "what does this moment reveal?" A student calling it an overstatement likely equates 'changed perspective' with 'every individual changed,' missing that Agyeya describes a cultural shift in theatrical literacy, not universal individual transformation.
