## DEFINITION
Relative clauses join with who/whom/whose/which/that — and the choice carries meaning: who/whom for people, which for things, that for either in defining clauses, whose for possession. A comma-wrapped relative clause is non-defining (extra info); without commas it defines which one.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student selects the correct relative pronoun, distinguishes defining from non-defining clauses by commas and meaning, and uses whom/whose correctly.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers relative pronoun selection and the defining/non-defining comma contrast. Does NOT cover reduced relatives ('the man standing there').

## WALKTHROUGH
The comma test: 'My brother, who lives in Delhi, is a doctor' — commas say I have one brother; the clause is a side-note. 'My brother who lives in Delhi is a doctor' — no commas; the clause picks WHICH brother. 'That' is banned after commas — non-defining clauses need who/which.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students use 'that' everywhere including after commas ('My father, that is a doctor,…'), drop 'whose' for 'of which' clumsily, and ignore that commas change the meaning, not just the style.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: People → who/whom; things → which/that; possession → whose. Step 2: Defining (which-one) → no commas, 'that' allowed. Step 3: Non-defining (extra info) → commas, only who/which — never 'that'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'The girl ___ won the prize is my sister.' who/which/that?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'who' or 'that' — defining clause about a person. A wrong answer 'which' misassigns the person/thing boundary.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'My car, ___ is ten years old, still runs well.' that or which?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'which' — comma-wrapped non-defining clauses cannot take 'that'. A wrong answer 'that' shows the comma-rule gap.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Does the meaning change: 'Students who cheat fail' vs 'Students, who cheat, fail'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Yes — first says only cheating students fail (defining); second says all students cheat and all fail (non-defining). A wrong answer 'same meaning' reveals the student reads commas as decoration.
