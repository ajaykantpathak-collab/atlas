## DEFINITION
English has exactly twelve tense forms — three times (present, past, future) crossed with four aspects (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect-continuous). Every sentence a student writes sits in exactly one cell of this grid, and the board's gap-fill and editing questions are all grid-navigation problems.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can place any given verb phrase into the correct cell of the 3×4 tense grid, name its time and aspect, and produce the same meaning in at least two other cells on demand.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the structure and meaning of all 12 forms with regular verbs. Does NOT cover stative-verb restrictions (node 90_002), sequence of tenses in reported/complex sentences (90_004), or modal perfects (chapter 92).

## WALKTHROUGH
Draw the grid on the board: rows = time, columns = aspect. Take ONE idea — 'Riya writes a letter' — and move it through all twelve cells aloud: writes / is writing / has written / has been writing, then past, then future. Students hear the same action wearing twelve different grammatical outfits. The aha: aspect is about HOW the action is viewed (done? ongoing? completed-with-relevance?), not WHEN.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students believe the tense name describes the verb alone ('wrote = past tense of write') and miss that aspect — the helper verbs — carries half the meaning. They also treat 'has been writing' as present tense because 'has' looks present.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Strip the sentence to subject + main verb + helpers. Step 2: Ask 'when does the action happen?' → that fixes the row. Step 3: Ask 'is it done, ongoing, or completed-but-connected?' → that fixes the column. Step 4: Rebuild the verb phrase from the cell, not from memory. Step 5: Say the same fact in simple and perfect form aloud and name what changed.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Sort these into time rows — past, present, future: 'She has finished', 'She will be finishing', 'She had finished'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'She has finished' → present (perfect aspect anchors it to now). 'She will be finishing' → future. 'She had finished' → past. A wrong answer putting 'has finished' in the past reveals the student reads the participle 'finished' as the tense carrier instead of the auxiliary 'has' — the tense lives in the helper, not the main verb.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Change ONLY the aspect, keep the time: 'He played cricket' → two other past forms.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'He was playing cricket' (continuous) and 'He had played cricket' (perfect). A wrong answer like 'he plays' or 'he will play' shows the student changed the TIME row, not the aspect column — they cannot yet decouple the two axes, which is the whole point of the grid.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Which form fits: 'At this moment, the baby ___ (cry).' Defend your choice.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'is crying' — present continuous, because 'at this moment' marks an ongoing action in present time. A wrong answer 'cries' or 'is cried' reveals the student matches tense to the verb's dictionary feel rather than to the time-marker in the sentence.
