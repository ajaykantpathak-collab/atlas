## DEFINITION
Present perfect connects a past action to NOW: 'I have finished' — the work is done AND matters now. The signal words are just/already/yet/ever/never/since/for. It differs from simple past, which is dead-and-gone: 'I finished yesterday' — done, closed, dated.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student chooses present perfect vs simple past using the connection-to-now test and signal words, forms have/has + V3 correctly, and knows yesterday/ago words KILL the perfect.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers present perfect vs simple past. Does NOT cover past perfect or perfect-continuous.

## WALKTHROUGH
The bridge rule: present perfect = past action with a bridge to now; simple past = past action in a closed box. 'I have lost my keys' — still lost (bridge). 'I lost my keys yesterday' — the story ended yesterday (box). Time-stamp words (yesterday, ago, last week) force the box — you cannot say 'I have seen it yesterday'.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students use present perfect with finished time ('I have gone yesterday'), and Hindi's single past makes both forms feel identical — the bridge test is invisible to them.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Is there a finished time-word (yesterday/ago/last)? → simple past, forced. Step 2: Does the result still matter now? → present perfect. Step 3: Form: have/has + V3 (eaten, gone, done).

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'I ___ (finish) my homework just now' vs 'I ___ (finish) it an hour ago.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
have finished / finished — 'just now' bridges to now; 'an hour ago' is a dead time-stamp → simple past. A wrong answer 'have finished' for the second shows the dated-perfect error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Fix: 'She has visited Agra last year.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'She visited Agra last year' — 'last year' is a closed box; present perfect cannot carry a finished time. A wrong answer keeping 'has visited' shows the signal-word rule isn't applied.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Which fits: 'I ___ never ___ (see) the Taj Mahal.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'have never seen' — 'never' is a perfect signal (life experience up to now). 'Seen' must be V3, not 'saw' — 'have saw' is a broken participle error.
