## DEFINITION
An informal invitation or reply inside a letter follows the warm-skeleton rules but adds event logistics: the invite must carry What/When/Where clearly, and the reply must accept or decline with a reason — both in first-person friendly register.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
create

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student writes an invitation containing all event logistics and a reply that accepts/declines with a stated reason, keeping informal register throughout.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers informal invitations and replies. Does NOT cover formal invitations (the card format with third-person wording).

## WALKTHROUGH
The logistics checklist inside the warmth: What (birthday dinner), When (Saturday 7 pm), Where (our place). The reply contract: answer the question asked — accept with enthusiasm or decline with a real reason and regret. 'I'd love to come!' or 'So sorry — we are travelling that weekend.'

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students write warm letters that forget the actual logistics (no time or place), or replies that never clearly say yes or no.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Invitation = warm greeting + What/When/Where + 'do come'. Step 2: Reply = thanks + clear accept/decline + reason if declining + warm close. Step 3: Informal register throughout — contractions and exclamations welcome.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is missing: 'Hi Neha, having a party at my place, do come! Love, Riya.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
When and What-occasion — 'party' with no date, time, or reason is unusable. A wrong answer 'nothing' shows the logistics checklist isn't running.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Reply to a birthday invite you can't attend — which line is right: 'Can't come, busy.' or 'So sorry Riya — we're visiting Nani that weekend. Hope it's the best party ever!'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The second — clear decline + reason + warmth. The first is curt to the point of rudeness; informal register still has manners.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Does an informal invitation letter need the receiver's address block at top?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
No — informal letters carry only the sender's address + date. A wrong answer adding the receiver block merges the formal and informal skeletons.
