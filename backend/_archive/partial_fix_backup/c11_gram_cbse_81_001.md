## DEFINITION
Transformation repackages one truth into different containers — simple (one finite verb), compound (coordinated equals), complex (subordinated). 'He was too tired to work' ≡ 'He was so tired that he could not work' ≡ 'He was tired, so he could not work' — the examiner scores meaning-preservation through structure.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student transforms among simple/compound/complex using too…to/so…that, participle reductions, and nominalizations, preserving truth conditions exactly.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the ICSE-style transformation inventory. Does NOT cover degree transformations (class 10) or synthesis of multiple sentences.

## WALKTHROUGH
The too-so engine: 'too tired to work' ↔ 'so tired that he could not work' — 'too' + adjective + 'to' hides a negative result that 'so…that + cannot' makes explicit. Participle reduction: 'Because he was tired, he slept' → 'Being tired, he slept' — the subordinate clause shrinks to a phrase.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students transform the words but not the logic — 'He was too tired to work' → 'He was so tired to work' drops the result clause and breaks the equivalence.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Identify the hidden logic — cause-result, concession, condition. Step 2: Pick the target container. Step 3: Rebuild preserving every logical piece — check that nothing (negation, condition) was dropped.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Transform to complex: 'He was too tired to work.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'He was so tired that he could not work.' 'too…to' = 'so…that + cannot'. A wrong answer 'so tired to work' loses the negative-result clause — the equivalence breaks.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Make it simple: 'Although he was ill, he attended the meeting.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'In spite of his illness, he attended the meeting' (or 'Despite being ill…'). A wrong answer keeping 'although' shows the student renamed rather than restructured — the clause must shrink to a phrase.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Transform: 'If you do not study, you will fail' → using 'unless'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'Unless you study, you will fail.' 'unless' = 'if not' — the negation moves into the connector. A wrong answer 'unless you do not study' double-negates the condition.
