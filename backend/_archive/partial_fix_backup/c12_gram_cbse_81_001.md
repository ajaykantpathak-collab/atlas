## DEFINITION
Phrasal verbs fuse verb + particle into a new meaning the parts don't predict: 'put off' = postpone, 'give in' = yield, 'look after' = care for. Separability matters: 'put off the meeting' / 'put the meeting off' both work, but pronouns force splitting — 'put it off', never 'put off it'.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student interprets common phrasal verbs in context, applies the pronoun-splitting rule to separable verbs, and distinguishes literal from idiomatic particle meanings.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers ~30 high-frequency phrasal verbs and the separability rule. Does NOT cover exhaustive lists or prepositional verbs vs phrasal verbs distinction formally.

## WALKTHROUGH
The pronoun rule is the testable core: separable phrasal verbs MUST split around pronouns — 'turn it down', 'pick her up', 'put it off' — while noun objects may split or stay together. Inseparables never split: 'look after the baby' but never 'look the baby after' — 'after' is glued.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students interpret particles literally ('put off' = put off a surface), split inseparables, and place pronouns after particles ('turn down it') — all three are marker-favourites.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Learn phrasal verbs as single vocabulary items, not verb+particle sums. Step 2: Pronoun object → must split (verb + pronoun + particle). Step 3: Inseparables (look after, run into, get over) never split.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'Please turn ___ the volume — it is too loud.' (down) — where does 'it' go if we replace 'the volume'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'turn it down' — pronouns must split separable phrasal verbs; 'turn down it' is illegal. A wrong answer 'turn down it' shows the pronoun rule missing.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'She ran ___ an old friend at the market.' into/across/after?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'into' — 'run into' = meet by chance; 'ran after' = chased. A wrong answer shows literal-particle reading instead of the fused meaning.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Which is wrong: 'look after the baby' / 'look the baby after' / 'look after her'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'look the baby after' — 'look after' is inseparable; the object follows the whole unit. Pronouns too: 'look after her' — inseparables never split even for pronouns.
