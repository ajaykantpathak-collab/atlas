## DEFINITION
Word order is not decoration — English meaning rides on position. 'The dog chased the cat' and 'The cat chased the dog' use the same words to say opposite things. Normal order is Subject–Verb–Object; moving words changes who does what.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student rearranges jumbled words into a grammatical S–V–O sentence, preserves meaning while reordering phrases, and detects when a rearrangement silently flips actor and receiver.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers affirmative S–V–O order and phrase-level reordering. Does NOT cover question inversion or passive voice (class 8).

## WALKTHROUGH
Jumble: 'school / to / goes / Meena / every day'. Rebuild: Meena goes to school every day. Then the trap pair: 'Ravi helped Mohan' vs 'Mohan helped Ravi' — same words, opposite helpers. Meaning lives in positions, not just words.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students accept any order that 'sounds okay' from spoken English — especially putting time phrases anywhere — and don't notice when actor and receiver swap silently.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Find the verb. Step 2: Ask who does it — that is the subject, put it first. Step 3: What receives it — the object. Step 4: Attach time/place phrases at the end (or front with a comma).

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Reorder: 'yesterday / the match / won / our team'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'Our team won the match yesterday.' A wrong answer 'Yesterday won our team the match' shows the student treats 'yesterday' as a movable prefix without checking what still makes grammatical sense.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Do these mean the same? 'Only Ravi ate the mango' / 'Ravi ate only the mango.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No — 'only' attaches to what follows it: first says nobody else ate, second says he ate nothing else. A wrong answer 'yes' reveals the student thinks 'only' floats freely — position changes meaning.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Fix: 'To school every day goes Meena.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'Meena goes to school every day.' A wrong answer keeping verb-first order shows the student is translating Hindi SOV word order — English needs the verb between subject and object.
