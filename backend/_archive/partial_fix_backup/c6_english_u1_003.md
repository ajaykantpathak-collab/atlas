## DEFINITION
A theme is the central, underlying message or "big idea" about life that an author explores within a story, such as the value of hard work or the importance of persistence. Determination is the quality of continuing to work toward a goal despite obstacles or the temptation to give up.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the core theme of a story and provide evidence (actions or choices of characters) that supports how that theme is developed.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers identifying themes like "Dreams and Determination" within narrative texts and interpreting character motivations. It does not cover external literary criticism or comparing this theme to other literary works.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wanted to learn a new skill, like playing the guitar or drawing, but felt like quitting because it was too hard? To understand a theme, imagine the story is a conversation where the author is trying to teach you a life lesson. First, look at what the main character wants (their dream). Second, look at what they do when things get difficult (their determination). Finally, look at how the story ends—does their hard work lead to success? By connecting these three points, you can uncover the "hidden" message the author wants you to remember.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is a 'theme' in a story?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A theme is the main message or life lesson an author shares. A wrong answer (like "the plot" or "the summary") reveals that the student is confusing *what happens* in the story with *why it matters*.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a character keeps trying to reach their goal even when people tell them it is impossible, what theme does this show?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
This shows the theme of determination. A student who says "greed" or "luck" is likely focusing on the outcome rather than the character's internal effort and persistence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
In a story, if a character stops working toward their goal because they are tired, what does this tell you about their level of determination?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
It shows their determination is low or weak. A student who cannot explain this connection needs to practice linking character actions (giving up) to character traits (lack of determination).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why might an author choose to show a character failing many times before they finally succeed?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
To highlight that determination is a process, not a one-time event. A student who misses this likely views "success" as something that should happen instantly, missing the point of the struggle.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Can a dream be considered a 'necessity' rather than a 'luxury'? Explain your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, because dreams provide the motivation needed to improve one's life. A student who argues it is a luxury hasn't grasped that dreams serve as the fuel for human growth and action.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Think of a person you know or a character from a book who showed great determination. Write a sentence explaining how their dream changed their actions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The student should identify a specific action taken because of a goal. A poor answer would be a vague statement like "they worked hard" without explaining *what* they were working toward.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the "Topic" (e.g., "Bananas" or "Gold") with the "Theme" (e.g., "The value of hard work"). They might say the theme of a story is "farming," when the theme is actually "the reward of persistence." This happens because topics are concrete and easy to name, while themes require abstract thinking.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student confuses topic and theme, ask: "What did the character learn?" rather than "What was the story about?" Have them list the character's biggest challenge and the final result. Then, ask them to form a sentence that connects those two points into a life lesson.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Analyze the theme of "A Bottle of Dew" regarding the character Rama Natha.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify what Rama Natha wanted (the magic potion for gold).
- step 2: Identify the action he took (tended the banana plantation for years).
- step 3: Identify the outcome (he found wealth through the plantation, not magic).
- step 4: Connect the effort to the outcome (his hard work created the wealth).
- step 5: State the theme (Hard work creates its own "magic" or success).
REASONING: By mapping the goal against the actual result, we see the author's message isn't about magic, but about the value of consistent labor.
ANSWER: The theme is that determination and hard work are the true paths to success.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A character wants to win a race but has a hurt leg. They keep running every day. What is the theme?
STEPS:
- step 1: Character goal = winning the race.
- step 2: Obstacle = hurt leg.
- step 3: Action = keeps running/training.
- step 4: Relationship = the persistence despite pain shows commitment.
- step 5: Theme = The power of human will and determination.
REASONING: The focus is not on the injury, but on the character's refusal to stop, which defines the theme.
ANSWER: The theme is the power of persistence and determination in overcoming physical limitations.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Compare a character who gives up when things get hard to one who persists.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify character A's behavior (quitting).
- step 2: Identify character B's behavior (persisting).
- step 3: Contrast the outcomes of both.
- step 4: Identify the lesson the author wants for the reader.
- step 5: Formulate the theme.
- step 6: Explain why B represents the theme.
REASONING: Comparing two opposite reactions makes the theme much clearer for the reader.
ANSWER: Character B illustrates the theme of determination, showing that perseverance leads to growth, whereas Character A shows that quitting leads to stagnation.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a story shows a character failing, succeeding, and then helping others, what is the theme?
COMPETENCY: Synthesis of character arc into theme.
STEPS:
- step 1: Track the character's journey from struggle to success.
- step 2: Note the character's final action (helping others).
- step 3: Connect success to the responsibility of helping.
- step 4: Formulate the message.
ANSWER: The theme is that success is most meaningful when it is used to help others, showing growth from personal ambition to communal care.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why does the author use the symbol of a 'river' or 'dew' to teach a lesson about determination?
COMPETENCY: Symbolic interpretation.
STEPS:
- step 1: Define what the symbol represents (e.g., flow, nature, persistence).
- step 2: Relate the symbol to the character's effort (e.g., steady collection of dew).
- step 3: Explain the author's purpose (to make the lesson memorable).
- step 4: Connect back to the theme (determination is as natural and steady as the river/dew).
- step 5: Final conclusion.
ANSWER: Symbols make abstract themes like 'determination' feel like real, natural parts of life, suggesting that if we are as steady as the dew or the river, we will eventually reach our goals.
DIFFICULTY: hard
