## DEFINITION
Narrative poetry is a form of poetry that tells a complete story, featuring a plot with a beginning, middle, and end, as well as characters, a setting, and a sequence of events. Unlike poems that only express personal feelings, narrative poems are designed to take the reader on a journey through a series of actions.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the core components of a narrative poem (characters, setting, plot) in a new piece of text and explain how the sequence of events drives the story forward.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers identifying features of narrative poetry (plot, setting, characters). It excludes epic poetry (long-form heroic tales) and dramatic poetry (plays written in verse).
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever told a friend about a movie you saw, explaining how it started, what the main characters did, and how it ended? A narrative poem does exactly that, but uses rhythm and poetic language to tell the story. To begin, look for a "who" (the characters) and a "where" (the setting). Then, trace the "what happened" (the plot) from the opening scene to the final resolution. Just like a storybook, a narrative poem isn't just about a feeling; it is about an experience or a lesson learned through events. The key insight is that even in a poem, the story must move from one point to another to reach a conclusion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the three essential components that make a poem a "narrative" poem?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A narrative poem must have characters, a setting, and a plot (sequence of events). A student who lists only "rhyme" or "feelings" shows they are confusing narrative poetry with general lyric or sound-based poetry.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a poem describes the beauty of a sunset without mentioning any characters or actions, is it a narrative poem? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, it is not a narrative poem because it lacks a plot, characters, and a sequence of events. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks any poem that uses descriptive words is a story.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A short poem describes a squirrel finding an acorn, hiding it, and then forgetting where it buried it. Identify the plot sequence here.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Beginning: The squirrel finds the acorn. Middle: The squirrel hides it. End: The squirrel forgets the location. Failing to identify these shows the student is not tracking the action sequence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How does the presence of a "problem" or "conflict" help a narrative poem move from the beginning to the end?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The problem creates a reason for characters to act, pushing the plot forward toward a solution. If a student says "it makes the poem sad," they are focusing on mood rather than the structural role of conflict in a narrative.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
In "The Raven and the Fox," the fox tricks the raven to get the food. Does this make it a narrative poem? Explain your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, because it has characters (Raven, Fox), a setting (a tree in the woods), and a plot (the fox tricks the raven, the raven loses its food). A wrong answer indicates the student is not distinguishing between "event-driven" stories and simple descriptions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a one-sentence "beginning" for a narrative poem about a boy who finds a lost puppy.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The answer must include a character and a starting action (e.g., "Rohan walked through the park and heard a soft whimper behind the bushes"). A poor answer would be "The puppy was very cute," as it is a description, not an event that starts a plot.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse "descriptive poetry" (imagery) with "narrative poetry" (storytelling). They assume that because a poem has words and imagery, it must be a story, failing to realize that narrative poetry requires a sequence of events that change the situation of the characters.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student struggles, ask them: "Does this poem tell you what happened next, or does it just tell you what something looks like?" Have them map the poem on a timeline: Step 1 (Beginning), Step 2 (Middle/Conflict), Step 3 (End). If they cannot fill in all three steps, it is likely not a narrative poem.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Analyze the narrative features of a poem where a farmer plants seeds, waits for rain, and finally harvests wheat.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the main character (The farmer).
- Step 2: Identify the setting (The farm/field).
- Step 3: Identify the sequence of events (Planting, waiting for rain, harvesting).
- Step 4: Confirm if it moves from beginning to end (Yes, the status of the field changes from empty to full).
REASONING: Following these steps confirms the existence of the three pillars of narrative poetry: Character, Setting, and Plot.
ANSWER: It is a narrative poem because the farmer (character) performs actions in the field (setting) that result in a change (plot).
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Can you find the narrative features in a poem that just lists colors of flowers?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Look for characters (Are there people or animals?).
- Step 2: Look for a setting (Where are they?).
- Step 3: Look for actions (Are they doing anything?).
- Step 4: Look for a sequence (Does one thing lead to another?).
REASONING: By checking for these features, we see if a poem is descriptive or narrative.
ANSWER: This is not a narrative poem; it is a descriptive poem because there are no characters, actions, or plot events.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Compare a poem about a rainy day (feeling) vs. a poem about a boy running to find shelter (narrative).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze poem A (Rainy day - focuses on mood/sounds).
- Step 2: Analyze poem B (Boy/Shelter - focuses on a character trying to solve a problem).
- Step 3: Check for plot in Poem A (None).
- Step 4: Check for plot in Poem B (Problem: Rain; Action: Running; Result: Shelter).
- Step 5: Contrast the two.
REASONING: Narrative poetry requires movement driven by characters; feeling-based poetry often remains static.
ANSWER: Poem B is narrative because it follows a character overcoming a conflict, while Poem A is lyrical/descriptive because it focuses on a static moment of observation.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Read a short poem about a cat sitting on a wall watching birds. If the cat jumps and catches a bird, how does this change the poem from a descriptive one to a narrative one?
COMPETENCY: Distinguishing between descriptive and narrative modes.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the state of the poem (static vs. dynamic).
- Step 2: Determine if an event (action) occurs.
- Step 3: Explain the shift from observation to plot.
ANSWER: It becomes narrative because the cat goes from just observing (static) to acting (dynamic), creating a plot with a conflict and an outcome.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why is the presence of a "sequence of events" more important in narrative poetry than "rhyming words"?
COMPETENCY: Conceptual analysis of poetic forms.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define what narrative poetry aims to do (tell a story).
- Step 2: Explain the role of sequence (creates the story).
- Step 3: Explain the role of rhyme (creates sound/aesthetic).
- Step 4: Compare which one is necessary for the definition.
ANSWER: Sequence is more important because a narrative poem is defined by its story-telling function; without a sequence of events, you have no plot, and therefore no narrative, regardless of whether it rhymes.
DIFFICULTY: hard
