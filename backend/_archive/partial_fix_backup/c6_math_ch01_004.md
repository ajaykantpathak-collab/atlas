## DEFINITION
Comparing numbers is the process of determining the relative size of two or more values to identify which is greater or smaller. We use the number of digits as the primary indicator, and if that is equal, we compare digits place-by-place starting from the left.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to order a set of four-digit to five-digit numbers from least to greatest and justify their choice by explaining the place-value comparison.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers comparing and ordering whole numbers up to five digits. It excludes negative numbers, decimals, fractions, and inequality symbols like < or >.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at the school library looking for books with specific code numbers, like 4567 and 4589. How do you know which one comes first on the shelf? First, count how many digits each number has; if one has more, it is definitely larger. If they both have the same number of digits, move your eyes from the left-most digit to the right, comparing each position one by one. The moment you find two digits in the same position that are different, the number with the larger digit at that position is the winner.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If you have two numbers, 8,999 and 10,001, which one is larger without needing to look at individual digits?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
10,001 is larger because it has 5 digits, while 8,999 only has 4. A wrong answer suggests the student is ignoring the total count of digits and only looking at the starting digit, which leads to thinking 8 is larger than 1.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
When comparing 5,672 and 5,627, why do we start looking at the thousands place instead of the ones place?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
We start from the left because the leftmost place value (thousands) carries the most weight in determining the total size of the number. A wrong answer shows a misunderstanding of place value, where the student might think all digits contribute equally to a number's magnitude.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Order these numbers from smallest to largest: 2,345, 2,312, 2,399.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The order is 2,312, 2,345, 2,399. We compare the thousands (all 2), then the hundreds (all 3), and finally the tens; since 1 < 4 < 9, the order is clear. A wrong answer indicates the student is not comparing digits systematically from left to right.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If two numbers have the same digits in the thousands, hundreds, and tens places, what determines the larger number?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The digit in the ones place determines the larger number. If a student says the numbers are equal, they have forgotten that every position is unique and contributes to the total value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it possible for a 4-digit number to be larger than a 5-digit number? Explain your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it is never possible because any 5-digit number starts at 10,000, which is already greater than the largest 4-digit number, 9,999. If a student thinks it is possible, they are likely confusing the value of individual digits with the total value of the number.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a 4-digit number that is larger than 6,543 but smaller than 6,550.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Any number like 6,544, 6,545, or 6,549 works. An incorrect answer usually shows the student doesn't understand the range between two values based on place-value progression.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often compare the "size" of the numbers by looking only at the first digit they see (from left to right) without checking the number of digits first. For example, they might wrongly claim 900 is larger than 1,000 because 9 > 1.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to write both numbers in a column, aligning the places (thousands, hundreds, tens, ones). Ask them to count the total digits in each number aloud. Remind them: "More digits always mean a larger number, no matter what the digits are."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper has three bags of rice weighing 4,520 kg, 4,250 kg, and 4,502 kg. Order them from lightest to heaviest.
STEPS:
- step 1: List the numbers: 4,520, 4,250, 4,502.
- step 2: All are 4-digit numbers, so check the thousands place (all are 4).
- step 3: Check the hundreds place: 5, 2, 5. Since 2 is smallest, 4,250 is the lightest.
- step 4: Compare the remaining: 4,520 and 4,502. They have the same hundreds (5). Check the tens: 2 vs 0. 0 is smaller.
- step 5: Result is 4,250 (lightest), 4,502, 4,520 (heaviest).
REASONING: By comparing place values from left to right, we filter the numbers step-by-step to find the relative order.
ANSWER: 4,250 kg, 4,502 kg, 4,520 kg
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Three students have the following scores in a contest: 12,500, 12,050, and 12,505. Rank them from first place (highest score) to third place (lowest score).
STEPS:
- step 1: Compare the number of digits (all are 5).
- step 2: Compare ten-thousands (all 1) and thousands (all 2).
- step 3: Compare hundreds: 5, 0, 5. 12,050 is the lowest.
- step 4: Compare the remaining 12,500 and 12,505 using the tens place (0 vs 0) and ones place (0 vs 5).
- step 5: 12,505 > 12,500 > 12,050.
REASONING: We move left to right; when digits are identical, we move to the next lower place value.
ANSWER: 1st: 12,505, 2nd: 12,500, 3rd: 12,050
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Arrange the following distances in kilometres: 9,800, 10,001, 8,999, and 10,010.
STEPS:
- step 1: Count digits: 9,800 (4), 10,001 (5), 8,999 (4), 10,010 (5).
- step 2: Group by digit count: (8,999, 9,800) and (10,001, 10,010).
- step 3: Compare the 4-digit group: 8 < 9, so 8,999 < 9,800.
- step 4: Compare the 5-digit group: 10,001 vs 10,010. Thousands are same (0), hundreds are same (0). Tens: 0 vs 1. 10,001 < 10,010.
- step 5: Combine groups: 8,999 < 9,800 < 10,001 < 10,010.
REASONING: Grouping by digit count first simplifies the problem before applying place-value comparison.
ANSWER: 8,999 km, 9,800 km, 10,001 km, 10,010 km
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are given two numbers, 7,432 and 7,423. A friend says they are the same because they use the same digits. Explain why the friend is wrong using place value.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the place values for each number.
- step 2: Compare the thousands and hundreds (both 7 and 4).
- step 3: Compare the tens digit (3 vs 2).
- step 4: Conclude that because 3 tens is greater than 2 tens, the numbers have different values.
ANSWER: The friend is wrong because, while they use the same digits, the digits occupy different places. In 7,432, the '3' is in the tens place (worth 30), while in 7,423, the '2' is in the tens place (worth 20). Therefore, 7,432 is larger.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A factory produces 23,456 parts on Monday and 23,465 parts on Tuesday. If a manager needs to pick the day with the higher production, how can you quickly compare these without writing them down?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- step 1: Recognize both are 5-digit numbers.
- step 2: Ignore identical thousands (23) and hundreds (4).
- step 3: Compare the remaining tens: 5 vs 6.
- step 4: Identify 6 > 5, so 23,465 is larger.
ANSWER: You can ignore the first three digits because they are the same (23,4). By comparing only the tens place, you see that 6 tens is greater than 5 tens, making Tuesday the day with higher production (23,465).
DIFFICULTY: hard
