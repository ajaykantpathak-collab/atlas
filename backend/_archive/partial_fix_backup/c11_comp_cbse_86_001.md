## DEFINITION
A speech script has a listener-contract: greeting ('Respected Principal, teachers, and friends'), a hook opening, 2-3 developed points addressed to the audience ('you', rhetorical questions), and a closing call or thank-you. Written for the ear — short sentences, direct address.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
create

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student drafts a 150-200 word speech with greeting, hook, audience-addressed points, and a call-to-action close, sustaining spoken register throughout.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers school assembly/competition speeches. Does NOT cover debates (for/against structure) or valedictory formats.

## WALKTHROUGH
Speeches fail when they read like essays — the audience disappears. The fix is address-markers: 'Have you ever wondered…', 'my friends', 'let us'. The skeleton: greeting → hook (question/story/statistic) → points with examples → 'Thank you' or call to action.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students write essay-style speeches — long subordinate sentences, no audience address, no greeting — earning content marks but losing format and register marks.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Greeting naming the audience order (Principal → teachers → friends). Step 2: Hook — a question or startling fact, not 'Today I am going to speak about'. Step 3: Points addressed to 'you'. Step 4: Close with a call or thanks.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which opening works for a speech: 'Respected Principal and dear friends, have you ever counted how much water we waste?' or 'I am going to speak about water conservation today.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The first — greeting + hook question pulls the audience in; the second announces the topic flatly and skips the greeting entirely.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
What marks spoken register in a written speech?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Direct address ('you', 'my friends'), rhetorical questions, short sentences, and a call-to-action close. A wrong answer listing 'formal vocabulary' confuses essay register with speech register.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A 200-word speech paragraph runs five subordinate clauses in one sentence — what is wrong?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Sentences written for the ear must be short — the audience cannot re-read. Break into 2-3 direct sentences. A wrong answer 'nothing' shows essay-habits bleeding into speech.
