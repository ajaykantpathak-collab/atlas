## DEFINITION
Multiplication is a mathematical operation representing repeated addition of the same number, used to find the total of several equal-sized groups. For large numbers, we use the standard algorithm to break the process into smaller, manageable steps based on place value.

## WALKTHROUGH
If your school canteen sells 125 samosas every day, how would you calculate the total sold in 23 days without adding 125 twenty-three times? Start by treating the multiplier (23) as 20 + 3. First, multiply 125 by the ones digit (3) to get your first partial product. Next, multiply 125 by the tens digit (2), but remember to place a zero at the end because you are actually multiplying by 20. Finally, add these two results together to find the final total; this method turns a long addition into two simple multiplications.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the definition of multiplication in terms of addition?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Multiplication is repeated addition of the same number. A wrong answer suggests the student views multiplication as an abstract, disconnected process rather than a shortcut for adding equal groups.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
When multiplying 456 by 23, why do we put a zero at the end of the second partial product (the result of 456 × 2)?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Because the '2' in 23 represents 20, not 2; the zero acts as a placeholder to shift the value to the tens place. If a student ignores the zero, they are treating a tens-place digit as a ones-place digit, which shows a failure to understand place value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate 312 × 14 using the standard algorithm.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
(312 × 4 = 1248) + (312 × 10 = 3120) = 4368. An incorrect result often shows a failure to carry numbers during addition or a mistake in the place-value shift of the second partial product.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If you know 15 × 10 = 150, how does that help you solve 15 × 11?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
You can add one more group of 15 to the result (150 + 15 = 165). This demonstrates that multiplication is about groups; failing to see this connection suggests the student relies solely on memorized algorithms rather than number sense.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is 25 × 12 the same as (25 × 10) + (25 × 2)? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, because 12 can be broken down into 10 + 2, and the distributive property allows us to multiply each part separately. A student who says "no" likely does not understand that multiplication can be decomposed to make large calculations easier.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-world problem where you would need to multiply a 3-digit number by a 2-digit number.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A bus carries 145 passengers, and there are 25 buses in the fleet; how many passengers total? A poor answer would just be a random math string without a logical real-world context like tickets, items in a carton, or time calculations.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often forget to include the "placeholder" zero when multiplying by the tens digit. For example, in 123 × 21, they might write 123 × 1 = 123 and 123 × 2 = 246, then add 123 + 246 = 369. This happens because they view digits as isolated symbols rather than values based on their position (20 instead of 2).
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to write the multiplier in expanded form (e.g., 21 as 20 + 1). Have them multiply 123 by 1, then 123 by 20, emphasizing that 20 is "two tens." Show them that 123 × 20 must result in a much larger number than 123 × 2, helping them realize the necessity of the placeholder zero.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A local dairy farm produces 425 litres of milk every day. How many litres of milk are produced in 15 days?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: 425 litres per day and 15 days.
- step 2: Method: Multiply 425 by 15 (break 15 into 10 + 5).
- step 3: First partial product: 425 × 5 = 2125.
- step 4: Second partial product: 425 × 10 = 4250.
- step 5: Final sum: 2125 + 4250 = 6375.
REASONING: We multiply by the ones (5) and then the tens (10) to account for the full 15 days. Summing these parts gives the total production over the entire period.
ANSWER: 6375 litres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A textbook printing press prints 312 pages every hour. How many pages are printed in 24 hours?
STEPS:
- step 1: Given: 312 pages/hour, 24 hours.
- step 2: Method: Multiply 312 by 24 (break 24 into 20 + 4).
- step 3: First partial product: 312 × 4 = 1248.
- step 4: Second partial product: 312 × 20 = 6240.
- step 5: Final sum: 1248 + 6240 = 7488.
REASONING: We solve the smaller components (4 hours and 20 hours) and combine them to cover the total 24-hour duration.
ANSWER: 7488 pages
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A school ordered 120 packets of notebooks. Each packet costs ₹145. If the school also paid a delivery fee of ₹500, what was the total amount spent?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify costs: 120 packets × ₹145/packet, plus ₹500 fee.
- step 2: Multiply 145 by 120 (145 × 100 + 145 × 20).
- step 3: 145 × 100 = 14500.
- step 4: 145 × 20 = 2900.
- step 5: 14500 + 2900 = 17400.
- step 6: Add delivery: 17400 + 500 = 17900.
REASONING: We first calculate the total cost of the goods using multiplication, then add the fixed cost of delivery to get the final bill.
ANSWER: ₹17900
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you need to multiply 200 × 15, which is faster: multiplying 200 × 10 and 200 × 5 and adding them, or using the long algorithm? Explain your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions.
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the numbers involved.
- step 2: Identify that 15 is easily split into 10 and 5.
- step 3: Mentally compute 200 × 10 = 2000 and 200 × 5 = 1000.
- step 4: Sum the results: 3000.
ANSWER: Splitting the multiplier is faster here because 10 and 5 are easy to work with mentally. The standard algorithm is more useful for numbers that are not easy multiples like 10 or 5.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student solved 215 × 12 and got 258. Identify the error and explain why the answer is unreasonable.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction.
STEPS:
- step 1: Estimate the answer: 200 × 10 = 2000.
- step 2: Compare the estimate with the student's answer (258).
- step 3: Identify the error: The student likely forgot to multiply by the tens digit and only multiplied by the ones place (215 × 1 = 215 or similar partial mistake).
- step 4: Provide the correct approach: 215 × 10 + 215 × 2.
ANSWER: The answer 258 is incorrect because 215 multiplied by 10 alone is 2150, so the total must be significantly higher than 2150. The student likely ignored the place value of the tens digit entirely.
DIFFICULTY: hard
