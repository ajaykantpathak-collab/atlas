## DEFINITION
The Great Bath of Mohenjo-daro was a large, rectangular public tank constructed from baked bricks and waterproofed with bitumen, designed to hold water for community use. It represents the advanced engineering and organized urban planning of the Harappan civilization.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the location (Mohenjo-daro), the primary material (baked bricks), the waterproofing method (bitumen), and the physical features (rectangular shape, steps, surrounding rooms) of the Great Bath.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the physical description and purpose of the Great Bath and public buildings in Mohenjo-daro. It excludes architectural analysis, religious interpretations, and comparisons with other world civilizations.
## WALKTHROUGH
Think about your school's swimming pool or a local community water tank; how is it kept from leaking, and how do people get in and out? The people of Mohenjo-daro solved these same problems thousands of years ago. To understand the Great Bath, first imagine a deep, brick-lined rectangle in the middle of a busy city. Next, visualize how they used special materials like bitumen to seal the bricks so the water wouldn't soak into the ground. Finally, realize that this wasn't just a random hole; it was carefully planned with steps for entry and rooms nearby for convenience. This tells us that early city dwellers were master planners who valued public spaces.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What were the two main materials used to make the Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro waterproof and durable?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The two main materials were baked bricks and bitumen. A wrong answer (like "cement" or "tiles") reveals the student is applying modern construction knowledge instead of historical context.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do archaeologists think the Great Bath was a significant part of the city of Mohenjo-daro?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It was significant because it was a large, carefully engineered public structure that suggests the city had a government or authority capable of organizing large-scale construction. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks it was just a regular house tank, missing the "public" scale of the building.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If you were exploring Mohenjo-daro, what specific features would you look for to confirm you found the Great Bath?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps on two sides, a rectangular shape, surrounding rooms/verandah, and a nearby well. A student who misses these features is failing to visualize the specific archaeological description provided.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How does the existence of a nearby well near the Great Bath explain the city's planning?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
It shows that the people did not rely on carrying water from far away, but planned the infrastructure to supply the bath directly. This reveals an understanding of "urban planning" (organized city systems).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Could the Great Bath be used by any individual at any time? Justify your answer based on its design.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It was likely a public building meant for group use, as indicated by its large size and the surrounding rooms. A student answering "no" without reasoning fails to connect the "public building" status with the scale of the structure.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Imagine you are an archaeologist; if you found a similar structure in another part of the ruins, what evidence would you search for to classify it as a public building?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Evidence would include its large size, connection to water systems (drains/wells), and its location in a central area of the city. A poor answer would focus on internal decorations rather than the utility and scale of the structure.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often assume the Great Bath was an Olympic-style swimming pool for sports. They project modern leisure activities onto the past because they cannot imagine other reasons for such a large water structure.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student: "If the city had no pipes or taps like we do today, why would they build a massive tank in the center of town?" Guide them to look at the surrounding rooms and the well. Explain that big public structures usually serve a community purpose rather than personal exercise.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Identify three features of the Great Bath that suggest it was a "public" building rather than a private home.
STEPS:
- Consider the size: It is a massive tank (12m x 7m x 2.4m), which is too large for one family.
- Analyze the surroundings: It has a verandah and rooms, suggesting a gathering place.
- Analyze the resource: It has a dedicated well, indicating a high-capacity water source.
- Conclusion: Public buildings serve many people; private homes would be smaller and have individual wells.
REASONING: These features collectively indicate a site designed for community use.
ANSWER: The size, the surrounding assembly rooms, and the dedicated water supply confirm it was a public structure.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Why was bitumen essential for the Great Bath, and what would happen if the Harappans used only mud mortar?
STEPS:
- Identify the function of the bath: To hold water for a long time.
- Analyze the material properties: Mud mortar dissolves in water; baked bricks are porous.
- Apply the concept of waterproofing: Bitumen acts as a sealant.
- Predict the outcome: Without bitumen, the water would leak out through the brick gaps into the ground.
- Verify: Bitumen ensures the structure stays functional as a tank.
REASONING: The use of specific materials shows advanced engineering knowledge.
ANSWER: Bitumen was essential to seal gaps between bricks; without it, the tank would leak and be unable to hold water.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: If the Harappans had not developed the technology to make baked bricks, could they have built the Great Bath? Explain your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Critical Thinking
STEPS:
- Compare the durability of sun-dried bricks vs. baked bricks.
- Evaluate the risk of water damage to mud-based structures.
- Conclude on the necessity of durable material for a massive, water-filled structure.
- Justify the link between material technology and historical development.
ANSWER: No, because sun-dried bricks would crumble when exposed to constant water, and mud mortar would wash away, making it impossible to sustain a large-scale public bath.
