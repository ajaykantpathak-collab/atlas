## DEFINITION
Commands and requests report with 'told/ordered/requested/asked + object + to-infinitive': 'Sit down' → 'He told me to sit down.' Negatives insert 'not' before 'to': 'Don't touch' → 'He ordered me not to touch.' Polite additions (please) are captured by 'requested' and can be dropped.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student reports commands, requests, advice, and warnings using the told/ordered/requested/advised/warned + object + (not) to-infinitive frame, choosing the reporting verb that matches the original's force.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers imperatives positive and negative. Does NOT cover statements (93_001) or questions (93_002).

## WALKTHROUGH
The frame is fixed: [reporting verb] + [object] + [to + base verb]. The only real choice is WHICH reporting verb carries the force — 'told' is neutral, 'ordered' is authority, 'requested' is polite, 'warned' is danger, 'advised' is suggestion. 'Please' dissolves into 'requested'. 'Don't run' → 'warned me not to run'.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students report commands as statements ('He said that sit down'), forget the object slot ('told to go' — told WHOM?), and place 'not' wrongly ('told me to not go' — boards prefer 'not to go' split-infinitive-free).

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Pick the reporting verb that matches the original's force. Step 2: Insert the object — who is being commanded. Step 3: to-infinitive carries the command; 'not' sits BEFORE 'to'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Report: 'Do not pluck the flowers,' the gardener said to the children.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'The gardener ordered/warned the children not to pluck the flowers.' A wrong answer 'the gardener said that do not pluck' reveals the student has no imperative frame at all — they're gluing 'that' onto an infinitive, which English forbids.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Report: 'Please help me carry this,' she said to me.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'She requested me to help her carry that.' 'please' → 'requested'; me→her; this→that. A wrong answer 'she asked me to please help' keeps 'please' in reported speech — pleading is a live-performance feature, it cannot survive reporting.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Report: 'Never swim alone,' the coach said to the boys.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'The coach warned/advised the boys never to swim alone' (or 'not to swim alone'). A wrong answer omitting the object ('the coach warned never to swim') reveals the student doesn't know imperative reporting requires a commanded party — English demands the object slot be filled.
