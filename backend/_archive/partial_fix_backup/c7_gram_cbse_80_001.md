## DEFINITION
A clause is a group of words with its own subject and verb. An independent clause stands alone ('She smiled'); a dependent clause cannot ('because she was happy'). A phrase is a word-group with no subject-verb pair ('in the morning').

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student separates phrases from clauses and independent from dependent clauses, and explains why 'when the bell rang' alone is incomplete.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers clause vs phrase vs sentence identification. Does NOT cover the three clause TYPES (noun/adjective/adverb — class 11) or complex-sentence construction.

## WALKTHROUGH
The subject-verb test decides everything: 'after the match ended' — subject 'match', verb 'ended' → clause. 'after the match' — no verb → phrase. Then the standing test: 'Because I was late' — grammatically a clause but it leans on something missing; it cannot stand alone.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students call any long word-group a clause — length is not the test, the subject-verb pair is. They also think dependent clauses are broken sentences rather than working parts.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Look for a subject-verb pair. None → phrase. Step 2: Found one → does it start with a leaning word (because/when/if/although/who/which)? → dependent clause. Step 3: Stands alone? → independent.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Phrase or clause: 'under the tall banyan tree' vs 'when the rain stopped'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
First = phrase (no subject-verb). Second = dependent clause (subject 'rain', verb 'stopped', leaning word 'when'). A wrong answer swapping them shows the student tests by length, not structure.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'Because she practised daily' — complete sentence or not?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Not — 'because' makes the clause lean on a missing main clause. Complete: 'She won because she practised daily.' A wrong answer accepting it alone shows the dependent-fragment error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
How many clauses: 'The sun set, and the birds flew home.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Two independent clauses joined by 'and'. A wrong answer 'one' shows the student counts sentences, not subject-verb pairs.
