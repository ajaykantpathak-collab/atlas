## DEFINITION
Article choice follows a two-question algorithm: (1) Is the noun specific/known to both speaker and listener? → 'the'. (2) If not, is it singular countable? → 'a/an' (an before vowel SOUND). Otherwise → zero article. 'The' marks shared knowledge; 'a/an' introduces something new.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student runs the specificity algorithm on any noun phrase — including uncountables, plurals, and proper nouns — and explains each choice by shared-knowledge vs first-mention, plus the vowel-sound rule for an (an hour, a university).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the core algorithm and standard exceptions (unique objects: the sun; superlatives: the best; rivers/ranges: the Ganga, the Himalayas; institutions-as-purpose: go to school = zero article). Does NOT cover the full determiner system (94_002).

## WALKTHROUGH
Narrate two sentences: 'I saw a dog. The dog was huge.' First mention = 'a' (new information), second = 'the' (now we both know which dog). That one paragraph IS the algorithm. Then the 'an' trap: it listens to SOUND not spelling — 'an hour' (h silent), 'a university' (yoo- sound), 'an M.A.' (em- sound).

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students choose 'an' by spelling (writing 'an university'), use 'the' for general categories ('the dogs are faithful animals' — generic plural takes zero), and omit 'the' before unique objects.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Ask 'does the listener know WHICH one?' → yes: 'the'. Step 2: No → is it singular countable? → a/an by SOUND. Step 3: Plural/uncountable + general → zero. Step 4: Check fixed patterns — superlatives, unique objects, holy books, rivers.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'___ honesty is the best policy.' Which article, and why?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Zero — abstract uncountable used generically. A wrong answer 'the honesty' reveals the student marks every noun as needing an article — the over-specification error, from Hindi where abstract nouns can take determiners differently.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Choose: '___ European' / '___ honest man'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'a European' (yoo- consonant sound) and 'an honest man' (silent h → vowel sound). A wrong answer 'an European' proves the student applies the rule to spelling, not pronunciation — the single most-tested determiner trap.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'She goes to ___ school by bus' vs 'The parents met at ___ school.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
First: zero article (school-as-purpose: to study). Second: 'the school' (the building, a place). A wrong answer putting 'the' in the first reveals the student doesn't know institutions drop 'the' when used for their purpose — go to school/hospital/church/bed.
