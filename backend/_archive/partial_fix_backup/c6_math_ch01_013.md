## DEFINITION
A factor is a whole number that divides another number exactly, leaving no remainder. A multiple is the product obtained when you multiply a given whole number by any other whole number.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to list all factors of a given number up to 50 and generate the first five multiples of any single-digit number without error.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the definitions and identification of factors, multiples, and prime numbers. It excludes prime factorization, GCD, LCM, and proofs regarding the number of primes.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you have 12 chocolate bars to share equally among friends. If you give them out in groups of 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, or 12, you have no leftovers—these are the "factors" of 12 because they divide it perfectly. Now, think about counting in steps of 3 (3, 6, 9, 12, 15...); each number you land on is a "multiple" of 3 because it is 3 times some whole number. If a number has only two factors (1 and itself), we call it a "prime number." To begin, always check if a number divides evenly; if there is a remainder, it is not a factor.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Is 7 a factor of 21? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Yes, 7 is a factor of 21 because 21 ÷ 7 = 3 with no remainder. A wrong answer suggests the student is confused between factors (divisors) and multiples (products).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain the difference between a factor and a multiple using the number 5.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
A factor of 5 is a number that divides 5 (only 1 and 5), while a multiple of 5 is a result of 5 multiplied by something (like 5, 10, 15). A wrong answer often indicates the student thinks factors and multiples are the same thing.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
List all the factors of 16.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The factors are 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16. If a student misses 2 or 8, they are likely not checking divisibility systematically starting from 1.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Is 1 a prime number? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
No, 1 is not prime because a prime number must have exactly two distinct factors (1 and itself), and 1 only has one factor. Students often mistakenly label 1 as prime because they don't realize the "two factor" rule.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If a number is prime, what can you say about its factors?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and the number itself. If a student says it has "many factors," they have confused prime numbers with composite numbers.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a list of numbers between 1 and 10 that are neither prime nor composite (excluding 1).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
There are no such numbers because every number greater than 1 is either prime or composite. A poor answer shows a lack of understanding of the definitions of prime and composite numbers.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse factors and multiples, thinking that because 2 × 6 = 12, then 12 is a factor of 2. They mistake the product (multiple) for the divisor (factor).
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to write the multiplication fact 2 × 6 = 12. Label 2 and 6 as "Factors" and 12 as the "Multiple." Have them check divisibility: "Does 12 divide by 2? Yes, so 2 is a factor. Does 2 divide by 12? No, so 12 is a multiple."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Radha has 20 metres of ribbon. She wants to cut it into equal pieces such that no ribbon is wasted. What are the possible lengths of the pieces (in whole numbers)?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify that we need factors of 20.
- step 2: Test divisibility by checking integers starting from 1.
- step 3: 20 ÷ 1 = 20, 20 ÷ 2 = 10, 20 ÷ 4 = 5.
- step 4: List the pairs: (1, 20), (2, 10), (4, 5).
- step 5: Conclude the possible lengths are 1m, 2m, 4m, 5m, 10m, and 20m.
REASONING: Since no ribbon is wasted, the piece length must be a divisor of the total length. Testing pairs ensures no factors are missed.
ANSWER: 1m, 2m, 4m, 5m, 10m, 20m
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A bus arrives at a station every 6 minutes. If the first bus arrives at 8:00 AM, list the times of the next 4 buses.
STEPS:
- step 1: Recognize this requires finding multiples of 6.
- step 2: Start from the first arrival time (8:00).
- step 3: Add 6, 12, 18, and 24 minutes to the starting time.
- step 4: 8:06, 8:12, 8:18, 8:24.
- step 5: Verify the interval is consistent.
REASONING: Multiples represent the timeline of the repeating events. Adding the base interval creates the sequence.
ANSWER: 8:06 AM, 8:12 AM, 8:18 AM, 8:24 AM
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A crate holds 15 kg of apples. If a shopkeeper buys 4 crates, what is the total weight? Is this weight a multiple of 15?
STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate total weight: 15 kg × 4 = 60 kg.
- step 2: Define a multiple: a number formed by multiplying the base (15) by an integer (4).
- step 3: Since 15 × 4 = 60, 60 is a multiple of 15.
- step 4: Confirm 60 is divisible by 15 (60 ÷ 15 = 4).
- step 5: Interpret: The total weight is a multiple of the single crate weight.
REASONING: This connects the concept of multiples to real-world addition/multiplication of quantities.
ANSWER: 60 kg; Yes, it is a multiple.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a number is a multiple of 8, is it also a multiple of 2? Explain your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Reasoning and Justification
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify that 8 = 2 × 4.
- step 2: Any multiple of 8 can be written as 8 × n.
- step 3: Substitute 8 with (2 × 4): (2 × 4) × n = 2 × (4 × n).
- step 4: Since it is 2 times another whole number, it must be a multiple of 2.
ANSWER: Yes, because 8 itself is a multiple of 2, any number that contains 8 as a factor must also contain 2.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Which is a factor of every number? Is this number prime or composite? Why?
COMPETENCY: Conceptual Analysis
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the number that divides everything: 1.
- step 2: Check if 1 is prime (must have 2 factors).
- step 3: Check if 1 is composite (must have more than 2 factors).
- step 4: Conclude 1 is neither.
ANSWER: The number is 1. It is neither prime nor composite because it has only one factor, which does not meet the definition of prime (2 factors) or composite (more than 2 factors).
DIFFICULTY: hard
