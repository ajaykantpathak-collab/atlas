## DEFINITION
Beyond a/an/the sits the determiner family: this/that/these/those (pointing), my/your/our (possession), some/any/many/much/few/little (quantity). Each has a home — 'many' counts things, 'much' measures stuff; 'some' offers, 'any' questions and negates.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student selects the right determiner family member — demonstrative by distance/number, quantifier by countability and sentence polarity (some+ / any−?).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers demonstratives, possessives, and core quantifiers. Does NOT cover the few/little attitude split (class 10) or complex determiner stacking.

## WALKTHROUGH
Two axes run the whole family. Axis 1 — pointing: this/these = near (singular/plural), that/those = far. Axis 2 — quantity: countables take many/few, uncountables take much/little. And the polarity rule: 'some' lives in offers and positives, 'any' in questions and negatives — 'Do you have ANY pen?' but 'Would you like SOME tea?'

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students use 'much' with countables ('much students'), 'some' in negatives ('I don't have some money'), and mix this/these by number.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Pointing? → near or far, one or many. Step 2: Quantity? → countable (many/few) or mass (much/little). Step 3: Polarity → some in +/offers, any in −/?.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'There isn't ___ milk left' vs 'Would you like ___ tea?'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
any / some — negation takes 'any', polite offer takes 'some' even in a question. A wrong answer 'some' in the first shows the polarity rule missing.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'___ money do you have?' much or many — and why?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'much' — money is uncountable. A wrong answer 'many' reveals the count/mass partition isn't built yet.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Point correctly: '___ shoes here are mine, but ___ over there are yours.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'these' (near + plural) and 'those' (far + plural). A wrong answer 'this/them' mixes the distance and number axes.
