## DEFINITION
Inside a sentence, nouns do different jobs: subject (does the action), object (receives it), and complement (completes being-verbs — 'She is a DOCTOR'). Finding each job is what makes agreement and voice possible later.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student labels subject, verb, object, and complement in simple sentences, distinguishes objects from complements (after being-verbs there is no object), and identifies transitive vs intransitive verbs.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the four functional slots in S–V–O and S–V–C patterns. Does NOT cover indirect objects in depth or object complements.

## WALKTHROUGH
Pattern hunt: 'Ravi kicked the ball' — who? Ravi (subject). Did what? kicked (verb). Kicked what? ball (object). Now 'Ravi is a doctor' — 'a doctor' is not kicked, not acted upon; it renames Ravi — complement. The verb decides: action verbs can take objects; being-verbs take complements.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students call every word after the verb 'the object' — including complements after is/was/seem. 'She became a teacher' has no object — 'a teacher' is what she IS, not what she did something to.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Find the verb. Step 2: 'Who/what + verb' = subject. Step 3: Action verb → 'verb + what/whom' = object. Being verb → what follows renames the subject = complement, not object.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Label the object: 'The teacher praised the diligent student.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'the diligent student' — receives the praising. A wrong answer 'teacher' shows subject-object confusion; 'praised' alone shows verb-object confusion.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'The sky turned grey.' Is 'grey' an object or a complement — and why?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Complement — 'turned' here is a being-verb (became); 'grey' describes the sky's state, nothing receives an action. A wrong answer 'object' reveals the everything-after-verb-is-object habit.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Which verb is intransitive (no object possible): 'She slept' / 'She ate' / 'She read'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'slept' — you cannot 'sleep something'; 'ate' and 'read' can take objects. A wrong answer reveals the student hasn't split verbs into transitive vs intransitive.
