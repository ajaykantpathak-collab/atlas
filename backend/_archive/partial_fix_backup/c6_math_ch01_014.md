## DEFINITION
Divisibility rules are simple patterns or shortcuts that allow you to check if one number can be divided by another without performing the long division process. They tell you if there will be a remainder of zero.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify if a given number is divisible by 2, 3, or 5 by applying the rules correctly and explaining why the result is true.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers divisibility rules for 2, 3, and 5. It excludes rules for 4, 6, 7, 8, and 11, and does not cover proofs or prime factorization.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you have a basket of 150 mangoes and you need to share them equally among 3 friends. Instead of drawing or dividing on paper, you can use a "secret code" for numbers. To see if 150 can be shared by 3, you just add the digits: 1 + 5 + 0 = 6. Since 6 is in the 3-times table, you instantly know 150 is divisible by 3! These shortcuts work like a cheat sheet for your brain, helping you spot divisibility by looking at the last digit or the sum of the digits. Once you learn these three rules, you will be able to scan large numbers and categorize them in seconds.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the rule to check if a number is divisible by 5?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A number is divisible by 5 if its last digit is either 0 or 5. A wrong answer here suggests the student is confusing this with the rule for 2, where they might incorrectly think any even number is divisible by 5.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you add the digits of 471 and get 12, is 471 divisible by 3? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Yes, 471 is divisible by 3 because the sum of its digits (12) is divisible by 3. If a student says "no," they likely forgot the rule for 3 or are incorrectly looking at the last digit instead of the sum.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Is the number 894 divisible by 2? Show your steps.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Yes, because the last digit is 4, which is an even number. If a student answers "no," they might be trying to divide the whole number instead of checking only the last digit.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the rules for 2 and 5. What do they have in common?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Both rules depend only on the last digit of the number. If a student tries to bring in the rule for 3, they are confusing the "last digit" method with the "sum of digits" method.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Can a number be divisible by both 2 and 5? If so, what must its last digit be?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, the last digit must be 0. If a student says "5," they forgot that 5 is not even, so it cannot be divisible by 2.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a 3-digit number that is divisible by 3 but not by 2 or 5. Explain how you chose it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A possible answer is 333 (sum is 9, so divisible by 3; last digit is odd, so not divisible by 2 or 5). A poor answer would show a number that is even or ends in 5, failing the "not by 2 or 5" condition.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to apply the "last digit" rule (used for 2 and 5) to the number 3. For example, they might think 313 is divisible by 3 because 3 is at the end, ignoring that the sum of digits is 7.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student applies the wrong rule, ask them to list the first five multiples of 3. Then, have them calculate the digit sums of those multiples (e.g., 12 -> 1+2=3, 15 -> 1+5=6). Once they see the pattern that digit sums of multiples of 3 are always multiples of 3, they will stop using the last-digit trick.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A library has 435 books. Can they be placed in 3 equal stacks?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the total (435) and the divisor (3).
- step 2: Apply the divisibility rule for 3 (sum of digits).
- step 3: Add the digits: 4 + 3 + 5 = 12.
- step 4: Check if 12 is divisible by 3: 12 ÷ 3 = 4 (remainder 0).
- step 5: Conclude that since the sum is divisible, the original number is divisible.
REASONING: By reducing the large number to a smaller sum, we bypass the need for long division. This works because the rule for 3 is based on digit properties.
ANSWER: Yes, they can be placed in 3 equal stacks.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper has 1,280 marbles. Can he pack them into bags of 5 without any marbles left over?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the total (1,280) and the divisor (5).
- step 2: Apply the divisibility rule for 5 (check the last digit).
- step 3: Observe the last digit of 1,280, which is 0.
- step 4: Apply the rule: Since the number ends in 0, it is divisible by 5.
- step 5: Conclude that no marbles will be left over.
REASONING: The rule for 5 is strictly about the last digit. If the last digit is 0 or 5, the number is inherently a multiple of 5.
ANSWER: Yes, he can pack them into bags of 5.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Is the number 2,046 divisible by both 2 and 3?
STEPS:
- step 1: Check divisibility by 2: Last digit is 6 (even), so yes.
- step 2: Check divisibility by 3: Sum of digits 2 + 0 + 4 + 6 = 12.
- step 3: Check if 12 is divisible by 3: 12 ÷ 3 = 4, so yes.
- step 4: Combine findings: Since it passes both tests, it is divisible by both.
- step 5: Conclude the result.
REASONING: Divisibility by multiple numbers requires satisfying each rule individually. If a number fails even one test, it is not divisible by the set.
ANSWER: Yes, 2,046 is divisible by both 2 and 3.
