## DEFINITION
Senior non-finite work: perfect infinitives ('to have done' — before the main verb's time), passive infinitives ('to be done'), gerunds after prepositions ('insisted on going'), and the bare infinitive after make/let/help and sense-verbs ('I saw him cross the road').

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student selects perfect/passive infinitive forms by time-order and voice, uses gerunds after prepositions, and applies the bare-infinitive rules after causatives and perception verbs.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers advanced non-finite forms. Does NOT cover the basic preference lists (class 8) or participial phrases in depth.

## WALKTHROUGH
Time-order first: 'He seems to have left' — the leaving precedes the seeming; 'to have + V3' flashes back. Voice: 'She wants to be invited' — she receives the inviting. The preposition rule is absolute: after ANY preposition, verbs go gerund — 'good at singing', 'insisted on leaving', never 'on to leave'.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students use 'to + V' after prepositions ('for to go'), miss the perfect-infinitive time-shift, and add 'to' after make/let ('made me to go').

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Preposition before a verb? → gerund, always. Step 2: Non-finite event earlier than the main verb? → to have + V3. Step 3: Receiving the action? → to be + V3. Step 4: After make/let/see/hear + object → bare infinitive (no 'to').

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'He apologised for ___ (be) late.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'being' — after a preposition ('for'), the verb must be a gerund. A wrong answer 'to be' breaks the absolute preposition-gerund rule.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'She is believed ___ (live) abroad since 2010.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'to have been living' or 'to have lived' — the living precedes the believing → perfect infinitive. A wrong answer 'to live' loses the time-order.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'The teacher made us ___ (rewrite) the essay.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'rewrite' — bare infinitive after 'made + object'. A wrong answer 'to rewrite' adds 'to' where causatives forbid it.
