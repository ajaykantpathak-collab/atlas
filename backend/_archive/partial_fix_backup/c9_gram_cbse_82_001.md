## DEFINITION
Class 9 concord adds the indefinite pronouns to the head-noun rule: each/every/everyone/nobody are always singular; few/many/several always plural; some/all/most take their number from the of-phrase. 'Neither…nor' agrees with the NEARER subject.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student applies head-noun agreement through decoy phrases, assigns fixed number to indefinites, resolves neither/either-or proximity, and handles collective nouns by the unit/members test.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the board's full concord list. Does NOT cover notional agreement or advanced inverted structures.

## WALKTHROUGH
The three-box sort: ALWAYS SINGULAR (each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, either, neither alone). ALWAYS PLURAL (few, many, several, both). DEPENDS (some, all, most, none — look at 'of ___'). Correlatives: 'neither A nor B' — B, the nearer one, governs the verb.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students pluralize 'everyone' (it means all people → 'everyone are'), sum neither-nor subjects to plural, and agree with of-phrase decoys.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Find the true subject, strip of-phrases and comma-inserts. Step 2: Indefinite? Check its box. Step 3: Some/all/most → agree with the noun after 'of'. Step 4: Neither-nor → nearer subject wins.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'Each of the students ___ (has/have) submitted the form.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'has' — 'each' is always singular. A wrong answer 'have' is semantic plurality: many students pictured, but 'each' singles them out grammatically.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'Neither the teacher nor the students ___ (was/were) ready.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'were' — the nearer subject 'students' governs. A wrong answer 'was' either reverses the proximity rule or treats 'neither' as the subject.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'Some of the water ___ (has/have) spilt.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'has' — 'some' takes its number from 'water' (uncountable). A wrong answer 'have' treats 'some' as inherently plural.
