## DEFINITION
A narrative poem is a type of poetry that tells a story, featuring characters, a setting, and a sequence of events. Its primary goal is to convey a lesson or message (moral) through the protagonist's journey and choices.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the protagonist's struggle, the specific action taken to overcome failure, and articulate the poem's moral message in their own words.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the narrative structure and thematic message of the King Bruce poem. It does not cover stanza-by-stanza analysis, specific literary devices, or other poems in the unit.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever felt like giving up on a math problem or a difficult drawing because you didn't get it right the first time? Think of King Bruce, who had lost many battles and felt like a failure, hiding in a cave. When you feel stuck, start by identifying the "spider moment"—the point in the story where the character observes something that changes their perspective. Next, ask yourself how that observation turned into action. Finally, connect that action to the result to understand the message: persistence is a bridge between failure and success.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is a narrative poem?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A narrative poem is a poem that tells a story. A student who misses this likely thinks all poems are just about describing feelings or objects, rather than having a plot or characters.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why did King Bruce watch the spider in the cave?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
He watched the spider because he was feeling defeated and looking for inspiration. A wrong answer suggests the student is not paying attention to the character's motivation, viewing the spider as just a random detail rather than a symbol.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If you try to build a tower of blocks and it falls three times, what is the 'King Bruce' way to handle it?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The 'King Bruce' way is to try a fourth time, perhaps changing your technique. If the student says "give up" or "be sad," they have failed to apply the concept of perseverance.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How does the spider act as a symbol for King Bruce?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The spider represents the quality of persistence; just as the spider refuses to stop spinning despite falling, Bruce refuses to stop fighting despite losing. A wrong answer shows the student doesn't understand that symbols represent abstract ideas.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it possible for a story to be a 'narrative poem' if it doesn't teach a lesson? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, a narrative poem is defined by telling a story, not by the moral it teaches. This tests if the student can separate the "form" (storytelling) from the "purpose" (the lesson).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Write one sentence about a time you failed at something and one sentence about how you 'tried again' like the spider.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The answer should show a clear link between a specific failure and a specific, persistent action. If the student provides a vague "I tried," they haven't captured the spirit of deliberate persistence.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think "persistence" means doing the exact same thing over and over again without changing. In reality, the spider (and Bruce) succeed because they keep trying *until* they succeed, often learning from each fall.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student: "If the spider fell and hit the same spot every time, would it ever reach the other side?" Guide them to see that persistence is about the *willingness to keep going*, not just repeating a mistake. Ask them to think of a time they changed how they held a pen or a ball to get better at a task.

## THIS_FORMULA
Failure + Persistence = Success (The "Bruce Principle").

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Analyze the transformation of King Bruce's attitude.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify Bruce’s attitude at the start (defeated, hiding).
- Step 2: Identify the turning point (observing the spider).
- Step 3: Identify the new behavior (returning to lead his army).
- Step 4: Summarize the shift (from hopelessness to determination).
REASONING: By mapping the emotional arc, we see that the poem is not just about a bug, but about a change in the King's mind.
ANSWER: Bruce moves from despair to determination by choosing to mirror the spider’s persistence.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Distinguish between the 'story' and the 'message'.
STEPS:
- Step 1: List the events (Battle -> Cave -> Spider -> Return -> Win).
- Step 2: Define the story as the list of events.
- Step 3: Define the message as the 'takeaway' for the reader.
- Step 4: Compare the two.
REASONING: This teaches students that a story is what happens, while a message is why it matters to them.
ANSWER: The story is a King watching a spider; the message is that success requires not giving up.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Compare the spider to an everyday student challenge.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Choose a challenge (e.g., learning to ride a bike).
- Step 2: List the 'falls' (e.g., wobbling, tipping over).
- Step 3: List the 'persisting' (e.g., getting back on the seat).
- Step 4: List the 'success' (e.g., balancing on the bike).
- Step 5: Connect to the poem (Spider = Student, King Bruce = The lesson learned).
REASONING: Applying the poem's structure to their own life confirms they understand the "narrative" logic.
ANSWER: Just like the spider's web, the bike ride requires falling to learn how to balance.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If King Bruce had seen the spider fall and decided, "Spiders are bad at spinning, I'm bad at fighting, we both should quit," how would the poem's message change?
COMPETENCY: Critical Thinking / Interpreting Themes
STEPS:
- Identify the original message (persistence leads to success).
- Predict the outcome of the new scenario (giving up).
- State the new (negative) message.
ANSWER: The message would change to "some things are just impossible," which contradicts the poem's actual lesson about perseverance.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why is it important that King Bruce is a King in the poem? Could the message still be the same if he were just a regular person?
COMPETENCY: Evaluation of Character
STEPS:
- Analyze the role of a King (leader, responsibility).
- Compare to a regular person (individual responsibility).
- Conclude if the message is universal.
ANSWER: The message is the same because persistence is a human quality, but making him a King shows that even powerful people need to learn from nature.
