## DEFINITION
Modal + have + past participle talks about a past that didn't happen or a past we deduce: 'should have studied' = regret/reproach, 'must have left' = confident deduction, 'could have won' = unrealized ability, 'might have forgotten' = weak past possibility.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student correctly deploys should have/must have/could have/might have for regret, deduction, and unrealized possibility, and distinguishes deduction-must from obligation-must.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the four board-tested modal perfects. Does NOT cover present modal functions (92_001).

## WALKTHROUGH
Three past scenarios on the board: (1) 'The lights are off — they ___ have gone out' → deduction → 'must'. (2) 'You failed because you didn't study — you ___ have studied' → reproach → 'should'. (3) 'He was fast enough but didn't enter — he ___ have won' → lost chance → 'could'. The form is identical (modal+have+V3); only the speaker's stance changes.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students write 'should had'/'must had' (double-past) or 'should have went' — and crucially, they use 'must have' for obligation ('you must have done your homework by tomorrow'), which is a calque of Hindi 'karna chahiye tha' confusion.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Confirm the event is PAST — that forces 'have + V3'. Step 2: Ask the stance — deduction (must), regret/advice-too-late (should), unrealized ability (could), weak guess (might). Step 3: Never write 'modal + had' — 'had' is for pluperfect clauses, not modal perfects.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'The ground is wet — it ___ have rained last night.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'must' — confident deduction from present evidence about a past event. A wrong answer 'should' reveals the student confuses deduction with advisability — the two functions share no overlap but students collapse them.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Correct the error: 'You should had apologised.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'should have apologised' — modal perfect needs 'have', never 'had'. A wrong answer keeping 'had' or changing to 'should apologised' shows the student doesn't know the fixed modal+have+V3 template.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'He ___ have caught the train, but he left late.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'could' — unrealized past ability/opportunity. A wrong answer 'must' or 'should' shows the student cannot yet map speaker stance (lost chance) onto the modal — the expressive core of this node.
