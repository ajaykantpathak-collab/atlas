## DEFINITION
Reported questions lose their question grammar: no question mark, no do/does/did, and statement word order returns. 'Where do you live?' → 'He asked where I lived.' Yes/no questions take 'if/whether'; wh-questions keep the wh-word as connector.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student reports wh-questions and yes/no questions with correct connector choice, restored statement order, and removed do-support, and can spot the residual question mark error.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers both question families. Does NOT cover commands/requests (93_003).

## WALKTHROUGH
The transformation has two moves students must separate: (1) the connector — wh-word stays, or if/whether for yes/no; (2) de-interrogation — 'did she go' flattens to 'she had gone/went'. Drill the before/after pair: 'Did you see it?' → 'He asked if I had seen it.' The question mark dies in reporting.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students keep question order inside reported frames ('He asked where did I live'), keep the question mark, or use 'if' for wh-questions ('asked if where I lived').

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Decide the type — yes/no → if/whether; wh- → keep the wh-word. Step 2: Delete do/does/did and restore statement order. Step 3: Backshift the tense. Step 4: Drop the question mark.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Report: 'Where did you go?' she asked me.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'She asked me where I had gone.' wh-word kept, 'did' deleted, order restored, went→had gone. A wrong answer 'where did I go' inside the frame reveals the student quotes the question verbatim — the core failure this node exists to fix.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Report: 'Do you like tea?' he asked.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'He asked (me) if/whether I liked tea.' yes/no needs if/whether, do-support removed, like→liked. A wrong answer 'he asked did I like tea' shows the student has no de-interrogation step — the question grammar survives intact into reporting.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Which is correct: 'She asked me that where was I going' or 'She asked me where I was going'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The second — 'that' cannot join a wh-question, and 'where was I' keeps question order. A wrong answer choosing the first reveals the student thinks 'that' is the universal reported-speech glue, regardless of sentence type.
