## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the two epigraphic fragments in the note: "uxkM+s dh rjg ctrs gSa 'kCn" (words scatter like hay) and "vkvks] feydj cpk,¡" (come, let's cover/embrace). Analyze how these two images establish the poem's central movement.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The first image—words scattered like hay—conveys fragmentation, waste, the aftermath of harvest/threshing: language broken by displacement. The second—"come, let's cover"—is an imperative of gathering, protection, repair. The movement is from dispersal to embrace, from the field's debris to the act of covering what is exposed. A wrong answer that treats them as unrelated quotes fails to see the poem's arc: it begins in the scatter and moves toward the cover.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, but with a crucial qualification: "cpkus" (to cover) here means both *to conceal* and *to protect/to witness*. The poet, as a teacher's son who left the village but returns in language, cannot simply "expose" (which risks voyeurism) nor "cover up" (which risks complicity). A weak evaluation treats "cpkus" as only "hiding"; a strong one sees the double valence: the poem covers the village's pain *with words* so that pain becomes legible, not erased.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION

They fail to see that the note explicitly frames the poem's central tension (agrarian vs. urban, dialect vs. standard Hindi, exposure vs. cover) and hands them the exact analytical categories they will need for every subsequent node. For example, a student who skips the note will later struggle to explain *why* the poem uses "tyok;q" instead of "tyok" or *why* the final stanza shifts from description to imperative—the answers are already seeded here.
