## DEFINITION
An event report for the school magazine/newspaper: headline → byline → place+date line → lead answering What/When/Where/Who → body in inverted pyramid (details, quotes, outcome) → closing line on participation/verdict. Past tense, third person, facts first.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student produces a complete report with all format elements, inverted-pyramid body, integrated attributed quotes, and zero first-person narrative drift.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers event reports at class 11-12 board standard. Does NOT cover analytical paragraphs (class 10) or journalistic feature writing.

## WALKTHROUGH
The pyramid discipline: the lead alone must let a reader leave informed — 'The annual science exhibition was held on 12 March in the main hall, drawing 40 projects from classes 9-12.' Middle paragraphs add a quote ('The innovation level was remarkable, said the Principal') and details; the tail carries what can be cut.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students write reports chronologically like stories ('First we assembled, then…'), insert first-person ('I really enjoyed'), and drop the headline or byline — treating format as optional.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Headline (<10 words, the whole event). Step 2: Byline + dateline. Step 3: Lead = 4W in one sentence. Step 4: Body = how + quote + outcome. Step 5: Audit — cut every 'I', every exclamation, every suspense device.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Order a report's elements: body details, headline, lead (4W), byline, quote.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
headline → byline → lead → body details + quote. A wrong answer putting the quote in the lead shows pyramid misunderstanding — the lead is pure 4W.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'I was thrilled to see the amazing projects!!!' — fix for a report.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'The exhibition featured 40 student projects' — quantify and attribute instead of exclaiming. A wrong answer removing only exclamation marks keeps the first-person stance — the whole reporting posture must change.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Where does 'said the Principal' attach, and why does the attribution matter?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
After the quoted clause — 'The standard was outstanding, said the Principal.' Attribution certifies the fact as reported, not invented — the mechanism that separates reports from opinions.
