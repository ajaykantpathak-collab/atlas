## DEFINITION
Preposition collocation is memorized territory: verbs, adjectives, and nouns each demand fixed partners — 'depend on', 'good at', 'afraid of', 'insist on', 'different from' (not 'than'), 'married to', 'listen to'. Wrong partners are the editing section's favourite error.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
remember

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student supplies the fixed preposition for ~40 high-frequency collocations and corrects the board-favourite errors (different-than, discuss-about, comprise-of).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers high-frequency verb/adjective + preposition collocations. Does NOT cover prepositional phrases or stranding.

## WALKTHROUGH
The correction list: 'different FROM' (never than), 'discuss' (no about), 'comprise' or 'comprised of' (active 'comprises' takes no of), 'return' (no back), 'enter the room' (no into for buildings when movement obvious — boards accept 'entered the room'), 'married to', 'angry with a person/about a thing'.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students translate Hindi postpositions into English prepositions — 'married with', 'angry on' — and add redundant partners to complete verbs ('discuss about', 'return back', 'repeat again').

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Learn collocations as units — 'afraid of', not 'afraid + some preposition'. Step 2: Redundancy hunt — about/back/again after complete verbs is suspect. Step 3: different FROM, similar TO, prefer X TO Y.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'She is different ___ her sister.' than/from/to?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'from' — 'different than' is the classic error boards test. A wrong answer 'than' shows unlearned collocation.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Fix: 'We discussed about the plan and then returned back home.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'discussed the plan' and 'returned home' — both partners are redundant; discuss and return are complete. A wrong answer fixing only one shows partial redundancy-auditing.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'He is married ___ a doctor.' with/to?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'to' — 'married to' is the fixed partner; 'married with' is Hindi-transfer ('se shaadi'). Same family: 'angry with', 'pleased with', 'familiar with/to'.
