## DEFINITION
Passive voice moves the object to subject position and demotes or deletes the doer: object→subject, verb→be+V3, subject→(by + agent, often dropped). Use it when the action or receiver matters more than the doer — 'The thief was caught', 'Rice is grown here'.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student converts all tenses active↔passive, preserves tense through the be-auxiliary, handles the double-object verbs (give/tell/teach) both ways, and knows when the agent must stay ('The storm destroyed…' vs anomalous cases).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers standard transitives across all tenses + when passive is preferred. Does NOT cover continuous-passive edge cases or stative passives.

## WALKTHROUGH
Show the surgery on one sentence: 'The cat chased the mouse.' Object 'the mouse' walks to the front; 'chased' becomes 'was chased' (be + V3, be carries the tense); 'the cat' demotes to 'by the cat' or vanishes. Then the insight: passive exists because sometimes the receiver IS the story — headlines prove it: 'Three injured in accident', nobody asks by whom.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students change the verb to V3 but forget the be-auxiliary ('The mouse chased'), keep the wrong tense in 'be' ('was chase' / 'is chased' for a past event), or produce passive where the doer is essential ('By me the homework was done').

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Find the object — that's your new subject. Step 2: Verb → be + V3, with 'be' matching the ORIGINAL tense and the NEW subject's number. Step 3: Old subject → 'by + agent' or delete it. Step 4: Sanity check — is the receiver the real story? If the doer matters, stay active.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Passive: 'The teacher praised the boy.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'The boy was praised (by the teacher).' boy→subject, praised→was praised (be carries past). A wrong answer 'the boy is praised' reveals the student changed the verb form but let the tense drift — the be-auxiliary must carry the original tense.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Passive: 'Someone has stolen my watch.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'My watch has been stolen.' Note the agent 'someone' is vague → deleted, not 'by someone'. A wrong answer 'my watch has stolen' shows the student forgot the be+V3 compound — participle alone can't carry voice.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Should this go passive? 'My grandmother knitted this sweater for me.' Defend.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Debatable, but staying active is defensible — the doer (grandmother) carries the emotional point. 'This sweater was knitted for me by my grandmother' buries the person who matters. A wrong answer mechanically passivizing reveals the student treats voice as a trick, not a meaning choice — the examiners' exact complaint.
