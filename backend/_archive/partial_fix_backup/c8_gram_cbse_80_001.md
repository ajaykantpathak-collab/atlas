## DEFINITION
By class 8 the tense system has nine working cells: three times × three aspects (simple, continuous, perfect). Perfect-continuous ('has been waiting') is the tenth cell — duration up to a reference point. The marker words (since, for, by the time, already, yet) select the cell before the verb is even touched.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student places verbs into all ten cells correctly, reads marker words to pre-select the tense, and distinguishes 'for/since' perfect-continuous use from simple perfect.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the full tense grid at identification and production level. Does NOT cover stative verbs or sequence of tenses (class 10-11).

## WALKTHROUGH
The duration test separates the perfects: 'I have waited an hour' (state, completed feel) vs 'I have been waiting an hour' (still waiting — duration emphasized). Since + starting point (since 2019), for + duration (for two years). Both demand the perfect family — never simple present.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students reach for the continuous whenever they see duration, producing 'I am waiting since morning' — the since-marker demands perfect-continuous, and the helper 'am' is the wrong anchor anyway.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Underline markers — since/for/yet/already/by the time. Step 2: Since/for + still-going → perfect-continuous. Step 3: Two ordered past events → earlier takes past perfect. Step 4: Build the verb phrase from the cell, not by feel.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'She ___ (wait) for the bus since morning.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'has been waiting' — 'since' + ongoing duration → present perfect continuous. A wrong answer 'is waiting' shows the student anchors to present instead of the since-marker — the most common class-8 slip.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'By the time the guests arrived, we ___ (finish) cooking.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'had finished' — 'by the time' orders two past events; earlier takes past perfect. A wrong answer 'finished' flattens the ordering the marker encodes.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Which is correct: 'I am knowing him for years' or 'I have known him for years'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The second — 'know' is stative and 'for years' demands the perfect family. A wrong answer accepting the first shows both the stative-ing error and marker-blindness at once.
