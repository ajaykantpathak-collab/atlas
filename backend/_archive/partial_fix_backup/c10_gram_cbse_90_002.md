## DEFINITION
Stative verbs — know, believe, love, own, seem, understand — describe states, not actions, and standard English refuses to put them in continuous forms. 'I am knowing' is wrong not because of a rule to memorize but because a state is already continuous by nature; adding -ing is double-marking.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Given a mixed verb list, the student separates stative from dynamic verbs with ≥90% accuracy, and corrects continuous-form statives in gap-fill items, including the know/think/have exceptions that flip meaning when used dynamically.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers common statives and the dynamic flips (think = ponder vs think = believe; have = possess vs have = eat/experience). Does NOT cover copular complements or tense grid mechanics (90_001).

## WALKTHROUGH
Contrast two board sentences: 'She owns a shop' vs '*She is owning a shop'. Ask: can you watch someone 'owning' for ten minutes? No — ownership is a state. Then flip 'have': 'I have a car' (state, no -ing) vs 'I am having lunch' (action, -ing fine). The test that works every time: can you DO it on purpose, right now, for a duration?

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students memorize a stative list without the state-vs-action test, so they freeze on unlisted verbs — and they over-apply the rule, writing '*I am not understanding' corrections where the informal continuous is actually accepted in modern usage for emphasis.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Ask 'is this something you DO, or something that IS?' Step 2: If it IS (a state), use simple form. Step 3: Check the trap verbs — think/have/see/taste — and ask which meaning is intended before deciding.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Correct or defend: 'I am not understanding this chapter.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Standard board answer: 'I do not understand this chapter' — 'understand' is stative. A wrong answer accepting it without comment reveals the student has no state/action test and relies on 'it sounds okay' — which fails on unlisted verbs.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'She is tasting the soup' vs 'The soup is tasting good' — which is correct and why?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The first is correct (tasting = the action of sampling), the second is wrong ('The soup tastes good' — taste = has-the-flavour is stative). A wrong answer accepting both shows the student missed that the SAME verb flips category with meaning — the node 's core insight.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Fill the blank: 'At the moment I ___ (believe) every word he says.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'believe' — simple present despite 'at the moment', because believe is stative. A wrong answer 'am believing' reveals the student let the time-marker trigger a continuous reflex without checking whether the verb can take -ing at all.
