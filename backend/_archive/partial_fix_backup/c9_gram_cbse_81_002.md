## DEFINITION
Modal perfects — should have, must have, could have, might have + V3 — talk about a past that is judged, deduced, or unrealized: 'should have studied' = reproach, 'must have left' = confident deduction, 'could have won' = lost chance.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student deploys the four modal perfects by speaker stance (reproach/deduction/unrealized-chance/weak-guess) and never writes 'modal + had'.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the modal perfect family. Does NOT cover present modal functions (81_001).

## WALKTHROUGH
The stance map: evidence about the past → 'must have' (deduction: 'the ground is wet — it must have rained'); unkept duty → 'should have' (reproach: 'you should have called'); lost opportunity → 'could have' ('he could have won'); weak guess → 'might have'. Form rule: always modal + have + V3 — 'should had' is always wrong.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students write 'should had' (double-past), confuse deduction-must with obligation-must, and use 'would have' without an if-condition.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Confirm the event is past → have + V3 is forced. Step 2: Name the stance — deduction (must), reproach (should), lost chance (could), weak guess (might). Step 3: 'would have' needs an if-clause or implied condition — don't float it.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'You failed — you ___ have studied harder.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'should' — reproach about an unkept duty. A wrong answer 'must' turns the sentence into a weird deduction — 'you must have studied' means the speaker concludes you DID study.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'The door is open — someone ___ have forgotten to lock it.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'must' — confident deduction from present evidence. A wrong answer 'should' confuses the two stances: evidence→must, duty→should.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Fix: 'He could had won the match.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'could have won' — modal perfects are modal + have + V3, never 'modal + had'. A wrong answer 'could won' drops the needed 'have' for past reference.
