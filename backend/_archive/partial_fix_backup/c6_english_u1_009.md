## DEFINITION
Letter writing is a structured way of communicating messages to specific people, divided into informal letters for friends and family, and formal letters for officials or businesses. Descriptive writing is the art of using sensory details—sight, touch, smell, taste, and sound—to paint a vivid picture of an object or experience for the reader.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to draft an informal letter with a greeting, body, and closing, and write a paragraph describing an object using at least three distinct sensory details.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the basic structure of informal letters and the use of adjectives for sensory description. It excludes formal letter formatting (like recipient addresses), email writing, and creative narrative essay writing.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wanted to tell your best friend about a new game you found, or describe the delicious taste of a mango to someone who has never eaten one? To begin, think of your audience: if you are writing to a friend, keep the language friendly and casual like a conversation. When describing something, don't just say "it is nice"; instead, pick up the object and ask, "How does it feel? Does it have a smell? What color is it?" By listing these specific details, you turn a simple sentence into a vivid description that helps the reader "see" what you are thinking.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the primary difference between an informal letter and a formal letter?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Informal letters are written to friends and family with casual language, while formal letters are for officials or businesses using specific formats. A wrong answer suggests the student hasn't grasped the purpose of the audience in communication.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do we use sensory details when writing a description?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
We use them to help the reader experience the object through their imagination, making the writing feel real. A wrong answer indicates the student thinks "description" is just listing facts rather than creating an experience.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
You are writing a letter to your cousin. Write the opening greeting and the first sentence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
"Dear [Name], I hope you are doing well." Steps: Identify the recipient, choose a warm salutation, and start with a friendly inquiry. A wrong answer shows a lack of understanding of informal tone.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the sentence "The banana is yellow" with "The banana has a smooth, yellow peel and tastes like creamy vanilla." Which is better for descriptive writing and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The second sentence is better because it adds texture, color, and taste. A wrong answer shows the student struggles to identify "adding detail" as the core of description.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If you are describing a rainy day, which set of sensory details is more effective: "It is raining and wet" or "The grey clouds rumble, the air smells like wet earth, and the water feels cold on my skin"?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The second set is more effective because it uses sound, smell, and touch. A wrong answer suggests the student confuses general statements with specific sensory imagery.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Write two sentences describing a fresh apple using at least two different senses.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
"The apple feels cool and smooth in my hand. When I bite it, it makes a crisp crunch and tastes sweet." A poor answer lacks sensory words or only describes the look of the apple.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often mistake descriptive writing for a list of adjectives. For example, they might write, "The dog is big, brown, fast, furry, and loud," which is just a list. Descriptive writing should link these qualities to an action or a sensory experience to create a scene.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student provides a list, tell them: "Choose one adjective and show me how it happens." Ask them, "Instead of saying 'the dog is loud,' can you describe how the sound makes you feel or what it sounds like?" Guide them to write: "The dog's loud bark echoed through the quiet street."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Describe a classroom desk for a friend who has never seen one.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the object (desk).
- step 2: Brainstorm senses (Touch: hard, smooth wood; Sight: brown, rectangular, scratched; Sound: hollow tapping).
- step 3: Select the strongest details (smooth, wood, scratched).
- step 4: Draft sentences.
- step 5: Combine into a paragraph.
REASONING: By focusing on specific senses, the reader can visualize the desk instead of just knowing its name.
ANSWER: The desk is made of smooth, brown wood. It is rectangular with small scratches on the top from years of use, and it makes a hollow sound when I tap on it.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Draft an informal letter to a friend about a school project.
STEPS:
- step 1: Choose a greeting (Dear Friend).
- step 2: State the reason for writing (to tell about the project).
- step 3: Include personal news (the project is about plants).
- step 4: Add a closing (Best wishes).
REASONING: Informal letters rely on a casual, friendly structure that builds personal connection.
ANSWER: Dear Rahul, I hope you are having a great week! I am writing to tell you about my new school project. We are growing different plants, and I chose to plant marigolds because they have such bright orange flowers. Let me know how your week is going! Best wishes, [Your Name].
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Improve the description "The market was crowded and noisy."
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the senses (Sight: crowded; Sound: noisy).
- step 2: Expand "crowded" (people bumping into each other, long lines).
- step 3: Expand "noisy" (vendors shouting, wheels clicking).
- step 4: Combine into a vivid paragraph.
- step 5: Review for flow and impact.
REASONING: Moving from abstract labels (crowded/noisy) to concrete actions makes the description vivid.
ANSWER: The market was filled with people bumping into each other in the narrow lanes. I could hear the vendors shouting their prices loudly, and the sound of wooden cart wheels clicking against the stone ground filled the air.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are writing to your friend about a visit to a banana plantation. Why should you describe the smell and the feeling of the leaves rather than just saying "the plantation was big"?
COMPETENCY: Reasoning/Understanding
STEPS:
- step 1: Compare abstract vs. concrete language.
- step 2: Explain how "big" is a vague measurement.
- step 3: Explain how sensory details evoke emotions.
- step 4: Conclude that sensory details make the reader feel like they are there.
ANSWER: Saying "the plantation was big" only gives a size, which is boring. Describing the scent of the fruit and the texture of the leaves makes the friend feel like they are actually walking through the plantation with you.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Review a partner's letter. If they have only written "It was a nice day," how can you help them improve it using the concepts of descriptive writing?
COMPETENCY: Evaluation/Application
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify that "nice" is a generic/weak adjective.
- step 2: Ask the partner what made the day nice (e.g., weather, food, friends).
- step 3: Suggest adding sensory details related to those factors.
- step 4: Provide an example replacement.
- step 5: Structure the advice clearly.
ANSWER: I would tell my partner to replace "nice" with specific details. I would ask, "What did you see or feel?" If they say they played in the sun, I would suggest they write, "The sun felt warm on my face, and the sky was a clear, bright blue."
DIFFICULTY: hard
