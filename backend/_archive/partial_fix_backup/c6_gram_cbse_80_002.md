## DEFINITION
Sentences do four jobs and each job has a name: declarative (states), interrogative (asks), imperative (commands/requests), exclamatory (exclaims). The punctuation is the costume — the job is the meaning: 'Close the door.' commands; 'What a shot!' exclaims.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
remember

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student names the type of any sentence from its job and punctuation, converts one type into another, and punctuates each correctly (. ? !).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the four types and their end-punctuation. Does NOT cover reported speech or question tags.

## WALKTHROUGH
Line up four sentences about one event: 'You scored a goal.' / 'Did you score a goal?' / 'Score a goal!' / 'What a goal!' Same event, four jobs. The test students can always run: is it telling, asking, ordering, or exclaiming?

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students classify by punctuation alone and miss that 'Please sit down' is imperative even when it is politely phrased — and they call every 'what/how' opening interrogative when 'What a day!' is exclamatory.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Ask what the sentence DOES — tell, ask, order, exclaim. Step 2: Match the job to the type name. Step 3: Punctuate to match — 'what/how' + no question intended = exclamation mark.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Classify: 'What a beautiful painting this is!'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Exclamatory — it exclaims admiration, it does not ask. A wrong answer 'interrogative' reveals the student reads 'what' as question-automatic — the exclamation mark and the admiring tone are the real signals.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Convert to interrogative: 'She finished the puzzle.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'Did she finish the puzzle?' A wrong answer 'She finished the puzzle?' relies on intonation alone — boards want the proper inverted form.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Which is imperative: 'The window is open' / 'Is the window open?' / 'Open the window' / 'How open the window is!'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'Open the window' — it gives a command; the subject 'you' is understood. A wrong answer picking the exclamatory shows the student matches keywords, not jobs.
