## DEFINITION
Science is a systematic method of exploring the natural world by making observations, asking questions about how things work, and testing those questions to find evidence-based answers. It is not merely a list of facts, but a continuous journey of discovery that helps us understand the universe around us.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the definition of science and the basic scientific method (observation, questioning, guessing, testing, analysis). It excludes the philosophy of science, historical timelines of scientists, and specific complex scientific laws.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine your favorite flashlight suddenly stops working; what is the first thing you do? Instead of just saying it is broken, you look at it, guess why (like a dead battery), and try a simple test (like swapping the battery). This is exactly how science works—you observe a problem, form a guess, test it, and see if your guess was right. Science is just this "detective" process applied to the world around us, from why flowers bloom to how stars shine. By asking "why" and "how," you move from just seeing the world to truly understanding it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the best way to describe "Science"?
A) A giant book containing all the facts about the world.
B) A process of observing, questioning, and testing ideas to understand the natural world.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is B. Choosing A shows a misunderstanding that science is static and finished, whereas B correctly identifies it as a dynamic, ongoing method of inquiry.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you see a plant wilting and decide to water it to see if it recovers, have you used the scientific method? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Yes, because you observed a problem (wilting), formed a hypothesis/guess (it needs water), and performed a test (watering it). A wrong answer suggests the student thinks only people in labs with equipment are "scientists," failing to see the method in daily life.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Your friend says, "Science is just memorizing facts from the textbook." How would you use the concept of the scientific method to explain why they are wrong?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Explain that if science were just memorization, it would never change; however, we know science changes as we discover new things. By following the steps—observing, questioning, and testing—we generate new knowledge that often proves old facts wrong or incomplete.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare these two: A student who reads that "water boils at 100°C" and a student who heats water and measures the temperature as it bubbles. Which one is "doing" science and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The second student is doing science because they are moving from observation to evidence-based testing. The first student is merely receiving information; the second is validating it through the scientific method.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
"If a scientific experiment gives a result that contradicts what is written in our book, the experiment must be wrong." Do you agree? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, I disagree. If an experiment is done carefully and consistently, the result is the evidence we use to update our knowledge. Science is an evolving puzzle, and new pieces (discoveries) often require us to rearrange what we thought we knew.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a short plan to investigate why a specific type of ball bounces higher than another. What are your four steps?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
1. Observation: Notice the difference in bounce height. 2. Question: "Does the material of the ball affect how high it bounces?" 3. Guess/Hypothesis: "The rubber ball will bounce higher than the plastic one." 4. Test: Drop both from the same height and measure the bounce. Poor answers skip the "test" or "guess" phase.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that science is "truth" that never changes or that it is only performed by experts in white coats. This occurs because textbooks present information as final, ignoring the "work" that went into discovering those facts. For example, a student might think "the earth is round" is just a rule to memorize, rather than a conclusion reached through centuries of observation and evidence.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student thinks science is just facts, have them pick a common item (like a pencil) and ask three "how" or "why" questions about it that the textbook doesn't answer (e.g., "Why does the graphite break so easily?"). Guide them to propose a test, such as applying different pressures, to find the answer. This shifts their focus from passive memorization to active investigation.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Your kitchen tap is dripping. Use the scientific method to fix it.
STEPS:
- 1. Observation: The tap is dripping water even when closed.
- 2. Question: "Is the washer inside worn out, or is the handle loose?"
- 3. Guess: "The washer is likely worn out and not sealing the pipe."
- 4. Test: Open the tap, inspect the washer for cracks, and if cracked, replace it with a new one.
- 5. Result: Observe if the dripping stops after the replacement.
REASONING: This follows the method by turning a problem into a testable hypothesis, ensuring we don't just guess randomly but investigate the specific cause.
ANSWER: The dripping stops because the evidence (cracked washer) led to a successful repair.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A notebook in your bag is wet, but it didn't rain. How do you find the cause?
STEPS:
- 1. Observation: The notebook is wet; the water bottle in the bag is also there.
- 2. Question: "Is the water bottle leaking, or did I spill water earlier?"
- 3. Guess: "The water bottle cap is loose."
- 4. Test: Fill the bottle with water, close it, and turn it upside down over a sink to see if drops fall.
- 5. Result: If water drips, the bottle is the cause.
REASONING: By isolating the potential source and creating a controlled test, we confirm the root cause through evidence.
ANSWER: The water bottle was the source of the leak.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: You notice your bicycle tire is flat. How do you apply the scientific method to solve this?
STEPS:
- 1. Observation: The bicycle tire is flat/soft.
- 2. Question: "Is there a hole in the tube, or is the valve leaking?"
- 3. Guess: "There is a puncture in the tube."
- 4. Test: Submerge the inflated tube in a bucket of water and look for air bubbles.
- 5. Result: Bubbles appear at a specific spot on the rubber.
- 6. Conclusion: The puncture is the cause, and the patch is needed.
REASONING: This combines observational skill with a physical test (the water bucket) to turn a "guess" into a verified fact, demonstrating the core of scientific inquiry.
ANSWER: A puncture in the rubber tube was identified using the bubble test.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A classmate says, "Science is just for people who are good at math." How would you explain that science is actually about the 'method' of thinking? Provide one example.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to scientific logic.
STEPS:
- 1. Define science as a way of asking questions.
- 2. Identify that asking "why" happens in all subjects, not just math.
- 3. Use an example like observing a plant's growth to show it requires no math, just observation.
ANSWER: Science is a method of inquiry. You don't need math to observe that a plant grows toward light; you only need to observe, question why, and test it by turning the plant away from the light.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: You have two different brands of paper towels. How would you design a simple experiment to find out which one absorbs more water?
COMPETENCY: Designing a systematic investigation.
STEPS:
- 1. Question: Which paper towel holds more water?
- 2. Hypothesis: Brand A will hold more because it is thicker.
- 3. Method: Dip equal-sized pieces in equal amounts of water for the same time.
- 4. Measurement: Remove them and measure how much water is left in the container.
- 5. Conclusion: The one that left less water in the container absorbed more.
ANSWER: By keeping the size of the paper and the volume of water the same (the "control"), you ensure the test is fair and the result is based on evidence.
DIFFICULTY: hard
