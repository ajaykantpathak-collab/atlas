## DEFINITION
Character analysis is the process of looking at what a character says, does, and feels to understand their personality, motivations, and why they act the way they do. It involves connecting a character's internal desires with their external life circumstances.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify at least two traits of a character and provide one piece of evidence from the story that supports each trait.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node focuses exclusively on analyzing the character of Jahnavi from "The Day the River Spoke." It does not cover other characters, plot summaries, or literary devices like metaphor or irony.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wanted to learn something new but felt like you didn't have the time or money to do it? To start analyzing Jahnavi, first think about what she *wants* (her dream) compared to what she *has* (her village life). Write down two things Jahnavi does that show her intelligence, and two things her family or village life does that stop her. By placing these side-by-side, you reveal the "tension" that defines her character. The key insight is that characters are defined not just by who they are, but by the obstacles they choose to overcome.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the main goal or dream that Jahnavi has in the story?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Jahnavi dreams of receiving an education and living a life beyond her village. A wrong answer here suggests the student has confused her personal aspirations with the general plot events.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is it difficult for Jahnavi to achieve her dream of education?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Her family's economic situation and gender expectations limit her access to schooling. If a student only mentions one (e.g., "she is poor"), they are missing the full picture of the barriers she faces.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If Jahnavi were offered a chance to travel to a city for school but had to leave her brothers behind, how might she react based on her character?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
She would likely feel torn; she values her family, but her desire for an education is strong enough to make her consider the opportunity. A wrong answer ignores the internal conflict that defines her.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare Jahnavi’s situation to her environment. How does the "coastal village" serve as more than just a place to live for her?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
It represents the "confines" or limits of her current world, acting as a barrier to her growth. A student failing to see this sees the setting as just a location rather than a character-shaping force.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Do you think Jahnavi’s intelligence makes her more or less frustrated with her life? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It makes her more frustrated because she is "bright and capable" enough to know what she is missing. A wrong answer treats intelligence as a neutral trait rather than a source of her internal tension.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Write a short diary entry from Jahnavi’s perspective describing one thing she did today that shows her desire for a different life.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The entry should show her noticing something beyond her village or working hard despite her limitations. A poor answer is generic and does not connect to her specific desire for education or her village life.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse a character's "wishes" with their "personality traits." For example, they might say "Jahnavi is educated" because she wants to be, rather than saying "Jahnavi is ambitious" because she *seeks* education despite her poverty.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to fill in a "T-chart." On the left side, list actions (what she does). On the right side, list the personality trait those actions prove (e.g., "She reads discarded newspapers" = "She is curious and ambitious"). Explain that traits are the *reason* for the actions, not the actions themselves.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Analyze Jahnavi’s character using the tension between her dreams and her reality.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify her dream (education beyond the village).
- step 2: Identify her reality (economic constraints and village life).
- step 3: Look for her traits (bright, capable, persistent).
- step 4: Combine these to form a character summary.
REASONING: By mapping these components, we see that her traits are the "fuel" for her dream, which is currently blocked by her reality.
ANSWER: Jahnavi is an ambitious and capable girl who feels trapped by the limited resources of her village.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Provide evidence for why Jahnavi is considered "capable."
STEPS:
- step 1: Define what "capable" means (having the power or skill to do something).
- step 2: Scan the text/story context for moments where she learns or understands things.
- step 3: Note her desire for learning as proof of her potential.
- step 4: Summarize why this makes her "capable."
REASONING: Evidence for character traits must always be grounded in what the character does or wants.
ANSWER: Jahnavi is capable because she shows a strong, sustained interest in learning that goes beyond her daily chores, proving she has the mind to study if given the chance.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Compare Jahnavi’s internal conflict with her external environment.
STEPS:
- step 1: List the external environment (coastal village, family responsibilities).
- step 2: List her internal conflict (desire for change vs. duty to family).
- step 3: Analyze how the village acts as a physical barrier.
- step 4: Analyze how the family acts as a social barrier.
- step 5: Synthesize the two to explain the "tension."
REASONING: This shows the complexity of her character—she is not just "unhappy," she is "in conflict."
ANSWER: Jahnavi’s external environment is a coastal village that provides safety but limits her future; her internal conflict arises because she loves her family but needs to leave them to grow, creating a persistent sense of tension.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a new library opened in Jahnavi’s village, how would she likely respond, and what does this tell us about her character?
COMPETENCY: Application of character analysis.
STEPS:
- step 1: Predict her action (she would go there immediately).
- step 2: Link action to desire (shows she seeks knowledge).
- step 3: Link action to trait (shows she is proactive).
- step 4: Conclude what this reveals.
ANSWER: She would be the first person at the library, which proves that her desire for education is a driving force in her life, showing she is determined and proactive.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Jahnavi’s brothers do not seem to have the same desire for education. How does this detail enhance our analysis of Jahnavi?
COMPETENCY: Comparative analysis.
STEPS:
- step 1: Contrast her brothers' lack of ambition with her own.
- step 2: Identify what this highlights about Jahnavi (she is an outlier).
- step 3: Consider the gender aspect mentioned in the scope.
- step 4: Explain why this makes her character more significant.
ANSWER: By contrasting her with her brothers, the story highlights that Jahnavi’s ambition is unique and likely faces additional gender-based hurdles, making her character more resilient and focused than those around her.
DIFFICULTY: hard
