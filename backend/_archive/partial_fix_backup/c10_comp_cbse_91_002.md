## DEFINITION
A notice is a public announcement in a box: organization name → NOTICE → date → heading → body answering What/When/Where/Who-contact → signature+designation. Fifty words max, third person, no 'I'. Every element is a mark; the box itself often carries one.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student drafts a complete notice under 50 words with all 6 elements, in third-person declaratives, with a body that answers the 4W questions, and can audit a defective notice for missing elements and register violations.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers school-event notices, lost/found, and meeting notices. Does NOT cover reports (92_001).

## WALKTHROUGH
Show a real school notice, then strip it: everything outside the box is the skeleton, everything inside answers What-When-Where-Whom. The register rule is the trap: notices are announcements, not requests — 'All students are informed' not 'I want to tell you'. Word limit is part of the format.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students exceed 50 words, write in first person, forget the issuing authority line, or write the body as an essay paragraph instead of clipped announcement sentences.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Box it — literally draw the box first. Step 2: Stack the header: SCHOOL NAME / NOTICE / date / heading. Step 3: Body in third person answering What/When/Where/Whom-to-contact. Step 4: Signature + designation. Step 5: Count words — over 50, cut.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What's wrong: 'NOTICE — I am happy to inform you all that we are having a very exciting and fun annual day celebration on 15th March at 10 am in the school auditorium and everyone should definitely come and bring their parents too!!!' (68 words)

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Nearly everything: first person, exclamations, no org name/date/heading/contact, 68 words, essay-tone. Correct: clipped third-person announcement under 50 words. A wrong answer listing only word-count shows the student sees the limit but not the register violations.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Reorder a notice's header: heading, date, NOTICE, organization name.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Organization name → NOTICE → date → heading. A wrong answer putting the heading first reveals the student treats the notice like an essay title instead of a bureaucratic instrument — the issuing authority leads because authority IS the format.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Which body sentence fits a notice: 'You should totally come!' or 'Interested students may register with the undersigned by 10 March'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The second — third person, action + deadline + contact, zero enthusiasm-performance. A wrong answer choosing the first reveals the student hasn't internalized that notices inform; they never persuade.
