## DEFINITION
A report records an event for readers who weren't there: headline → byline → place+date → body in inverted pyramid (what happened → key details → quotes → outcome). Past tense, third person, passive-friendly, factual. Newspaper reports add the dateline; school reports drop the headline.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student structures a report in inverted-pyramid order, maintains past-tense third-person objectivity, integrates one quote correctly, and distinguishes report register from narrative register (no suspense, no 'I was so excited').

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers event reports for school magazines/newspapers. Does NOT cover analytical paragraphs on data (92_002).

## WALKTHROUGH
The inverted pyramid IS the genre: first paragraph answers What-When-Where in one breath; the middle adds the how and a quote; the tail carries the least-critical detail — so an editor can cut from the bottom. Compare a report opening vs a story opening: 'The annual science exhibition was held on 12 March in the main hall' vs 'It was a bright morning when…' — same event, different contract with the reader.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students write reports as narratives — chronological, first-person, suspenseful — because stories are the only long-form model they have. The report's job is opposite: give the ending first.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Headline answers the whole event in <10 words. Step 2: Lead paragraph = What+When+Where. Step 3: Body = how it unfolded + one attributed quote ('said the principal'). Step 4: Close with outcome/participation. Step 5: Audit — every 'I', every exclamation, every suspense word gets cut.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which opening is a report: 'The inter-school debate was held on 5 September at the auditorium' or 'The morning air buzzed with excitement as we entered the hall'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The first — it front-loads the 4W facts; the second is narrative scene-setting. A wrong answer reveals the student can't yet separate the report contract (inform first) from the story contract (immerse first).

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'I really enjoyed the amazing science exhibition, it was the best day ever!!!' — fix for a report.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'The exhibition drew over 500 visitors, according to the organisers' — factual, third-person, quantified. A wrong answer that merely removes exclamation marks keeps first-person enthusiasm — the fix is changing the reporting stance, not the punctuation.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Where does 'said the Principal' belong: 'The event was a great success, said the Principal' — correct quote integration?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Yes — quote with attribution tag is standard. A wrong answer rewriting to 'the Principal was very happy' shows the student paraphrases away the attribution structure that reports use to certify factuality.
