## DEFINITION
The class 12 article adds argumentative depth: title + byline → hook intro → body paragraphs each advancing a distinct angle (causes, effects, solutions) with examples → a takeaway conclusion. Semi-formal register permits a controlled voice; 150-200 words.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
create

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student writes an article with a hook opening, distinct-angle body paragraphs supported by examples, and a forward-looking conclusion — controlling voice within semi-formal bounds.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers argumentative/analytical articles. Does NOT cover editorials or opinion columns.

## WALKTHROUGH
The angle-map keeps paragraphs distinct: a 'digital distraction' article runs causes (notification design), effects (attention metrics), solutions (digital hygiene) — each paragraph owns one angle with an example. The conclusion points forward — a suggestion or a measured prediction, never a summary.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students write three paragraphs that restate the thesis in different words, open with 'I am going to write about', and close by repeating the title.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Title + byline. Step 2: Hook — question, statistic, or micro-anecdote. Step 3: Body = distinct angles (causes/effects/solutions), each with an example. Step 4: Conclusion = takeaway or suggestion. Step 5: Register check — voice yes, slang no.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which body-plan is stronger for 'Exam Stress': (a) three paragraphs each saying stress is harmful, or (b) causes → effects → coping strategies?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
(b) — distinct angles advance the article; restating the thesis thrice adds words, not development. A wrong answer shows the student equates length with development.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Which opener is article-grade: 'Every night, a million students stare at glowing screens until 2 am — and call it studying' or 'This article is about exam stress'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The first — a scene-setting hook with a claim embedded. The second announces the article instead of beginning it.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Is 'Exams are literally destroying our generation, no cap' acceptable in a school-magazine article?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
No — articles permit voice but hold semi-formal register; 'literally/no cap' slang breaks it. A wrong answer shows over-correction from formal to casual.
