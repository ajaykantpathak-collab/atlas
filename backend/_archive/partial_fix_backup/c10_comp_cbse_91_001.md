## DEFINITION
An informal letter keeps a lighter skeleton — sender's address, date, 'Dear [name]', conversational body, 'Yours lovingly/affectionately' — and ADDS what formal letters ban: contractions, personal voice, questions to the reader, idioms. The register is friendly; the skeleton is still marked.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student produces the informal skeleton correctly and sustains a warm, personal register — including choosing the correct salutation-subscription pair (Dear Rahul ↔ Yours lovingly) and writing a body that sounds like speech, not a report.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the informal skeleton and its register permissions. Does NOT cover formal formats (chapter 90) or notices (91_002).

## WALKTHROUGH
Same skeleton diagram, fewer blocks: no receiver's address, no subject. The body test: read it aloud — if it sounds like a person talking to a friend ('Guess what! I finally learned to swim.'), it's right; if it sounds like a report ('Swimming lessons were undertaken'), it's wrong. Informal letters are marked on sounding human.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students either keep the full formal skeleton (adding subject lines to letters to friends) or drop all structure (no address/date), and they import formal stiffness ('I am writing to inform you that I won the prize') where warmth belongs.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Skeleton — address, date, Dear ___, body, loving sign-off. Step 2: Write like you speak — contractions allowed, questions to the reader encouraged. Step 3: Pair 'Dear Name' with 'Yours lovingly/affectionately' — never 'faithfully'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Pick the right sign-off for a letter to your cousin: Yours faithfully / Yours lovingly / Yours sincerely.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'Yours lovingly' (affectionately also fine) — personal relationship → personal subscription. A wrong answer 'faithfully' reveals the student memorized one pair and applies it universally — the pairing is relationship-indexed, not letter-indexed.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Fix this informal opener: 'I am writing to apprise you of my recent academic achievement.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Something like 'You'll never guess — I topped the class!' A wrong answer merely removing contractions shows the student thinks informality is a surface feature — the real signal is communicative intent (sharing excitement), not grammar.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Does an informal letter need a subject line?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
No — subject lines belong to formal/business formats. A wrong answer adding one shows the student hasn't separated the two skeletons and treats 'letter' as one format with optional parts.
