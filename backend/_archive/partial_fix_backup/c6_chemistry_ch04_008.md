## DEFINITION
Metals are elements typically characterized by a shiny (lustrous) appearance, hardness, and the ability to conduct heat and electricity, whereas non-metals generally lack these properties, appearing dull and acting as poor conductors.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to classify a given list of common materials into metals and non-metals based on experimental observations like shine, hardness, and conductivity.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the identification and physical property comparison of metals and non-metals as per Class 6 standards; it excludes atomic structure, electron configuration, and chemical reactivity trends.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wondered why your kitchen spoons are made of stainless steel while your water bottle might be made of plastic? To begin, look at the objects around you and test them: does it shine when you rub it with sandpaper? Is it hard enough that you can't dent it with your thumbnail? By observing properties like lustre, hardness, and conductivity, we can group materials into the "Metal" family or the "Non-metal" family. Remember, metals are usually the "shiny, hard, and conductive" ones, while non-metals are the "dull, brittle, or soft" ones. The key insight is that an object's material determines its use in our daily lives.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Name two physical properties that generally distinguish metals from non-metals.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Metals are typically lustrous (shiny) and good conductors of electricity, while non-metals are typically dull and poor conductors. A wrong answer here suggests the student is confusing physical properties with chemical ones or simply guessing without reading the definitions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you find a material that is dull and breaks easily when you hit it with a hammer, is it likely a metal or a non-metal?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It is likely a non-metal. If a student says "metal," they have failed to connect the property of "hardness" and "lustre" to the definition of metals, indicating a need to re-examine the physical characteristics provided in the text.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A piece of copper wire conducts electricity, but a piece of plastic tube does not. How does this help us categorize them?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Copper is a metal because it conducts electricity, and plastic is a non-metal because it is a poor conductor. The student must use the property of conductivity as a test; a wrong answer shows they are not applying the "conductor" criteria to the classification process.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why do we say that "all that glitters is not gold" when classifying metals?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Some non-metals or synthetic materials can be made to look shiny through polishing or coatings, even though they aren't metals. A wrong answer indicates the student is over-relying on the single property of "shine" without considering other properties like hardness or conductivity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If an object is very hard but does not conduct heat, would you classify it as a metal? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it is likely not a metal because metals generally possess both hardness and the ability to conduct heat. A wrong answer shows the student thinks one property (hardness) is enough for classification, ignoring the requirement for multiple properties.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple test to determine if a mysterious, unlabelled rod found in your garage is a metal.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The student should suggest: 1) Checking if it is shiny (lustre), 2) Checking if it is hard (cannot be scratched by a fingernail), and 3) Testing if it conducts electricity (using a battery and bulb). A poor answer fails to list at least two distinct properties, showing a lack of systematic observation skills.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that because a material is shiny, it MUST be a metal. For example, they might classify polished plastic or glass as a metal. This happens because they prioritize the "lustre" property over "conductivity" and "hardness," forgetting that surface coatings can make non-metals appear metallic.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to touch the object and try to scratch it with their nail to check hardness. Second, ask them to check if the object can complete an electrical circuit with a small bulb. Explain that "lustre" is only one clue, and a true metal must pass the "hard" and "conductive" tests as well.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You have a spoon (iron), a rubber eraser, a gold ring, and a wooden block. Group them into two categories.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine each item for lustre (shine).
- Step 2: Examine each item for hardness (scratch test).
- Step 3: Group items that are both shiny and hard.
- Step 4: Group items that are dull or soft.
REASONING: Metals are defined by the combination of shine and hardness. The spoon and ring share these properties, while the eraser and wood do not.
ANSWER: Metals: Iron spoon, gold ring. Non-metals: Rubber eraser, wooden block.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A material is flexible, dull, and light. Is it a metal or non-metal?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Check lustre (dull = non-metal characteristic).
- Step 2: Check hardness (flexible = not hard = non-metal characteristic).
- Step 3: Compare with metal properties (shiny, hard).
- Step 4: Conclude based on the majority of properties.
REASONING: Since the material lacks both lustre and hardness, it fits the definition of a non-metal perfectly.
ANSWER: Non-metal.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: You have a piece of graphite (from a pencil) and a piece of aluminium foil. Both look somewhat shiny. How do you distinguish which is the metal?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Test hardness: Scratch both. Aluminium is hard, graphite is brittle/soft.
- Step 2: Test conductivity: Both conduct, so this isn't the differentiator.
- Step 3: Analyze the "metal" definition: Metals must be hard.
- Step 4: Confirm aluminium is the metal because it meets the "hardness" criteria.
REASONING: While graphite is an exception in conductivity, it fails the hardness test for metals, helping us identify the true metal.
ANSWER: Aluminium foil is the metal.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have a shiny necklace. How would you prove it is made of metal without using fire or chemicals?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Step 1: Observe lustre (is it shiny?).
- Step 2: Check hardness (is it easily scratched?).
- Step 3: Test conductivity (does it conduct electricity?).
- Step 4: Compare results to the definition of metals.
ANSWER: If it is hard and conducts electricity, it is likely metal. If it is easily scratched or does not conduct, it might be a coated non-metal.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why do we use copper for electrical wires but not for clothes? Use your knowledge of metal properties.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to scientific properties
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify properties of copper (hard, conductive, lustrous).
- Step 2: Identify requirements for clothes (soft, flexible, insulating).
- Step 3: Compare requirements to properties.
- Step 4: Justify why the properties of metals (hardness) make them unsuitable for clothes.
ANSWER: Copper is a metal and is too hard and rigid to be worn as clothing; clothes need to be flexible and soft (non-metal properties), whereas copper is used for wires because it is an excellent conductor of electricity.
DIFFICULTY: hard
