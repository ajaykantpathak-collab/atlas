## DEFINITION
The full reported-speech machine at class 9: statements take 'that' + backshift; questions take if/whether (yes/no) or the wh-word, with statement order restored and the question mark dropped; commands take told/ordered/requested + object + (not) to-infinitive.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student converts all three sentence types between direct and indirect speech with correct backshift, connector choice, and deictic changes, and reports with appropriate verbs (asked, told, ordered, requested, advised, warned).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the complete board-tested reported speech system. Does NOT cover reporting exclamations or question tags.

## WALKTHROUGH
Type-check first, transform second: (1) statement → said/told + that + backshifted clause; (2) yes/no question → asked + if/whether + statement order; (3) wh-question → asked + wh-word + statement order; (4) command → told + object + (not) to-infinitive. Every type kills the original's grammar and rebuilds inside the report frame.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students memorize the statement rule and apply it to everything — 'He asked that where did I go' mixes all three frame errors at once.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Identify the sentence type. Step 2: Pick the frame — that / if-whether / wh-word / to-infinitive. Step 3: Rebuild inside the frame — statement order, backshift, deictics. Step 4: Reporting verb matches the original's force (please→requested, don't→warned).

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Report: 'Where are you going?' she asked me.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'She asked me where I was going.' wh-word kept, order restored, are→was. A wrong answer 'where was I going' keeps question order inside the report.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Report: 'Please wait here,' the teacher said to us.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'The teacher requested us to wait there.' 'please' → requested; here→there. A wrong answer 'the teacher said that please wait' glues 'that' onto an imperative — illegal frame.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Report: 'I may go to Delhi tomorrow,' he said.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'He said (that) he might go to Delhi the next day.' may→might, tomorrow→the next day. A wrong answer 'he may go tomorrow' skips both shifts.
