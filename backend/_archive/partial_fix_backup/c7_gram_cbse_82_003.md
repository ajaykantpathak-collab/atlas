## DEFINITION
Adjectives describe nouns (a TALL building), adverbs describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs (runs QUICKLY, VERY tall, quite quickly). The -ly ending is a hint, not a rule — 'fast' doubles as both, and 'friendly' is an adjective despite the -ly.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student identifies what a modifier modifies, forms adverbs correctly (quick→quickly, easy→easily), uses good/well and hard/hardly correctly, and avoids double adverbials.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers adjective vs adverb distinction and tricky pairs. Does NOT cover degrees of comparison systematically (ch 83) or adverb placement rules in depth.

## WALKTHROUGH
The job test: ask 'what does it describe?' 'A quick runner' — quick describes the noun (adjective). 'She runs quickly' — quickly describes the verb (adverb). Trap pairs: 'good' is the adjective, 'well' is its adverb ('She is a good dancer, she dances well'); 'hard' works both ways ('work hard'), but 'hardly' means 'almost not' — a different word entirely.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students use adjectives as adverbs ('She sings beautiful'), trust the -ly test blindly ('friendly' is an adjective), and miss that 'hardly' flips meaning to 'almost not'.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Find what the word modifies — noun → adjective; verb/adjective/adverb → adverb. Step 2: Form adverbs: +ly; y→ily (easy→easily). Step 3: Learn the rebel pairs: good/well, hard/hardly, fast/fast, late/lately.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'She sings beautiful.' — fix and explain.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'sings beautifully' — the word modifies the verb 'sings', so it must be an adverb. A wrong answer keeping 'beautiful' shows the student doesn't apply the modifies-what test.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'He works ___ all day.' hard or hardly?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'hard' — works with effort. 'Hardly' = almost not ('He hardly works' = almost never works) — a wrong answer shows the trap pair isn't learned as separate words.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'friendly' — adjective or adverb?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Adjective — 'a friendly dog'. It cannot be an adverb despite the -ly (the adverb form is 'in a friendly way'). A wrong answer 'adverb' shows the -ly-test is being trusted blindly.
