## DEFINITION
Rounding numbers is the process of replacing an exact number with a nearby, simpler value to make calculations or communication easier. It is used when exact precision is not required, such as estimating the number of people in a crowd or the total cost of groceries.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the target digit and use the "round up/round down" rule to correctly round any 3-digit number to the nearest ten or hundred.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers rounding whole numbers to the nearest ten and hundred. It does not cover decimal rounding, significant figures, or error analysis of estimates.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at a local market with ₹48 in your pocket. If a friend asks how much money you have, you might say "about ₹50" because it’s easier to say and remember. To round 48, look at the digit to the right of the tens place: the ones digit (8). Since 8 is 5 or greater, we "round up" the tens digit (4 becomes 5) and turn the ones digit to 0, giving us 50. If you had ₹43, the 3 is less than 5, so you "round down" (keep the 4 as is) and get 40.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the rule for rounding a digit when the number to its right is 4 or less?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
If the digit to the right is 4 or less, you "round down," meaning the target digit stays the same and all digits to its right become zero. A wrong answer suggests the student confuses "round down" with changing the target digit to a lower value instead of keeping it constant.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
When rounding 167 to the nearest ten, why do we focus on the number 7?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
We focus on 7 because it is in the ones place, which determines whether the tens digit (6) stays at 6 or increases to 7. Choosing the wrong digit reveals a misunderstanding of place value positions relative to the target place.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Round 342 to the nearest ten and then round 342 to the nearest hundred.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Nearest ten: 340 (since 2 < 5). Nearest hundred: 300 (since 4 < 5). An error here shows the student is not identifying the correct "neighbor" digit for different target places.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If a stadium holds 855 people, why might rounding to 900 be a better estimate than 800?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
855 is closer to 900 than to 800 on the number line, and the rule for rounding 5 is to round up. Students who prefer 800 are ignoring the rule that 5 or more requires rounding up to the next hundred.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it always better to round to the nearest hundred than the nearest ten? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it depends on the context; rounding to the nearest ten provides more precision, while rounding to the nearest hundred provides a quicker, rougher estimate. An answer stating "yes" shows the student doesn't understand that precision levels change based on the need for detail.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a 3-digit number that rounds to 500 when rounded to the nearest hundred, but rounds to 490 when rounded to the nearest ten.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A valid answer is 492, 493, or 494. A poor answer (like 501) indicates the student does not understand how rounding to different place values creates different results.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to round by looking at the wrong digit, such as rounding 167 to the nearest ten by looking at the 1 instead of the 7. This happens because they do not realize the "neighboring digit to the right" is the only one that dictates the change.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to underline the digit in the place they are rounding to (e.g., the tens place). Then, tell them to draw an arrow to the digit immediately to its right. Explain: "The arrow points to the 'boss' digit—it decides whether the underlined digit stays the same or goes up."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You bought a notebook for ₹128. Round this cost to the nearest ten to tell your friend.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the tens digit (2) and the neighbor to its right (8).
- step 2: Compare 8 to 5. Since 8 > 5, we round up.
- step 3: Increase the tens digit (2) by 1 to get 3.
- step 4: Change all digits to the right of the tens place to zero.
- step 5: The result is 130.
REASONING: Rounding to the nearest ten requires looking at the ones place to see if we are closer to the lower ten or the next higher ten. 128 is closer to 130 than to 120.
ANSWER: ₹130
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A local library has 443 books. Round this to the nearest hundred.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the hundreds digit (4) and the neighbor to its right (4).
- step 2: Compare 4 to 5. Since 4 < 5, we round down.
- step 3: Keep the hundreds digit (4) the same.
- step 4: Change all digits to the right of the hundreds place to zero.
- step 5: The result is 400.
REASONING: Since the tens digit (4) is less than 5, the number is closer to the current hundred than the next hundred.
ANSWER: 400 books
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A farmer has 765 kilograms of wheat. Round to the nearest ten, then round to the nearest hundred.
STEPS:
- step 1: Round to nearest ten: Target is 6, neighbor is 5. 5 means round up. Tens digit 6 becomes 7. Result: 770.
- step 2: Round to nearest hundred: Target is 7, neighbor is 6. 6 means round up. Hundreds digit 7 becomes 8. Result: 800.
- step 3: Compare results.
- step 4: Verify that rounding to a larger place value (hundreds) often changes the number more than rounding to a smaller place value (tens).
REASONING: Rounding rules are applied independently based on the target place.
ANSWER: 770 kg and 800 kg
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are estimating the number of people at a fair. You count 248 people. Your friend counts 252. If you both round to the nearest ten, do you get the same result? Explain why.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Round 248 to the nearest ten: 8 > 5, so 248 becomes 250.
- step 2: Round 252 to the nearest ten: 2 < 5, so 252 becomes 250.
- step 3: Compare the results.
ANSWER: Yes, both round to 250 because 248 is just below 250 and 252 is just above 250. This shows that rounding can help different people arrive at the same useful estimate.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A shopkeeper says he has "about 600" apples. If he actually has 550 apples, is he rounding correctly to the nearest hundred? If he has 649, is he rounding correctly?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- step 1: Check 550: Hundreds digit 5, neighbor 5. Rule says round up to 600. Correct.
- step 2: Check 649: Hundreds digit 6, neighbor 4. Rule says round down to 600. Correct.
- step 3: Conclude whether the shopkeeper is using the rounding rule correctly for both cases.
ANSWER: Yes, the shopkeeper is correct in both cases. Rounding 550 to the nearest hundred gives 600, and rounding 649 to the nearest hundred also gives 600.
