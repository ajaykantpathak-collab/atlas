## DEFINITION
Narrative and descriptive essays follow the show-don't-tell contract: scene before summary, senses before abstractions, specific detail before general claims. Structure is looser (opening scene → rising detail → closing image/insight) but the register is the skill — 'the market was crowded' tells; 'the vegetable seller's voice cut through the bargaining din' shows.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
create

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student converts tell-statements into show-sentences using sensory detail and specific verbs, and structures a narrative arc with an earned closing insight — no morals bolted on ('And thus I learned honesty is the best policy').

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers descriptive technique and narrative arc. Does NOT cover argumentative structure (93_001) or story-writing with a given outline.

## WALKTHROUGH
The transformation drill IS the lesson: 'The room was messy' → show it: 'Books lay spine-cracked on the bed; a week of biscuit packets fossilized on the desk.' Same fact, one earns marks. Then the ending rule: the insight must grow from the events — if 'honesty is the best policy' could end ANY essay, it ends none.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students summarize instead of dramatizing ('we had a lot of fun', 'the place was very beautiful'), and append stock morals that the events never earned — because they learned essays end with morals, not that insights must be earned.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: For each tell-statement, ask 'what did I SEE/HEAR that made me think that?' — write THAT. Step 2: Replace being-verbs (was/seemed) with doing-verbs. Step 3: End on the last image or the earned insight — never a proverb.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Show, don't tell: 'The market was very crowded and noisy.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Something like 'Shoulders brushed mine at every step; above the bargaining din a vendor's cry rose and fell.' A wrong answer adding intensifiers ('extremely crowded and very noisy') reveals the student thinks showing = stronger adjectives — it's actually swapping evaluation for evidence.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Which ending earns marks: 'And that's why honesty is the best policy' or 'Grandmother's torn note lay in my palm — the five rupees I'd almost kept, now heavier than anything I'd ever held'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The second — the insight is dramatized through an image the story built; the first is a bolted-on proverb that could close any essay. A wrong answer reveals the student believes conclusions exist to state morals rather than to land meaning.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Fix this tell: 'I was very nervous before the race.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Something like 'My spikes felt wrong on my feet; I retied them twice, then a third time.' A wrong answer 'I was extremely nervous' shows the student reaches for intensifiers — the tell-tale sign they haven't learned that nervousness lives in actions, not in the word.
