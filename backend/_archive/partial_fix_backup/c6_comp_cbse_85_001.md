## DEFINITION
A paragraph is one idea in a wrapper: a topic sentence that names the idea, three or four supporting sentences that feed it, and a closing sentence that ties it off. Every sentence must serve the one idea — anything off-topic is a weed in the paragraph.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student writes a 5-6 sentence paragraph with a clear topic sentence, on-topic supports, and a closing line; and can name which sentence in a given paragraph wanders off-topic.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers single-paragraph descriptive/factual writing. Does NOT cover essays, letters, or narrative structure.

## WALKTHROUGH
Build one aloud — topic 'My school library': topic sentence ('Our school library is my favourite place'), supports (the books, the silence, the librarian), closer ('It is where I travel without moving'). Then the weed test: 'My dog is cute' planted in the middle — rip it out.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students open with 'I am going to write about X' (announcing instead of starting), list facts with no thread, and end mid-air with no closing line.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Name the ONE idea in the topic sentence — don't announce it ('I will write about'), state it. Step 2: Every next sentence must add to that idea. Step 3: Close with a line that could only end THIS paragraph.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which is a real topic sentence: 'I am going to write about my school' or 'My school is the noisiest, friendliest building I know'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The second — it states an idea about the school; the first announces the writing instead of doing it. A wrong answer shows the student doesn't see announcing as a dodge.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Find the weed: 'My garden has many flowers. Roses are red and smell sweet. My uncle lives in Jaipur. The marigolds bloom in winter.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'My uncle lives in Jaipur' — off-topic. A wrong answer keeping it reveals the student grades paragraphs by sentence count, not by idea-unity.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Write the closing line for a paragraph about your pet's funny habits.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Any line that lands on the pet — e.g., 'With a tail that never stops wagging, she makes every day a little circus.' A wrong answer 'And that's all about my pet' shows the announcing-error at the closing end.
