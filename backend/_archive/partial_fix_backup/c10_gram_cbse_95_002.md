## DEFINITION
The three degrees must preserve exact meaning through fixed frames: positive 'as…as', comparative '…er/more…than', superlative '…est/most…in/of'. 'No other girl is as tall as Asha' ≡ 'Asha is taller than any other girl' ≡ 'Asha is the tallest girl' — one fact, three legal frames.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student transforms among the three degrees preserving truth conditions — including the 'other/else' word that keeps the compared entity inside or outside its group correctly.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the three frames and the any-other/no-other/very-few starters. Does NOT cover absolute comparatives or Latin comparatives (senior/junior to).

## WALKTHROUGH
Write the same fact three ways on the board and mark what each frame forces: superlative → 'the tallest IN the class' (entity inside group); comparative → 'taller THAN any OTHER girl' (entity must be excluded from the comparison set — that's why 'other' exists); positive → 'NO OTHER … as tall as'. The whole chapter is set membership wearing grammar.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students drop 'other' — 'Asha is taller than any girl' logically includes Asha herself, an impossibility — and mix frames ('more taller', 'most tallest' double-marking).

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Identify which degree the source uses. Step 2: Pick the target frame template. Step 3: Insert the set-control word (other/else/no other/very few) so the compared entity lands on the correct side of the group boundary. Step 4: Never double-mark — one 'more/-er' OR one 'most/-est', not both.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Transform to comparative: 'Asha is the tallest girl in the class.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'Asha is taller than any other girl in the class.' The 'other' is mandatory — it removes Asha from the comparison set. A wrong answer 'taller than any girl' creates the logical impossibility of Asha exceeding herself — the precise error the frame prevents.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Transform to positive: 'Mumbai is bigger than most cities in India.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'Very few cities in India are as big as Mumbai.' 'Most' maps to 'very few' at the top of the set. A wrong answer 'no city is as big' overstates — comparative 'most' leaves room for rivals; 'no other' claims absolute top — the student changed the fact, not just the form.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Correct: 'She is more taller than her sister.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'She is taller than her sister' — double comparative marking. A wrong answer accepting it reveals the student treats 'more' and '-er' as separate decoration slots rather than alternate realizations of ONE feature.
