## DEFINITION
Continuous tenses add 'be + V-ing' to show action in progress: present continuous (is reading), past continuous (was reading), future continuous (will be reading). The -ing is the film, not the snapshot.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student forms all three continuous tenses with correct be-forms, applies -ing spelling rules, and distinguishes continuous (in progress) from simple (habit/completed) in context.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the three continuous forms. Does NOT cover stative restrictions or perfect-continuous (class 9+).

## WALKTHROUGH
The film test: continuous = a movie playing, simple = a photograph. 'She was reading at 8' — film rolling at 8. 'She read the book' — snapshot, done. Be-form carries the time: is/was/will be. Spellings: run→running, write→writing, lie→lying.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students drop the be ('she reading'), use continuous for completed facts ('I was going to school yesterday' when the whole trip is over), and misspell -ing forms.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Is the action a film (in progress) or a snapshot (done/habit)? Step 2: Film → be (right time) + V-ing. Step 3: Never -ing alone — 'she reading' is broken English.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'At 8 pm yesterday, I ___ (do) my homework.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'was doing' — a specific past moment caught mid-action → past continuous. A wrong answer 'did' treats a film-moment as a snapshot.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Fix: 'She is read her book now.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'is reading' — continuous needs be + V-ing; 'is read' mixes forms. A wrong answer 'she reading' drops the be entirely — the helper is half the tense.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Which is a snapshot and which a film: 'He wrote a letter' / 'He was writing a letter'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
First = snapshot (completed), second = film (in progress). This single distinction drives every continuous-vs-simple choice in the class.
