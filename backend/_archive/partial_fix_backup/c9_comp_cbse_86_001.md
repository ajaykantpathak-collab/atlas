## DEFINITION
The formal letter skeleton carries dedicated format marks: sender's address → date → receiver's address → subject → salutation → three-paragraph body (purpose → details → request) → subscription → name. 'Dear Sir/Madam' pairs with 'Yours faithfully'; named recipients pair with 'Yours sincerely'.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student produces the 8-element skeleton in legal order for complaints/inquiries/letters-to-editor, pairs salutation↔subscription correctly, and audits defective letters for missing blocks.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the standard formal skeleton. Does NOT cover register/word-choice rules (86_002) or informal letters.

## WALKTHROUGH
Think like the receiver: who is writing (sender) → when (date) → who am I (receiver) → what about (subject, one line, noun-phrase) → greeting → body → sign-off matching the greeting. Every misplaced or missing block is a mark lost before content is judged.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students merge addresses, drop the subject line (the most-lost mark), and pair 'Dear Sir' with 'Yours sincerely' — the pairing is contract, not choice.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Write the 8-block checklist before drafting. Step 2: Subject = noun-phrase headline of the issue. Step 3: Match the pair — unknown name → Dear Sir/Madam + Yours faithfully; known name → Dear Mr X + Yours sincerely.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Order: salutation, subject, receiver's address, sender's address, date.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
sender's → date → receiver's → subject → salutation. A wrong answer putting subject first shows the student knows pieces, not positions — format marks are positional.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
A letter opens 'Dear Madam' and closes 'Yours sincerely' — what's wrong?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The pairing — 'Dear Madam' (name unknown) locks to 'Yours faithfully'. A wrong answer 'nothing' shows the pairing contract isn't learned.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Write the subject line for: inquiry about computer courses at an institute.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Something like 'Inquiry regarding computer courses offered' — noun-phrase, one line. A wrong answer writing a full sentence ('I want to know about…') makes the subject a sentence, not a headline.
