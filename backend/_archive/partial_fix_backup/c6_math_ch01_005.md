## DEFINITION
The Indian number system is a method of grouping and naming large numbers using specific units like Lakhs (1,00,000) and Crores (1,00,00,000), characterized by a unique placement of commas after the hundreds place. It groups digits from the right into sets of three (for hundreds) and then sets of two (for thousands, lakhs, and crores).

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever looked at a news headline about the population of India or the budget of a project in crores? To make these large numbers easy to read, we use the Indian system of grouping. Start by identifying the ones, tens, and hundreds places; place your first comma after these three digits. Then, continue moving to the left, but now group every two digits together with a comma. This creates "pockets" for thousands, lakhs, and crores, which helps your brain process the size of the number without getting lost in the zeros.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
In the Indian number system, where do you place the first comma when writing a number?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The first comma is placed after the third digit from the right (the hundreds place). A wrong answer here suggests the student is trying to force international grouping (every three digits) onto the Indian system.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why we write 1,00,000 instead of 100,000 in the Indian system.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
We write 1,00,000 to show the transition from the "thousands" period to the "lakhs" period. A wrong answer shows the student does not understand that commas serve as visual separators for different unit periods.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Write the number "fifty-five lakh twenty thousand four hundred" using the correct comma placement.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is 55,20,400. If the student writes 5,520,400, they are confusing the Indian system with the international system (grouping by threes).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare 10 lakh and 1 crore. Which one is larger, and how many times larger is it?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
1 crore is larger; it is 10 times larger than 10 lakh. If a student says 100 times, they are miscounting the zeros in the place value transition.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If someone writes a number as 10,00,00,000, can you tell if they are using the Indian system? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, because the commas are placed after the hundreds, thousands, and lakhs periods, which is unique to the Indian system. If the student focuses only on the zeros, they miss the purpose of the comma structure.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a 7-digit number and write it in words using the Indian system.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The student should produce a number like 45,67,890 and write "forty-five lakh sixty-seven thousand eight hundred ninety." A poor answer lacks the correct place-value names or fails to group digits into the correct lakhs/thousands periods.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to place commas every three digits (e.g., 10,000,000 instead of 1,00,00,000) because calculators and computers often default to the international format. This leads to confusion when reading numbers in Indian textbooks or newspapers.
## ON_FAILURE
Direct the student to write the number in a place value table with columns labeled: Crores, Ten Lakhs, Lakhs, Ten Thousands, Thousands, Hundreds, Tens, Ones. Have them fill the digits into the columns first, then draw the commas only at the boundaries of these columns. Remind them: "Indian commas are only at the boundary of the hundreds, then every two steps after that."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A local bridge construction project cost 8456700 rupees. Write this number with proper commas and in words using the Indian system.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the number: 8456700.
- Step 2: Place the first comma after the hundreds place: 8456,700.
- Step 3: Place the next comma after two more digits (thousands): 84,56,700.
- Step 4: Name the periods: 84 is in the Lakhs period, 56 is in the Thousands period, and 700 is in the Hundreds period.
- Step 5: Combine into words: Eighty-four lakh fifty-six thousand seven hundred.
REASONING: Grouping by two digits after the initial three ensures we align with the Indian place value names (Lakhs and Crores).
ANSWER: 84,56,700; Eighty-four lakh fifty-six thousand seven hundred.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: The government allocated 50000000 rupees for a new school. Write this in the Indian system.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Count the digits: 50,000,000 (8 digits total).
- Step 2: Place commas starting from the right: 5,00,00,000.
- Step 3: Identify the place of the '5': It is in the Ten-Crore place.
- Step 4: Write the number in words: Five crore.
REASONING: By correctly placing commas, we can identify that the number reaches the crore place, making it easier to name.
ANSWER: 5,00,00,000; Five crore.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A farmer has 1234567 kg of wheat. How many lakhs and thousands are in this number?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write the number: 1234567.
- Step 2: Apply Indian commas: 12,34,567.
- Step 3: Identify the Lakhs period: 12 (Twelve Lakhs).
- Step 4: Identify the Thousands period: 34 (Thirty-four thousand).
- Step 5: Identify the remainder: 567 (Five hundred sixty-seven).
- Step 6: Interpret: There are 12 lakhs and 34 thousand.
REASONING: The comma structure acts as a divider between the periods, allowing us to extract the specific value of lakhs and thousands directly.
ANSWER: 12,34,567; 12 Lakhs and 34 thousand.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you have the number 9,87,65,432, identify the digit in the 'Ten Lakh' place and explain why it is in that position.
COMPETENCY: Place value identification.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write the number clearly: 9, 87, 65, 432.
- Step 2: Label the periods: Crores (9), Lakhs (87), Thousands (65), Hundreds (432).
- Step 3: Within the Lakhs period (87), the '7' is the Lakhs place and the '8' is the Ten Lakhs place.
- Step 4: Conclude the position of the digit.
ANSWER: The digit in the Ten Lakhs place is 8. This is because, in the Indian system, the lakhs period contains two positions: the Lakhs place and the Ten Lakhs place.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why does the number 1,00,000 have two commas, while 10,000 has only one? Explain using the period structure.
COMPETENCY: Explaining the logic of grouping.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define the period for 10,000: This is in the Thousands period.
- Step 2: Define the period for 1,00,000: This enters the Lakhs period.
- Step 3: Explain the comma rule: Commas separate periods.
- Step 4: Conclude why the extra comma is needed.
ANSWER: 10,000 only occupies the Thousands period, requiring one comma to separate it from the Hundreds. 1,00,000 moves into the Lakhs period, so a second comma is required to separate the Lakhs period from the Thousands period.
DIFFICULTY: hard
