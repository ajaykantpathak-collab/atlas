## DEFINITION
In complex sentences the subordinate tense must harmonize with the main: past main → past-family subordinates ('He worked hard so that he might pass'); present/future main → free subordinates. Purpose clauses take may/might, not will/would-after-past confusions.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student applies tense harmony across object, purpose, result, and time clauses — including might-for-past-purpose and the truth-exemption.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers systematic tense harmony beyond reported speech. Does NOT cover conditional harmony (82_001) or free indirect style.

## WALKTHROUGH
The purpose-clause rule: 'He studies so that he may pass' (present main) vs 'He studied so that he might pass' (past main) — may→might follows the zone. Time clauses: 'I will call you when I arrive' — future meaning hides in present form; 'will' is banned after 'when' for future reference.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students put 'will' inside future time-clauses ('when I will arrive') — a direct Hindi-transfer — and keep 'may' in past purpose clauses.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Fix the main clause's zone. Step 2: Purpose — may (present zone) / might (past zone). Step 3: Time clauses for the future use present form — 'when I arrive', never 'when I will arrive'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'He worked day and night so that he ___ support his family.' may/might?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'might' — past main clause drags the purpose modal back. A wrong answer 'may' ignores the zone-harmony.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'I will phone you as soon as I ___ (reach) home.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'reach' — future time-clauses use present form; 'will reach' is banned after 'as soon as'. The Hindi-transfer error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'She said that she ___ come the next day.' will/would?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'would' — past reporting zone shifts will→would. A wrong answer 'will' keeps the speaker's modal instead of the reporter's.
