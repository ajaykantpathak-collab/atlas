## DEFINITION
Editing tasks at class 12 run a four-error inventory per passage: tense/form errors, concord errors, determiner errors, and word-form/preposition errors — typically one wrong word per line with the correction expected.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student locates one error per line in editing passages across the four categories and supplies the correction, naming the governing rule.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the editing-passage question type comprehensively. Does NOT cover sentence reordering or cloze.

## WALKTHROUGH
The audit checklist per line: (1) verb — right tense for its marker? (2) subject-verb agreement — head noun governs? (3) determiners — a/an/the and quantifiers legal? (4) prepositions and word-forms — 'discuss about'→'discuss', 'return back'→'return'. Most errors are the old friends in disguise.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students proofread for meaning instead of grammar — the passage reads fine, so they find nothing. Editing demands a category checklist, not a vibe check.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Read for sense once. Step 2: Re-audit each line against the four categories. Step 3: The common traps — 'didn't went', proximity agreement, 'less students', 'discuss about', 'comprise of'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Edit: 'One of my friend have gone to Delhi last week.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Two faults — 'friend' → 'friends' (one of + plural) and 'have' → 'has' (head noun 'one' is singular). A wrong answer fixing only one shows single-pass auditing.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Edit: 'He discussed about the problem with great details.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'discussed about' → 'discussed' (discuss takes no preposition) and 'great details' → 'great detail'. A wrong answer changing only 'about' leaves the detail-error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Edit: 'The news are really shocked everyone in the village.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'are really shocked' → 'really shocked' — 'news' is singular so 'are' is wrong AND 'shocked' needs no auxiliary here, or recast as 'was really shocking'. Either way the 'are' must go.
