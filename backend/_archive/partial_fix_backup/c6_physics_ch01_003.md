## DEFINITION
Science is a systematic way of observing, questioning, and experimenting to understand how the natural world and the universe function. It is organized into branches like Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and Earth Science, each focusing on specific patterns and phenomena in nature.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to categorize a given real-world phenomenon (e.g., a ball rolling, a plant growing) into its correct scientific branch and explain why it fits there.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the identification of major scientific branches (Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Earth Science, Astronomy) and their core focus. It excludes the history of scientific discoveries, social sciences, and complex sub-disciplines.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wondered why your bicycle tyre goes flat, or why a seed turns into a tall tree? Start by asking a question about a daily event, then observe what is happening—is it about movement, a change in material, or a living process? If it involves motion or energy, it falls under Physics; if it involves life and growth, it is Biology. By grouping these questions, you learn that science isn't just one giant subject, but a collection of organized "lenses" we use to view the world. The key insight is that while the branches are separate, they all work together to solve the "giant puzzle" of our universe.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the four main branches of science mentioned in the chapter, and what is the primary focus of Physics?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The four branches are Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and Earth Science. Physics focuses on matter, energy, and fundamental forces, such as motion and gravity. A wrong answer here suggests the student has not yet distinguished between the "what" (matter) and the "how" (forces) of nature.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you are studying why ice melts into water when heated, which branch of science would this primarily fall under and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
This falls under Chemistry, because it involves a change in the state of a substance. Students who say "Biology" are likely confusing "nature" with "living things," failing to distinguish between chemical properties and biological life functions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A scientist is tracking the path of a planet through the solar system. Which branch of science is this, and how does it relate to the branch that studies the Earth?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
This is Astronomy. It relates to Earth Science because both study large-scale systems (planets/stars vs. rocks/oceans). A wrong answer here shows the student does not see the connection between the "Earth system" and the "Universe system."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the study of a forest (Biology) with the study of the rocks beneath it (Earth Science). How are these two branches connected in a real-world environment?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
They are connected because the rocks provide the soil and nutrients that the living organisms in the forest need to survive. A student who says they are "completely unrelated" fails to understand that all branches of science overlap in the real world.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
"Science is just about memorizing the names of the branches." Is this statement true or false? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
False. Science is a process of inquiry, not just a list of names. Memorizing names does not help you understand how the world works; the actual "doing" (observing, guessing, testing) is what makes it science.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Think of a kitchen activity, like boiling milk. Can you describe how two different branches of science could explain what is happening?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer would explain: 1) Physics (the heat energy from the stove moving into the milk) and 2) Chemistry (the changing properties of milk proteins as they heat up). A poor answer only names one branch without explaining the "why."
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that "Science" is only one subject and that the branches are completely isolated, like separate boxes. They might believe that a tree is "only" Biology and ignore that it also follows laws of Physics (gravity/sunlight) or Chemistry (photosynthesis). This leads to an inability to see how a single object can be studied by multiple branches.
## ON_FAILURE
If the student struggles, ask them to pick one object in their room, like a wooden desk. Ask: "Is it alive?" (Biology), "Is it made of wood/atoms?" (Chemistry), "Does it have weight or can it be moved?" (Physics). By guiding them to ask these three questions about one object, they will see that branches are just different ways to look at the same thing.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You observe a magnet picking up iron pins. Identify the branch of science and the process.
STEPS:
- step 1: Observe the phenomenon (a magnet attracting metal).
- step 2: Identify the core concept (force and matter).
- step 3: Match to a branch (Physics studies forces and matter).
- step 4: Conclude it is Physics.
REASONING: Since magnetism is a force acting on matter, it fits the definition of Physics.
ANSWER: Physics.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: You observe a caterpillar turning into a butterfly. Identify the branch and why.
STEPS:
- step 1: Observe the phenomenon (growth and transformation of a living organism).
- step 2: Identify the core concept (life cycles and evolution).
- step 3: Match to a branch (Biology studies living things).
- step 4: Conclude it is Biology.
REASONING: Biology is specifically for living things; the transformation is a biological life function.
ANSWER: Biology.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A pot of water is left in the sun and dries up. Use Physics and Chemistry to explain this.
STEPS:
- step 1: Observe the water vanishing from the pot.
- step 2: Physics perspective: Heat energy from the sun causes water molecules to move faster (motion).
- step 3: Chemistry perspective: Liquid water is changing its state to water vapour (substance change).
- step 4: Combine the perspectives: Both are needed to explain the full event.
REASONING: Science often uses multiple branches to explain one event; Physics explains the energy, Chemistry explains the material change.
ANSWER: Physics (energy) and Chemistry (material state change).
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Imagine a car driving on a road. List two different scientific questions you could ask about this, and assign each to a branch of science.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to scientific branches.
STEPS:
- step 1: Pick the car and its surroundings.
- step 2: Formulate Q1 (How fast is it moving?).
- step 3: Formulate Q2 (What is the fuel turning into?).
- step 4: Assign to Physics and Chemistry.
ANSWER: Q1: How does the engine push the car? (Physics). Q2: How does the petrol burn to create energy? (Chemistry).
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why is it helpful for a scientist studying the ocean (Earth Science) to know about Biology?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying interdisciplinary connections.
STEPS:
- step 1: Define the role of Earth Science (studying oceans).
- step 2: Define the role of Biology (studying life).
- step 3: Find the connection (oceans contain fish, algae, and plankton).
- step 4: Conclude why they must work together.
ANSWER: An Earth Scientist needs to understand Biology because the ocean is full of living things that affect the water chemistry and the environment; you cannot study the ocean fully without knowing what lives inside it.
DIFFICULTY: hard
