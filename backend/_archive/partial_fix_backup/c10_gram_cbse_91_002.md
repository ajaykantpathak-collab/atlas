## DEFINITION
Indefinite pronouns have fixed agreement: each/every/everyone/someone/nobody are always singular; few/many/several are always plural; some/all/most agree with their of-phrase. 'Neither/either' are singular alone but follow the nearer subject in 'neither…nor' pairs.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student correctly assigns singular/plural agreement for all common indefinites, including each/every distributed subjects, neither-nor proximity, and some/all/most of-phrases.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the board-tested indefinites and correlative pairs. Does NOT cover collective nouns (91_003) or head-noun decoys (91_001).

## WALKTHROUGH
Three boxes on the board: ALWAYS SINGULAR (each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, either, neither), ALWAYS PLURAL (few, many, several, both), DEPENDS (some, all, most, none — look at 'of ___'). Then the correlative rule: in 'neither A nor B', the verb agrees with B — the nearer one: 'Neither the teacher nor the students were ready.'

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students think 'everyone/everybody' is plural because it means 'all people' — semantic plurality — and write 'everyone are'. They also forget the neither-nor proximity rule entirely.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Name the subject word. Step 2: If it's an indefinite, check which box it lives in. Step 3: For some/all/most, agree with the noun after 'of'. Step 4: For neither-nor, agree with the subject CLOSER to the verb.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'Each of the boys ___ (has/have) a book.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'has' — 'each' is always singular even though it distributes over many boys. A wrong answer 'have' reveals semantic plurality — the student pictures many boys and forgets 'each' grammatically singles them out one at a time.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'Neither the players nor the captain ___ (was/were) present.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'was' — verb agrees with 'captain', the nearer subject. A wrong answer 'were' shows the student summed both subjects as plural, missing the proximity rule that governs correlative pairs.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'Some of the milk ___ (has/have) spilt.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'has' — 'some' takes its number from 'milk' (uncountable → singular). A wrong answer 'have' reveals the student treats 'some' as inherently plural instead of looking through the of-phrase.
