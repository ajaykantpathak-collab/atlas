## DEFINITION
An informal letter to a friend or relative has a light skeleton: your address and date on top, a warm greeting ('Dear Priya'), a chatty body sharing news and asking questions, and a loving sign-off ('Yours lovingly'). It should sound like talking on paper.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student writes a complete informal letter with correct skeleton and warm register — contractions allowed, questions to the reader included — and can spot a letter that sounds like a formal report.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the friendly-letter format. Does NOT cover formal letters (class 9+).

## WALKTHROUGH
Read two openings aloud: 'I am writing to inform you that I secured first position' vs 'Didi, guess what — I came first!' Same news; the second sounds like a person. The skeleton: address → date → Dear ___ → body → Yours lovingly. Questions to the reader are welcome: 'How is your new school?'

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students either write too stiffly (formal phrases in a letter to a friend) or drop the skeleton entirely — no address, no date, no sign-off.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Skeleton — your address, date, Dear ___, body, loving sign-off. Step 2: Write like you talk — contractions, exclamations, questions all allowed. Step 3: Sign off by relationship — lovingly/affectionately for family and friends.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which opening sounds right for a letter to your cousin: 'I am writing to apprise you of my results' or 'You won't believe it — I passed with 90%!'?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The second — informal letters are marked on sounding human. A wrong answer shows the student treats 'letter' as one register — it's relationship-indexed.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
What is missing: letter with 'Dear Rahul', a body, 'Yours lovingly, Amit'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The address and date — the skeleton still applies even when the register is casual. A wrong answer 'nothing' shows the student thinks informal means structure-free.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Which sign-off fits a letter to your grandmother: Yours faithfully / Yours lovingly / Yours sincerely?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'Yours lovingly' — family takes the affectionate pair. A wrong answer 'faithfully' reveals one-size-fits-all sign-off habits.
