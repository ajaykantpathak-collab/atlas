## DEFINITION
The goals of psychological enquiry are the five systematic objectives that guide scientific research in psychology: **description** (accurately recording a behaviour or phenomenon to distinguish it from others), **prediction** (forecasting the occurrence of a behaviour under specific conditions based on observed relationships), **explanation** (identifying the causal factors or antecedent conditions that produce a behaviour), **control** (manipulating antecedent conditions to make a behaviour happen, reduce it, or enhance it), and **application** (using the generated knowledge to bring about positive changes in people's lives).

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you notice that some students in your class score very high on exams while others struggle, even though they study for similar hours. You wonder: *What exactly are the high scorers doing differently?* That curiosity is the starting point of psychological enquiry. First, you would **describe** their study habits in precise detail (e.g., "they use active recall every evening"). Next, you notice a pattern: students who use active recall tend to score above 90%. This allows you to **predict** that a new student adopting this habit will likely score high. But *why* does active recall work? You investigate and find it strengthens memory retrieval paths — that's **explanation**. Then, you design a study schedule incorporating active recall to **control** (enhance) their performance. Finally, you share this schedule with the school to **apply** the knowledge and help all students. Each goal builds on the previous one, transforming raw curiosity into scientific knowledge and practical solutions.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
A researcher records the exact frequency, duration, and intensity of aggressive acts displayed by children during free play in a preschool. She makes no attempt to explain why they occur or predict future aggression. Which goal of psychological enquiry is she primarily fulfilling?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
**Correct answer:** Description. The researcher is systematically recording the characteristics of a behaviour (aggression) to distinguish it from other behaviours and create a precise behavioural profile, without inferring causes or making forecasts.  
**What a wrong answer reveals:** If a student answers "explanation," they confuse *recording what happens* with *explaining why it happens*. If they answer "prediction," they mistake *documenting current behaviour* for *forecasting future behaviour*. This shows they cannot differentiate the foundational goal (description) from higher-order goals that require description as a prerequisite.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
"Prediction in psychology means the researcher can state with 100% certainty that a specific behaviour will occur under given conditions." — True or False? Justify your answer using the concept of 'margin of error' from the textbook.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
**Correct answer:** False. Prediction in psychology is probabilistic, not deterministic. The textbook states: "you can then forecast that under certain conditions this particular behaviour may occur **within a certain margin of error**." Human behaviour is influenced by multiple interacting variables, so predictions are expressed as likelihoods (e.g., positive correlation between study time and marks), not certainties.  
**What a wrong answer reveals:** A student answering "True" holds a deterministic misconception, likely borrowed from physical sciences (e.g., predicting a projectile's path). They fail to grasp that psychological prediction deals with complex, variable human behaviour where exceptions are the norm, and the "margin of error" is an inherent, acknowledged feature of the science.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A school counsellor observes that students who participate in the morning mindfulness program report lower stress levels during exams. She recommends the program to all students to reduce exam anxiety. Identify the TWO goals of psychological enquiry demonstrated here and explain the logical link between them.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
**Correct answer:** The two goals are **explanation** and **control**.  
**Steps:**  
1. The counsellor identifies a *causal relationship* (mindfulness participation → lower stress) — this is **explanation** (identifying the determinant/antecedent condition).  
2. She then *manipulates the antecedent condition* (implements the program for all students) to *enhance a desired behaviour* (reduce anxiety) — this is **control** (making a behaviour happen/reducing it).  
**Logical link:** Control is only possible *after* explanation establishes a cause-effect relationship; you cannot effectively manipulate a cause unless you know what the cause is.  
**What a wrong answer reveals:** If a student identifies "prediction and application," they miss that the counsellor is not just forecasting but actively *intervening* based on a known cause. If they say "description and application," they overlook the causal reasoning step. This shows inability to trace the logical sequence: explanation → control → application.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the goals of **explanation** and **control** in psychological enquiry. Using the textbook example of "study hours and achievement," explain why explanation is a necessary prerequisite for control, but control is not a necessary prerequisite for explanation.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
**Correct answer:**  
- **Explanation** identifies *why* a behaviour occurs (e.g., "increased study hours cause higher achievement because they allow deeper encoding and retrieval practice"). It establishes the cause-effect mechanism.  
- **Control** manipulates the identified cause to change the behaviour (e.g., *mandating* 3 hours of daily study to *enhance* achievement).  
**Why explanation precedes control:** You cannot deliberately manipulate a variable to produce a desired effect unless you *know* that variable is a cause. Control *requires* explanatory knowledge.  
**Why control does not precede explanation:** You can discover a cause-effect relationship (explanation) through observation or correlation *without* ever manipulating the variable. For example, psychologists explained that trauma causes PTSD long before they developed therapies (control) to treat it.  
**What a wrong answer reveals:** If a student claims they are independent or that control comes first, they confuse the *logical dependency* of intervention on causal knowledge. This reflects a failure to understand that scientific control is *evidence-based manipulation*, not arbitrary trial-and-error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A psychologist develops a new therapy for social anxiety based on the explanation that "avoidance behaviour maintains anxiety by preventing disconfirmation of feared outcomes." She tests the therapy on 50 patients and finds a 60% reduction in symptoms. She publishes the therapy manual for other clinicians to use. Evaluate whether this work fulfills the goal of **application**. Justify your evaluation with two specific criteria from the textbook definition of application.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
**Correct answer:** Yes, this fulfills the application goal.  
**Justification using textbook criteria:**  
1. **"Bring out positive changes in the lives of people"**: The therapy produced a measurable reduction in anxiety symptoms (60% improvement), directly enhancing patients' quality of life.  
2. **"Solve problems in various settings"**: The published manual allows the solution to be implemented beyond the original research setting (clinics, schools, community centers), addressing the problem broadly.  
**What a wrong answer reveals:** A student who says "No, because it wasn't tested on a large enough sample" confuses *application* with *experimental validation* (which belongs to the explanation/control phase). Application is about *utilisation of knowledge for benefit*, not the rigor of the prior test. This shows they cannot distinguish between the *generation* of knowledge (explanation/control) and its *deployment* (application).

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a mini research scenario (2-3 sentences) that clearly demonstrates **all five goals** of psychological enquiry in sequence, using the context of "smartphone usage and sleep quality in adolescents." Label each sentence with the goal it represents.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
**Expected answer (example):**  
1. **Description**: "The researcher records the average daily screen time, bedtime phone usage duration, and sleep onset latency for 200 adolescents using digital logs and sleep diaries."  
2. **Prediction**: "Based on the strong negative correlation found, the researcher forecasts that adolescents exceeding 3 hours of bedtime phone use will take >45 minutes to fall asleep."  
3. **Explanation**: "The researcher identifies blue light suppression of melatonin and cognitive arousal from social media as the causal mechanisms linking phone use to delayed sleep."  
4. **Control**: "An intervention group is asked to use 'night mode' and stop phone use 60 minutes before bed; their sleep onset latency decreases significantly compared to controls."  
5. **Application**: "The school adopts a 'digital sunset' policy and parents receive guidelines, leading to improved sleep hygiene across the district."  
**Criteria for a good answer:** Each goal is distinctly represented in correct logical order; the scenario uses the same variables throughout; the control step manipulates an antecedent identified in explanation; the application step scales the solution.  
**What a poor answer reveals:** If goals are out of order (e.g., control before explanation), the student doesn't grasp the hierarchical dependency. If variables change across goals (e.g., description studies phone use, but control manipulates caffeine), they fail to see enquiry as a coherent investigation of *one* phenomenon. If application is just "publishing a paper," they confuse *dissemination* with *real-world problem solving*.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
**Misconception:** "Description is just 'listing what you see' and is the least scientific goal because it doesn't involve numbers or statistics."  
**Why students make this error:** Students equate "scientific" with "quantitative" or "experimental." They see description as passive note-taking (like a diary entry) rather than *systematic, operationalised, and theory-guided measurement*. In Class 10 science, they mostly encountered experiments with numerical data, so they undervalue the rigorous categorisation, behavioural coding schemes, and inter-rater reliability that make psychological description scientific.  
**Concrete wrong answer example:** A student writes: "Description is when a psychologist watches children play and writes down what they do. It's not real science because there are no variables or hypotheses." This ignores that description *requires* selecting specific behaviours (selection), using coding systems (recording), and establishing reliability — all scientific processes.

## ON_FAILURE
**Step-by-step remediation:**  
1. **Show, don't tell:** Give the student two observation records of the same classroom: (A) "Kids were noisy and disruptive" and (B) "During 30-min observation: 12 call-outs (verbalisations without raising hand), 5 out-of-seat events, 3 physical contacts. Coded using Flanders Interaction Analysis Category 8 (student talk). Inter-rater reliability κ = 0.82."  
2. **Ask:** "Which record allows another researcher to replicate the observation? Which lets you compare this class with another? Which lets you test a hypothesis about teacher praise reducing call-outs?"  
3. **Explain:** "Description in psychology is *operationalisation* — turning a vague concept ('disruptive') into measurable, reliable, comparable data. That *is* science."  
4. **Practice:** Have the student operationalise "good study habits" into 5 observable, countable behaviours with a coding scheme. This transforms their intuition into the scientific skill of description.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A researcher wants to study "procrastination in Class 11 students." She defines procrastination as "delaying the start of a scheduled academic task by ≥30 minutes despite intending to begin." She then observes 50 students during a 2-week period, recording the frequency and duration of such delays using a time-stamped app. She finds that students who delay starting homework by ≥30 minutes on ≥4 days/week have a 0.72 correlation with lower term grades. She then conducts an experiment: Group A uses a "5-minute start rule" (commit to work for just 5 minutes), Group B receives no intervention. Group A shows 40% reduction in delay frequency. The school adopts the "5-minute start rule" in study halls.  
**Task:** Map each phase of this research to the five goals of psychological enquiry. Justify each mapping with the specific action taken.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the phase where the researcher *defines and records* the behaviour in measurable terms (frequency, duration, app-based time stamps). This is **Description** — operationalising a vague concept into quantifiable data.
- Step 2: Identify the phase where the researcher *uses the observed relationship* (correlation 0.72) to *forecast* that a new student with high delay frequency will likely have lower grades. This is **Prediction** — probabilistic forecasting within a margin of error.
- Step 3: Identify the phase where the researcher *identifies the cause* (e.g., "task aversion due to fear of failure causes delay") and *tests it* by manipulating an antecedent (the "5-minute start rule" reduces aversion). This is **Explanation** (causal mechanism) leading to **Control** (manipulating the cause to change the effect).
- Step 4: Identify the phase where the *school implements the validated intervention* broadly to *solve the problem* for all students. This is **Application** — using knowledge for positive real-world change.
- Step 5: Verify the logical sequence: Description → Prediction → Explanation → Control → Application. Each step uses the output of the previous step as its input.

REASONING: This example traces a complete research trajectory from defining a behaviour to solving a real problem. The key is recognising that each goal requires a specific *type of action*: measuring, forecasting, causal testing, manipulating, and deploying. The "5-minute start rule" is a manipulation of an antecedent condition (task initiation threshold) identified through explanation, making it control, not just a tip.

ANSWER: 
1. Defining/recording procrastination → Description  
2. Correlating delay frequency with grades → Prediction  
3. Identifying task aversion as cause & testing "5-minute rule" → Explanation & Control  
4. School adopting the rule → Application  

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A psychologist reads about a rare case: a 14-year-old who developed perfect pitch after a concussion. She conducts an in-depth study: interviews the adolescent, parents, teachers; reviews medical records; administers auditory perception tests; and analyses brain scans. She concludes that the concussion caused neuroplastic reorganization in the auditory cortex, leading to perfect pitch. She publishes the case, suggesting that controlled auditory training *might* induce similar plasticity in others.  
**Task:** Identify which goals of psychological enquiry are demonstrated in this case study and which are NOT. Explain why the missing goals cannot be achieved through this method.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the *detailed recording* of a unique phenomenon (perfect pitch post-concussion) using multiple sources (interviews, tests, scans). This is **Description** — rich, contextualised, multi-method description.
- Step 2: Identify the *causal inference* made: concussion → neuroplasticity → perfect pitch. This is **Explanation** — proposing a cause-effect mechanism based on converging evidence.
- Step 3: Check for **Prediction**: The study is on a *single case* (N=1). No statistical relationship is established to forecast behaviour in others. The suggestion that training "might" work is a hypothesis, not a prediction with a margin of error.
- Step 4: Check for **Control**: No manipulation of variables occurred. The concussion was not induced; the training suggestion is speculative, not tested. Control requires experimental manipulation.
- Step 5: Check for **Application**: The finding is not yet used to solve a problem or change practice. It only *suggests* a future application.

REASONING: Case studies excel at **Description** (depth, context) and can suggest **Explanation** (plausible mechanisms), but they *cannot* achieve **Prediction** (requires population-level data), **Control** (requires experimental manipulation), or **Application** (requires validated intervention). This distinction is crucial for method selection: case studies *generate* hypotheses for experiments, they don't *test* them.

ANSWER: 
Demonstrated: Description, Explanation (tentative)  
NOT demonstrated: Prediction, Control, Application  
Reason: Single-case design lacks generalisability for prediction, has no manipulation for control, and no validated intervention for application.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Combine the goals of enquiry with the *steps of scientific research* (conceptualising, data collection, drawing conclusions, revising conclusions). A researcher hypothesises: "Growth mindset intervention increases academic resilience in low-SES adolescents." She selects 100 students, randomly assigns them to intervention (growth mindset workshop) or control (study skills workshop), measures resilience pre/post using a validated scale, analyses data with t-tests, finds significant improvement in intervention group, and revises her theory to include "perceived control" as a mediator.  
**Task:** Create a 5-row table mapping each *step of scientific research* to the *goal(s) of enquiry* it primarily serves in this study. Justify each mapping in one sentence.

STEPS:
- Step 1: **Conceptualising a problem** → serves **Explanation** (formulating a causal hypothesis: mindset → resilience) and **Prediction** (hypothesis is a testable forecast).
- Step 2: **Collecting data** (random assignment, workshops, pre/post measurement) → serves **Description** (measuring resilience scores precisely) and **Control** (manipulating the independent variable: workshop type).
- Step 3: **Drawing conclusions** (t-test showing significant difference) → serves **Explanation** (confirming the causal link) and **Prediction** (validating the forecast for this population).
- Step 4: **Revising conclusions** (adding "perceived control" as mediator) → serves **Explanation** (deepening the causal model) and **Application** (identifying a new target for future interventions).
- Step 5: **Application** (not a formal step in the 4-step cycle but the *ultimate purpose*) → served by the revised theory guiding new interventions for low-SES students.

REASONING: The 4 steps of scientific research are the *process*; the 5 goals are the *purposes*. They are not 1:1 mapped. One step can serve multiple goals, and one goal (especially explanation) spans multiple steps. The hypothesis (conceptualising) embodies prediction/explanation. Data collection embodies description/control. Analysis embodies explanation/prediction. Revision embodies explanation/application. This integration shows how the *process* operationalises the *goals*.

ANSWER: 
| Step of Research | Primary Goal(s) Served | Justification |
|------------------|------------------------|---------------|
| Conceptualising | Explanation, Prediction | Hypothesis proposes a cause (explanation) and a testable forecast (prediction). |
| Data Collection | Description, Control | Measures resilience (description); manipulates workshop type (control). |
| Drawing Conclusions | Explanation, Prediction | Statistical test confirms cause-effect (explanation) and validates forecast (prediction). |
| Revising Conclusions | Explanation, Application | Adds mediator to causal model (explanation); identifies new intervention target (application). |
| (Ultimate Purpose) | Application | Revised theory guides real-world resilience programs for low-SES youth. |

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A newspaper headline reads: "Psychologists Discover That Eating Chocolate Causes Nobel Prizes!" The article describes a study showing a strong positive correlation (r = 0.79) between per capita chocolate consumption and Nobel laureates per 10 million people across 23 countries.  
(a) Which goal of psychological enquiry does this study *actually* demonstrate?  
(b) Which goal does the headline *falsely claim* it demonstrates?  
(c) Explain why correlation alone cannot support the headline's claim, using the concepts of "explanation" and "control."  
(d) Design a study that *could* legitimately test the causal claim. Specify the independent variable, dependent variable, and one control technique.

COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions / Comparing and justifying / Error identification and correction

STEPS:
- Step 1: Recognise that a correlation study only establishes a relationship (co-variation), not causation.
- Step 2: Map the study's actual achievement to "Prediction" (forecasting one variable from another).
- Step 3: Map the headline's claim ("causes") to "Explanation" (causal mechanism) and implied "Control" (manipulate chocolate to get Nobel).
- Step 4: Explain the third-variable problem (e.g., wealth → both chocolate access and research funding) and the lack of manipulation.
- Step 5: Design an experiment: IV = chocolate consumption (manipulated: high vs. low dose), DV = cognitive performance proxy (e.g., problem-solving tasks), Control = random assignment, double-blind, placebo (white chocolate).

ANSWER: 
(a) Prediction — the correlation allows forecasting Nobel rates from chocolate consumption (or vice versa) within a margin of error.  
(b) Explanation (and Control) — the headline asserts a causal mechanism (chocolate → Nobel) and implies manipulability.  
(c) Correlation only shows association. Explanation requires identifying the *causal pathway* (e.g., flavonoids → neurogenesis → cognition) and ruling out confounds (wealth, education). Control requires *manipulating* chocolate intake and observing the effect — impossible here because the "treatment" (national diet) cannot be randomly assigned.  
(d) Experiment: IV = Daily dark chocolate (70% cocoa) vs. placebo (white chocolate) for 6 months. DV = Composite cognitive score (working memory, attention, reasoning). Control technique = Random assignment + double-blind + matching on baseline IQ/SES.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A school implements a "peer mentoring" program to reduce bullying. The principal asks the psychology teacher to evaluate it. The teacher:  
(1) Records bullying incidents (type, frequency, location) for 3 months pre-program.  
(2) Finds that bullying peaks in unsupervised corridors during transitions.  
(3) Hypothesises: "Lack of adult presence in corridors during transitions causes bullying."  
(4) Implements the program: senior students mentor juniors in corridors during transitions.  
(5) Records incidents for 3 months post-program: 55% reduction.  
(6) Surveys students: 80% feel safer; mentors report leadership growth.  
(7) Presents findings to the district board; two other schools adopt the model.  
**Task:** For each numbered step (1-7), identify the *primary* goal of psychological enquiry it serves. Then, explain why Step 2 is critical for Step 4 to be considered "Control" rather than just "trial and error."

COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction / Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions

STEPS:
- Step 1: Map each action to a goal using the definitions (description, prediction, explanation, control, application).
- Step 2: Identify Step 2 as the *explanation* (identifying the antecedent condition: unsupervised corridors).
- Step 3: Identify Step 4 as *control* (manipulating that antecedent: adding mentors/supervision).
- Step 4: Articulate the logical necessity: Control = deliberate manipulation of a *known cause*. Without Step 2's explanatory insight, Step 4 would be an arbitrary guess.

ANSWER: 
1 → Description (systematic recording of behaviour)  
2 → Explanation (identifying causal antecedent: unsupervised transitions)  
3 → Prediction (hypothesis forecasts effect of manipulation)  
4 → Control (manipulating the antecedent identified in Step 2)  
5 → Prediction (post-data validates the forecast) & Explanation (confirms cause-effect)  
6 → Description (recording subjective experiences)  
7 → Application (scaling the solution to new settings)  

**Why Step 2 is critical for Control:** Control is "making a particular behaviour happen, reducing it, or enhancing it *by making changes in its antecedent conditions*." Step 2 *identified* the antecedent condition (lack of adult presence). Step 4 *changed* that specific condition (added peer mentors as surrogate supervision). Without Step 2, the program would be a random intervention (e.g., "let's try posters") — that is trial and error, not scientific control. Scientific control is *evidence-based manipulation*.
