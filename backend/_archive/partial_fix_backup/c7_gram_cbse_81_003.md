## DEFINITION
Past perfect is the earlier-past: 'When I reached the station, the train HAD LEFT' — two past events, and 'had' marks which came first. Without it, two past events read as simultaneous.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student orders two past events correctly — earlier event in past perfect (had + V3), later event in simple past — with when/after/before/by-the-time triggers.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers past perfect in two-event sequences. Does NOT cover future perfect or perfect-continuous.

## WALKTHROUGH
The timeline test: two past events — draw them. 'The patient died. The doctor came.' Which happened first? 'The patient HAD DIED before the doctor came' — 'had' is a timestamp saying 'this one is earlier'. Simple past tells the story; past perfect flashes back inside it.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students put 'had' on the wrong event (the later one) or use it for single past actions where no ordering exists ('I had gone to school' — alone, no second event, the 'had' is a mistake).

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Find the two past events. Step 2: Which is earlier? → had + V3. Step 3: Later event stays simple past. Step 4: One event alone never takes 'had'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'When we arrived, the film ___ (start).'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'had started' — the starting precedes the arriving. A wrong answer 'started' makes both simultaneous — 'when + arrived' forces ordering.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Fix: 'I had gone to the market and bought vegetables.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'I went to the market and bought vegetables' — both events form one past sequence told in order; no flashback needed. 'Had' alone with no earlier-vs-later contrast is an error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'The show ___ (end) before we got tickets.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'had ended' — 'before' explicitly orders the events; earlier event → past perfect. A wrong answer 'ended' erases the two-event structure.
