## DEFINITION
The verb agrees with the head of the subject, not with whatever noun stands nearest. 'The bunch of keys IS on the table' — 'bunch' is the head, 'keys' is a decoy inside an of-phrase. Prepositional phrases between subject and verb are grammatically invisible.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student locates the head noun through of-phrases and parenthetical inserts, chooses the matching verb form, and handles 'there is/are' inversion.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers head-noun agreement with decoys. Does NOT cover indefinites, collectives, or correlatives (class 9-10).

## WALKTHROUGH
Strip surgery: 'The colour of the walls ___ (is/are) fading.' Delete 'of the walls' → 'the colour IS fading'. The insert rule: 'as well as', 'along with', 'together with' do NOT make the subject plural — they are passengers in commas. 'There' sentences invert: the real subject follows the verb — 'There ARE three eggs left.'

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students agree with the nearest noun ('the walls are') and treat 'as well as' like 'and' — the two most common concord errors at this level.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Find the verb, ask 'who/what + verb'. Step 2: Cross out of-phrases and comma-inserts. Step 3: Agree with the surviving head. Step 4: In there-sentences, look past 'there' to the noun after the verb.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'The box of apples ___ (is/are) on the shelf.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'is' — head is 'box'. A wrong answer 'are' is proximity agreement — matching the verb to 'apples', the decoy standing closest.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'Ravi, along with his friends, ___ (was/were) invited.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'was' — 'along with' is a comma-insert; it does not pluralize. A wrong answer 'were' shows the and-equivalence error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'There ___ (is/are) a dog and two cats in the yard.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'are' — compound subject after 'there' takes plural (boards expect 'are'). A wrong answer 'is' applies proximity where the rule requires summing.
