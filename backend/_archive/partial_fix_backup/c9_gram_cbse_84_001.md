## DEFINITION
The board determiner set: a/an/the/zero by the specificity algorithm; a-vs-an by SOUND; the few/little attitude pair (few=almost none, a few=some); count-mass pairing (few/many vs little/much, fewer vs less); and fixed patterns (the+superlative, zero article for school-as-purpose).

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student runs the specificity algorithm, selects quantifiers by countability and attitude, and corrects the board-favourite errors (an university, less students, the honesty).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the class 9 determiner list. Does NOT cover advanced determiner stacking or partitives.

## WALKTHROUGH
The algorithm: (1) specific/shared → the; (2) new + singular countable → a/an by sound; (3) general/plural/uncountable → zero. The attitude pair: 'a little money' = enough to help; 'little money' = nearly broke. Countables take few/fewer/many; masses take little/less/much.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students apply a/an to spelling, use 'less' with countables, and treat few/a few as spelling variants rather than opposite attitudes.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Run the specificity algorithm for articles. Step 2: For quantifiers — count or mass first, then attitude (a- = positive). Step 3: Check the fixed traps — an-before-vowel-SOUND, fewer-for-countables.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'___ European union' — a or an, and why?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'a' — 'European' starts with a yoo- consonant SOUND. A wrong answer 'an' reveals letter-matching instead of sound-matching.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'He has ___ friends, so he feels lonely.' few or a few?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'few' — 'lonely' signals the negative reading (almost none). A wrong answer 'a few' misses that the 'a' flips scarcity to sufficiency — attitude, not style.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Fix: 'Less students attended the match today.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'Fewer students' — countables take fewer; 'less' is for mass nouns. A wrong answer accepting 'less' shows the count/mass partition isn't built.
