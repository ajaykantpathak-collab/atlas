## DEFINITION
A verb is the engine word — it names an action (run, eat, think) or a state (is, seem, have). Every complete sentence needs at least one, and its form changes with time: 'She walks', 'She walked', 'She will walk' — same engine, three gears.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
remember

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student finds the verb(s) in any sentence, distinguishes action verbs from being-verbs, and identifies which gear (present/past/future) the sentence is in.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers verb identification and the three simple tenses. Does NOT cover continuous/perfect aspects (class 7) or subject-verb agreement details (ch 83).

## WALKTHROUGH
Find the engine: 'The bird flew over the tall tree slowly.' Which word can you act out? 'flew'. Then being-verbs: 'The soup IS hot' — 'is' does no action but still drives the sentence. Gears: yesterday→walked, every day→walks, tomorrow→will walk.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students pick the action-WORD wrong — in 'Running is fun' they miss that 'is' is the verb and 'running' acts as a noun here. They also think 'will' alone is the future verb instead of 'will + base verb' together.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Ask which word carries the action or the being. Step 2: Check for helpers (is/was/will/has) — they ride with the main verb. Step 3: Match the gear: -ed/past form = past, will+base = future, plain = present.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Find the verb: 'Swimming in the morning keeps me healthy.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'keeps' — 'swimming' is the subject here (an activity as a thing), not the verb. A wrong answer 'swimming' reveals the student labels any -ing word a verb without checking what drives the sentence.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Which gear: 'She will visit her grandmother tomorrow.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Future — will + visit. A wrong answer 'present because visit looks plain' shows the student ignores the helper 'will' which IS the gear lever.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Verb or not: 'The tired boy slept soundly.' — is 'tired' a verb here?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
No — 'tired' describes the boy (adjective); 'slept' is the verb. A wrong answer shows the student labels any past-form word a verb without checking its job in the sentence.
