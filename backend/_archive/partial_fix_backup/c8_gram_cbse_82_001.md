## DEFINITION
Reported speech retells what someone said without quoting them: 'I am tired,' she said → 'She said (that) she was tired.' Three things move together — tense steps back, pronouns re-anchor to the reporter, and pointing words shift (now→then, today→that day).

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student converts statements direct→indirect with the full three-part shift (tense, pronouns, deictics), choosing said/told correctly ('told' needs an object).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers statement reporting. Does NOT cover questions and commands (82_002) or universal-truth exemptions in depth (class 10).

## WALKTHROUGH
The camera moves: direct speech is the speaker's camera; reported speech is the reporter's camera — everything re-frames. 'I am happy here' → 'He said he was happy there'. Watch the told/said distinction: 'told' demands a listener ('told me'), 'said' does not ('said that').

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students shift the verb but leave pronouns and pointing words anchored to the original speaker — 'He said he is tired today' — a half-done conversion boards mark wrong.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Pronouns re-anchor to the reporter's view. Step 2: Tense steps back (am→was, will→would, can→could). Step 3: Deictics shift (now→then, here→there, this→that, today→that day). Step 4: said + that / told + object + that.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Report: 'I will come tomorrow,' he said.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'He said (that) he would come the next day.' A wrong answer 'he will come tomorrow' shifts nothing — verb, pronoun, and time-word must all move.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
'She ___ me that she was leaving.' said or told?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'told' — 'told' requires the listener object ('told me'); 'said' cannot take 'me' directly. A wrong answer 'said me' is a classic Indian-English slip boards penalize.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Report: 'I have finished my work today,' Ravi said to his mother.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'Ravi told his mother (that) he had finished his work that day.' has→had, today→that day. A wrong answer keeping 'has finished' shows partial backshift.
