## DEFINITION
A lunar eclipse is a celestial event that occurs when the Earth passes directly between the Sun and the Moon, causing the Earth’s shadow to fall upon the Moon's surface. This blocks the sunlight that usually illuminates the Moon, causing it to darken.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to describe the relative positions of the Sun, Earth, and Moon during a lunar eclipse and explain why the Moon darkens.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever played with a flashlight and your hand to cast a shadow on a wall? Imagine the Sun is the flashlight, your hand is the Earth, and the wall is the Moon. When the Earth moves exactly into the path of light, it acts like an obstacle, stopping the Sun's rays from hitting the Moon. Because the Moon reflects sunlight to reach our eyes, if you block that light, the Moon disappears or darkens from our view. The key insight is that an eclipse is simply a shadow-casting event on a cosmic scale.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What three objects must be in a line for a lunar eclipse to occur?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The Sun, the Earth, and the Moon must be in a line. A wrong answer (like swapping the Earth and Moon) shows a failure to understand that the Earth acts as the "blocker" of sunlight.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why does the Moon become dark during a lunar eclipse?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The Moon darkens because the Earth blocks the sunlight that would normally hit it. A wrong answer suggests the Moon itself loses its light, which confuses stars (which produce their own light) with the Moon (which only reflects light).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If the Moon passes through the darkest part of the Earth's shadow, what do we call this specific region?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
It is called the umbra. An incorrect answer indicates the student has not mastered the terminology for shadows; the umbra is the part of the shadow where the light source is completely obstructed.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the visibility of a lunar eclipse to a solar eclipse; why is it easier to see a lunar eclipse?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
A lunar eclipse is visible from the entire night side of the Earth, whereas a solar eclipse is only visible from a small, specific path. A wrong answer suggests misunderstanding the scale of shadows cast by planets vs. the Moon.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If the Moon moved out of the Earth's shadow line, would the lunar eclipse continue? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, the eclipse would end because the Sun's rays would once again reach the Moon's surface. This tests the student's ability to judge the cause-and-effect relationship between alignment and light.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a drawing or a simple model description showing the arrangement of the Sun, Earth, and Moon during a total lunar eclipse.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A correct response shows the Sun on one end, the Earth in the middle, and the Moon inside the Earth's shadow (umbra). A poor answer shows the Moon in the middle, which would cause a solar eclipse instead.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse lunar and solar eclipses, thinking the Moon blocks the Sun in both. This happens because "eclipse" is a blanket term, and they don't visualize the order of the objects. For example, they might draw the Moon between the Earth and the Sun when asked to explain a lunar eclipse.
## ON_FAILURE
First, draw a straight line on a paper. Label the left side "Sun," the middle "Earth," and the right side "Moon." Explain that since the Sun provides the light, the object in the middle creates the shadow. Tell the student: "If the Earth is in the middle, it casts a shadow on the object behind it (the Moon)."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: If the Moon is 384,000 km away and enters the Earth's shadow, explain the order of celestial bodies.
STEPS:
- Identify the light source (Sun).
- Identify the obstruction (Earth).
- Identify the object being shadowed (Moon).
- Arrange them in order: Sun - Earth - Moon.
REASONING: The eclipse is caused by the obstruction of light. The obstruction must be between the source and the target.
ANSWER: Sun, then Earth, then Moon.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Why do we not see a lunar eclipse every month?
STEPS:
- Recall that the Moon orbits the Earth.
- Note the tilt of the orbit.
- Realize that the three bodies must be in a perfect straight line.
- Conclude that the alignment is rare due to the orbit tilt.
REASONING: If the orbits were perfectly flat, eclipses would happen monthly; the tilt ensures they only align occasionally.
ANSWER: Because the orbits are tilted, the Sun, Earth, and Moon rarely align in a perfect straight line.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student claims the Earth's shadow is smaller than the Moon. Is this correct? Use the concept of the umbra.
STEPS:
- Analyze the relative sizes of Earth and Moon.
- Identify the shape of the Earth's shadow (tapering).
- Consider the umbra region.
- Compare the shadow width to the Moon's size.
REASONING: The Earth is much larger than the Moon, so its umbra is wide enough to cover the entire Moon.
ANSWER: No, the Earth is significantly larger than the Moon, allowing its shadow to fully engulf the Moon during a total eclipse.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Imagine you are a sailor at night. You see the Moon slowly darkening. Which celestial body is blocking the light? Explain why.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to physical models.
STEPS:
- Identify the event as a lunar eclipse.
- Apply the Sun-Earth-Moon alignment rule.
- Determine the middle object.
ANSWER: The Earth is blocking the light. Because it is a lunar eclipse, the Earth must be positioned between the Sun and the Moon to cast its shadow on the Moon.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: If you were standing on the Moon during a lunar eclipse, what would you see if you looked toward the Earth?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying perspectives in space.
STEPS:
- Visualize the position of the Moon within the Earth's shadow.
- Determine what the Earth would look like from the Moon.
- Understand that the Earth would be blocking the Sun.
ANSWER: You would see the Earth as a dark circle surrounded by a ring of light (the Sun's light being bent around the edges). This is because the Earth is blocking the direct rays of the Sun from your position on the Moon.
DIFFICULTY: hard
