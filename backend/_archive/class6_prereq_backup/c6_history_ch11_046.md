## DEFINITION
The Non-Cooperation Movement was a nationwide peaceful protest launched by Mahatma Gandhi in 1920, urging Indians to stop supporting British institutions to weaken colonial rule. It was a strategy of "satyagraha," where citizens deliberately refused to participate in the British system to gain independence.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to list at least three specific actions of non-cooperation and explain why these actions were considered a challenge to British authority.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This covers the goals and methods of the 1920 movement. It excludes regional variations, the Khilafat movement, and the details of Gandhi’s life before 1915.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine your school authorities pass a rule you feel is unfair, but instead of fighting, you and all your friends simply decide to stop attending classes, using the library, or following their instructions. How would the school function without students? This is exactly what the Non-Cooperation Movement was about. Gandhi realized the British ruled India because Indians cooperated by working in their offices, attending their schools, and paying their taxes. By withdrawing this support, the movement aimed to make the British government unable to function, proving that power comes from the consent of the people.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
In what year did Mahatma Gandhi launch the Non-Cooperation Movement?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The movement was launched in 1920. A wrong answer here suggests the student has not yet grasped the timeline of the freedom struggle, requiring a review of the chapter’s historical chronology.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why did Gandhi ask Indians to resign from government jobs?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
He wanted to withdraw Indian support so the British government could not function. A student who says "to get better jobs" misses the point of non-cooperation as a tool of political pressure rather than personal gain.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If you were a student in 1920 supporting this movement, what would you do differently in your daily routine?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps: 1. Identify the action (boycotting school). 2. Explain the impact (empty classrooms). 3. Connect to the goal (weakening British influence). A wrong answer implies the student thinks the movement was about physical fighting rather than peaceful withdrawal.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How does "non-violent resistance" differ from a violent revolt?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Non-violent resistance uses refusal to participate, whereas a revolt uses force. Confusing these two shows a failure to understand the philosophy of "satyagraha" as a moral force.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Was the Non-Cooperation Movement meant to be a regional protest? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it was an all-India mass movement involving people across the country. Stating it was regional shows a lack of understanding of the movement's scale and reach.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a slogan that a person in 1920 might use to explain why they are not paying taxes to the British.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The slogan should focus on the idea that "our money should not support those who rule us." A poor answer focuses on greed or laziness rather than the political protest element.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think Non-Cooperation meant attacking British soldiers. This is incorrect because it confuses "non-cooperation" with "armed rebellion." The movement was specifically designed to be non-violent to show the moral strength of the Indian people.
## ON_FAILURE
First, clarify that "non-cooperation" means "refusing to help." Use the analogy: "If you stop helping someone who is being mean to you, they lose their power over you." Then, explain that Gandhi believed violence would only lead to more British force, whereas peaceful refusal would make the system collapse on its own.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A government clerk decides to join the Non-Cooperation Movement. What are the logical steps he takes?
STEPS:
- 1: Recognize he is part of the British administrative system.
- 2: Decide to stop working for the British to withdraw his labor.
- 3: Resign from his job in the government office.
- 4: Accept the personal sacrifice involved for the country's freedom.
REASONING: This demonstrates the "non-cooperation" logic where the individual stops being a cog in the colonial machine.
ANSWER: He resigns to deprive the British of his administrative support.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A village community decides to stop paying taxes. How does this challenge the British?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify that taxes fund the British administration and police.
- 2: Understand that withholding taxes leaves the government without money.
- 3: Stop paying the tax collector.
- 4: Accept that this is a peaceful way to protest without using weapons.
REASONING: Taxes are the "fuel" for the government; stopping them is a non-violent way to stop the "engine."
ANSWER: It weakens the British financial ability to maintain their rule.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Why would boycotting British schools be more effective than just complaining about them?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify that a system needs participants to survive (students/teachers).
- 2: Recognize that complaining does not stop the system from functioning.
- 3: Decide that leaving the system (boycott) directly reduces its authority.
- 4: Conclude that a mass boycott makes the institution irrelevant.
- 5: Acknowledge the goal is total non-participation.
REASONING: Complaining keeps you in the system; leaving it destroys the system's purpose.
ANSWER: Boycotting removes the participants, effectively closing down the institution.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If Gandhi had encouraged a violent uprising instead of non-cooperation, what might have been the result for the Indian people?
COMPETENCY: Critical Thinking
STEPS:
- 1: Analyze the power difference (British had weapons/army).
- 2: Predict the British response (harsh suppression).
- 3: Compare with non-violence (which is harder to suppress morally).
- 4: Conclude that violence would likely have caused more suffering.
ANSWER: It would likely have led to severe crackdowns and justified British military force, harming many more Indians.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: How does the concept of "truth-force" (satyagraha) make the Non-Cooperation Movement more powerful than a secret, violent plot?
COMPETENCY: Comparative Analysis
STEPS:
- 1: Define Satyagraha (moral power).
- 2: Define secret plot (fear-based, limited reach).
- 3: Compare transparency (Satyagraha is public).
- 4: Compare impact (Satyagraha invites mass participation).
- 5: Conclude why public, non-violent movements are stronger.
ANSWER: Satyagraha is public and moral, which makes it harder for the British to paint the protesters as "criminals," and it allows thousands of people to join openly, unlike a secret plot.
