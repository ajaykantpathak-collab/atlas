## DEFINITION
Speed is a measure of how fast an object changes its position, calculated by dividing the total distance travelled by the time taken to cover that distance. It tells us the rate at which motion occurs without considering the direction.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to calculate speed given distance and time, and identify the correct SI units for these measurements. They should demonstrate this by solving word problems involving uniform motion.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the definition and calculation of speed using the formula Speed = Distance / Time. It excludes velocity, acceleration, relative speed, and non-uniform motion calculations.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you and your friend are both running to the school gate. If you reach in 2 minutes and your friend reaches in 3 minutes, how do we describe who was "faster"? We look at the distance covered and divide it by the time it took. Start by identifying the distance (how far you went) and the time (how long the clock ticked while you moved). Once you have these two numbers, divide the distance by the time to get your speed. The key insight is that speed is simply a "rate"—it tells us how much distance is packed into every single unit of time (like every hour or every second).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the mathematical formula used to calculate the speed of a moving object?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The formula is Speed = Distance / Time. A student who answers "Distance times Time" or "Time divided by Distance" misunderstands that speed is a rate of change over time, not a total accumulation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a car travels 100 km in 2 hours, does it mean the car was moving at exactly 50 km/h the entire time?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, it means the *average* speed is 50 km/h. A student who says "Yes" assumes that motion is always uniform, failing to realize that the car might have sped up or slowed down during the journey.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A cyclist travels 40 km in 4 hours. What is the speed of the cyclist?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The speed is 40 / 4 = 10 km/h. A student who gets this wrong likely divided the time by the distance (4 / 40 = 0.1), showing a confusion between the numerator and denominator in the formula.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why is speed described as a positive number without direction?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Speed only measures "how fast," not "where to." A wrong answer suggests the student is confusing speed with velocity, which explicitly requires a specific direction (like "North" or "towards home").
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If one object moves at 10 m/s and another moves at 10 km/h, which one is faster?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The one moving at 10 m/s is faster because 10 m/s is 36 km/h. A wrong answer shows the student is comparing the numbers (10 vs 10) without considering the importance of units.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a scenario where knowing the speed of an object helps you predict when it will arrive at a specific destination.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A student should describe a trip, such as: "If a bus travels at 60 km/h and the town is 120 km away, I know it will take 2 hours to arrive." A poor answer lacks either the distance or the speed component needed to make the prediction.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the units of speed (km/h vs m/s) and treat them as interchangeable without conversion. They might think 50 m/s is the same as 50 km/h because the number is the same, ignoring the vast difference in the length of a metre versus a kilometre.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to define 1 km (1000 m) and 1 hour (3600 seconds). Then, have them write the formula as Speed = (Distance in metres) / (Time in seconds) for m/s. Show them that 1 km/h is actually 1000m / 3600s, which is a much smaller value than 1 m/s, to prove the units represent different scales of measurement.

## THIS_FORMULA
Speed = Distance / Time
## FORMULA_VARIABLES
Distance: The total space covered by an object. Time: The duration taken to cover the distance. Speed: The rate of motion.
## FORMULA_UNITS
m/s (metres per second), km/h (kilometres per hour).
## COMPUTATION_TYPE
arithmetic
## CROSS_SUBJECT

## PREREQ_CONCEPT
The concept of a standard unit of measurement.
## PREREQ_BEFORE
Be able to perform long division and understand basic units like metre and kilometre.
## PREREQ_STUCK

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A delivery boy rides his bicycle 15 kilometres to deliver a parcel to a shop in 3 hours. What is his speed?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: Distance = 15 km, Time = 3 hours.
- step 2: Identify the formula: Speed = Distance / Time.
- step 3: Substitute the values: Speed = 15 / 3.
- step 4: Calculate: 15 / 3 = 5.
- step 5: Apply the unit: 5 km/h.
REASONING: Since speed is the distance covered per unit of time, dividing the total kilometres by the total hours tells us how many kilometres are covered in a single hour.
ANSWER: 5 km/h
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An athlete runs a 200-metre race in 40 seconds. Calculate the athlete's speed in m/s.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: Distance = 200 m, Time = 40 s.
- step 2: Identify the formula: Speed = Distance / Time.
- step 3: Substitute the values: Speed = 200 / 40.
- step 4: Calculate: 200 / 40 = 5.
- step 5: Apply the unit: 5 m/s.
REASONING: By using the units provided (metres and seconds), the result naturally expresses the speed in metres per second, which is the standard SI unit.
ANSWER: 5 m/s
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A bus leaves a station and travels 240 km. If the bus maintains a constant speed of 60 km/h, how long does the trip take?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: Distance = 240 km, Speed = 60 km/h.
- step 2: Rearrange the formula: Time = Distance / Speed.
- step 3: Substitute the values: Time = 240 / 60.
- step 4: Calculate: 240 / 60 = 4.
- step 5: Verify the unit: Since distance is in km and speed is in km/h, the resulting time is in hours.
REASONING: Using algebraic manipulation, we can find the time by dividing the total distance by the rate of travel (speed).
ANSWER: 4 hours
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You measure the time it takes for a toy car to travel across a 2-metre floor. If you start the stopwatch late, will your calculated speed be higher or lower than the actual speed? Explain.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the formula Speed = Distance / Time.
- step 2: Identify the effect of a "late start" on the time value (the recorded time will be smaller than the actual time).
- step 3: Determine how a smaller denominator affects the result of a division.
- step 4: Conclude that the calculated speed will be higher than the true value.
ANSWER: The calculated speed will be higher. Because the recorded time is smaller than the actual time taken, dividing by a smaller number results in a larger speed value.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two cars start at the same time from Delhi. Car A reaches Jaipur (240 km away) in 4 hours. Car B reaches Jaipur in 5 hours. Which car was faster, and by how much?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate the speed of Car A (240 / 4 = 60 km/h).
- step 2: Calculate the speed of Car B (240 / 5 = 48 km/h).
- step 3: Compare the two results.
- step 4: Calculate the difference (60 - 48 = 12 km/h).
ANSWER: Car A was faster. Car A travelled at 60 km/h, while Car B travelled at 48 km/h. Therefore, Car A was faster by 12 km/h.
DIFFICULTY: hard
