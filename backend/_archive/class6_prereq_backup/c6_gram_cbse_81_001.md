## DEFINITION
Nouns sort into families by what they name: common (boy, city), proper (Ravi, Delhi — always capitalised), collective (team, flock — one word for a group), abstract (honesty, fear — things you cannot touch). The family decides the grammar that follows.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
remember

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student classifies any noun into its family, explains the test for abstract nouns (cannot see/touch/weigh it), and capitalises proper nouns inside sentences.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the four families plus countable/uncountable awareness (water, rice — no plural). Does NOT cover cases (possessives are a separate lesson) or gender.

## WALKTHROUGH
Touch test on the board: can you touch it, see it, weigh it? 'Table' yes → concrete. 'Honesty' no → abstract. Then the group test: 'a ___ of players' → team = collective — one word, many members. Proper nouns get capitals because they name ONE specific thing.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students think abstract = 'difficult word' — so they call 'mountain' abstract because it is big, and 'idea' concrete because they use it daily. The test is physical: can your senses detect it?

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Is it a name of one specific thing? → proper (capitalise). Step 2: Does it name a group as one word? → collective. Step 3: Can senses detect it? → no = abstract, yes = common concrete.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Classify: 'kindness', 'flock', 'Mumbai', 'chair'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
kindness=abstract (cannot touch), flock=collective (one word for a group), Mumbai=proper (specific name, capital), chair=common. A wrong answer calling 'flock' common shows the student missed that one word names the whole group.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Is 'strength' abstract or concrete? Defend in one line.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Abstract — you cannot touch or weigh strength itself, only its effects. A wrong answer 'concrete because wrestlers have it' reveals the student tests the OWNER not the word.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Why is 'rice' usually singular? 'Rices are on the table' — right or wrong?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Right answer: wrong — rice is uncountable (a mass, not items); we say 'grains of rice'. A wrong answer accepting 'rices' shows the student pluralizes by habit without checking countability.
