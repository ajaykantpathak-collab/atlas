## DEFINITION
Present tense has two gears students must separate: simple present for habits and facts ('She reads daily', 'The sun rises in the east') versus present continuous for actions happening NOW ('She is reading'). Mixing them is the most common class-6 tense error.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student chooses simple present vs present continuous by asking 'habit/fact or happening-now?', uses is/am/are + V-ing correctly, and handles the spelling rules (run→running, write→writing).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the two present forms and -ing spelling rules (double consonant, drop silent e). Does NOT cover present perfect or stative restrictions (class 10).

## WALKTHROUGH
Two pictures: a girl who reads every day vs a girl reading right now. 'She reads daily' — habit, simple. 'She is reading' — now, continuous. Signal words sort them: daily/always/usually → simple; now/at this moment/look! → continuous. Spelling: run doubles the n (running), write drops the e (writing).

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students use continuous for everything now-adjacent ('I am going to school daily') and forget the helpers — writing 'She reading' with no is/am/are.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Ask habit-or-now. Step 2: Habit/fact → simple (add -s for he/she/it). Step 3: Now → is/am/are + V-ing, never -ing alone. Step 4: Spell the -ing: double final consonant after short vowel, drop silent e.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'Look! The baby ___(cry).' Which form and why?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'is crying' — 'look!' signals happening-now → continuous. A wrong answer 'cries' reveals the student ignores signal words; 'is cry'/'crying' alone shows the missing-helper error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Fix: 'I am going to school every day.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'I go to school every day' — 'every day' is a habit → simple present. A wrong answer keeping 'am going' shows the student defaults to continuous whenever the action feels current.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Spell the -ing: 'swim', 'dance', 'sit'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
swimming (double m after short vowel), dancing (drop silent e), sitting (double t). Wrong answers 'swiming/danceing' show the two spelling rules aren't yet automatic.
