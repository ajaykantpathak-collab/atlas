## DEFINITION
A ratio is a way to compare two quantities of the same kind by showing how many times one quantity contains the other, usually written as a fraction or with a colon. It tells us the relative size of two or more values.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to write a ratio given two quantities and simplify that ratio to its lowest terms by dividing by a common factor.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the definition of ratios, representation (colon, fraction, words), and simplification. It excludes rates, proportions, and continued ratios.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are making lemonade and you need to mix 2 cups of sugar for every 6 cups of water. To start, ask yourself: how many cups of sugar are there for every cup of water? We write this comparison as 2:6. Just like simplifying fractions, we can divide both numbers by 2 to get 1:3, which means for every 1 cup of sugar, you must use 3 cups of water. The key insight is that a ratio is just a relationship between two numbers, and it behaves exactly like a fraction that can be simplified.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the three ways to write the ratio of 5 apples to 8 oranges?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The three ways are 5:8, 5/8, and '5 to 8'. A wrong answer suggests the student does not recognize these as equivalent representations, which is a foundational recall task.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
The ratio must follow the order stated; since red is mentioned first, it must be 10:15. A wrong answer suggests the student does not understand that order matters in ratios, unlike simple addition.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
**Correct answer:**
The ratio must follow the order specified in the question, which asks for red to blue; therefore, the number of red balls (10) must come before the number of blue balls (15), making the correct ratio 10:15. Stating it as 15:10 incorrectly assigns the blue quantity to the red category.

**A wrong answer reveals:**
A wrong answer reveals that the student does not understand that a ratio is an **ordered pair**, meaning they have failed to grasp that the sequence of numbers must correspond directly to the sequence of items mentioned in the problem statement.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Simplify the ratio 12:18 to its lowest terms.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Divide both by 6 to get 2:3. If a student gets 6:9, they failed to find the greatest common divisor and did not fully simplify the ratio.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
You have 2 meters of ribbon and 50 centimeters of lace. Can you write a ratio of 2:50? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
No, you cannot. You must convert 2 meters to 200 centimeters first, making the ratio 200:50 or 4:1. A wrong answer shows the student ignores the rule that units must be the same.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is the ratio 2:4 the same as the ratio 4:8? Explain your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, both simplify to 1:2. A wrong answer reveals the student doesn't see that ratios represent a constant relationship, even if the numbers look different.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-world scenario where a ratio of 3:2 describes the situation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An example is: "In a class of 5 students, 3 are boys and 2 are girls." A poor answer uses unrelated units (like 3 apples and 2 meters) or fails to provide a context that fits the ratio.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often forget to convert units before writing a ratio, or they treat a ratio like a set of independent numbers. For example, when comparing 1 kg of rice to 500 grams of dal, a student might write 1:500 instead of 1000:500 (or 2:1).
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to identify the units of both items. If they are different, explain that you cannot compare "apples to oranges" or "meters to centimeters" directly. Have them convert the larger unit into the smaller unit first, then write the ratio and simplify it by finding common factors.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A gardener has 4 meters of fencing and 120 centimeters of wire. What is the ratio of fencing to wire?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify units (meters and centimeters).
- 2: Convert to the same unit (4 meters = 400 cm).
- 3: Write the ratio (400 : 120).
- 4: Simplify by dividing by the common factor 40 (400/40 : 120/40).
- 5: Final ratio is 10:3.
REASONING: We convert units so we are comparing the same "size," then reduce the ratio to its simplest form to see the relationship clearly.
ANSWER: 10:3
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In a classroom, there are 24 girls and 16 boys. Find the ratio of boys to girls in simplest form.
STEPS:
- 1: Identify order requested (boys first).
- 2: Write values (16 boys : 24 girls).
- 3: Find the greatest common factor of 16 and 24, which is 8.
- 4: Divide both by 8 (16/8 : 24/8).
- 5: Final ratio is 2:3.
REASONING: Order is crucial; by identifying boys as the numerator/first term, we ensure the ratio accurately reflects the question.
ANSWER: 2:3
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A juice box contains 500ml of water and 200ml of concentrate. A larger pitcher has 1.5 liters of water and 600ml of concentrate. Are the ratios of water to concentrate the same?
STEPS:
- 1: Convert 1.5 liters to 1500ml.
- 2: Write ratio 1 (500:200).
- 3: Simplify ratio 1 (divide by 100) to 5:2.
- 4: Write ratio 2 (1500:600).
- 5: Simplify ratio 2 (divide by 300) to 5:2.
- 6: Compare the two results.
REASONING: By converting all volumes to milliliters, we can compare the relationships directly and see they are equal.
ANSWER: Yes, both ratios are 5:2.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you have a ratio of 2:3, can you tell me if this ratio represents the exact number of items in a bag? Why or why not?
COMPETENCY: Conceptual understanding of ratios vs. absolute quantities.
STEPS:
- 1: Recognize that a ratio represents a relationship or a "part-to-part" comparison.
- 2: Recall that 2:3 could come from 2 and 3, or 20 and 30, or 4 and 6.
- 3: Conclude that it represents a proportion, not specific counts.
ANSWER: No, it represents a relationship. The bag could have 2 and 3 items, or any multiple (like 20 and 30).
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare the ratios 3:5 and 4:7. Which one is "larger" (represents a higher proportion of the first value to the second)?
COMPETENCY: Comparing ratios using fraction conversion.
STEPS:
- 1: Write both as fractions: 3/5 and 4/7.
- 2: Find a common denominator (35).
- 3: Convert fractions: 21/35 and 20/35.
- 4: Compare numerators: 21 > 20.
ANSWER: 3:5 is larger because 21/35 > 20/35.
DIFFICULTY: hard
