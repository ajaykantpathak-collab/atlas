## DEFINITION
Plant growth is the biological process where a seed absorbs water, splits its coat, and develops into a mature plant by pushing roots downward and a shoot upward to reach light. It involves the transformation of stored energy in the seed into the physical structures of a new plant.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to describe the sequence of events from a dormant seed to a germinating seedling and identify the essential requirements for this growth.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the physical process of germination and early growth (root/shoot emergence). It does not include photosynthesis (the process of making food), plant classification (taxonomy), or farming techniques.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wondered how a tiny, dry chickpea in your kitchen cupboard can turn into a leafy green plant if left in a wet cloth? It begins when a seed "wakes up" because it senses moisture, warmth, and air. First, the seed absorbs water and swells up like a sponge, causing its hard outer shell to crack open. Then, the tiny root pushes down into the soil to find water, while a shoot grows upward toward the light. The seed provides the "packed lunch" (stored food) that keeps the plant alive until its leaves are ready to make their own food.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the three main things a seed needs to begin the process of growing into a plant?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A seed needs moisture, warmth, and air. Answering only "water" reveals the student ignores the importance of temperature and oxygen, which are critical for the seed to "breathe" and activate its growth enzymes.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you plant a seed upside down, in which direction will the root grow? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The root will still grow downward. This shows the student understands that plants have a natural "direction" for growth (geotropism), regardless of how the seed was initially placed. A wrong answer suggests they think the root just follows the seed's orientation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A seed is planted in a dark, dry, and cold room. Why will it fail to grow?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
It fails because it lacks the "trigger" conditions: moisture (to swell the seed) and warmth (to activate growth). If a student says "because it is dark," they are missing the point that germination happens underground in the dark; the absence of light stops the *later* stage of leaf development, but water and warmth are needed for the initial sprout.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the job of the root and the shoot during the early days of a seedling's life.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The root acts as an anchor and a water-seeker, while the shoot acts as a light-seeker. If a student confuses these, they have not internalized the basic structural function of plant survival.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is a seed "alive" while it is sitting in a bag of grains? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, it is "dormant" or alive but inactive. It contains a miniature plant and stored food, waiting for the right conditions to start the metabolic process of growth. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks growth is the only sign of life.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design an experiment to prove that a seed needs water to grow. What would you do?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Place two sets of seeds in identical containers with soil and warmth. Provide water to the first set and none to the second. If the student fails to keep the other conditions (warmth/light) the same, they do not understand the concept of a "controlled experiment."
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that plants "eat" the soil or that the soil is the source of the seed's growth energy. In reality, the seed relies on its own stored food (cotyledons) for the initial growth phase before it has leaves to make food.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student: "If the seed is in the soil, how does it get food before it has leaves?" Guide them to dissect a soaked seed like a kidney bean to see the two halves (cotyledons) which act as a food store. Explain that the soil only provides support and water, not the energy for the very first sprout.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student plants 20 gram-seeds in a pot. After 5 days, only 15 seeds have sprouted. What percentage of the seeds germinated, and what does this tell us?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify total seeds (20) and sprouted seeds (15).
- step 2: Use the percentage formula: (Part/Whole) * 100.
- step 3: (15 / 20) = 0.75.
- step 4: 0.75 * 100 = 75%.
- step 5: Interpretation: 75% germinated; 25% failed due to lack of water, bad seed quality, or temperature.
REASONING: Calculating the success rate helps quantify the process of germination. It shows that environmental factors affect different seeds even in the same pot.
ANSWER: 75% of seeds germinated.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: If a plant's shoot grows 2 cm in 4 days, what is the average growth per day?
STEPS:
- step 1: Total growth = 2 cm. Total time = 4 days.
- step 2: Average growth = Total Growth / Total Time.
- step 3: 2 cm / 4 days = 0.5 cm/day.
- step 4: The result represents the daily rate of growth.
REASONING: Growth is a rate-based process. Dividing the distance by time helps us track the plant's development speed.
ANSWER: 0.5 cm per day.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A pea seed weighs 0.5 grams. After soaking in water for 24 hours, it weighs 0.8 grams. How much water did it absorb, and why?
STEPS:
- step 1: Initial mass = 0.5g. Final mass = 0.8g.
- step 2: Water absorbed = Final mass - Initial mass.
- step 3: 0.8g - 0.5g = 0.3g.
- step 4: This shows the seed is actively absorbing water to trigger growth enzymes.
REASONING: The increase in mass confirms the physical intake of water (swelling), which is the first step in germination.
ANSWER: 0.3 grams of water.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have two bean seeds. You put one in a dry bowl and one in a damp paper towel. After 3 days, only the damp one has a sprout. Why?
COMPETENCY: Explaining the necessity of environmental conditions.
STEPS:
- step 1: Compare variables (dry vs. damp).
- step 2: Recall that water triggers metabolic activity in seeds.
- step 3: Conclude that the dry seed remained dormant because it lacked the "unlocking" factor: water.
ANSWER: The damp towel provided the moisture needed to swell the seed and activate the embryo, whereas the dry environment kept the seed in a dormant state.
DIFFICULTY: easy
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare the "energy source" of a baby plant inside a seed to a baby human.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying biological concepts.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the food source of the seed (cotyledons).
- step 2: Identify the food source of a human baby (milk/external).
- step 3: Contrast "internal storage" vs "external supply."
ANSWER: A seed has an internal food store (cotyledons) attached to it, allowing it to start growing without needing to find food immediately. A human baby relies on an external food source provided by the parent.
DIFFICULTY: medium
