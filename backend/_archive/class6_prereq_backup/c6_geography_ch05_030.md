## DEFINITION
India’s maritime neighbours are sovereign island nations located in the Indian Ocean that share a sea border with India. Sri Lanka and the Maldives are our primary maritime neighbours, connected to us through deep historical, cultural, and trade ties across the sea.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify Sri Lanka and the Maldives on a map and explain at least two ways (cultural, historical, or economic) in which they are connected to India.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the geographical location and general relationship of India with Sri Lanka and the Maldives. It does not cover maritime laws, naval strategies, or specific fishing disputes.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are standing on the southern tip of India looking out at the vast Indian Ocean; have you ever wondered what lies beyond the horizon? To begin, look at a map and locate the teardrop-shaped island just below Tamil Nadu—that is Sri Lanka. Then, look further southwest to find the small cluster of islands called the Maldives. Think of these countries not just as dots on a map, but as neighbors who share our ocean, our history, and our trade routes. By observing their position relative to the Indian peninsula, you will realize that the ocean acts as a bridge for connection rather than just a barrier.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Name the two island nations that are considered India’s maritime neighbours.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The two nations are Sri Lanka and the Maldives. A student who misses this suggests they have not yet located the Indian Ocean region on a map.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
How is the geographical location of Sri Lanka different from the Maldives in relation to India?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Sri Lanka lies directly to the south of India, separated by a narrow stretch of water called the Palk Strait, while the Maldives is located further to the southwest. A wrong answer suggests a failure to distinguish between directions and distances on the map.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If a tourist travels from India to Sri Lanka, what is one major connection they might see between the two countries?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
They might see shared cultural or religious connections, such as the influence of Buddhism or the presence of Tamil-speaking communities. A wrong answer shows a lack of understanding that "connections" go beyond just geography to include history and people.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why do we call Sri Lanka and the Maldives "maritime" neighbours instead of "land" neighbours?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
They are called maritime neighbours because they are separated from India by the sea rather than a land boundary. A wrong answer suggests the student is confusing "maritime" (sea-based) with "terrestrial" (land-based) boundaries.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it accurate to say that the Indian Ocean acts only as a barrier between India and its maritime neighbours? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it is a bridge. It facilitates trade, tourism, and cultural exchange, which have historically linked these nations. A wrong answer reveals a failure to interpret the ocean as a space for interaction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
If you were to create a travel brochure for India's maritime neighbours, what "shared history" point would you highlight?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer would mention the spread of Buddhism from India to Sri Lanka or the shared stories from the Ramayana. A poor answer is generic and fails to cite specific historical/cultural links mentioned in geography.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think "neighbours" must touch the land border of India. They struggle to understand that countries separated by water are still neighbors. They might mistakenly assume only countries like Pakistan or Nepal are "neighbours" because they are visible on a land map.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to visualize their own house. If a neighbor lives across a small river or a road, are they still a neighbor? Explain that "maritime neighbour" simply means a neighbor you reach by crossing the sea, just like a neighbor across a street. Point to the Palk Strait on the map and explain it as a "sea-street" connecting the two lands.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student wants to visit India's closest maritime neighbor. Which one is it, and how does the map show this?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify all maritime neighbors (Sri Lanka, Maldives).
- step 2: Compare distances on the map from the southern tip of India.
- step 3: Observe that Sri Lanka is separated by a very thin line (Palk Strait) compared to the distance to the Maldives.
- step 4: Conclude that Sri Lanka is the closest.
REASONING: Spatial analysis allows us to measure proximity by identifying the narrowest body of water between two landmasses.
ANSWER: Sri Lanka is the closest, as the Palk Strait is a much shorter gap than the distance to the Maldives.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Identify two ways in which India and Sri Lanka interact.
STEPS:
- step 1: Research historical/cultural links (Buddhism, Ramayana).
- step 2: Research current economic links (Trade, Tourism).
- step 3: Select one of each category.
- step 4: Draft a summary of these links.
REASONING: Categorizing interactions helps differentiate between ancient ties and modern economic relationships.
ANSWER: They interact through cultural ties like the spread of Buddhism and economic ties through tourism and trade.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Why is the Indian Ocean important for the relationship between India, Sri Lanka, and the Maldives?
STEPS:
- step 1: Define the role of the ocean as a transport route.
- step 2: Identify the ocean as the source of shared resources (marine life).
- step 3: Explain how the ocean makes trade easier compared to land transport.
- step 4: Conclude that the ocean is a shared space for economic growth.
- step 5: Verify the logic by checking if coastal nations depend on the sea.
REASONING: Understanding the ocean's function explains why these countries are not isolated but connected.
ANSWER: The ocean serves as a highway for trade, a source of food, and a cultural connector, making the three nations economically and socially interdependent.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If the sea level rose and the Palk Strait became wider, would Sri Lanka still be a maritime neighbour? Why?
COMPETENCY: Critical Thinking
STEPS:
- step 1: Define 'maritime neighbour' as a nation separated by sea.
- step 2: Analyze if the change in distance affects the definition.
- step 3: Conclude that distance does not change the status of being a neighbor.
- step 4: Formulate the final explanation.
ANSWER: Yes, because 'maritime' refers to the nature of the border (sea), not the distance. As long as there is sea between them, they remain maritime neighbours.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare the nature of India’s land border with Bangladesh to its maritime border with the Maldives. What is the primary difference in how people move across these borders?
COMPETENCY: Comparative Analysis
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify that the border with Bangladesh is a land boundary.
- step 2: Identify the border with Maldives as a maritime (water) boundary.
- step 3: Compare movement: land borders allow walking/road transport; maritime borders require ships or planes.
- step 4: Analyze how this impacts trade and travel.
- step 5: Summarize the difference in accessibility.
ANSWER: The land border allows for road and rail transport, whereas the maritime border strictly requires sea or air travel, which changes the type of infrastructure needed for trade and human movement.
DIFFICULTY: hard
