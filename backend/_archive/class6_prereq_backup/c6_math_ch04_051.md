## DEFINITION
Problem solving with decimals involves using operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division on numbers with decimal points to resolve real-world quantities such as currency, length, or weight. It requires carefully aligning decimal points and managing place values to ensure the final result maintains accurate magnitude.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at a local market with 500 rupees, and you want to buy 3.5 kg of apples priced at 85.50 rupees per kg. How do you know if you have enough money? To begin, you must identify the operation: "per kg" tells you to multiply the quantity by the unit price. Align your decimal multiplication carefully, count the total decimal places in the numbers being multiplied, and place your decimal point in the answer accordingly. Finally, compare your total to your budget. The key insight is that decimal problems are just regular arithmetic where the "dots" act as a guide for place value—never ignore them or you change the value of your money.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the most important rule to follow when adding or subtracting two decimal numbers like 12.5 and 3.45?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The decimal points must be aligned vertically in the same column. A wrong answer (like adding 12.5 to 3.45 to get 4.70) reveals that the student is ignoring place value and treating decimal digits like whole numbers, failing to recognize that 5 tenths plus 4 tenths is different from 5 hundredths.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you multiply 2.5 by 0.2, why is the answer 0.5 and not 5.0?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The answer is 0.5 because there is one decimal place in 2.5 and one in 0.2, totaling two decimal places in the result; 25 x 2 = 50, and moving the decimal two places left gives 0.5. A wrong answer (like 5.0) shows the student does not understand how decimal multiplication affects the magnitude of the product.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A ribbon is 12.4 meters long. If you cut it into 4 equal pieces, how long is each piece? Show your steps.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
12.4 ÷ 4 = 3.1 meters. Step 1: Divide 12 by 4 to get 3. Step 2: Bring down the 4 after the decimal point. Step 3: Divide 4 by 4 to get 1. Step 4: Place the decimal point after 3. A wrong answer (like 31 or 0.31) shows the student has difficulty maintaining the decimal position during division.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare 5.50 rupees and 5.5 rupees. Are they the same value? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Yes, they are equal because adding a trailing zero after the decimal point does not change the value of the number. A wrong answer (saying 5.50 is larger) shows a misunderstanding of decimal place value where the student thinks more digits always mean a larger number.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A shopkeeper sells 10 kg of sugar at 42.50 rupees/kg. If the cost price was 400 rupees total, is the profit calculation (10 x 42.50) - 400 correct?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, the calculation is correct because (10 x 42.50) gives the total selling price (425 rupees), and subtracting the cost price (400) gives the profit (25 rupees). An incorrect judgment shows the student cannot sequence operations correctly in a multi-step word problem.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a simple word problem that requires the division of a decimal by a whole number.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good problem would be: "If 5 liters of milk cost 275.50 rupees, how much does 1 liter cost?" A poor answer (like asking to add decimals instead) reveals the student does not recognize the "unit rate" structure of division problems.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often "align digits to the right" instead of aligning the decimal points. For example, when adding 1.25 and 0.5, they write 0.5 under 25, resulting in 1.75, which is correct, but if they add 1.25 and 0.05, they might align the 5s and get 1.30 instead of 1.30. This error happens because students treat decimals like integers without respecting the positional value of tenths, hundredths, and thousandths.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student struggles with alignment, have them use a grid paper. Instruct them to place the decimal point in its own dedicated box. Tell them to "fill the empty spaces with zeros" so every number has the same number of decimal places (e.g., change 0.5 to 0.50), which makes the addition or subtraction visually intuitive and prevents digit-shifting errors.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student buys 3 notebooks at 25.50 rupees each and 2 pens at 12.25 rupees each. How much money did the student spend in total?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate the cost of 3 notebooks: 3 x 25.50 = 76.50 rupees.
- Step 2: Calculate the cost of 2 pens: 2 x 12.25 = 24.50 rupees.
- Step 3: Add the two subtotals: 76.50 + 24.50.
- Step 4: Align decimals and add: 76.50 + 24.50 = 101.00.
REASONING: Multiplying the unit cost by quantity gives the category cost, and addition combines these into a grand total. Aligning the decimal point ensures the "tenths" are added to "tenths" and "rupees" to "rupees".
ANSWER: 101.00 rupees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A water tank holds 150.5 liters. If 5 buckets are filled from the tank, each holding 4.5 liters, how much water remains in the tank?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Find total water removed: 5 x 4.5 = 22.5 liters.
- Step 2: Set up the subtraction for remaining water: 150.5 - 22.5.
- Step 3: Align decimal points: 150.5 minus 22.5.
- Step 4: Subtract: 150.5 - 22.5 = 128.0.
REASONING: We calculate total usage first, then subtract from the starting amount. Keeping the decimal points in a straight line ensures the subtraction is performed by place value.
ANSWER: 128.0 liters
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A piece of cloth 10.5 meters long is divided into 3 equal pieces. If each piece is then sold for 50.25 rupees per meter, what is the total value of the 3 pieces?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Find the length of one piece: 10.5 ÷ 3 = 3.5 meters.
- Step 2: Calculate the value of one piece: 3.5 meters x 50.25 rupees/meter.
- Step 3: Perform multiplication: 3.5 x 50.25 = 175.875.
- Step 4: Round to the nearest paisa (two decimal places): 175.88 rupees.
- Step 5: Multiply by 3 to find total value: 175.875 x 3 = 527.625.
REASONING: We must determine individual piece length, then value per piece, then total value. The decimal multiplication requires careful counting of decimal places (three places total).
ANSWER: 527.63 rupees
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have 100 rupees. You need to buy 4 packets of biscuits costing 15.75 rupees each. Do you have enough money? Explain your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- Step 1: Multiply 4 by 15.75.
- Step 2: 4 x 15 = 60, 4 x 0.75 = 3.00. Total = 63.00.
- Step 3: Compare 63.00 with 100.
- Step 4: Since 63.00 < 100, the answer is yes.
ANSWER: Yes, you have enough money. The total cost is 63 rupees, which is less than 100 rupees, leaving you with 37 rupees change.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student calculated 12.5 x 0.4 and got 50.0. Is this correct? If not, what is the correct answer and what mistake did the student likely make?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- Step 1: Multiply 125 by 4 to get 500.
- Step 2: Count total decimal places: 12.5 (one) + 0.4 (one) = two decimal places.
- Step 3: Place decimal point in 500 two places from the right: 5.00.
- Step 4: Compare 5.00 with 50.0.
ANSWER: The answer is incorrect. The student ignored the decimal places and treated the numbers as whole numbers (125 x 4), then placed the decimal point incorrectly. The correct answer is 5.0 or 5.
DIFFICULTY: hard
