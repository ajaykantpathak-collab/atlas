## DEFINITION
The balance model visualizes an equation as a two-pan balance scale where both sides must maintain equal weight. An algebraic equation remains balanced as long as any mathematical operation—addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division—is performed equally on both sides.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to describe why performing the same operation on both sides preserves equality and demonstrate this by solving a simple linear equation like 2x + 3 = 7 by isolating the variable.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the conceptual balance model of equations and basic isolation of a single variable. It excludes complex algebraic structures, formal logical proofs, and multi-step multi-variable equations.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at the market with an old-fashioned balance scale. On the left pan, you have two bags of pulses (each weighing $x$) and a 3kg weight. On the right pan, you have a 7kg weight. Because the scale is level, we know $2x + 3 = 7$. If you want to find the weight of one bag ($x$), you must "clear" the scale. First, take a 3kg weight off both sides to keep it level ($2x = 4$). Then, since you have two bags, split the weight on the right side into two equal groups; this is equivalent to dividing both sides by 2, leaving you with $x = 2$. The key insight is that the "equals" sign is a pivot point: whatever you do to the left, you MUST do to the right to keep the balance.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
In an equation like $x + 5 = 10$, if I add 2 to the left side, what must I do to the right side to keep the equation true?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
You must also add 2 to the right side. A wrong answer suggests the student views the equation as a sum to be calculated rather than a balanced relationship that must be maintained.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do we subtract 3 from both sides of the equation $2x + 3 = 7$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
We subtract 3 to "isolate" the term containing $x$ by removing the constant. Answering "because the teacher said so" or "to get the answer" shows the student lacks an understanding of the goal of isolation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Solve $3x = 12$ using the balance model.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Divide both sides by 3 to get $x = 4$. If a student just writes "4", they have missed the balance model step (division) and may struggle when the numbers become more complex.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If $2x = 10$, what happens to the balance if you divide the left side by 2 but leave the right side as 10?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The scale tips to the right because the left side becomes smaller ($x$) than the right side (10). This confirms the student understands that operations change the weight and must be applied symmetrically.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Can you solve $x - 4 = 6$ by adding 4 to both sides? Explain why this works.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, adding 4 to both sides cancels the "-4" on the left, leaving $x = 10$. This works because adding the same amount to equal weights keeps the pans level.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a balance-scale problem where the unknown $x$ is doubled, then 5 is added, and the total is 15. Write it as an equation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The equation is $2x + 5 = 15$. A poor answer would be $x + 5 = 15$ or $2(x+5)=15$, revealing a failure to translate "doubled" and "added" correctly into algebraic terms.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to "move" a number to the other side by changing its sign without performing the balance operation (e.g., changing $2x + 3 = 7$ to $2x = 7 + 3$ instead of $2x = 7 - 3$). They view the equals sign as a "gate" to jump over rather than a balance point, which leads to sign errors.
## ON_FAILURE
If the student makes a sign error, have them draw two pans on paper. Ask them: "If you remove 3kg from the left, how much must you remove from the right to keep the scale level?" Physically write "-3" under both sides of the equation. This forces them to see the balance action rather than just "moving" numbers.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper puts two identical bags of sugar (each $x$ kg) and a 2kg weight on one side of a scale. The other side has a 10kg weight. Find the weight of one bag.
STEPS:
- Identify: $2x + 2 = 10$.
- Method: Remove 2kg from both sides to clear the constant.
- Application: $2x + 2 - 2 = 10 - 2 \rightarrow 2x = 8$.
- Compute: Divide both sides by 2 to isolate $x$.
- Result: $x = 4$.
REASONING: We remove the constant 2kg first because it is not attached to the variable. Then, we divide by the coefficient 2 because we need to know the weight of one bag, not two.
ANSWER: 4 kg
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A bucket contains $x$ litres of water. If you triple the amount and then add 5 litres, you get 20 litres. Find $x$.
STEPS:
- Identify: $3x + 5 = 20$.
- Method: Subtract 5 from both sides.
- Application: $3x + 5 - 5 = 20 - 5 \rightarrow 3x = 15$.
- Compute: Divide both sides by 3.
- Result: $x = 5$.
REASONING: By subtracting 5 first, we isolate the term $3x$. Then, by dividing by 3, we undo the "tripling" effect to find the value of a single $x$.
ANSWER: 5 litres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A rope of length $x$ is folded into 4 equal pieces. If you add a 2 metre extension to these pieces, the total length is 6 metres. Find $x$.
STEPS:
- Identify: $\frac{x}{4} + 2 = 6$.
- Method: Subtract 2 from both sides.
- Application: $\frac{x}{4} = 4$.
- Method: Multiply both sides by 4 to isolate $x$.
- Result: $x = 16$.
REASONING: This combines the balance model with fractions. We clear the addition first, then use multiplication as the inverse of division to isolate $x$.
ANSWER: 16 metres
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Your friend says that in the equation $x - 7 = 3$, the answer is $x = -4$ because they "moved" the 7 and subtracted it from 3. How would you use the balance model to show them the correct answer?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- Explain that the scale is currently $x - 7 = 3$.
- Show that adding 7 to both sides is the only way to "remove" the -7 from the left.
- $x - 7 + 7 = 3 + 7$.
- Conclude that $x = 10$.
ANSWER: The friend is wrong because they treated the equation as a simple calculation rather than a balance. Adding 7 to both sides correctly isolates $x$.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: You have $3x + 10 = 25$. If you multiply the entire left side by 2 but do not change the right side, what happens to the balance? What would you need to do to the right side to fix it?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Determine that the left side weight has doubled.
- Observe that the scale is now unbalanced.
- Realize that the right side must also be doubled to restore equilibrium.
- Multiply the right side by 2 ($25 \times 2 = 50$).
ANSWER: The balance tips to the left because the left side is now heavier. To fix it, you must multiply the right side by 2 to keep the equation equal.
DIFFICULTY: hard
