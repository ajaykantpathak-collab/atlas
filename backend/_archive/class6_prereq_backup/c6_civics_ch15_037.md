## DEFINITION
Media ethics are the set of moral rules that guide journalists to report the truth accurately, fairly, and independently. It acts as a code of conduct to ensure the media serves the public interest in a democracy rather than personal or political agendas.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify whether a news report follows ethical standards (accuracy, objectivity, fairness, independence, and privacy) by analyzing a provided news snippet.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the core principles of media ethics (accuracy, objectivity, fairness, independence, privacy) within the context of democracy. It excludes legal discussions, defamation cases, and the specific functions of press councils.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever seen two people tell the same story differently? Imagine a fight happens in the school playground; if you only ask one student what happened, you might get a biased story. To get the truth, you must ask both sides, check if they are telling facts or just rumors, and make sure you aren't favoring a friend. Journalism works the same way. Start by asking, "Is this reporter telling me just the facts, or are they trying to make me feel a certain way?" Then, check if they mentioned the other side of the story. The key insight is that media isn't just about sharing news—it’s about sharing *truthful* news that respects everyone involved.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What does "accuracy" mean in the context of media ethics?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Accuracy means reporting facts correctly and verifying information with reliable sources before publishing it. A wrong answer suggests the student confuses "accuracy" with "speed" or "popularity," missing the core requirement of truth-telling.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is "independence" an important ethical principle for the media?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Independence ensures that news is not influenced by advertisers, politicians, or powerful people who might want to hide the truth. If a student thinks independence means "the media can say anything," they misunderstand that it actually means "the media cannot be bought or controlled."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If a newspaper only interviews the winning team of a match and ignores the losing team, which ethical principle have they violated?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
They have violated "fairness" and "objectivity." To fix this, they should include the perspectives of both teams. A wrong answer suggests the student doesn't understand that ethical journalism requires presenting multiple sides of a story.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why is it ethically wrong for a reporter to share the private home address of a crime victim?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
It violates the principle of "privacy," which mandates that the media must protect the dignity and safety of individuals, especially victims. If the answer focuses on "law," the student is straying outside the scope of ethics into legal territory.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Should a newspaper accept money from a political party to write only good things about them? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, because it violates "independence." Taking money creates a conflict of interest, making the reporting biased rather than objective. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks journalism is a business first rather than a public service.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a checklist a reporter should use before posting a news story on social media.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The checklist should include: 1) Are these facts verified? 2) Have I heard from both sides? 3) Am I being pressured by anyone? 4) Am I respecting the privacy of the people in the story? Poor answers will lack these specific ethical pillars.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe "media" is the same as "opinion." They assume that because a person is on TV or writing a blog, everything they say is a "fact." They fail to distinguish between objective reporting (facts) and subjective commentary (personal views).
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to find two different news reports on the same event from two different sources. Ask them to underline "facts" in one color and "opinions" in another. Point out how the factual reporting stays the same, but opinions change, helping them see the difference between reporting ethics and personal bias.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A journalist is writing about a new school policy. They only talk to the Principal, not the students. Is this ethical?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the missing principle (Fairness).
- step 2: Define fairness (Giving all parties a chance to respond).
- step 3: Apply to scenario (The students are a "party" affected by the policy).
- step 4: Analyze (By only talking to the Principal, the story is one-sided).
REASONING: Following the definition of fairness requires including the voice of all parties, especially those directly affected by a decision.
ANSWER: No, it is not ethical because the journalist failed the principle of fairness by excluding the students' perspective.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A TV channel reports a robbery. They show the victim's face clearly and mention where they live. Analyze the ethics.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the principle involved (Privacy).
- step 2: Explain why privacy matters (Protecting dignity/safety of victims).
- step 3: Evaluate the action (Showing the face and address is harmful).
- step 4: Conclusion (It is an ethical violation).
REASONING: Ethical journalism must balance the public's right to know with the individual's right to safety and privacy.
ANSWER: This is unethical because it violates the principle of privacy, putting the victim at further risk.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A reporter receives a tip that a local politician is involved in a corruption scandal. They rush to print the story without checking if the documents are real.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the principle (Accuracy/Fact-checking).
- step 2: Explain the requirement (Verify information with sources before publishing).
- step 3: Analyze the behavior (The reporter acted out of speed, not truth).
- step 4: Identify the consequence (Risk of spreading false information).
- step 5: Determine the ethical path (Verify documents first).
REASONING: Accuracy is the foundation of trust; rushing a story compromises the truth and the public's perception.
ANSWER: This is unethical because the reporter failed to verify the accuracy of the sources, which is a fundamental requirement of responsible journalism.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a news channel shows a protest but only interviews the people who support the protest and ignores the people who are against it, which specific ethical principle is being ignored?
COMPETENCY: Application of Ethical Principles
STEPS:
- step 1: Recall the principle of Objectivity.
- step 2: Contrast this with the provided scenario.
- step 3: Identify the bias in the scenario.
- step 4: Conclude that the omission of opposing views violates objectivity.
ANSWER: The principle of objectivity is being ignored because the news channel is not presenting multiple sides of the story.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why might a media outlet choose to be "independent" even if it means losing money from a powerful company that wants to control their stories?
COMPETENCY: Evaluation of Value-based Decision Making
STEPS:
- step 1: Define Independence (freedom from influence).
- step 2: Connect independence to the purpose of journalism (public interest).
- step 3: Analyze the tension between profit and ethics.
- step 4: Argue why ethics takes priority (trust and democracy).
- step 5: Conclude that losing money is a small price for maintaining public trust.
ANSWER: The media outlet chooses independence to maintain their credibility. If they are controlled by an advertiser, they lose the trust of the public, which destroys their role in a democracy.
