## DEFINITION
Multiplication of integers is the process of finding the product of two whole numbers that includes their signs, where the final sign depends on whether the signs of the numbers are the same or different. If the signs are the same, the result is positive; if the signs are different, the result is negative.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to multiply two integers by first calculating the product of their absolute values and then assigning the correct sign based on whether the signs are the same or different.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the multiplication of integers including zero and negative numbers. It excludes algebraic expressions involving variables like $x$ or $y$ and excludes formal proofs of associative or commutative properties.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at the "Building of Fun" and the lift button presses represent integers. If you press the "up" button (positive) 3 times for 5 floors each, you go up 15 floors ($+3 \times +5 = +15$). What happens if you press the "down" button (negative) 3 times for 5 floors each? You are moving down, so your position changes by $-15$ floors ($+3 \times -5 = -15$). The key insight is to multiply the numbers normally first (the "size" of the movement) and then use the sign rule: "Same signs make a positive result; different signs make a negative result."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the rule for the sign of the product when multiplying a positive integer by a negative integer?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The product is always negative. A student who says "positive" is confusing multiplication with the rule for adding two negative numbers or simply forgetting the sign rules.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Without calculating, is the product of $(-7) \times (-4)$ positive or negative, and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It is positive because both integers have the same (negative) sign. A student who says "negative" thinks that because the inputs are negative, the output must be negative, failing to apply the "same sign" rule.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate: $(-6) \times 8$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Multiply absolute values $6 \times 8 = 48$. Step 2: Since signs are different (negative and positive), the result is $-48$. A student answering $48$ has forgotten the negative sign, while a student answering $-14$ has likely added instead of multiplied.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If the product of two integers is positive, what can you conclude about the signs of the two integers?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Either both are positive or both are negative. An answer stating "both are positive" is incomplete because it ignores the possibility that two negatives also produce a positive.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Compare the results of $(4 \times -5)$ and $(-4 \times 5)$. Are they the same? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, both equal $-20$. This shows that the order of integers in multiplication does not change the result, and different signs always yield a negative product. A student saying "no" may be struggling with the sign rules.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a multiplication problem involving a negative integer that results in $-24$. Explain your choice.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Examples: $(-3) \times 8$ or $6 \times (-4)$. The criteria for a good answer is that one factor must be negative and the other positive, and their absolute values must multiply to 24. A poor answer would use two positive numbers or two negative numbers.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often mistakenly apply the addition rule for signs to multiplication. For example, they might think $(-3) \times (-4) = -7$ because they are "combining" the negative values, or they assume the result must be negative because the inputs are negative.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student adds instead of multiplies, ask them to write the problem as repeated addition: $(-3) \times 4$ means adding $(-3)$ four times: $(-3) + (-3) + (-3) + (-3) = -12$. Then, show them that $(-3) \times (-4)$ represents the opposite of adding $(-3)$ four times, which leads to $+12$. Use the "same signs = positive" rule as a final memory hook.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A submarine is at sea level (0 metres). It dives down at a speed of 5 metres per minute. What will be its depth after 8 minutes?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify "diving down" as a negative movement ($-5$ m/min) and time as positive ($8$ min).
- Step 2: Set up the expression: $(-5) \times 8$.
- Step 3: Multiply the absolute values: $5 \times 8 = 40$.
- Step 4: Apply the sign rule: different signs (negative and positive) result in a negative.
- Step 5: The result is $-40$ metres.
REASONING: Since the submarine moves in the negative direction (below sea level) for a positive amount of time, the total displacement must be negative.
ANSWER: $-40$ metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A business loses 200 rupees every day due to a faulty machine. What is the total change in the balance after 6 days?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the daily loss as $-200$ rupees and duration as $6$ days.
- Step 2: Set up the expression: $(-200) \times 6$.
- Step 3: Multiply absolute values: $200 \times 6 = 1200$.
- Step 4: Apply sign rule: negative $\times$ positive = negative.
- Step 5: The result is $-1200$ rupees.
REASONING: Losing money is represented as a negative change, and repeating this over several days is multiplication by a positive factor.
ANSWER: $-1200$ rupees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A water tank loses 10 litres of water every hour due to a leak. What was the change in volume 4 hours ago?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the leak as $-10$ litres/hour.
- Step 2: Identify "4 hours ago" as time moving in the negative direction, so $-4$ hours.
- Step 3: Set up the expression: $(-10) \times (-4)$.
- Step 4: Multiply absolute values: $10 \times 4 = 40$.
- Step 5: Apply sign rule: negative $\times$ negative = positive.
- Step 6: The result is $+40$ litres.
REASONING: If the leak is negative and we are looking at the past (negative time), the change relative to the current state is positive (the tank had more water before the leak started).
ANSWER: $+40$ litres
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you have a sequence of integers: $2, -4, 8, -16, \dots$, what is the next number? Explain your reasoning using multiplication.
COMPETENCY: Pattern recognition and applying multiplication rules.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Divide $-4$ by $2$ to find the multiplier: $-2$.
- Step 2: Divide $8$ by $-4$ to verify the multiplier: $-2$.
- Step 3: Multiply the last term ($-16$) by $-2$.
- Step 4: Calculate: $16 \times 2 = 32$; negative $\times$ negative = positive.
ANSWER: The next number is $32$. The pattern is multiplying by $-2$ each time.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students, Aarav and Meera, are asked to solve $(-5) \times (-3) \times (-2)$. Aarav says the answer is $-30$. Meera says the answer is $+30$. Who is correct and why?
COMPETENCY: Evaluating and justifying mathematical expressions.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Multiply the first two: $(-5) \times (-3) = +15$.
- Step 2: Multiply the result by the third factor: $(+15) \times (-2)$.
- Step 3: Calculate absolute values: $15 \times 2 = 30$.
- Step 4: Apply sign rule: positive $\times$ negative = negative.
ANSWER: Meera is incorrect and Aarav is correct. The product of three negatives is negative because the first two make a positive, and that positive multiplied by the final negative results in a negative product.
DIFFICULTY: hard
