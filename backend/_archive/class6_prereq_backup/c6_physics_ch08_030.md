## DEFINITION
Uniform motion occurs when an object moves in a straight line at a constant speed, covering equal distances in equal intervals of time. It represents a steady, unchanging rate of travel.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to calculate speed given distance and time, and identify uniform motion by observing if distance increases by the same amount for every equal unit of time.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the definition of uniform motion, the speed formula, and the interpretation of straight-line distance-time graphs. It excludes acceleration, vector directions, and inertial reference frames.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are riding a bicycle on a long, straight, empty road, and you keep your legs moving at the exact same pace the entire time. Because your effort doesn't change, your bicycle covers 10 metres every 2 seconds, no matter which part of the road you are on. To understand uniform motion, start by looking at a table of "Distance vs. Time." If the distance adds the same amount every time the clock ticks forward by one second, you have found uniform motion. Think of this as a "steady rhythm" of movement, which we can visualize as a perfectly straight line on a graph.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If a toy train travels 5 cm every 1 second, how far will it travel in 4 seconds if it is in uniform motion?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
It will travel 20 cm. If a student answers differently, it reveals they do not understand that "equal distances in equal intervals of time" means the distance must be multiplied by the time elapsed.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
A car’s distance-time graph is a perfectly straight, sloped line. What does this tell you about the car's speed?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The speed is constant. If a student says the speed is increasing, they are confusing a straight line with a curved line, failing to grasp that a constant slope means a constant rate of change.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A bus travels 100 km in 2 hours. Calculate its speed.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Speed = Distance / Time = 100 km / 2 h = 50 km/h. If a student multiplies instead of divides, they are misapplying the basic algebraic relationship between the three variables.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Two cars, A and B, are moving in uniform motion. Car A has a steeper line on the graph than Car B. Which car is faster?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Car A is faster. If a student guesses Car B, they do not understand that the "steepness" (slope) of the line represents how much distance is covered per unit of time.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If a cyclist covers 10m in the first second, 10m in the second, and 15m in the third, is this uniform motion? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it is not uniform motion because the distance covered is not equal for every equal time interval. A wrong answer shows the student has not grasped the "equal distance" requirement of the definition.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple experiment to prove a rolling marble is in uniform motion. What would you look for in your data?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Measure the marble's position at every 1-second interval. To show uniform motion, the difference in distance between each interval must be identical. A poor answer fails to mention the necessity of consistent time intervals.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse "constant speed" with "constant velocity" or assume that if an object moves, it must be in uniform motion. They might incorrectly label a ball rolling down a ramp (which speeds up) as uniform motion simply because it moves in a straight line.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to draw a table with two columns: Time (1s, 2s, 3s) and Distance (5m, 10m, 15m). Ask them to calculate the difference between the distances for each step. Explain that uniform motion is only present if that difference remains the same every single time.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A local train travels between two stations. It covers 10 km in the first 15 minutes, 10 km in the second 15 minutes, and 10 km in the third 15 minutes. Is the motion uniform? Calculate the speed in km/h.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify that the distance is 10 km per 15-minute interval.
- step 2: Confirm intervals are equal (15 mins) and distances are equal (10 km). This is uniform motion.
- step 3: Convert 15 minutes to hours (15/60 = 0.25 hours).
- step 4: Use Speed = Distance / Time = 10 km / 0.25 h.
- step 5: Result = 40 km/h.
REASONING: By confirming the intervals are consistent, we satisfy the condition for uniform motion; converting time units ensures the final speed is in standard units.
ANSWER: 40 km/h
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An athlete runs 400 metres in 80 seconds at a constant rate. What is her speed in m/s?
STEPS:
- step 1: Given distance = 400 m, time = 80 s.
- step 2: Identify the formula: Speed = Distance / Time.
- step 3: Substitute the values: 400 / 80.
- step 4: Divide 400 by 80 to get 5.
- step 5: State the final unit as m/s.
REASONING: Since the rate is constant, the speed calculated for the total trip applies to every second of the race.
ANSWER: 5 m/s
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A runner covers 100m in 20s. If they keep the same speed for 1 minute (60s), how much total distance will they cover?
STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate speed: 100m / 20s = 5 m/s.
- step 2: Identify that 1 minute = 60 seconds.
- step 3: Use the rearranged formula: Distance = Speed × Time.
- step 4: Calculate: 5 m/s × 60 s.
- step 5: Total distance = 300 m.
REASONING: Since uniform motion implies constant speed, we can use the speed calculated from the first 20 seconds to predict the distance covered over a longer duration.
ANSWER: 300 m
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are looking at a graph of a car's journey. The line is curved, not straight. Can you describe this as uniform motion? Explain your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating and justifying based on graphical representation.
STEPS:
- step 1: Observe the shape of the line.
- step 2: Recall the definition of uniform motion (requires a straight line).
- step 3: Conclude that a curve implies the speed is changing.
- step 4: State that uniform motion only occurs when speed is constant.
ANSWER: No, this is not uniform motion. A straight line on a distance-time graph represents constant speed, while a curved line indicates that the distance covered per unit of time is changing.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students, A and B, record the distance a ball rolls. Student A says the motion is uniform because the ball moves in a straight line. Student B says we must check if the distances are equal every second. Who is correct and why?
COMPETENCY: Identifying and correcting misconceptions about uniform motion.
STEPS:
- step 1: Compare the two conditions given by the students.
- step 2: Recall that uniform motion requires BOTH a straight path AND constant speed.
- step 3: Determine if one condition is enough.
- step 4: Conclude that Student B is correct because straight movement does not guarantee constant speed.
ANSWER: Student B is correct. Moving in a straight line is only half of the requirement; the speed must also remain constant. A ball could roll in a straight line while slowing down due to friction, which is not uniform motion.
DIFFICULTY: hard
