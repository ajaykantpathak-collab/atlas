## DEFINITION
Media refers to the various channels or tools used to communicate information to a large number of people, categorized into print, electronic, and digital formats. These tools act as the bridge between current events and the public, helping citizens stay informed about their society.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to correctly categorize a given media example (e.g., a newspaper vs. a news app) into its correct type and explain one unique characteristic of that category.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the identification and basic features of print, electronic, and digital media. It excludes media economics, government regulation, and technical media convergence.
## WALKTHROUGH
Think about how you learned about the weather this morning or the latest cricket score. Did you look at a physical newspaper, watch it on TV, or check a notification on a smartphone? Media is simply the "messenger" that brings this information to you. To begin, we look at the physical format (paper), the broadcast format (waves/signals), and the internet format (data). By distinguishing how information reaches us, we can better understand how different media shape our view of the world.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the three main types of media discussed in this chapter?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The three types are Print, Electronic, and Digital media. A student who misses one type likely hasn't grasped the classification system and needs to review the basic definitions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is print media still considered important in rural India even though we have smartphones?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Print media remains important because it does not rely on electricity or high-speed internet access to function. A student who says "it's faster" is incorrect, as print media is actually slower to distribute than digital media.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If you are reading a news story on a website using your laptop, which category of media are you using?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
You are using digital media. If the student answers "electronic," they are confusing devices (like a laptop) with the method of transmission (internet/data), suggesting they need to distinguish between the hardware and the network.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the speed of news delivery between print media and electronic media. Which is faster?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Electronic media (TV/Radio) is faster because it transmits news in real-time or near real-time, whereas print media requires a printing and physical delivery process. A student who struggles here may not understand the logistical difference between broadcasting and physical printing.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is a magazine considered digital media? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, a traditional magazine is print media because it is physically printed on paper. If a student says "yes," they are likely confusing "magazines" with "online blogs" or "e-magazines," showing a need for clarification on the medium of delivery.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a plan for a small town that has no internet access to receive information about a new government rule. Which media type would be most effective?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The most effective would be print media (newspapers/leaflets) or electronic media (radio). A poor answer would suggest an app or website, ignoring the lack of internet connectivity mentioned in the prompt.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think "Electronic" and "Digital" are the same thing because both use electricity. In this curriculum, "Electronic" refers to traditional broadcast media like TV and Radio, while "Digital" refers to internet-based information.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student if they need an internet connection to watch a standard TV channel (like Doordarshan). Once they say "no," explain that TV is electronic. Then, ask if they need Wi-Fi to read a news blog, explaining that this requirement makes it digital.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You want to tell your village about a health camp happening tomorrow morning. You have a printing press, a radio station, and a local website. Which do you choose?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the urgency (tomorrow morning).
- step 2: Evaluate print: Too slow to distribute by tomorrow.
- step 3: Evaluate website: Not everyone has internet.
- step 4: Evaluate radio (Electronic): Reaches everyone instantly.
- step 5: Conclusion: Radio is the best fit.
REASONING: The choice depends on the speed of delivery and the accessibility of the audience.
ANSWER: Radio (Electronic media) is the best choice for fast, wide-reaching communication.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Identify the medium for each: (A) A textbook, (B) A live news broadcast on TV, (C) A Twitter news feed.
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze A: It is paper-based -> Print.
- step 2: Analyze B: It is broadcasted via signals -> Electronic.
- step 3: Analyze C: It is internet-based -> Digital.
- step 4: Classify all three.
- step 5: Verify.
REASONING: Categorization is based on the delivery mechanism.
ANSWER: (A) Print, (B) Electronic, (C) Digital.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Why do major newspapers like 'The Hindu' also launch websites?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the source: Newspaper (Print).
- step 2: Identify the addition: Website (Digital).
- step 3: Analyze the benefit: Speed (digital) + In-depth analysis (print).
- step 4: Conclusion: To reach a wider audience and provide faster updates.
REASONING: Media often uses multiple formats to ensure coverage for all types of readers.
ANSWER: They do this to combine the in-depth analysis of print with the real-time speed of digital technology.

COMPETENCY: Critical Thinking
STEPS:
- step 1: List the media types.
- step 2: Check power dependency.
- step 3: Eliminate digital and electronic.
- step 4: Choose the remaining option.
ANSWER: Print media (newspapers), as they do not require electricity to be read.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare the "depth" of a 30-second TV news headline versus a 1000-word newspaper editorial. Which is better for understanding a complex law?
COMPETENCY: Analytical Reasoning
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the nature of the TV headline (fast, brief).
- step 2: Identify the nature of the newspaper editorial (slow, detailed).
- step 3: Define "complex law" needs (detail, reflection).
- step 4: Match need to medium.
- step 5: Formulate argument.
ANSWER: The newspaper editorial is better because print media allows for in-depth analysis and reflection, which is necessary to understand complex laws, whereas TV headlines are designed for quick information.
