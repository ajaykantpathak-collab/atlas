## DEFINITION
Supply is the total amount of a product that sellers are willing to offer at a specific price, while demand is the total amount that buyers are willing and able to purchase at that same price. Together, these two forces interact to determine the market price of goods.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to predict whether a price will rise or fall based on a provided scenario of changing supply or demand levels. They must also be able to correctly identify if a situation describes "supply" or "demand."
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the fundamental definitions of supply and demand and how their imbalance creates price changes. It does not cover mathematical curves, price elasticity, or government-regulated price ceilings/floors.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wondered why the price of umbrellas goes up when the monsoon starts, or why mangoes get cheaper in the middle of summer? Start by observing your local market: if many farmers bring mangoes to the market (high supply) but not many people want them (low demand), the price drops. If a sudden festival creates a rush to buy sweets (high demand) but there aren't enough boxes in the shop (low supply), the shopkeeper raises the price. The key insight is that prices act like a "see-saw" that tries to balance how much is available versus how much people want.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the difference between supply and demand?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Supply is the amount sellers are willing to sell, while demand is the amount buyers are willing to purchase at a specific price. A wrong answer here suggests the student is mixing up the roles of the seller and the buyer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a sudden storm destroys half of the apple crop, what happens to the supply of apples in the market?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The supply decreases. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks "supply" refers to the demand or the price, rather than the quantity of goods physically available.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If a popular new toy becomes a trend and every child wants one, what happens to the price if the number of toys stays the same?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The price will rise because the demand is higher than the supply. A wrong answer shows the student does not understand that high demand creates competition among buyers, pushing prices up.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare a scenario where mangoes are in season versus out of season. How does the quantity of mangoes affect their price?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
When in season, supply is high, making prices lower. When out of season, supply is low, making prices higher. If a student gets this wrong, they fail to see the inverse relationship between quantity available and price.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Should a shopkeeper lower prices if they have a huge pile of unsold vegetables that will spoil by tomorrow? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, they should lower prices to increase demand and sell the goods before they spoil. If the student says "raise prices," they ignore the need to clear stock when supply exceeds current interest.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Imagine you own a lemonade stand. How would you change your price if it is a very hot day and you only have one jug of lemonade left?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
You would raise the price because demand is high and supply is very low. A poor answer would suggest keeping the price the same, ignoring the scarcity of the product.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that "high demand" automatically means "high price" regardless of supply. They fail to realize that if supply is *also* extremely high (e.g., a bumper crop of onions), the price might stay low even if many people want to buy them.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to visualize a scale: one side is "Quantity Available" (Supply) and the other is "Quantity Wanted" (Demand). Tell them to imagine if one side is very heavy. If the "Wanted" side is heavy, the price needs to go up to push the scale back to center. Use the onion example: if the market is flooded with onions, the "Available" side is heavy, so the price must go down to encourage people to take them off the scale.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A local farmer brings 100 watermelons to the market on a cool day. Only 20 people want to buy them. What happens to the price?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify supply (100) vs demand (20).
- step 2: Compare the two. Supply is much higher than demand.
- step 3: Apply the rule: When supply > demand, prices drop.
- step 4: Conclude: The farmer must lower the price to encourage more of the 80 people who didn't want them to buy them.
REASONING: Prices are determined by the balance of what is available versus what is needed. When there is a surplus, the price must fall.
ANSWER: The price will decrease to attract more buyers.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A sudden festival increases the need for milk. There is only a fixed amount of milk delivered to the shop. What happens to the price?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify that supply is limited (fixed).
- step 2: Identify that demand has increased (festival).
- step 3: Apply the rule: When demand > supply, buyers compete.
- step 4: Conclude: Sellers will raise the price because the milk is scarce.
REASONING: Scarcity increases the value of a good to the buyers.
ANSWER: The price will rise.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A new road makes it easier for trucks to bring potatoes to the city. What happens to the price of potatoes?
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the change: The road increases the quantity of potatoes arriving.
- step 2: Identify the effect: Supply increases.
- step 3: Assume demand stays the same for now.
- step 4: Apply the rule: Higher supply leads to lower prices.
- step 5: Conclude: The price of potatoes will likely fall.
REASONING: Transportation changes directly impact the supply side of the market.
ANSWER: The price will fall due to increased supply.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are a shopkeeper. You have 50 umbrellas. It starts to rain heavily, and 100 people come to your shop wanting an umbrella. What do you do?
COMPETENCY: Decision making based on market forces
STEPS:
- step 1: Recognize that demand (100) is greater than supply (50).
- step 2: Identify the scarcity.
- step 3: Recognize that you can raise prices because people really need the item.
- step 4: Conclude that raising the price is the market-based response to shortage.
ANSWER: I would raise the price because the demand is much higher than the supply, and umbrellas are now a scarce resource.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why does the price of a specific type of fish rise during the monsoon season in coastal India even if people love eating it all year round?
COMPETENCY: Analyzing factors behind supply changes
STEPS:
- step 1: Research/Identify that fishing is restricted during monsoon for safety/breeding.
- step 2: Conclude that supply drops significantly.
- step 3: Identify that demand remains steady or high.
- step 4: Apply the rule: Low supply + steady demand = higher price.
- step 5: Finalize the reasoning: The price rises due to the reduction in supply caused by the season.
ANSWER: The price rises because during the monsoon, fishing becomes difficult or is paused, leading to a low supply. Since people still want to eat the fish (constant demand), the scarcity forces the price up.
DIFFICULTY: hard
