## DEFINITION
Inverse proportion is a relationship between two quantities where an increase in one causes a proportional decrease in the other, such that their product remains constant. If you multiply the first quantity by a number, you must divide the second quantity by the same number to maintain the balance.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify inverse relationships in word problems and calculate the missing value using the constant product rule ($a \times b = c \times d$).
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers identifying inverse proportion and solving for missing values using the product rule. It excludes compound proportion, joint variation, and graphical representations.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you have a bag of 24 candies to share among your friends. If you share them with 4 friends, each gets 6 candies. But if 8 friends arrive, each gets only 3 candies. Do you see how the number of friends increased, but the number of candies per person decreased? To start, identify the two quantities (workers/time, speed/time, people/shares). Check if one rising causes the other to fall. If it does, multiply the two known values to find the "constant product," then divide that constant by the new value you were given to find the missing piece. The key insight is that the product of the two quantities never changes.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If 5 workers take 10 days to finish a wall, what is the product of the number of workers and the number of days?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The product is 50. A student answering "15" or "2" is likely confusing inverse proportion with addition or direct division, failing to recognize that the relationship relies on multiplication.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why does the time taken to complete a task decrease when more workers are added?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Because the total amount of work is fixed; adding more hands to the job means each person does a smaller share of the work, finishing the project faster. An answer stating "it just happens" shows a lack of conceptual understanding of the "fixed total" requirement.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A car travels at 40 km/h and reaches a destination in 6 hours. If the speed is increased to 80 km/h, how long will the journey take?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Product = 40 × 6 = 240. Step 2: New speed = 80. Step 3: Time = 240 / 80 = 3 hours. A student who guesses "12 hours" is incorrectly applying direct proportion (thinking more speed means more time).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If a machine produces 100 items in 2 hours, and a faster machine produces 200 items in the same 2 hours, is this an inverse proportion? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
No, this is not inverse proportion. In inverse proportion, one variable must decrease as the other increases, whereas here, the number of items increased while the time stayed the same.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Two friends are packing books. If they take 4 hours, would 4 friends take 2 hours or 8 hours? Justify your choice.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
They would take 2 hours. Since the number of workers doubled (from 2 to 4), the time must be halved (from 4 to 2) to keep the total work product constant at 8.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-world scenario involving inverse proportion that is NOT about workers or time.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: "A fixed amount of money (₹100) is used to buy pens. If the price per pen increases, the number of pens you can buy decreases." A poor answer would be "Buying more apples costs more money," which is direct, not inverse, proportion.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to use the "cross-multiplication" method from direct proportion ($a/b = c/d$) for inverse problems. They do this because they are used to solving ratios and forget that in inverse proportion, the *product* stays the same, not the ratio. For example, if 2 workers take 4 days, they might incorrectly think 4 workers take 8 days ($2/4 = 4/8$).
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to calculate the "Total Work" first. Tell them: "If 2 workers work for 4 days, how many 'worker-days' of effort are needed?" Once they calculate 8, ask: "If we have 4 workers, can they finish those 8 worker-days in 8 days, or would that take much longer than the original job?" This helps them see that the time must decrease.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A contractor hires 6 laborers to build a wall in 10 days. If he wants to finish the wall in 5 days, how many laborers does he need?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: 6 laborers, 10 days.
- step 2: Calculate total work: $6 \times 10 = 60$ laborer-days.
- step 3: Set up the equation with the new time: $x \times 5 = 60$.
- step 4: Solve for $x$: $x = 60 / 5 = 12$.
- step 5: Interpretation: He needs 12 laborers to finish in 5 days.
REASONING: Since the total work is constant (60 laborer-days), we divide this constant by the target number of days to find the required labor force.
ANSWER: 12 laborers.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A car traveling at 60 km/h takes 4 hours to reach a city. How fast must it travel to reach there in 3 hours?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: $60 \text{ km/h}$, $4 \text{ hours}$.
- step 2: Calculate the distance: $60 \times 4 = 240 \text{ km}$.
- step 3: Set up the equation with the new time: $x \times 3 = 240$.
- step 4: Solve for $x$: $x = 240 / 3 = 80$.
- step 5: Interpretation: The car must travel at 80 km/h.
REASONING: The distance between the cities is the constant product. To cover the same distance in less time, the speed must increase proportionally.
ANSWER: 80 km/h.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A farmer has enough feed to last 20 cows for 12 days. If he sells 5 cows, how many days will the feed last for the remaining cows?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: 20 cows, 12 days.
- step 2: Total feed units: $20 \times 12 = 240$ cow-days.
- step 3: New number of cows: $20 - 5 = 15$ cows.
- step 4: Solve for days: $15 \times \text{days} = 240$.
- step 5: Calculate days: $240 / 15 = 16$.
- step 6: Interpretation: The feed will now last 16 days.
REASONING: Fewer cows mean the food supply lasts longer, confirming the inverse relationship. We find the constant total units and divide by the new cow count.
ANSWER: 16 days.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A group of 10 students plans to paint a fence in 6 hours. If 2 students leave before starting, how much longer will the job take for the remaining students?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions.
STEPS:
- step 1: Total work units = $10 \text{ students} \times 6 \text{ hours} = 60$ student-hours.
- step 2: Remaining students = $10 - 2 = 8$.
- step 3: New time = $60 / 8 = 7.5$ hours.
- step 4: Find the difference: $7.5 - 6 = 1.5$ hours.
ANSWER: The job will take 1.5 hours longer.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two rectangles both have an area of 48 sq cm. If Rectangle A has a length of 8 cm, and Rectangle B has a length of 12 cm, which rectangle has a smaller width? Explain why this is an inverse proportion.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate width of A: $48 / 8 = 6$ cm.
- step 2: Calculate width of B: $48 / 12 = 4$ cm.
- step 3: Compare: 4 cm < 6 cm.
- step 4: Justify: The area (constant product) = length $\times$ width. As length increases (8 to 12), width must decrease (6 to 4) to keep the area at 48.
ANSWER: Rectangle B has a smaller width (4 cm). It is an inverse proportion because the product (Area) is fixed, so increasing one dimension forces the other to decrease.
DIFFICULTY: hard
