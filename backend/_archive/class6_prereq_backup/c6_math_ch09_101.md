## DEFINITION
The median is the middle value of a data set that has been arranged in order from smallest to largest (ascending order). It represents the exact center point of the data, where half the values are below it and half are above it.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to arrange a given set of numbers in ascending order and correctly identify the middle value (or calculate the average of two middle values) to determine the median.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers finding the median for simple numerical data sets with odd or even numbers of items. It excludes quartiles, box plots, and grouped frequency distribution tables.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you and your friends line up by height. To find the "middle" person, you naturally arrange yourselves from shortest to tallest. In data handling, the median works the same way: if you have a list of numbers, you must first sort them in ascending order. If you have an odd number of items, the median is the single value sitting exactly in the middle. If you have an even number, you don't have one middle person, so you take the two people in the center and find their average (add them and divide by 2) to find the "middle" ground. Always remember: sorting is the non-negotiable first step!
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If you have the numbers 7, 2, and 9, what is the first thing you must do before finding the median?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
You must arrange the numbers in ascending order (2, 7, 9). A wrong answer often reveals that the student thinks the median is simply the middle number as it appears in the original, unsorted list.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Given the data set {10, 2, 5, 8, 3}, what is the median and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The median is 5. After sorting (2, 3, 5, 8, 10), 5 is the middle value. A wrong answer suggests the student failed to sort the data or chose the mean instead of the median.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A shopkeeper notes the price of 4 pens: 5, 12, 8, and 15 rupees. What is the median price?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
First, sort: 5, 8, 12, 15. Since there are two middle numbers (8 and 12), calculate the average: (8 + 12) / 2 = 10. The median is 10 rupees. A wrong answer shows the student does not know how to handle an even number of values.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare finding the median of {1, 3, 5} versus {1, 3, 5, 7}. What is the fundamental difference in the process?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
In the first set, there is an exact middle number (3). In the second set, there is no single middle number, so you must average the two center numbers (3 and 5) to get 4. This tests the student's ability to differentiate between odd and even data counts.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If a student claims the median of {100, 10, 1} is 100 because it is the biggest, how would you evaluate their reasoning?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The reasoning is incorrect because the median is the middle value, not the largest value. The correct median is 10 (after sorting: 1, 10, 100). This evaluates the student's ability to correct a common conceptual error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a data set of 6 numbers where the median is 10.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Any set where the average of the 3rd and 4th values (when sorted) is 10 will work (e.g., 2, 5, 9, 11, 15, 20). A poor answer would show a lack of understanding that the median is determined by the center values, not the total count or range.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to find the median without sorting the numbers first. For example, in the set {5, 1, 9}, they might pick 1 because it is in the middle of the original list. They make this error because they view the list as a static sequence rather than a collection of values to be organized.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to physically rearrange the items or numbers on their desk from smallest to largest using cards or blocks. Say: "Before we look for the center, we must make sure the line is organized by size, just like a queue." Once they sort them, have them cross off one from each end until they reach the middle.

## THIS_FORMULA

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A teacher records the marks of 5 students in a math test: 15, 10, 20, 5, 25. What is the median mark?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the values given: 15, 10, 20, 5, 25. Total values (n) = 5.
- Step 2: Arrange the values in ascending order: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25.
- Step 3: Find the middle position. Since 5 is odd, the middle is the (5+1)/2 = 3rd position.
- Step 4: Identify the 3rd value in the sorted list, which is 15.
- Step 5: Verify that there are two values below 15 and two values above it.
REASONING: By sorting the data, we ensure every value is in its correct relative position. Selecting the 3rd value identifies the balance point of the set.
ANSWER: 15 marks
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: The heights of 6 siblings in centimeters are 120, 135, 125, 140, 130, 145. Find the median height.
STEPS:
- Step 1: List values: 120, 135, 125, 140, 130, 145. (n = 6).
- Step 2: Sort the values: 120, 125, 130, 135, 140, 145.
- Step 3: Identify the two middle values (the 3rd and 4th positions): 130 and 135.
- Step 4: Calculate the average: (130 + 135) / 2 = 265 / 2 = 132.5.
- Step 5: The median is the midpoint between the two central values.
REASONING: Because there is an even number of values, no single value occupies the center; we must calculate the numerical midpoint between the two central candidates.
ANSWER: 132.5 cm
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A boy counts the number of cars passing his house in 7 minutes: 4, 7, 2, 9, 5, 3, 10. Find the median.
STEPS:
- Step 1: List the counts: 4, 7, 2, 9, 5, 3, 10.
- Step 2: Sort: 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 10.
- Step 3: Check if the set is odd or even: n=7 (odd).
- Step 4: Find the middle (4th) position: 5.
- Step 5: Interpretation: Half the time there were fewer than 5 cars, half the time more.
REASONING: Sorting provides a clear view of the distribution, allowing us to pick the middle observation. The median here acts as a typical value for the car counts.
ANSWER: 5 cars
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have two sets of data: A = {2, 8, 10} and B = {2, 4, 8, 10}. Both have a median. Explain why finding the median of set A is different from set B.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Step 1: Determine the number of items in each set.
- Step 2: Apply the median rule for odd vs even counts.
- Step 3: Calculate the median for both.
- Step 4: Compare the processes used.
ANSWER: Set A has 3 items (odd), so the median is the 2nd value (8). Set B has 4 items (even), requiring the average of the 2nd and 3rd values (4+8)/2 = 6. The process differs because B requires an extra step of calculating an average.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A family spends 500, 200, 800, 1000, and 300 rupees on groceries in 5 weeks. If they spend 600 rupees in the 6th week, how does the median change?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- Step 1: Find the initial median of the 5 weeks.
- Step 2: Add the new value to the list.
- Step 3: Sort the new set of 6 values.
- Step 4: Find the new median.
- Step 5: Compare the old and new median.
ANSWER: Initial sorted: 200, 300, 500, 800, 1000. Median = 500. New set sorted: 200, 300, 500, 600, 800, 1000. New median = (500+600)/2 = 550. The median increases because a higher value was added.
DIFFICULTY: hard
