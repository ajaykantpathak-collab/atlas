## DEFINITION
Economic activities are actions performed by individuals to earn money or acquire goods and services of monetary value, such as working for a salary or selling produce. Non-economic activities are actions performed out of love, care, or social responsibility that do not involve financial gain.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to categorize a list of daily activities into "economic" or "non-economic" and explain why they belong to that category based on the presence or absence of financial exchange.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the classification of activities into economic and non-economic sectors. It excludes detailed economic theory, GDP calculations, and the informal sector.
## WALKTHROUGH
Think about your morning: did you see a shopkeeper opening their store or a parent cooking breakfast for the family? To start, list three things you see people doing in your neighborhood. Ask yourself: "Does this person get paid in money or goods for this specific task?" If the answer is yes, it is an economic activity; if it is done for love or care, it is non-economic. Remember, the key is the exchange of value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the primary difference between an economic activity and a non-economic activity?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Economic activities are done for money or monetary value, while non-economic activities are done for personal care, love, or social service. A wrong answer here suggests the student is confusing "work" (effort) with "earning" (reward).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If your neighbor spends their weekend painting your house for free because they are your friend, is this an economic activity? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, it is a non-economic activity because it is performed out of friendship/care and no money or payment was exchanged. A wrong answer shows the student thinks any "productive work" is an economic activity regardless of payment.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A tailor stitches a school uniform and charges ₹200 for it. Identify the type of activity and explain the "value" involved.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
This is an economic activity. The value involves the exchange of service (stitching) for money (₹200). A wrong answer indicates difficulty linking "service" to "monetary compensation."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare a doctor treating a patient in a private clinic versus a doctor volunteering at a free health camp. How do these compare in terms of economic classification?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The first is an economic activity (earning a fee), while the second is non-economic (service to society). A wrong answer reveals an inability to see that the same profession can perform different types of activities based on payment status.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
"Economic activities are more important than non-economic activities because they generate wealth." Do you agree? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
I disagree; while economic activities provide money for survival, non-economic activities (like parenting or volunteering) provide social and emotional support that is essential for a healthy society. A wrong answer shows a lack of appreciation for the "value of work" beyond money.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a scenario where an activity changes from non-economic to economic.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A student teaching their sibling math for free (non-economic) decides to start a tuition center and charges a monthly fee (economic). A poor answer fails to show the shift from "care" to "formal exchange of money."
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that "working hard" or "being busy" automatically makes an activity economic. They might assume that cleaning one's own house is an economic activity because it is "hard work." This is incorrect because economic activity requires a transaction of money or value between two parties.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student: "Who paid you money for this task?" If the answer is "no one" or "my parents/self," explain that for an activity to be economic, there must be an external exchange. Use the example of a professional cleaner versus a family member cleaning to clarify.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Identifying activities in a marketplace.
STEPS:
- 1. Observe a vegetable seller.
- 2. Ask: "Is money changing hands?"
- 3. Observe a person sweeping the street in front of their own house.
- 4. Ask: "Is there a salary involved?"
- 5. Classify based on these observations.
REASONING: Economic activities require a market transaction; chores do not.
ANSWER: Vegetable seller = Economic; Sweeping own house = Non-economic.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Analyzing a teacher's day.
STEPS:
- 1. Identify morning job: teaching at a school for a salary.
- 2. Identify evening activity: helping a child with homework.
- 3. Compare payment status.
- 4. Categorize each.
REASONING: Salary indicates economic status, while personal help indicates non-economic status.
ANSWER: School teaching = Economic; Helping child = Non-economic.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Assessing "Payment in Kind."
STEPS:
- 1. Recognize the labor performed.
- 2. Note the payment received (not money, but goods).
- 3. Check if the goods have monetary value.
- 4. Confirm the exchange aspect.
REASONING: If an item has monetary value, it counts as "money's worth" even if cash isn't used.
ANSWER: This is an economic activity because the goods (e.g., mangoes) represent an agreed-upon value for the work done.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a person builds a bookshelf for their own room, is it economic or non-economic? If they build it for a client, what changes?
COMPETENCY: Categorization
STEPS:
- 1. Analyze the purpose of the first bookshelf (personal use).
- 2. Analyze the purpose of the second (exchange for money).
- 3. Apply the definition of economic activity.
ANSWER: Building for self is non-economic; building for a client is economic because the transaction creates monetary value.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why does society label some non-economic activities (like volunteering) as "valuable" even if they don't add to a person's income?
COMPETENCY: Critical Thinking
STEPS:
- 1. Define the role of non-economic activities (social welfare).
- 2. Identify the benefit to the community (e.g., cleaner streets, educated kids).
- 3. Explain how this improves the quality of life.
ANSWER: Non-economic activities are valuable because they address social needs, build community trust, and improve collective well-being, which money alone cannot always achieve.
