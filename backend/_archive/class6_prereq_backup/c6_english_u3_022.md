## DEFINITION
Character analysis is the process of examining a character's traits, motivations, and behaviors to understand their role and personality in a story. It involves looking at what a character says, does, and how they react to challenges.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify at least three character traits of Anand and provide a specific example of an action or behavior from the text that supports each trait.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers identifying character traits for Anand as presented in the story "My Brother's Great Invention." It excludes character development over time and analysis of secondary characters like the sister.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever met someone who keeps trying to build something, even if it falls apart every time? To analyze a character like Anand, start by asking: "What does he do when things go wrong?" and "How does he view his own work?" Look for descriptive words in the text that tell you about his inner thoughts, like 'stubborn' or 'curious.' Compare his reaction to failure with his sister’s practical viewpoint to see what makes his personality unique. Remember, a character's traits are the "why" behind their actions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is one trait that describes Anand’s attitude toward his inventions?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Anand is curious or confident. A wrong answer like "he is a professional engineer" shows the student is confusing the character's hobby/self-image with his actual reality in the story.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why does the author describe Anand as "stubborn"?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
He is described as stubborn because he continues to work on his inventions even after they fail repeatedly. A wrong answer like "because he listens to his sister" shows the student has not understood the conflict in the story.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If Anand’s invention fails, how would he likely react compared to a "practical" person?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Anand would likely try to fix it or start a new version, while a practical person might stop to assess if the design is actually possible. If the student says "he would give up," they are failing to apply the trait of "confidence" mentioned in the text.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How does the narrator's perspective highlight Anand’s naivety?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The narrator (his sister) sees the flaws in the designs that Anand ignores, showing he lacks a realistic perspective. A student who misses this fails to see how the narrator serves as a foil to Anand.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is Anand’s persistence a positive or negative trait in this story? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It can be argued as positive (shows dedication) or negative (shows a lack of realism). The justification must cite his repeated failures vs. his refusal to stop. A wrong answer lacks evidence from the text.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Describe a new invention Anand might make and how his traits (curiosity/stubbornness) would influence him.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An answer should describe an imaginative but likely flawed machine and explain that Anand would keep tinkering with it despite others telling him it won't work. A poor answer ignores his specific personality traits.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse "what a character does" with "who a character is." They might say "Anand is an inventor" instead of identifying the trait of "curiosity." This happens because they focus on the plot event rather than the underlying personality.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student: "If an inventor stops after one try, are they still an inventor?" Guide them to describe the *feeling* or *motive* behind the action. Use the prompt: "What word describes a person who doesn't stop even when things are hard?" to lead them to "persistent" or "stubborn."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Identify one trait for Anand and prove it.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Scan text for an instance where Anand fails.
- Step 2: Observe his reaction (does he cry or try again?).
- Step 3: Select a trait word (e.g., "Persistent").
- Step 4: Write the sentence: "Anand is persistent because he continues to tinker after his invention fails."
REASONING: By linking a specific action to a trait label, we move from just recounting the story to analyzing the character.
ANSWER: Trait: Persistent. Evidence: He continues to work on his inventions despite repeated failures.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Contrast Anand with his sister Anita.
STEPS:
- Step 1: List Anand's traits (e.g., imaginative).
- Step 2: List Anita's approach (e.g., practical).
- Step 3: Identify the difference (Anand ignores flaws; Anita notices them).
- Step 4: Summarize the contrast in one sentence.
REASONING: Comparing characters helps clarify their individual traits by showing how they differ.
ANSWER: Anand is imaginative and ignores flaws, whereas Anita is practical and identifies the flaws in his designs.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Why does the author include Anand's "naivety" as a trait?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define naivety (lack of experience or judgment).
- Step 2: Find a moment where Anand fails to see the obvious.
- Step 3: Connect this to his inventions.
- Step 4: Explain the effect (it adds humor or conflict to the story).
REASONING: Analyzing the purpose of a trait helps understand the author's craft.
ANSWER: His naivety is included to show he is a dreamer; it creates conflict because his inventions are destined to fail, which the reader sees but he does not.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If Anand were asked to fix a broken toy, how would he approach it based on his traits?
COMPETENCY: Character Prediction
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall Anand's traits (curiosity, tinkering).
- Step 2: Predict his immediate action (opening the toy).
- Step 3: Predict his reaction to failure (he won't give up).
- Step 4: Connect to his character (he prefers invention over simple repair).
ANSWER: Anand would immediately take the toy apart to see how it works (curiosity) and would spend hours trying to modify it, even if he couldn't put it back together, because he is stubborn and sees himself as an inventor.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why is the sister's perspective essential for the reader to understand Anand?
COMPETENCY: Perspective Analysis
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze how we know Anand is naive.
- Step 2: Identify who points out the flaws.
- Step 3: Consider the story without the sister's voice.
- Step 4: Explain the necessity of the contrast.
- Step 5: Formulate the final argument.
ANSWER: Without the sister's practical observations, the reader might think Anand is a genius who succeeds. Her perspective acts as a mirror that shows the reader his flaws, making his character traits (stubbornness and naivety) clear.
DIFFICULTY: hard
