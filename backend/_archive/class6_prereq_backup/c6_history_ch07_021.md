## DEFINITION
The Delhi Sultanate was the first major Islamic empire established in northern India, beginning in 1206 CE when Qutbuddin Aibak declared himself the independent ruler of Delhi. It marked the start of a new political era that would last for over 300 years.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify Qutbuddin Aibak as the founder and explain that the Sultanate was a political change that lasted from 1206 to 1526 CE.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the transition of power under Qutbuddin Aibak and the timeline of the Delhi Sultanate. It excludes specific dynastic battles, administrative details, and religious doctrines.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever seen a sports team get a new coach who changes how the whole game is played? Starting in 1206 CE, India experienced a similar change in "leadership" when a former general named Qutbuddin Aibak took charge of Delhi. Think of it like a relay race: Aibak took the baton from his previous leader, Muhammad of Ghur, but instead of continuing to serve someone else, he began his own independent rule. We begin by looking at how a person who was once a slave could rise to become the Sultan, and why this moment is called the "founding" of a new era. By understanding this shift, you realize that history is often shaped by individuals making big decisions at the right moment.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Who is credited with founding the Delhi Sultanate in 1206 CE?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Qutbuddin Aibak founded the Delhi Sultanate. A wrong answer suggests the student is confusing him with later rulers or the previous leader, Muhammad of Ghur, indicating they haven't distinguished between the founder and the predecessor.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do historians call 1206 CE the start of the Delhi Sultanate?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It is the year Qutbuddin Aibak declared himself independent ruler after his leader died. If a student says it's just a random date, they misunderstand that the Sultanate began with a specific political action of independence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculation: 1526 minus 1206 equals 320 years. A wrong answer reveals a struggle with basic chronological subtraction, which is vital for understanding historical timelines.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The Delhi Sultanate lasted for a total of 320 years. This is calculated by subtracting the starting year of 1206 from the ending year of 1526.

A wrong answer reveals that the student likely lacks the foundational skill of chronological subtraction or is struggling with the concept of "duration" in historical timelines, perhaps attempting to add the two years together instead of finding the difference between them.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How did a "former slave" become a Sultan? What does this tell us about the society of that time?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
It shows that military skill and loyalty could lead to high positions regardless of birth status. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks only people born into royal families could lead, missing the point of Aibak's unique rise.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it accurate to say the Delhi Sultanate was ruled by only one family for 300 years? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it was ruled by five successive dynasties. A wrong answer indicates the student thinks "Sultanate" implies a single family line, missing the diversity of the five different groups that held power.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Imagine you are a historian writing a headline for the year 1206 CE. What would it be?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer would mention "A New Era Begins" or "From General to Sultan." A poor answer is vague, like "The Sultanate happened," which fails to capture the significance of the transition.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think Qutbuddin Aibak conquered India all by himself as an independent king from the start. They miss the fact that he was a general under Muhammad of Ghur and only became the Sultan *after* his leader's death. This is common because they often simplify history into "one hero" stories rather than seeing the transition of power.
## ON_FAILURE
1. Ask the student to draw a timeline showing two people: Muhammad of Ghur and Qutbuddin Aibak. 2. Ask them to write "1206" above the point where their roles switch. 3. Explain that Aibak was a "general" (a team member) before he became a "Sultan" (the captain).

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: If a sultanate lasts for 320 years and is divided into 5 dynasties, what is the average time a dynasty ruled?
STEPS:
- identify total years: 320
- identify number of dynasties: 5
- perform division: 320 / 5
- calculate: 64
REASONING: Dividing the total time by the number of parts gives the average. This helps us see that power shifted relatively frequently.
ANSWER: 64 years.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Qutbuddin Aibak served as a general under Muhammad of Ghur before 1206. If he became a general at age 25 and served for 10 years, how old was he when he became Sultan?
STEPS:
- identify age at start: 25
- identify years served: 10
- sum the years: 25 + 10
- calculate result: 35
REASONING: Basic addition of time periods helps track a person's life milestones.
ANSWER: 35 years old.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student claims the Delhi Sultanate ended in 1400 CE. How far off is this from the actual date (1526)?
STEPS:
- identify actual end date: 1526
- identify student's claimed date: 1400
- subtract the difference: 1526 - 1400
- interpret: 126 years
REASONING: Comparing dates helps students visualize the span of history.
ANSWER: The student is off by 126 years.

COMPETENCY: Historical interpretation
STEPS:

- identify the change in status for Delhi

- state why this is a "turning point"
