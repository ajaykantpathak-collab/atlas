## DEFINITION
Poetic devices are tools used by poets to create deeper meaning and musical effects, including **Alliteration** (repeating initial sounds), **Personification** (giving human traits to non-human things), and **Symbolism** (using objects to represent ideas).

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever felt like your favorite tree was actually listening to your secrets? Just like Amber talking to 'Neem Baba' in your textbook, poets often use 'Personification' to make nature feel like a friend. To start, read the poem aloud and listen for repeated sounds like "busy bees" (Alliteration). Next, look for objects that seem to stand for something bigger, like a paper boat representing a child’s dreams (Symbolism). Finally, ask yourself, "If this object were a person, what would it say?" to spot Personification. By doing this, you stop reading just words and start seeing the hidden pictures the poet painted for you.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What do we call it when a poet repeats the same starting sound in words placed close together?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
This is called alliteration. If a student answers "rhyme," they are confusing the sound of the *start* of the word with the sound of the *end* of the word.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
In the sentence, "The angry wind howled at the window," why is this an example of personification?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It is personification because the wind is given the human action of "howling" and "being angry." A wrong answer suggests the student thinks it is just a literal description of weather, missing the human comparison.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
This is symbolism. The student must identify that the dove is a literal bird but stands for the abstract idea of peace. A wrong answer shows the student is taking the image only at face value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
[Correct answer] The poetic device being used is **symbolism**, where a concrete object (the white dove) is used to represent an abstract idea (peace). By assigning a deeper meaning beyond its literal definition, the poet uses the dove as a universal signifier for tranquility.

A wrong answer reveals that the student likely confuses **symbolism** with **metaphor** or **personification**. If they choose "metaphor," they may not understand that a metaphor makes a direct comparison between two unlike things (e.g., "The snow is a white blanket"), whereas symbolism relies on cultural or universal associations to represent a concept.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Read the phrase: "Seven silver stars shimmered." Analyze why this is alliteration and how it affects the reader.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
It is alliteration because the 's' sound repeats at the start of every word. It creates a soft, hissing, musical rhythm that makes the reader imagine the stars moving or sparkling.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Critique this statement: "Personification is only used to make poems funny." Is this true or false, and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It is false. While it can be funny, personification is often used to make nature feel alive, sad, or powerful, helping the reader connect emotionally to non-human things like trees or clouds.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a one-line sentence using alliteration about a cat, then add a touch of personification to it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: "The clever cat crept cautiously, whispering secrets to the moon." A poor answer would lack the repeated initial sounds or fail to give the cat a human-like action.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse "Personification" with "Simile" (a comparison using 'like' or 'as'). For example, they might think "The tree stood tall like a soldier" is personification, but it is a simile because it uses "like." Personification MUST state the object *is* doing the action (e.g., "The tree guarded the house").
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to rewrite the sentence without using "like" or "as." If they wrote "The clouds are like fluffy sheep," tell them: "Change it so the clouds are actually doing something human." They should change it to: "The clouds grazed across the sky."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Identify the devices in: "The golden sun smiled at the sleepy sea. Silently, soft shadows spread."
STEPS:
- Step 1: Look for repeated starting sounds. 'Silently, soft shadows spread' uses the 's' sound.
- Step 2: Identify the device. That is Alliteration.
- Step 3: Look for human actions. The 'sun' is 'smiling'.
- Step 4: Identify the device. That is Personification.
- Step 5: Summarize. The poet uses these to make the morning scene feel warm and alive.
REASONING: By isolating the sound patterns and the actions, we clearly separate the musical device from the humanizing device.
ANSWER: Alliteration is used in the second sentence; Personification is used in the first.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In the poem "What a Bird Thought," if the bird calls the shell "blue," is that symbolism?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define symbolism (an object representing an abstract idea).
- Step 2: Check if 'blue' acts as an idea. No, it is a literal color description.
- Step 3: Check if the 'shell' represents something. It represents the bird's limited early world.
- Step 4: Conclude. The shell is a symbol of confinement/limited knowledge.
REASONING: Not every descriptive word is a symbol; we must look for what the object represents in the poem's story.
ANSWER: No, 'blue' is just imagery, but the 'shell' itself is a symbol for the bird's small, early worldview.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Explain the difference between "The wind is fast" and "The wind raced the clouds."
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze "The wind is fast." This is a literal fact.
- Step 2: Analyze "The wind raced the clouds." The wind is 'racing' (a human/animal act).
- Step 3: Identify the device in the second sentence. It is personification.
- Step 4: Compare. One is a report; the other is a story-telling tool.
- Step 5: Final conclusion. Personification adds life to the wind.
REASONING: By comparing literal facts with figurative descriptions, we see how poetic devices change the "vibe" of a sentence.
ANSWER: The first is literal; the second is personification because it gives the wind the ability to compete in a race.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a poem says, "The flowers danced in the breeze," why is this more effective than saying "The flowers moved in the breeze"?
COMPETENCY: Critical Analysis
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the device (Personification).
- Step 2: Define 'danced' vs 'moved'.
- Step 3: Explain the emotional impact.
- Step 4: Conclude.
ANSWER: 'Moved' is a boring fact, but 'danced' uses personification to suggest the flowers are happy and joyful, making the reader feel the beauty of the garden.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: How does the symbol of a 'nest' change from Stanza 2 to Stanza 4 in a poem about a bird growing up?
COMPETENCY: Literary Interpretation
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the nest in Stanza 2 (safety/small world).
- Step 2: Identify the nest in Stanza 4 (the past/limitations).
- Step 3: Explain the shift.
- Step 4: Link to the bird's growth.
- Step 5: Summarize.
ANSWER: In Stanza 2, the nest symbolizes safety and a mother’s care. By Stanza 4, it symbolizes a small, restrictive place that the bird has outgrown. The symbol changes from a place of comfort to a place of limitation as the bird matures.
DIFFICULTY: hard
