## DEFINITION
Place value in decimals is an extension of the whole number system, where digits to the right of the decimal point represent fractions of a whole, specifically tenths (1/10), hundredths (1/100), and thousandths (1/1000). Each position to the right has a value exactly one-tenth of the position to its immediate left.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the place value of any digit in a decimal number up to three decimal places and express that digit as both a fraction and a decimal value.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers identifying place values for tenths, hundredths, and thousandths. It excludes scientific notation, binary decimals, and calculations involving extremely small decimals (like millionths).
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever looked at a price tag like ₹45.75 and wondered why the 7 and 5 aren't just whole numbers? Think of the decimal point as a mirror: to the left, we have our familiar ones, tens, and hundreds; to the right, the numbers get smaller, representing parts of a whole. To begin, draw a simple table with columns for Tens, Ones, Decimal Point, Tenths, and Hundredths. Place a number like 12.34 into the table, realizing that 3 is 3/10 and 4 is 4/100. Always remember that as you move one step to the right, the value of the place is divided by ten, showing us how we break a whole into smaller, equal pieces.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
In the number 15.67, what is the place value of the digit 6?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The place value is the tenths place (or 0.6). A student who answers "tens" is confusing the tenths place (to the right of the decimal) with the tens place (to the left of the decimal).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is the digit 3 in 0.03 worth less than the digit 3 in 0.3?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The digit 3 in 0.3 is in the tenths place (3/10), while the 3 in 0.03 is in the hundredths place (3/100). A student who says "because it is on the right" is missing the specific fractional value of each position.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
The value is 8/100. A student who writes 8/10 or 8/1000 has failed to count the positions correctly from the decimal point (tenths, hundredths, thousandths).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The value of the digit 8 in the number 24.582 is 8/100. This is because the 8 is in the hundredths place, meaning it represents eight parts out of one hundred.

A wrong answer reveals that the student likely confuses the place value positions of decimals, such as misidentifying the hundredths place as the tenths (8/10) or the thousandths (8/1000) place. It may also indicate a failure to understand that the denominator of a decimal fraction is determined by the digit's distance from the decimal point.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If you move a digit one place to the right in a decimal number, how does its value change?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Its value becomes 1/10th of its previous value. Answering "it gets smaller" is too vague; the student must understand the specific power-of-ten relationship between adjacent decimal places.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is 0.50 greater than, less than, or equal to 0.5? Justify your answer using place value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
They are equal because 0.5 is 5/10, and 0.50 is 50/100, which simplifies to 5/10. A student who claims 0.50 is larger is mistakenly treating the decimal part like a whole number (thinking 50 is bigger than 5).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a decimal number where the digit 4 has a value of 0.004 and the digit 7 has a value of 70.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A correct answer is 70.004 or 72.404 (as long as 4 is in the thousandths place and 7 is in the tens place). An incorrect answer reveals the student cannot correctly map the digit to its required fractional or whole-number position.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often treat the digits to the right of the decimal point as whole numbers, believing that 0.45 is larger than 0.6 because "45 is bigger than 6." This happens because they ignore the place value (tenths vs. hundredths) and only look at the face value of the digits.
## ON_FAILURE
Direct the student to draw a Place Value Chart including columns for "Tenths (1/10)" and "Hundredths (1/100)." Ask them to write 0.45 and 0.6 in the chart, adding a zero to 0.6 to make it 0.60. Point out that 60 hundredths is clearly larger than 45 hundredths, correcting the whole-number bias.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper weighs 3.425 kg of sugar. Identify the value of each digit in this decimal.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the decimal point as the separator between whole numbers (left) and parts (right).
- Step 2: The 3 is to the left of the decimal, so it is 3 ones = 3.
- Step 3: The 4 is the first digit to the right, so it is 4 tenths = 0.4 or 4/10.
- Step 4: The 2 is the second digit to the right, so it is 2 hundredths = 0.02 or 2/100.
- Step 5: The 5 is the third digit, so it is 5 thousandths = 0.005 or 5/1000.
REASONING: Breaking the number into its component parts allows us to see the total weight as a sum of its place values. This confirms the position of each digit relative to the whole unit (kg).
ANSWER: 3 ones, 4 tenths, 2 hundredths, 5 thousandths.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Express the value 0.7 + 0.09 + 0.003 as a single decimal number.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Align the decimal points vertically to ensure columns match.
- Step 2: Place 0.7 in the tenths column.
- Step 3: Place 0.09 in the hundredths column.
- Step 4: Place 0.003 in the thousandths column.
- Step 5: Add the values in each column to get 0.793.
REASONING: By lining up the decimal points, we ensure we are adding tenths to tenths, hundredths to hundredths, and so on, preventing place-value errors.
ANSWER: 0.793
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A notebook costs ₹25.50 and a pen costs ₹15.75. Which digit has a higher value in the tenths place?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the digit in the tenths place for both numbers.
- Step 2: For ₹25.50, the tenths digit is 5 (value 0.5).
- Step 3: For ₹15.75, the tenths digit is 7 (value 0.7).
- Step 4: Compare the values: 0.7 > 0.5.
- Step 5: Conclude that the pen has the higher value in the tenths place.
REASONING: We isolate the specific place value requested (tenths) and ignore the others to perform a direct comparison, reinforcing that place value determines the magnitude of the digit.
ANSWER: The pen (7 is greater than 5 in the tenths place).
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you have the number 8.42, can you rewrite it using only tenths and hundredths? What happens if you add a zero to the end (8.420)?
COMPETENCY: Translating and interpreting decimal representations.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Breakdown 8.42 into 8 + 4/10 + 2/100.
- Step 2: Recognize 8.420 as 8 + 4/10 + 2/100 + 0/1000.
- Step 3: Compare values.
ANSWER: 8.42 is the same as 8.420 because the thousandths place is zero, meaning it adds no value. It is 8 ones, 4 tenths, and 2 hundredths.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student claims that 0.125 is smaller than 0.13 because 125 is less than 130. Is this student correct? Explain why or why not using place value.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and justification.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Compare the tenths place (both have 1).
- Step 2: Compare the hundredths place (0.12 vs 0.13).
- Step 3: Determine which is larger based on the hundredths place.
- Step 4: Explain the student's error regarding whole numbers vs decimals.
ANSWER: The student is incorrect. While 0.125 has more digits, we compare place by place: 0.1 = 0.1, but in the hundredths place, 3 is greater than 2. Therefore, 0.13 is greater than 0.125.
DIFFICULTY: hard
