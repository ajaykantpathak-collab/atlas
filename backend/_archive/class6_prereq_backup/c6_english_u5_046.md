## DEFINITION
An acrostic poem is a creative writing form where the first letter of each line, when read vertically from top to bottom, spells out a specific word or phrase related to the poem’s theme. Each line must be a complete phrase or sentence that describes or connects to that topic.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student will be able to construct an original acrostic poem of at least five lines where every line logically relates to the chosen subject word.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the structure and composition of acrostic poems only. It excludes other poetic forms like cinquains, haikus, or found poetry.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever tried to hide a secret message inside your writing? An acrostic poem does exactly that by turning the very first letters of your lines into a hidden label for your topic. To begin, pick a simple word like 'INDIA' or 'BRAVE'. Write each letter of your word vertically down the left side of your page. Then, write a line starting with each letter that describes something about your chosen word. Your key insight is that the vertical word acts as the anchor for all the ideas you write afterward.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What happens when you read the first letter of every line in an acrostic poem vertically?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The first letters spell out the subject word or phrase of the poem. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks the poem is read horizontally or doesn't understand the vertical structure.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you are writing an acrostic poem for the word "DANCE," why must every line relate to movement or rhythm?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Because the lines must describe the topic to create a meaningful poem. A wrong answer shows the student treats it as a random word game rather than a form of creative expression.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Write the first two lines of an acrostic poem using the word "SKY" to describe a sunny day.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
S: Sun shining bright above. K: Keeping the clouds away. A wrong answer often fails to start the line with the required letter or ignores the theme of a "sunny day."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze this poem: "B-Bright sun, R-Rain falls, A-All are happy." Is this a good acrostic poem for "BRAVE"? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
It is not a good acrostic because the lines do not describe being 'BRAVE'. A wrong answer suggests the student only cares about the letters and misses the requirement for thematic consistency.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Does an acrostic poem require the lines to rhyme at the end?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it does not. A wrong answer reveals a common misconception that all poems must rhyme, whereas acrostic poems focus on the vertical spelling constraint.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a 4-line acrostic poem for the word "HOME."
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
H-Happiness lives inside, O-Open doors for friends, M-Many memories made, E-Everything feels safe. A poor answer would lack a theme or fail to use the correct starting letters.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often write the vertical word but then write sentences that have nothing to do with that word, or they repeat the word itself in every line. This happens because they focus entirely on the "puzzle" of the letters rather than the "poetry" of the content. For example, if the word is "TREE," they might write "T-Top, R-Red, E-Eat, E-End" instead of describing a tree.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to state the topic of their poem aloud before they begin writing. Tell them to brainstorm three adjectives and two actions related to that topic first. Then, have them write those ideas on a scratchpad and map them to the letters of their vertical word.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Write an acrostic poem for the word "ART".
STEPS:
- step 1: Define the topic (Art/Creativity).
- step 2: List the letters A-R-T vertically.
- step 3: Think of a phrase starting with A: "Always painting colors".
- step 4: Think of a phrase starting with R: "Really beautiful shapes".
- step 5: Think of a phrase starting with T: "Talent shown by all".
REASONING: Following the vertical structure ensures the word is visible, while the content matches the theme.
ANSWER: Always painting colors / Really beautiful shapes / Talent shown by all.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Write an acrostic poem for "KITE".
STEPS:
- step 1: Define the topic (A flying kite).
- step 2: List the letters K-I-T-E vertically.
- step 3: K: "Kept high in wind".
- step 4: I: "In the blue sky".
- step 5: T: "Tail waving around".
- step 6: E: "Enjoying the breeze".
REASONING: By focusing on the movement and parts of a kite, the poem becomes descriptive rather than just a list of words.
ANSWER: Kept high in wind / In the blue sky / Tail waving around / Enjoying the breeze.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Write a 5-line acrostic for "INDIA" focusing on culture.
STEPS:
- step 1: Topic is Indian culture.
- step 2: Letters: I, N, D, I, A.
- step 3: I: "Incredible land of traditions".
- step 4: N: "Numerous languages spoken".
- step 5: D: "Diverse arts and dances".
- step 6: I: "India is our home".
- step 7: A: "Always united we stand".
REASONING: This combines the structural constraint with the unit's theme of unity.
ANSWER: Incredible land of traditions / Numerous languages spoken / Diverse arts and dances / India is our home / Always united we stand.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you were asked to write an acrostic poem for the word "FRIEND," and you wrote "F-Fish, R-Rabbit, I-Ice, E-Egg, N-Nest, D-Dog," why would this be considered a weak poem?
COMPETENCY: Critical Analysis
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the vertical word (FRIEND).
- step 2: Identify the theme of the lines (Animals/Random objects).
- step 3: Compare the lines to the theme (They don't relate to friendship).
- step 4: Evaluate the poem's quality (Lack of unity).
ANSWER: It is weak because it lacks a common theme related to the word 'FRIEND'. The lines are just random lists of items starting with those letters.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: How would you change the line starting with 'B' in an acrostic for the word 'BRAVE' to make it more descriptive than just "B-Be brave"?
COMPETENCY: Creative Improvement
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand the goal (Improve description).
- step 2: Identify the current line (Be brave).
- step 3: Brainstorm stronger verbs/adjectives (Bold, Battle, Bright).
- step 4: Draft a new, longer phrase (Battle against fear with courage).
- step 5: Check if it starts with 'B'.
ANSWER: A better line would be "Battle against fear with courage." It keeps the 'B' but adds descriptive depth, which is the goal of poetry.
DIFFICULTY: hard
