## DEFINITION
The functions of local government are the specific tasks performed by elected village and urban councils to provide essential services and improve daily life for residents. These include maintaining public facilities, managing resources, and implementing development programs at the grassroots level.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to categorize at least three specific tasks of a Gram Panchayat and explain why these tasks are necessary for the village community.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the basic service-oriented roles of rural and urban local bodies. Excludes complex service delivery logistics, e-governance systems, and public-private partnerships.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you wake up and find the village hand pump is broken and the main road is blocked by mud after a heavy rain—who fixes it? You don't call the President of India for a broken pump; you reach out to your local Gram Panchayat. Begin by identifying three things that make your own neighborhood comfortable, like clean water, paved paths, and street lights. Connect these "comforts" to the responsibility of local representatives who live right there in the community. The key insight is that local government is the closest form of power that solves problems right where we live.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is one primary function of a Gram Panchayat regarding village infrastructure?
(Recall)
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The Gram Panchayat maintains village roads and drains. A student who mentions "laws for the whole country" is confusing the national government with local government.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is it better for a village to have its own Panchayat instead of waiting for the state government to fix every small issue?
(Understand)
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The Panchayat is closer to the people and understands specific local needs faster. A student who says "they have more money than the state" is incorrect; they are just more accessible and specialized in local matters.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If a village wants to start a new sanitation campaign to keep the streets clean, which local body is responsible for implementing it?
(Apply)
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The Gram Panchayat is responsible. If the student names the "police" or "District Collector," they fail to recognize that development schemes are the mandate of the elected local council.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How do the roles of a Gram Panchayat differ from the roles of a higher-level government (like the State or Central government)?
(Analyze)
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Local government focuses on immediate daily needs like water and street lights, while higher levels handle broad policy, national defense, and state-wide laws. A student failing this sees no difference between the scale of governance.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it fair to expect the Gram Panchayat to solve every single problem in a village, such as a major highway construction? Why or why not?
(Evaluate)
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, because the Panchayat has limited resources and jurisdiction; major highways involve state and central planning. A student who says "yes" ignores the reality of administrative limits and resource allocation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
If you were the Sarpanch of a village with no library, what steps would you take to provide one for the children?
(Create)
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The student should suggest: 1) Proposing the idea at a Gram Sabha meeting, 2) Checking available budget, 3) Seeking community support/space, 4) Implementing the plan. A poor answer lacks the democratic process of consulting the community.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think the Sarpanch "owns" the village and decides everything alone. This is incorrect because the Panchayat is a democratic body that must consult the Gram Sabha (all voters).
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that the Sarpanch is an "elected representative," not a ruler. Ask them: "Who gives the Sarpanch their power?" and "What happens in a Gram Sabha meeting?" to reinforce that the people have the ultimate say.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: The village well has dried up. How does the Gram Panchayat resolve this?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the need (water scarcity).
- 2: Consult the Gram Sabha for public opinion.
- 3: Review funds allocated for water management.
- 4: Decide on a solution (e.g., digging a new well or cleaning the old one).
- 5: Implement and monitor the work.
REASONING: This shows the democratic process of identifying a problem and using local authority to solve it.
ANSWER: The Panchayat uses public funds to restore the water supply after discussing the issue with the villagers.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A primary school needs a new roof before the monsoon season.
STEPS:
- 1: Recognize the maintenance responsibility of the school.
- 2: Present the issue to the Panchayat Secretary.
- 3: Include the request in the next development plan.
- 4: Approve the expenditure during a meeting.
- 5: Hire local labor to fix the roof.
REASONING: Maintaining primary education infrastructure is a core function of the local body.
ANSWER: The Panchayat coordinates the repair to ensure student safety during rains.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Two neighbors are fighting over a broken fence; should the Sarpanch decide the outcome alone?
STEPS:
- 1: Analyze if the issue is a criminal or a civil local dispute.
- 2: Recognize the role of the Panchayat in resolving local conflicts.
- 3: Call a meeting of Panchayat members.
- 4: Hear both sides fairly.
- 5: Propose a mediation/solution.
- 6: Document the agreement.
REASONING: The Panchayat acts as a mediator for local harmony.
ANSWER: No, the Sarpanch should not decide alone; they should mediate through the council to maintain fairness.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a village has a high rate of children dropping out of school, what specific initiative could the Gram Panchayat take?
COMPETENCY: Problem Solving
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the root cause (e.g., distance, cost, or lack of interest).
- 2: Discuss with the Gram Sabha.
- 3: Propose a solution like a "Bal Panchayat" or school facility upgrade.
- 4: Convince parents to send children back to school.
ANSWER: The Panchayat can build safer paths, improve midday meals, or hold awareness meetings to encourage parents.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare the role of a Gram Panchayat in a village to the role of a Municipal Corporation in a city regarding waste management.
COMPETENCY: Comparative Analysis
STEPS:
- 1: Define the role in the village (Gram Panchayat).
- 2: Define the role in the city (Municipal Corporation).
- 3: Identify the common goal (cleanliness).
- 4: Identify differences in scale/method (e.g., garbage collection frequency).
- 5: Conclude why both are essential.
ANSWER: Both are local governments responsible for sanitation, but the Municipal Corporation handles larger, denser urban areas with more complex disposal systems, while the Panchayat handles village-level collections.
DIFFICULTY: hard
