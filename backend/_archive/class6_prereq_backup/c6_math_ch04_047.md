## DEFINITION
Multiplying decimals is a process where two decimal numbers are multiplied as whole numbers first, followed by placing the decimal point in the final product based on the total number of decimal places in the original numbers. The number of decimal places in the product is equal to the sum of the decimal places in the two factors.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to multiply two decimal numbers correctly by counting decimal places and placing the decimal point accurately in the product. They should also be able to explain why the decimal point shifts in the final answer.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the multiplication of decimals (e.g., 0.5 x 0.2) and whole numbers by decimals (e.g., 5 x 0.2). It excludes algebraic multiplication, scientific notation, and significant figures.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at the grocery store buying 3.5 kilograms of rice, and the price is ₹42.50 per kilogram. How do you find the total cost? You begin by ignoring the decimal points and multiplying the numbers as if they were whole numbers (35 x 4250). Once you have that result, you count how many digits are behind the decimal points in both 3.5 (one digit) and 42.50 (two digits), making a total of three places. Finally, you move the decimal point in your big answer three spots from the right to the left. The key insight is that the decimal point acts like a counter for how small the pieces you are multiplying actually are.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the first step in multiplying 1.2 by 0.3?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The first step is to multiply the numbers as if they were whole numbers (12 x 3 = 36). If a student says "line up the decimal points," they are confusing multiplication with addition or subtraction, which is a common error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you multiply a number with one decimal place by a number with two decimal places, how many decimal places should the final answer have?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The answer should have three decimal places. If a student answers "one" or "two," they are failing to realize that the decimal places must be added together, not just taken from the largest factor.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate 0.4 x 0.5. Show your steps.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
First, multiply 4 x 5 = 20. Second, count the decimal places: 0.4 has one and 0.5 has one, totaling two. Third, place the point two spots from the right, giving 0.20 or 0.2. A student who answers "2.0" likely ignored the decimal places entirely.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
2 x 0.5 = 1.0, while 2 x 5 = 10. The decimal point makes the multiplier "smaller than one," which reduces the total product significantly compared to multiplying by a whole number. A student who thinks the result should be the same fails to see the decimal as a fraction of a whole.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
[Correct answer] The product of 2 x 0.5 is 1, while the product of 2 x 5 is 10. The decimal point in 0.5 shifts the value to be one-tenth of the whole number 5, causing the final product to be ten times smaller.

A wrong answer reveals that the student likely ignores the place value of the decimal, perhaps treating "0.5" as the whole number "5" or failing to recognize that multiplying by a decimal less than one results in a product smaller than the original whole number.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is 0.1 x 0.1 equal to 0.1? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it is 0.01. Because 0.1 is one-tenth, one-tenth of one-tenth is one-hundredth. A student answering 0.1 is ignoring the "shrinking" effect that occurs when multiplying numbers less than one.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a multiplication problem using two decimals that results in a product of 0.06.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Any pair like 0.2 x 0.3 or 0.6 x 0.1 works. If a student provides 6 x 0.01, they understand the product but missed the instruction to use two decimals; if they provide 0.6 x 0.6, they show a misunderstanding of place value.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to align decimal points vertically as they do in addition. They don't realize that in multiplication, the decimal places "shift" and combine. For example, in 0.2 x 0.3, they might write 0.6 instead of 0.06 because they don't count the total decimal places.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student fails, ask them to write the decimals as fractions first (e.g., 2/10 x 3/10). Show them that 2/10 x 3/10 = 6/100, which is 0.06. This visualizes why the decimal point must move two places instead of one.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A notebook costs ₹22.50. If Rohan buys 4 notebooks, what is the total cost?
STEPS:
- identify given: price = 22.50, quantity = 4.
- method: multiply 2250 x 4 as whole numbers.
- apply: 2250 x 4 = 9000.
- compute: 22.50 has two decimal places, so shift the decimal in 9000 two places left.
- result: 90.00.
REASONING: We treat 22.50 as 2250 hundredths. Multiplying by 4 gives 9000 hundredths, which translates back to 90.00.
ANSWER: ₹90.00
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A ribbon is 0.8 meters long. If you cut 0.5 of it, how long is the piece?
STEPS:
- identify given: 0.8 m and 0.5 part.
- method: multiply 0.8 x 0.5.
- apply: multiply 8 x 5 = 40.
- compute: count decimal places (one in 0.8, one in 0.5 = two total).
- shift: move decimal point two places left in 40 to get 0.40.
REASONING: Multiplying by a decimal smaller than 1 means we are finding a "fraction of a fraction." The result must be smaller than the original number.
ANSWER: 0.40 meters
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A rectangular plot measures 3.2 meters by 2.4 meters. What is the area?
STEPS:
- identify given: length = 3.2 m, breadth = 2.4 m.
- method: area = length x breadth.
- apply: 32 x 24 = 768.
- compute: total decimal places = 1 (from 3.2) + 1 (from 2.4) = 2.
- shift: move decimal point two places left in 768 to get 7.68.
- verify: 3 x 2 = 6, so the answer should be slightly more than 6. 7.68 makes sense.
REASONING: Area requires multiplying two dimensions. Using decimal multiplication allows us to calculate areas of non-whole units accurately.
ANSWER: 7.68 square meters
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Without calculating, determine which product is larger: 0.5 x 0.8 or 0.5 x 0.9. Explain your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- understand: compare the factors.
- method: recognize that 0.5 is constant, so we compare 0.8 and 0.9.
- apply: 0.9 is greater than 0.8.
- interpret: multiplying a positive number by a larger factor results in a larger product.
ANSWER: 0.5 x 0.9 is larger because 0.9 > 0.8, and since the multiplier (0.5) is the same, the larger factor yields the larger product.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student calculated 0.3 x 0.4 = 1.2. Identify the error and provide the correct calculation.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- understand: the student treated the decimals as whole numbers and put the point in the wrong place.
- identify: the student ignored the requirement to add decimal places (1 + 1 = 2).
- correct: 3 x 4 = 12. Move decimal two places left: 0.12.
- verify: 1.2 is 12/10, which is too large; 0.12 is 12/100, which is correct.
ANSWER: The student's error was failing to count the total decimal places. They likely tried to add the decimal points like 0.3 + 0.4. The correct answer is 0.12.
DIFFICULTY: hard
