## DEFINITION
Digestion is the biological process of breaking down large, complex food molecules into smaller, simpler units that the body can absorb and use for energy, growth, and repair. This process happens in a sequential path starting from the mouth and moving through the digestive tract.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to name the major organs of the digestive system in the correct sequence and describe the specific function of each part (mouth, oesophagus, stomach) in preparing food for absorption.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the stages of food movement and mechanical/chemical breakdown in the mouth, oesophagus, and stomach. It excludes specific enzyme chemical formulas, clinical diseases, or plant nutrition.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wondered how a crunchy apple you eat for a snack becomes the energy that helps you run during games? It doesn't just disappear; your body acts like a factory. First, the "machinery" in your mouth grinds the food to increase the surface area for digestion, while saliva begins the chemical breakdown. Then, the food is shaped into a soft ball called a bolus and travels down a tube called the oesophagus using rhythmic muscle movements. Finally, it reaches the stomach to be mixed with acid, starting the next phase of transformation. The key insight is that digestion is a step-by-step assembly line where each organ has a specialized job.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the name of the tube that carries food from the mouth to the stomach?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The tube is called the oesophagus. A student who says "windpipe" or "throat" confuses the digestive pathway with the respiratory system.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is it important for our teeth to grind food into smaller pieces before we swallow it?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Grinding increases the surface area of the food, allowing saliva and digestive juices to work more efficiently. A student who answers "to make it taste better" or "to make it fit in the throat" misses the scientific purpose of mechanical breakdown.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If you were to drink a glass of water, how does the body move it down to the stomach even if you were standing on your head?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The body uses rhythmic muscular contractions (peristalsis) to push food and liquid down the oesophagus. A student who thinks it just "falls" due to gravity ignores the muscular nature of the digestive tract.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the role of the mouth and the stomach in digestion. How are their functions different?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The mouth performs mechanical grinding and starts sugar breakdown, while the stomach acts as a mixing chamber where food is churned with acid. A student who says they both do the same thing fails to recognize the progression of the process.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the statement: "Digestion is only about making food small enough to swallow." Why is this incomplete?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
This is incomplete because digestion also involves chemical transformation into absorbable units, not just physical size reduction. A student focusing only on swallowing ignores the chemical changes required for the body to actually use the nutrients.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Imagine a food item that is not chewed properly. Based on what you know about digestion, what might happen to the effectiveness of the stomach's work?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The stomach will have to work much harder to break down the large chunks, which might lead to incomplete digestion or discomfort. A good answer explains that the stomach's "mixing" is less effective on large pieces than on smaller ones.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think digestion happens all at once in the stomach. They ignore the mouth's role in the chemical breakdown of starch and the importance of the oesophagus as an active transport tube, assuming food just "drops" into the stomach.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to trace the path of a bite of chapati using a diagram. Point out that the mouth is the "first station" where chemical action starts, not just a place to chew. Have them explain what saliva does to starches compared to what the stomach does to mix food with acid, reinforcing that every step has a specific chemical purpose.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student eats a piece of bread. Trace the state of the bread from the moment it enters the mouth until it leaves the stomach.
STEPS:
- Step 1: In the mouth, teeth grind bread into small particles.
- Step 2: Saliva chemically begins breaking down starch in the bread.
- Step 3: The tongue shapes it into a bolus for easy swallowing.
- Step 4: The oesophagus uses rhythmic contractions to move the bolus down.
- Step 5: The stomach receives the bolus and mixes it with acid to continue digestion.
REASONING: Digestion is a sequence; each step prepares the food for the next station.
ANSWER: The bread changes from a solid piece to a ground mixture, then a swallowed bolus, and finally a stomach-mixed acidic fluid.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Compare the work done by teeth vs. the work done by the stomach on a piece of potato.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Teeth perform mechanical action (grinding).
- Step 2: Stomach performs mechanical action (churning) and chemical action (acid).
- Step 3: Teeth prepare the surface area for saliva.
- Step 4: Stomach prepares the food for the small intestine.
REASONING: Mechanical action is consistent across the tract, but chemical environments differ.
ANSWER: Teeth perform physical breakdown to increase surface area, while the stomach combines physical churning with chemical acid treatment.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Why do marathon runners need simple sugars (glucose) instead of complex foods like pulses right before a race?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that complex foods (proteins/starches) require a long digestion time.
- Step 2: Identify that glucose is already a small, absorbable unit.
- Step 3: Conclude that glucose skips the "mouth to stomach" breakdown time.
- Step 4: Explain that this provides instant energy.
REASONING: The digestive "factory" takes time to break down complex molecules; skipping these steps allows for immediate fuel.
ANSWER: Glucose is preferred because it requires little to no digestion, providing immediate energy compared to complex foods.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a person has a condition where their oesophagus muscles do not contract properly, what would be the primary difficulty they face?
COMPETENCY: Predicting consequences of system failure.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recognize that the oesophagus uses rhythmic contractions to move food.
- Step 2: Determine that lack of contraction means food cannot reach the stomach.
- Step 3: Conclude that the person would have difficulty swallowing or getting food to the digestion site.
ANSWER: The person would likely experience difficulty swallowing or the sensation of food being "stuck" because the muscles cannot push the bolus down to the stomach.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A scientist creates a machine that mimics the stomach. It has a container, a stirring rod, and acid. Which part of the human digestion system is this machine missing, and why is that missing part crucial?
COMPETENCY: Analyzing system components.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that the machine has a container (stomach) and stirring (churning).
- Step 2: Note the missing mouth and oesophagus.
- Step 3: Explain the mouth's role in surface area increase and starch breakdown.
- Step 4: Explain that without these, the machine would have to work much harder to break down large food items.
ANSWER: It is missing the mouth. The mouth is crucial because it physically grinds food to increase surface area, allowing the stomach's acid to work faster and more effectively.
DIFFICULTY: hard
