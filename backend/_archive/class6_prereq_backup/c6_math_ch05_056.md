## DEFINITION
A linear equation in one variable is a mathematical statement of equality containing exactly one unknown value (variable) where the variable has a power of 1. It is represented in the form ax + b = 0, where 'a' and 'b' are known numbers and 'x' is the unknown we need to find.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to isolate the variable using inverse operations on both sides of the equation and verify their result by substituting it back into the original expression.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers solving equations of the form ax + b = c (linear, one variable). It excludes systems of equations, quadratic equations, and algebraic inequalities.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you have a mystery bag of marbles. You know there are 3 extra marbles outside, and the total number of marbles you have is 7. You can write this as "the mystery bag (x) plus 3 equals 7" (x + 3 = 7). To find the mystery, you need to "undo" the +3 by doing the opposite: subtracting 3 from both sides. When you remove 3 from the total (7 - 3), you are left with 4, so the bag must have 4 marbles. The key insight is that an equation is like a balanced scale; whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other to keep it balanced.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the variable in the equation 5x - 2 = 8?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The variable is 'x'. If a student answers "5" or "8", they are confusing the coefficient (the number multiplying the variable) or the constant with the unknown letter itself.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If x + 5 = 12, why do we subtract 5 from both sides to find x?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
We subtract 5 to isolate 'x' by creating a zero pair (+5 - 5 = 0). If a student says "to get the answer", they lack the conceptual understanding of inverse operations needed to solve more complex equations.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Solve for x: 3x = 15.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Divide both sides by 3: 3x/3 = 15/3, so x = 5. A common error is adding or subtracting instead of dividing, which reveals a misunderstanding of the operation connecting the coefficient (3) and the variable (x).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Given 2x + 4 = 10, compare the effect of adding 2 to both sides versus subtracting 4 from both sides. Which helps us find x?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Subtracting 4 helps us isolate the term with x; adding 2 does not bring us closer to x. Students who think both are "correct" steps don't understand the goal of isolating the variable.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Your friend says that for 4x - 6 = 10, the first step should be to divide by 4. Is this the most efficient way? Explain.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It is not the most efficient; while mathematically possible, it leads to fractions (x - 1.5 = 2.5). Adding 6 first is better. A student who says "it's wrong" lacks the flexibility to see multiple paths to a solution.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create your own "mystery bag" word problem that results in the equation 2x + 5 = 15.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An example would be: "I have 2 identical bags of apples and 5 loose apples. In total, I have 15 apples. How many apples are in one bag?" A poor answer would just be a list of numbers without a real-world scenario.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often forget to perform the same operation on both sides of the equation. For example, in 3x + 2 = 8, they might subtract 2 from the left side but forget to subtract it from the 8, resulting in 3x = 6 instead of 3x = 6. This happens because they view the equals sign as a "go to the answer" arrow rather than a balance scale.
## ON_FAILURE
Draw a physical balance scale on paper. Place the equation on the scale. Ask the student, "If I take 2kg off the left pan, what happens to the balance if I don't take 2kg off the right pan?" Show them that they must subtract from both sides to keep the scale level, reinforcing that the equals sign is a point of symmetry.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A tailor has 5 metres of cloth. He uses some cloth to make 2 identical shirts and has 1 metre left over. How much cloth was used for one shirt?
STEPS:
- identify: 2 shirts (2x) + 1 metre leftover = 5 total metres. Equation: 2x + 1 = 5.
- method: Subtract 1 from both sides to isolate the 2x term.
- compute: 2x + 1 - 1 = 5 - 1 => 2x = 4.
- compute: Divide both sides by 2 to find x => 2x/2 = 4/2 => x = 2.
- verify: 2(2) + 1 = 4 + 1 = 5. It matches.
REASONING: We must remove the extra constant (1m) first to see how much cloth was used for the shirts alone. Then, dividing by 2 shares that amount equally between the two shirts.
ANSWER: 2 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Reena has 3 packets of biscuits and 4 extra loose biscuits. If she has 25 biscuits in total, how many are in one packet?
STEPS:
- identify: 3 packets (3x) + 4 extra = 25 total. Equation: 3x + 4 = 25.
- method: Subtract 4 from both sides.
- compute: 3x + 4 - 4 = 25 - 4 => 3x = 21.
- compute: Divide by 3 to isolate x => 3x/3 = 21/3 => x = 7.
- verify: 3(7) + 4 = 21 + 4 = 25. Correct.
REASONING: We isolate the variable by reversing the addition first, then reversing the multiplication. This balances the equation at every step.
ANSWER: 7 biscuits
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A bus driver starts with some passengers (x). At the first stop, 10 people get off, and 20 people get on. Now there are 45 people. How many started?
STEPS:
- identify: x - 10 + 20 = 45. Simplify: x + 10 = 45.
- method: Subtract 10 from both sides.
- compute: x + 10 - 10 = 45 - 10.
- compute: x = 35.
- verify: 35 - 10 = 25; 25 + 20 = 45. Correct.
REASONING: We simplify the expression first (combining like terms) to transform it into our standard form (ax + b = c). This makes solving much easier.
ANSWER: 35 passengers
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are given 4x + 8 = 20. A student solves it by writing 4x = 20 + 8. Is this correct? Why or why not?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- understand: The student added 8 instead of subtracting 8 to move it.
- method: Identify the inverse operation of addition.
- solve: The correct step is 4x = 20 - 8 = 12.
- explain: The student failed to apply the inverse operation.
ANSWER: No, it is incorrect. The student should have subtracted 8 from both sides because the inverse of adding 8 is subtracting 8. They treated the 8 like it had a negative sign, which is a common error.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: If you have the equation 3x + 5 = 20 and you want to find the value of x, can you multiply everything by 2 first? What would the equation look like? Is it easier to solve?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- understand: Apply multiplication property of equality to the whole equation.
- apply: 2(3x + 5) = 2(20) => 6x + 10 = 40.
- solve: 6x = 30 => x = 5.
- compare: It is not easier because the numbers become larger and the steps remain the same.
ANSWER: Yes, you can multiply both sides by 2, resulting in 6x + 10 = 40. However, it is not easier to solve because the numbers are larger, and you still have to perform the same number of steps to isolate x. It is generally better to simplify before complicating.
DIFFICULTY: hard
