## DEFINITION
The absolute value of an integer is its distance from zero on the number line, expressed as a non-negative value regardless of whether the integer is positive or negative. It is denoted by vertical bars: |a|.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to convert any integer into its absolute value and explain that this value represents a distance, which is always neutral or positive. They should correctly identify that |5| = 5 and |-5| = 5.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the absolute value of integers, the definition of distance, and the use of vertical bars. It excludes absolute value equations, the triangle inequality, and the absolute value of real numbers or fractions.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are in a lift in "Bela’s Building of Fun." Whether you go 3 floors up to the Book Store (+3) or 3 floors down to the Video Games shop (-3), the actual distance you have traveled from the ground floor (0) is exactly 3 floors. We use the absolute value to describe this "how far" distance, ignoring the "up" or "down" direction. To find the absolute value, simply remove the negative sign if one exists; if the number is already positive or zero, it stays the same. Think of it as a "distance-only" measurement that never reports a negative distance.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the absolute value of -12?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The absolute value is 12. A student who answers -12 likely forgets that absolute value measures distance, which cannot be negative, and is simply stripping the bars without changing the value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If |x| = 5, does x have to be 5? Explain why or why not.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, x could be 5 or -5. A student who says "yes" does not understand that both 5 and -5 are exactly 5 units away from zero on the number line.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A diver is at 8 metres below sea level, and a bird is flying 8 metres above sea level. Represent their distances from sea level using absolute value notation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The diver is at |-8| = 8 metres, and the bird is at |8| = 8 metres. A student who writes |-8| = -8 is still struggling to accept that distance is always non-negative regardless of the starting position.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare |-10| and |4|. Which is greater?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
|-10| is 10 and |4| is 4; therefore, |-10| > |4|. A student who thinks |-10| < |4| is comparing the original integers (-10 < 4) rather than their absolute values.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Can the absolute value of an integer ever be negative? Justify your answer using the concept of the number line.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, because the absolute value measures distance from zero. Since distance is a measure of "how far" something is, it must be zero (if at the origin) or positive (if moved in any direction). A student who says "yes" is confusing the sign of the integer with the result of the absolute value function.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a scenario involving money (credits and debits) where two different amounts result in the same absolute value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A credit of ₹50 (+50) and a debit of ₹50 (-50) both have an absolute value of 50. A poor answer would use numbers with different values, showing a failure to grasp that absolute value focuses on the magnitude of the change, not the direction (credit vs debit).
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that vertical bars act like a "negative" sign, turning positive numbers into negative ones. For example, they might think |5| = -5. This happens because they see the bars and feel forced to "do something" to the number, often confusing this with the rule for multiplying by a negative sign.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to draw a number line and mark 0, 5, and -5. Ask them to count the steps from 0 to 5, and then from 0 to -5. Remind them that "distance" is just a count of steps, and since you cannot take a negative number of steps, the answer must be positive.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A merchant records his transactions: a profit of ₹200 and a loss of ₹200. What is the absolute value of these transactions?
STEPS:
- Identify the integers: +200 (profit) and -200 (loss).
- Identify the goal: Find the absolute value of each.
- Apply the rule: |200| = 200 and |-200| = 200.
- Interpret: Both transactions have a magnitude of 200.
REASONING: The absolute value shows that the "size" of the profit and the loss is identical, even though one is a gain and one is a debt.
ANSWER: 200
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Compare the distances of two points from zero: -15 and +12.
STEPS:
- Find the absolute value of the first point: |-15| = 15.
- Find the absolute value of the second point: |12| = 12.
- Compare the two results: 15 > 12.
- Conclude: |-15| is greater than |12|.
REASONING: We convert the positions into distances from the origin (0) to see which point is further away.
ANSWER: |-15| > |12|
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A submarine is at -500m and a research drone is at -300m. Which is further from the surface (0m)?
STEPS:
- Represent the submarine position: -500.
- Represent the drone position: -300.
- Calculate distance from surface: |-500| = 500m and |-300| = 300m.
- Compare: 500 > 300.
- Determine the result: The submarine is further from the surface.
REASONING: Because the submarine's absolute value (500) is greater than the drone's (300), it has traveled a greater distance from the reference point.
ANSWER: The submarine is further (500m vs 300m).
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you are at Floor -7 in the Building of Fun and your friend is at Floor +7, explain why you both are the same distance from the Welcome Hall (Floor 0).
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions.
STEPS:
- Define the Welcome Hall as 0.
- Determine your distance: |-7| = 7.
- Determine your friend's distance: |7| = 7.
- Compare: Since 7 = 7, the distances are equal.
ANSWER: Both are 7 units away because absolute value measures distance, and distance does not depend on whether you are above or below ground.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two integers, A and B, have absolute values |A| = 10 and |B| = 10. Are A and B equal? Provide two possible pairs for A and B.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- Recognize that absolute value ignores the sign.
- Determine that A could be 10 or -10.
- Determine that B could be 10 or -10.
- Conclude: A and B are not necessarily equal.
ANSWER: No, A and B are not necessarily equal. Pair 1: A=10, B=10. Pair 2: A=-10, B=10.
DIFFICULTY: hard
