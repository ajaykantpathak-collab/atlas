## DEFINITION
The mean (or average) is a single value that represents the central, typical point of a data set. It is found by calculating the total of all values and dividing that sum by the number of individual items in the set.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student will be able to calculate the mean of a given data set and explain that the mean represents a "balanced" or "typical" value for that group.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the arithmetic mean of a data set. It does not cover weighted mean, median, mode, or standard deviation.
## WALKTHROUGH
If your friends each have a different number of stickers, how can you describe the "typical" amount each person has without listing every number? To find this, you first combine all the stickers into one big pile (the sum) and then share them equally among your friends (the count). This "fair share" value is what we call the mean. When you are stuck, always remember: "Add them up, then divide by how many there are."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the formula for calculating the mean of a set of numbers?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The mean is the sum of all values divided by the number of values. A wrong answer here usually implies the student is confusing "mean" with "total," which indicates they missed the division step.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If five students have 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10 rupees, is the mean 6? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Yes, because (2+4+6+8+10) = 30, and 30 divided by 5 is 6. If a student says "no," they might be struggling with the addition or the division, revealing a need to practice multi-step arithmetic.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A cricket player scores 0, 50, and 100 runs in three matches. Calculate the mean score.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
(0 + 50 + 100) = 150. Then 150 / 3 = 50 runs. A wrong answer might happen if the student ignores the '0' as a value, which is a common error in data handling.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If a student has a mean of 80 marks across 4 subjects, what is the total of their marks?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Total = 320. The student multiplies the mean (80) by the count (4). An incorrect answer shows they do not understand the relationship between the mean and the sum.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Does adding a very large value (an outlier) to a data set always change the mean? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, it increases the sum significantly, which raises the mean. If a student says "no," they don't yet understand that the mean is sensitive to extreme values.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a set of 4 numbers that has a mean of 10. Can you create a different set that also has a mean of 10?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Any four numbers that add up to 40 (e.g., 5, 5, 15, 15 or 10, 10, 10, 10). A poor answer suggests the student hasn't grasped that multiple combinations can lead to the same "fair share."
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often ignore zeros in a data set when calculating the mean (e.g., thinking 0, 5, 10 mean = 15/2 instead of 15/3). They treat '0' as "no value" rather than a valid data point that affects the count.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student fails to include zero, ask them: "If you played 3 games and scored 0, 5, and 10, did you play 3 games or 2?" Guide them to count the total number of items, including zeros, as they represent actual observations (or matches played). Have them list the count explicitly before dividing.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Four children have weights of 30kg, 35kg, 40kg, and 35kg. Find the mean weight.
STEPS:
- Identify values: 30, 35, 40, 35. Count = 4.
- Sum the values: 30 + 35 + 40 + 35 = 140.
- Divide by the count: 140 / 4.
- Calculate: 140 / 4 = 35.
- Interpret: The typical weight is 35kg.
REASONING: By adding them together, we find the total weight of the group; dividing by the number of children ensures we find the value if everyone weighed the same.
ANSWER: 35 kg
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In 5 matches, a team scores 20, 0, 45, 10, and 25 runs. Find the mean.
STEPS:
- Identify values: 20, 0, 45, 10, 25. Count = 5.
- Sum the values: 20 + 0 + 45 + 10 + 25 = 100.
- Divide by count: 100 / 5.
- Calculate: 100 / 5 = 20.
- Interpret: The mean performance is 20 runs per match.
REASONING: Including the zero is crucial because it was a match played where the team scored nothing, which impacts the "typical" average.
ANSWER: 20 runs
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student has 3 math scores with a mean of 90. If they add a 4th score of 70, what is the new mean?
STEPS:
- Find the sum of the first 3: 90 * 3 = 270.
- Add the new score: 270 + 70 = 340.
- Identify new count: 3 + 1 = 4.
- Calculate the new mean: 340 / 4.
- Compute: 85.
REASONING: We must calculate the total sum before we can determine the new average, as the mean depends on the total volume of data.
ANSWER: 85 marks
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Imagine two classes. Class A has 5 students with marks 10, 10, 10, 10, 10. Class B has 5 students with marks 2, 8, 10, 12, 18. Both have a mean of 10. Are the classes identical in performance? Explain.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- Calculate means for both (both are 10).
- Compare the spread of numbers.
- Explain that mean only shows the "center," not how spread out the marks are.
ANSWER: No, they are not identical. While the mean is 10, Class A is very consistent, while Class B has a large range of scores. The mean hides these differences.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Your friend calculates the mean of 5, 10, and 15 as (5+10+15) / 2 = 15. What is wrong with their logic and how do you fix it?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction.
STEPS:
- Count the actual numbers in the set (there are 3).
- Identify the error (they divided by 2 instead of 3).
- Calculate the correct sum (30).
- Divide by the correct count (3).
ANSWER: The friend used 2 as the count, but there are 3 numbers. The correct mean is 30 / 3 = 10.
DIFFICULTY: easy
