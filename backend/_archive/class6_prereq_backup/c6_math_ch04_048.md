## DEFINITION
Division of decimals is the process of splitting a decimal number into equal parts, performed by adjusting the divisor into a whole number before dividing. It is essentially scaling both the dividend and divisor by powers of ten to simplify the operation.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to convert any decimal divisor into a whole number by multiplying both the dividend and divisor by 10, 100, or 1000, and then correctly perform the long division to find the quotient.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers dividing a decimal by a decimal and a decimal by a whole number. It excludes repeating decimals, significant figure rules, and algebraic expressions involving variables.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you have 4.5 metres of ribbon and you want to cut it into small pieces that are each 0.5 metres long. How many pieces can you get? To solve this, it is hard to divide by a decimal, so we "get rid" of the point. By multiplying both 4.5 and 0.5 by 10, we turn the problem into 45 ÷ 5, which is much easier to solve! Just remember: whatever you do to the divisor to make it a whole number, you must do the exact same to the dividend to keep the balance.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the first step to take when dividing 6.25 by 0.25?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The first step is to multiply both 6.25 and 0.25 by 100 to make the divisor a whole number (25). A student who simply divides 6.25 by 0.25 without moving the decimal point is likely to place the decimal in the wrong position or become confused by the divisor.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you multiply the divisor by 10, why must you also multiply the dividend by 10?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Multiplying both numbers by 10 is equivalent to multiplying the entire fraction by 10/10, which is equal to 1. This ensures the value of the quotient remains unchanged. If a student only multiplies the divisor, they are changing the value of the problem entirely.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate: 12.6 ÷ 0.6.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Multiply both by 10 to get 126 ÷ 6. Step 2: Divide 126 by 6. Step 3: 12 ÷ 6 = 2, and 6 ÷ 6 = 1. Answer: 21. A student who gets 2.1 or 0.21 has likely failed to move the decimal point correctly in the dividend.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
8.4 ÷ 2 = 4.2, while 8.4 ÷ 0.2 = 42. The second quotient is larger because you are dividing by a smaller fraction, which means the number "fits" into the dividend more times. Students who think the second answer should be smaller than 4.2 are confusing division with multiplication.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
**Correct Answer:** Dividing 8.4 by 2 gives a quotient of 4.2, while dividing 8.4 by 0.2 gives a quotient of 42. The quotients are different because when you divide by a number less than 1, you are essentially finding how many times a fraction fits into the whole, which results in a larger value than the original dividend.

**A wrong answer reveals** a misunderstanding of the relationship between the divisor and the quotient, specifically the misconception that division always results in a smaller number. The student likely assumes that because 0.2 is smaller than 2, the final answer must also be smaller, failing to recognize that dividing by a decimal represents partitioning the number into smaller, more numerous parts.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is 0.5 ÷ 0.1 the same as 5 ÷ 1? Defend your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, both equal 5. Multiplying both 0.5 and 0.1 by 10 transforms the problem into 5 ÷ 1, preserving the ratio. A student who says "no" does not understand that scaling both terms by the same factor maintains the division's result.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a division problem involving decimals that results in a whole number quotient of 8.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Any problem following the pattern (8 × x) ÷ x = 8 works, such as 1.6 ÷ 0.2. A poor answer would be a problem that does not result in 8, showing a lack of understanding of the relationship between divisors and dividends.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to "move the decimal" only in the dividend or forget to move it in the dividend after moving it in the divisor. For example, in 4.5 ÷ 0.5, they might incorrectly calculate 4.5 ÷ 5 = 0.9. They treat the decimal point as a static object rather than a indicator of place value that must be shifted consistently.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student is stuck, have them write the division as a fraction: 4.5 / 0.5. Ask them, "What can we multiply the denominator by to make it a whole number?" Once they say 10, tell them, "Since a fraction is like a balance scale, you must multiply the top by 10 too." This visual representation prevents them from forgetting to shift the dividend's decimal.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A container has 7.5 litres of milk. You want to pour it into bottles that hold 0.25 litres each. How many bottles do you need?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify dividend (7.5) and divisor (0.25).
- step 2: Multiply both by 100 to shift decimals: 7.5 becomes 750, 0.25 becomes 25.
- step 3: Set up the division: 750 ÷ 25.
- step 4: Divide: 75 ÷ 25 = 3, so 750 ÷ 25 = 30.
- step 5: Verify: 30 bottles × 0.25 litres = 7.5 litres.
REASONING: By scaling both by 100, we removed the decimals to use standard long division. This keeps the total quantity of milk and bottle capacity in the same ratio.
ANSWER: 30 bottles
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A piece of wire is 9.6 metres long. If you cut it into 3 equal pieces, how long is each piece?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify dividend (9.6) and divisor (3).
- step 2: Since the divisor is already a whole number, no scaling is needed.
- step 3: Place the decimal point in the quotient directly above the dividend's decimal point.
- step 4: Divide 9.6 by 3: 9 ÷ 3 = 3, 6 ÷ 3 = 2.
- step 5: Result is 3.2.
REASONING: Dividing a decimal by a whole number does not require moving decimals; we simply treat the decimal point as a fixed marker in the quotient.
ANSWER: 3.2 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: You have ₹15.50 and each pen costs ₹2.50. How many pens can you buy?
STEPS:
- step 1: Write as 15.50 ÷ 2.50.
- step 2: Multiply both by 100 to get 1550 ÷ 250.
- step 3: Simplify by dividing both by 10 to get 155 ÷ 25.
- step 4: Calculate: 155 ÷ 25 = 6 with a remainder of 5.
- step 5: 5/25 = 0.2, so 6.2.
- step 6: Since you cannot buy 0.2 of a pen, you can buy 6 pens.
REASONING: The decimal division gives the exact mathematical result, but the context requires us to interpret if we need a whole number (pens) or a decimal (litres).
ANSWER: 6 pens
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you divide 12.4 by 0.4, you get 31. Without calculating, predict what happens if you divide 12.4 by 0.04. Will the result be larger or smaller? Explain.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the divisor change (0.4 to 0.04).
- step 2: Recognize that 0.04 is 10 times smaller than 0.4.
- step 3: Apply the rule: dividing by a smaller number yields a larger quotient.
- step 4: Conclude result is 10 times larger (310).
ANSWER: The result will be larger (310) because dividing by a smaller number means it fits into the dividend more times.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student solved 4.8 ÷ 0.2 by moving the decimal in the divisor but forgetting to move it in the dividend, getting 0.24. How would you correct their work?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the student's error (only shifted divisor).
- step 2: Explain the "balance" rule: multiply both by 10.
- step 3: Show the corrected operation: 48 ÷ 2.
- step 4: Compute correct result (24).
ANSWER: You must multiply both 4.8 and 0.2 by 10 to keep the division balanced, turning it into 48 ÷ 2 = 24. The student's answer of 0.24 is incorrect because they changed the value of the divisor without adjusting the dividend.
DIFFICULTY: hard
