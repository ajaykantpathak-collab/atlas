## DEFINITION
Voice explains the relationship between the doer of an action (the subject) and the action itself. In Active Voice, the subject performs the action, while in Passive Voice, the subject receives the action.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to correctly identify whether a given sentence is active or passive and explain who is performing the action in that sentence.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the identification and conceptual difference between active and passive voice. It excludes tense changes, formal transformation rules, and middle voice.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever noticed that saying "I ate the mango" sounds different from saying "The mango was eaten by me"? In the first, you are the star of the sentence—you are the doer. In the second, the mango is the focus, and it is just sitting there waiting to be eaten. To begin, always look for the subject (the person or thing doing the main job). If the subject is the one doing the action, it is active. If the subject is just "receiving" the action, it is passive. Remember: Active = Actor First; Passive = Receiver First.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
In the sentence "The artist painted a picture," who is performing the action?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The artist is performing the action. A wrong answer suggests the student is confused about identifying the "doer" versus the "thing being acted upon" in a sentence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do we say the sentence "The border was defended by the soldiers" is in the passive voice?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It is passive because the subject ("The border") is not doing the defending; it is receiving the action of being defended. A wrong answer indicates the student thinks any sentence with a long phrase at the end is passive, regardless of who does the work.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Identify if the following sentence is active or passive: "The teacher told a story to the students."
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Active. The steps are: 1) Identify the subject (The teacher). 2) Identify the verb (told). 3) Ask, "Did the teacher tell the story?" Yes. Since the subject does the action, it is active.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If you change a sentence from "The women made beautiful patterns" to "Beautiful patterns were made by the women," what has changed about the focus of the sentence?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The focus shifts from the women (the creators) to the patterns (the creation). A wrong answer shows the student does not understand that voice changes the emphasis of a sentence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it always better to use active voice? Explain your judgment.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Not necessarily. Active voice is better for clarity and energy, but passive voice is useful when we want to highlight the object or when the doer is less important than the result. A wrong answer shows a lack of understanding that both have specific purposes in writing.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create one active voice sentence about a student drawing a picture. Then, rewrite the focus so the picture becomes the subject.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Active: "The student draws a picture." Passive: "The picture is drawn by the student." A poor answer fails to shift the subject or incorrectly attempts a complex transformation.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that any sentence with the word "by" is automatically passive, or that passive voice is "wrong." This happens because they focus on the "by" marker rather than looking at whether the subject is performing the action. For example, "I sat by the river" has "by," but it is NOT passive because the subject (I) is performing the action (sat).
## ON_FAILURE
1. Ask the student to circle the subject. 2. Ask the student to identify the action verb. 3. Ask: "Is the subject doing this action, or is the action happening to them?" 4. If they are still stuck, use the "Actor vs. Receiver" chart to map the sentence components.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Analyze the sentence "The soldiers defended the border."
STEPS:
- Identify the subject: "The soldiers."
- Identify the action: "defended."
- Ask: "Who defended the border?" Answer: The soldiers.
- Determine the relationship: Since the soldiers are the ones doing the defending, the sentence is Active.
- Final Result: Active Voice.
REASONING: By mapping the subject to the action, we confirm the subject is the actor.
ANSWER: The sentence is in the Active Voice because the soldiers perform the action.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Analyze the sentence "The border was defended by the soldiers."
STEPS:
- Identify the subject: "The border."
- Identify the action: "was defended."
- Ask: "Did the border defend itself?" Answer: No.
- Determine the relationship: The action of defending is happening to the border.
- Final Result: Passive Voice.
REASONING: The subject is the recipient of the action, not the initiator.
ANSWER: The sentence is in the Passive Voice because the border receives the action.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Compare "The women wove the cloth" vs "The cloth was woven by the women."
STEPS:
- Identify the actor in both: The women.
- Identify the receiver in both: The cloth.
- Check sentence 1: Subject (Women) is the actor -> Active.
- Check sentence 2: Subject (Cloth) is the receiver -> Passive.
- Interpret the difference: Sentence 1 highlights the women; Sentence 2 highlights the cloth.
REASONING: Comparing both shows how the subject determines the voice.
ANSWER: The first is active (actor-focused), and the second is passive (receiver-focused).
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: In a news report about a disaster, why might a reporter use the passive voice: "The houses were destroyed by the storm"?
COMPETENCY: Application of voice for specific purposes.
STEPS:
- Identify the subject: The houses.
- Identify the action: Were destroyed.
- Analyze the intent: Is the storm or the damage more important?
- Conclusion: The focus is on the damage (the houses), not the storm.
ANSWER: The reporter uses passive voice to emphasize the destruction of the houses (the result) rather than the storm itself.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: If you are writing a biography about Ila Sachani, and you want to focus on her talent rather than her hardships, how would you rewrite "Hardships challenged Ila" into a passive construction?
COMPETENCY: Syntactic control for emphasis.
STEPS:
- Identify the object: Ila.
- Identify the verb: Challenged.
- Identify the agent: Hardships.
- Shift the structure: Move the object to the subject position.
- Rewrite: "Ila was challenged by hardships."
ANSWER: "Ila was challenged by hardships." This shifts the focus onto Ila (the subject of the biography) rather than starting with the negative concept of "hardships."
