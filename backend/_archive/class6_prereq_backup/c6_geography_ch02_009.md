## DEFINITION
A thermometer is a scientific instrument designed to measure the degree of hotness or coldness of the air, known as temperature. It operates on the physical principle that liquids inside a sealed glass tube expand when heated and contract when cooled.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify a thermometer's main components and explain, in their own words, how liquid movement corresponds to temperature changes. They must also be able to read a basic temperature scale on a provided diagram.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers liquid-in-glass thermometers and the relationship between liquid volume and temperature. It does not cover digital thermometers, infrared sensors, or complex unit conversion formulas.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever noticed how your mother checks if you have a fever by using a device, or how you feel a difference between the air in the morning and at noon? To measure exactly how "hot" or "cold" it is, we use a thermometer. Start by imagining a thin glass straw filled with a tiny bit of red liquid or silver mercury. When the air outside gets warmer, the liquid inside the straw gets "excited" and starts climbing higher; when it gets colder, the liquid shrinks back down. By looking at the numbers marked next to the tube, we can give that feeling a specific value. The key insight is that the liquid is a messenger: its height tells us the temperature of the surrounding air.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the primary substance inside the tube of a standard thermometer that allows it to work?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
It uses a liquid like mercury or alcohol. A wrong answer suggests the student hasn't grasped the physical mechanism of expansion, perhaps confusing it with a digital screen.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you take a thermometer out of a cold room and put it into the hot sun, what happens to the liquid inside the tube?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The liquid expands and rises up the tube. If a student says it "contracts," they have reversed the basic principle of thermal expansion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
You are looking at a thermometer where the liquid level is at the mark '20'. If the temperature drops, will the liquid level go above or below the 20 mark?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
It will go below the 20 mark because cold causes contraction. A wrong answer shows a failure to visualize the relationship between liquid volume and temperature.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why is it necessary for the glass tube of a thermometer to have a "narrow bore" (a very thin opening)?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
A narrow bore makes it easier to see small changes in the volume of the liquid. If the tube were too wide, the liquid would barely move, making it hard to measure precise changes.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Could a thermometer work if the glass tube were open at the top?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, the liquid would evaporate or spill out, and the pressure changes would make it inaccurate. This tests the understanding that a "sealed" environment is crucial for consistent measurement.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
If you were to design a simple scale for a thermometer, what two "anchor points" would you choose to mark the tube?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The freezing point of water and the boiling point of water. This shows an understanding of using fixed physical states to create a reliable measurement scale.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe the liquid "gets hot" and therefore moves. The misconception is that the liquid is reacting to its own heat, rather than expanding because of the energy transfer from the environment. They fail to distinguish between the object being measured (the air) and the mechanism of the tool (the liquid expansion).
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student: "Does the liquid move because it is hot, or does it move because the heat forces it to take up more space?" Use a balloon example: if you trap air in a balloon and heat it, the air takes up more space and the balloon stretches. Explain that the liquid in the thermometer is just like the air in the balloon—it needs more room when it gets warm.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A thermometer is placed in a bowl of ice-cold water, and the liquid stands at 5 units. It is then moved to a bowl of warm water, and the liquid rises to 15 units. How much did the liquid expand?
STEPS:
- Identify initial level: 5 units.
- Identify final level: 15 units.
- Calculate the difference: 15 - 5 = 10 units.
- Interpret: The liquid expanded by 10 units due to the heat in the warm water.
REASONING: Subtraction helps quantify the physical change in the liquid's volume, which represents the change in temperature.
ANSWER: The liquid expanded by 10 units.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: If a thermometer's liquid moves 2 units for every 5 degrees of temperature change, how many units will it move if the temperature increases by 15 degrees?
STEPS:
- Determine the ratio: 2 units / 5 degrees.
- Find the multiplier: 15 degrees / 5 degrees = 3.
- Apply the multiplier: 3 * 2 units = 6 units.
- Verify: Since 15 is 3 times 5, the movement should be 3 times 2.
REASONING: Proportional reasoning allows us to predict the thermometer's behavior based on observed patterns.
ANSWER: The liquid will move 6 units.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: You observe a thermometer outdoors. At 8:00 AM, the liquid is at 10 units. By 2:00 PM, it is at 25 units. At 6:00 PM, it drops back to 15 units. Describe the temperature trend.
STEPS:
- Note the rise from 10 to 25 (15 unit gain).
- Note the fall from 25 to 15 (10 unit loss).
- Compare the times: The temperature was rising for the first part of the day and falling in the evening.
- Conclude: The day reached its peak warmth around 2:00 PM.
REASONING: Tracking liquid levels over time allows us to map daily weather patterns like peak heat times.
ANSWER: The temperature increased significantly during the day, peaking around 2:00 PM, and began cooling by 6:00 PM.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you hold the bulb of a thermometer with your warm hands, the liquid rises. Is the thermometer measuring the temperature of your hand or the air?
COMPETENCY: Scientific Inquiry
STEPS:
- Identify what the thermometer is touching.
- Determine the source of heat transfer.
- Conclude that the thermometer measures the object it is in direct contact with.
ANSWER: It is measuring your hand because the heat is transferring directly from your skin to the liquid bulb.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Design a simple experiment to test if a thermometer is accurate using two bowls of water: one with ice and one at room temperature.
COMPETENCY: Experimental Design
STEPS:
- Place the thermometer in the ice water and record the level.
- Place the thermometer in room temperature water and record the level.
- Compare the readings to see if they match expected physical states (lower for ice, higher for room temp).
- Analyze if the thermometer reacts consistently each time.
ANSWER: By using known states (freezing and room temp), you can verify if the thermometer moves as expected, confirming its functionality.
DIFFICULTY: hard
