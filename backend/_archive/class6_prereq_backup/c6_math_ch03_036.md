## DEFINITION
Dividing by a fraction is the process of finding how many times one fractional amount fits into another, which is mathematically equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second. The reciprocal is found by swapping the numerator and the denominator of the divisor fraction.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to convert any division of fractions problem into a multiplication problem using the reciprocal and correctly simplify the final result to its simplest form.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the division of proper and improper fractions and whole numbers. It does not cover algebraic fractions, complex fractions (fractions within fractions), or the formal mathematical proof of why the reciprocal rule works.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you have 3/4 of a litre of milk and you want to pour it into small glasses that hold 1/8 of a litre each; how many glasses do you need? Instead of trying to visualize this division directly, we use a "flip and multiply" shortcut. First, we identify the fraction we are dividing by (the divisor) and flip it upside down to get its reciprocal (e.g., 1/8 becomes 8/1). Then, we replace the division sign with a multiplication sign and multiply our original fraction by this new flipped fraction. The key insight is that division by a fractional part is the same as counting how many of those parts fit into the whole, which is exactly what multiplication by the reciprocal calculates.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the reciprocal of 5/9, and what is the reciprocal of the whole number 4?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The reciprocal of 5/9 is 9/5, and the reciprocal of 4 (which is 4/1) is 1/4. A student who says 9/5 and 4 (instead of 1/4) likely does not understand that whole numbers must be written as fractions with a denominator of 1 before flipping.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why 2/3 ÷ 1/4 is not the same as 2/3 × 1/4.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Dividing by 1/4 means finding how many groups of 1/4 fit into 2/3, whereas multiplying by 1/4 finds a small part of 2/3. A student who says they are the same is confusing the operation of division with multiplication and missing the concept of the reciprocal.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Solve: 3/5 ÷ 2/7.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Keep 3/5. Step 2: Flip 2/7 to 7/2. Step 3: Multiply 3/5 × 7/2 = 21/10. Step 4: Convert to mixed fraction 2 1/10. A student who gets 6/35 multiplied the numerators and denominators without flipping the divisor.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
The result will be smaller. A student who says "larger" is likely applying rules from multiplication (where multiplying by a number > 1 makes a value larger) and failing to analyze how division by a value > 1 reduces the original quantity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
**Correct Answer:**
If you divide a fraction by a number greater than 1, the result will be smaller than the original fraction. This is because dividing by a number larger than 1 is equivalent to partitioning the fraction into smaller, equal-sized pieces.

**Explanation of a Wrong Answer:**
A wrong answer (stating the result would be larger) reveals that the student is incorrectly applying the "division makes things smaller" rule they learned with whole numbers to fractions. They are failing to recognize that when the divisor is greater than 1, the quotient must decrease, showing they have not yet grasped the inverse relationship between the size of the divisor and the size of the result.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it possible for the result of 1/2 ÷ 1/4 to be 2? Defend your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, because 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 1/2 × 4/1 = 4/2 = 2. It shows that two quarters (1/4 + 1/4) make up exactly one half. A student who says "No" may be intimidated by the fractions and failing to visualize that dividing by a small fraction (less than 1) results in a larger whole number.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-world story problem that requires calculating 3 ÷ 1/2.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A student should describe having 3 whole items (like 3 rotis) and dividing them into pieces of 1/2 size, resulting in 6 pieces. An answer that fails to show the division context or uses an incorrect operation reveals a lack of connection between the math and physical reality.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to divide the numerators and denominators separately (e.g., 4/5 ÷ 2/3 = 2/1.66) or simply multiply across without flipping the divisor. This happens because students over-generalize the "multiply across" rule they learned for simple multiplication of fractions.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student fails, have them write the divisor fraction on a sticky note and physically turn it upside down before writing the multiplication problem. Remind them: "Division by a fraction is a special request to use the flip-trick; you cannot just multiply straight across!" Practice 3-5 problems where they MUST write the reciprocal step as a separate line before calculating.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A tailor has 5/2 metres of fabric. He needs 1/4 metre to make one napkin. How many napkins can he make?
STEPS:
- identify: Total fabric = 5/2 m, fabric per napkin = 1/4 m.
- method: Divide 5/2 by 1/4 to find the number of napkins.
- apply: 5/2 ÷ 1/4 = 5/2 × 4/1.
- compute: (5 × 4) / (2 × 1) = 20 / 2 = 10.
- verify: 10 napkins × 1/4 m = 10/4 = 5/2 m. Correct.
REASONING: We are grouping a large amount of fabric into equal smaller sizes, which is the definition of division. Multiplying by the reciprocal converts this grouping task into a calculation.
ANSWER: 10 napkins
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A juice seller has 4 litres of juice. He wants to serve it in glasses of 2/3 litre each. How many full glasses can he serve?
STEPS:
- identify: Total juice = 4 litres, glass size = 2/3 litre.
- method: Divide 4 (or 4/1) by 2/3.
- apply: 4/1 ÷ 2/3 = 4/1 × 3/2.
- compute: (4 × 3) / (1 × 2) = 12 / 2 = 6.
- verify: 6 glasses × 2/3 litre = 12/3 = 4 litres. Correct.
REASONING: We divide the total volume by the capacity of one glass to find the number of glasses. Treating the whole number 4 as 4/1 makes the flipping process standard.
ANSWER: 6 glasses
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Shanti has 3 1/2 kg of sugar. She puts it into containers that hold 1/2 kg each. How many containers does she need?
STEPS:
- identify: Total sugar = 3 1/2 kg, container size = 1/2 kg.
- convert: 3 1/2 = 7/2 kg (improper fraction).
- method: Divide 7/2 by 1/2.
- apply: 7/2 ÷ 1/2 = 7/2 × 2/1.
- compute: (7 × 2) / (2 × 1) = 14 / 2 = 7.
REASONING: Mixed numbers cannot be divided directly. Converting to an improper fraction first allows the "flip and multiply" rule to be applied correctly.
ANSWER: 7 containers
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you divide 3/4 kg of rice into packets weighing 1/8 kg each, you get 6 packets. If you divide 3/4 kg of rice into packets weighing 1/4 kg each, will you get more or fewer packets? Explain without fully calculating.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- understand: We are comparing dividing by 1/8 vs dividing by 1/4.
- method: Analyze the size of the divisor.
- apply: 1/4 is larger than 1/8.
- verify: If the portion size increases, the total number of portions must decrease.
ANSWER: You will get fewer packets because 1/4 is a larger portion size than 1/8; therefore, you can fit fewer of them into the same 3/4 kg of rice.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student solved 2/5 ÷ 3/4 as 2/5 × 4/3 = 8/15. Another student solved it as 6/20. Which student is correct and why is the other student's method incorrect?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- understand: Comparing two different methods of division.
- method: Perform the division correctly using the reciprocal rule.
- apply: 2/5 ÷ 3/4 = 2/5 × 4/3 = 8/15.
- verify: The second student multiplied the original numerators and denominators without using the reciprocal.
ANSWER: The first student is correct. The second student simply multiplied 2/5 × 3/4 (which is 6/20), failing to realize that division requires the second fraction to be flipped.
DIFFICULTY: hard
