## DEFINITION
Symmetry is a property where a shape or object can be divided by a line into two parts that are perfect mirror images of each other. The specific line that creates these two identical, overlapping halves is known as the line of symmetry.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to visually identify if a shape is symmetrical and accurately draw its line(s) of symmetry on paper.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers reflectional symmetry and identifying lines of symmetry in basic geometric shapes and everyday objects. It excludes rotational symmetry, point symmetry, and transformation geometry.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever looked at a beautiful butterfly and noticed how the patterns on its left wing perfectly match the patterns on its right wing? To begin understanding symmetry, take a piece of plain paper, fold it in half, and cut a simple shape along the folded edge. When you open the paper, you see a symmetric shape where the fold itself acts as a mirror line. Symmetry is all about balance; if you place a mirror exactly along that fold, the reflection will complete the shape perfectly. Start by observing objects around your house—like a leaf or a ruler—and ask yourself, "If I draw a line through the middle, does one side look like a perfect reflection of the other?"
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What do we call the line that divides a shape into two identical mirror-image halves?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
It is called the line of symmetry. If a student answers "middle line" or "bisector," they lack the specific geometric vocabulary, which is essential for identifying that the parts must be mirror images, not just equal in area.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you fold a paper shape along a line and the two halves do not overlap perfectly, is the shape symmetrical? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, it is not symmetrical. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks "symmetry" just means "equal size," whereas it actually requires the parts to be exact reflections of each other (matching points, corners, and edges).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Draw a rectangle on a piece of paper. How many lines of symmetry can you find by folding it?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
A rectangle has two lines of symmetry: one horizontal and one vertical. If a student says "four," they are confusing a rectangle with a square; if they say "one," they likely missed the second axis of reflection.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Look at the English letter 'A'. Does it have a line of symmetry? If so, where is it?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Yes, it has one vertical line of symmetry passing through the top point down to the base. A student saying it has no symmetry fails to see that the left and right sides are mirror reflections.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Can a triangle have more than one line of symmetry? Explain your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, an equilateral triangle has three lines of symmetry because all three sides and angles are equal. A student saying "no" or "only one" is likely only picturing an isosceles or scalene triangle.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple shape that has at least two lines of symmetry and draw it on a grid.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The student should draw a shape like a cross or a rectangle. A poor answer would be an irregular, lopsided shape, showing the student doesn't grasp that symmetry requires balanced, mirrored construction.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse "symmetry" with "congruence" (equal size). They might think that any shape cut into two equal areas is symmetric. For example, a scalene triangle cut into two pieces of equal area is NOT symmetric because the pieces are not mirror images of each other.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to place a small hand mirror on the line they drew through the shape. Explain that if the reflection in the mirror makes the original shape look complete, then it is a line of symmetry. If the reflection looks distorted or creates a different shape, the line is incorrect.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A rectangular piece of paper measures 10 cm by 6 cm. Find the lines of symmetry.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the shape as a rectangle.
- step 2: Attempt to fold the paper vertically.
- step 3: Check if the left and right sides overlap. (They do).
- step 4: Attempt to fold the paper horizontally.
- step 5: Check if the top and bottom sides overlap. (They do).
REASONING: Folding confirms that the rectangle aligns perfectly along these two central axes, meaning both are lines of symmetry.
ANSWER: 2 lines of symmetry.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A square piece of paper has a side length of 8 cm. How many lines of symmetry does it have?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the shape as a square.
- step 2: Fold vertically (1).
- step 3: Fold horizontally (2).
- step 4: Fold along one diagonal (3).
- step 5: Fold along the other diagonal (4).
REASONING: Because all sides and angles of a square are equal, the paper overlaps perfectly in all four of these specific directions.
ANSWER: 4 lines of symmetry.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: You have a paper cut-out of the letter 'H'. How many lines of symmetry can you find?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the structure of the letter 'H'.
- step 2: Draw a vertical line through the middle of the crossbar.
- step 3: Check if the left side reflects to the right. (Yes).
- step 4: Draw a horizontal line through the middle of the vertical legs.
- step 5: Check if the top reflects to the bottom. (Yes).
REASONING: The vertical symmetry exists because the legs are identical, and horizontal symmetry exists because the top and bottom of the legs are identical.
ANSWER: 2 lines of symmetry.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are given a drawing of a house with a door in the center and a window on the left. Does this drawing have a line of symmetry? Justify your answer.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the drawing for balance.
- step 2: Note the placement of the door (center) and window (left).
- step 3: Imagine a vertical line through the center.
- step 4: Compare the reflection: there is no window on the right to match the window on the left.
ANSWER: No, it does not have a line of symmetry because the window on the left does not have a matching window on the right to create a mirror image.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A design consists of a circle with a triangle drawn inside it. If the triangle is equilateral, how many lines of symmetry does the whole design have? Explain.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the symmetry of a circle (infinite lines).
- step 2: Identify the symmetry of an equilateral triangle (3 lines).
- step 3: Combine them: The design is only as symmetric as its least symmetric part.
- step 4: Conclude that the lines must pass through the vertices of the triangle.
ANSWER: The design has 3 lines of symmetry. Even though a circle has infinite lines, the triangle restricts the symmetry to the 3 axes that pass through its vertices and the center of the circle.
DIFFICULTY: hard
