## DEFINITION
A government is the system or group of people responsible for making and enforcing laws to keep a society organized. Governments are categorized based on how the leaders gain power: democracy (elected by people), monarchy (inherited by royalty), or dictatorship (taken by force or total control).
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the key difference in power acquisition between democracy, monarchy, and dictatorship and provide one real-world example for each.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the three primary forms of government: democracy, monarchy, and dictatorship. It excludes political theory, theocracy, and hybrid regimes.
## WALKTHROUGH
Think about how your classroom runs: do you all vote to pick a class leader, does the teacher always choose one person based on their family, or does one person make all the rules without asking anyone? Begin by identifying who holds the power to make the final "law" in your scenario. In a democracy, imagine a large group of people "handing over" their power to a representative through a vote. In a monarchy, visualize a crown being passed down within a family. In a dictatorship, picture one person keeping all the power to themselves. The key insight is that the source of power—the people, a family line, or a single ruler—defines the type of government.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Who holds the power in a democracy?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The people hold the power and use it to elect representatives. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks power resides only with the leader, failing to recognize the role of the voter.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
How does a leader usually come to power in a monarchy?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Power is usually inherited through birth, meaning it stays within the royal family. A wrong answer suggests the student confuses monarchy with democracy (voting) or dictatorship (force).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If a person forces their way into power and refuses to listen to the citizens, what type of government is this?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
This is a dictatorship. Steps: 1. Identify the lack of public choice. 2. Identify the use of force or absolute control. 3. Match these traits to the definition of a dictatorship. A wrong answer indicates an inability to distinguish between "forceful control" and "elected authority."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare a democracy and a monarchy: what is the main difference in how the leader changes?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
In a democracy, the leader changes through elections; in a monarchy, the leader changes through family succession. A wrong answer reveals a failure to differentiate between "public choice" and "birthright."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it possible for a country to be both a democracy and a monarchy?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, some countries have a monarch as a symbolic head, but the actual power to make laws rests with elected representatives (a constitutional monarchy). A wrong answer suggests a rigid, overly simplistic view that doesn't account for modern political systems.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
If you were to design a new government for a school club, which type would you choose and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer chooses a system and justifies it using the concept of power (e.g., "I choose democracy so everyone has a voice"). A poor answer fails to connect the choice to the source of power.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think "government" only refers to the people in the capital city. They fail to realize that the *type* of government (democracy/monarchy/dictatorship) is actually about the *rules of the game* for power, not just the location of the office. For example, they might think the UK is not a democracy because it has a Queen, ignoring that the citizens actually elect the parliament that makes the laws.
## ON_FAILURE
1. Ask the student: "Does the leader have to ask the people for permission to stay in power?" 2. Explain that if the answer is "Yes," it is a democracy. 3. If "No," ask: "Did they get the job because of their parents?" 4. If "Yes," it is a monarchy; if "No" and they use force, it is a dictatorship.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Identify the government type for a country where citizens vote for the Prime Minister every five years.
STEPS:
- 1: Identify who chooses the leader (the citizens).
- 2: Identify the method of choice (voting/elections).
- 3: Compare with definitions: Democracy involves voting; Monarchy involves birth; Dictatorship involves force.
- 4: Conclude: Since citizens vote, it is a democracy.
REASONING: The power originates from the people via the electoral process.
ANSWER: Democracy.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Identify the government type where a Queen rules because her mother was the previous Queen.
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the source of power (the previous Queen/family line).
- 2: Identify the process (inheritance/birth).
- 3: Compare with definitions: Democracy requires elections; Monarchy requires inheritance.
- 4: Conclude: Since power is inherited, it is a monarchy.
REASONING: Power is passed down through family bloodlines rather than public choice.
ANSWER: Monarchy.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A leader takes over a country by using the military to remove the previous government. No one can vote. What type is this?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify how the leader got power (used the military).
- 2: Identify if people have a say (no, they cannot vote).
- 3: Identify the nature of control (absolute control by one).
- 4: Compare with definitions: Democracy (people), Monarchy (family), Dictatorship (force/individual).
- 5: Conclude: It is a dictatorship.
REASONING: The absence of elections and the use of force define this as a dictatorship.
ANSWER: Dictatorship.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: In a country, the citizens vote for representatives, but there is also a royal family that performs ceremonies. How is this possible?
COMPETENCY: Critical Thinking
STEPS:
- 1: Recognize that democracy and monarchy can exist together.
- 2: Define the role of the elected representatives (making laws).
- 3: Define the role of the monarch (ceremonial).
- 4: Explain that power is separated between the two.
ANSWER: It is a constitutional monarchy where the monarch is a figurehead and the elected parliament holds the actual governing power.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare the "source of power" in all three types of government in a table.
COMPETENCY: Analytical Comparison
STEPS:
- 1: Create a table with three rows: Democracy, Monarchy, Dictatorship.
- 2: Create a column for "Source of Power."
- 3: Fill in: People/Elections, Birth/Inheritance, Force/Individual Control.
- 4: Add a summary sentence explaining the fundamental difference.
ANSWER: Democracy (People), Monarchy (Family/Inheritance), Dictatorship (Individual/Force). The fundamental difference lies in who grants the authority to rule.
DIFFICULTY: hard
