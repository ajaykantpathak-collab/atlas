## DEFINITION
Vocabulary in context refers to determining the meaning of a word by analyzing the clues provided by the sentences and the overall situation surrounding it, rather than relying solely on a dictionary. It involves understanding how a word's specific usage clarifies a character's traits or the tone of a story.

The student can identify the meaning of an unfamiliar word using context clues and explain how that word creates a specific mood or character trait within a story.

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever seen a word in a story that you didn't know, but you still understood what was happening? Start by reading the sentence before and after the word to find "clues"—these are other words that relate to the same idea. If a story describes an elephant "wagging" his tail when receiving food, even if you don't know "wagging," the words "food" and "thanks" tell you it is a positive, happy action. Always ask yourself, "If I replace this word with a simpler one, does the feeling of the story stay the same?" By connecting the word to the character's actions, you move from just knowing a definition to understanding the story's heart.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What does it mean to understand a word "in context"?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
It means using the surrounding sentences and the story's events to figure out what a word means. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks they must always use a dictionary, failing to use clues in the text.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
In the story of Gajaraj, if the text says he "winced" when his friend was pulled away, why is "winced" a sign of sadness?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Because the sentence mentions "tears" and the friend being taken away, showing that "winced" is a reaction to pain or distress. A wrong answer shows the student is ignoring the emotional clues provided in the paragraph.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If a character is described as "determined" to finish a task, find a synonym that fits the context of someone who refuses to give up.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
"Resolute" or "persistent." The student must identify that the context implies strength of will. A wrong answer like "tired" shows they are not connecting "determined" to the action of continuing despite difficulty.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze how the word "strayed" in the story helps us understand the dog's situation before he met the elephant.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
"Strayed" implies the dog was lost or wandered away by mistake, which explains why he was "tired and hungry." A wrong answer shows the student missed the connection between the word and the dog's vulnerable state.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Critique the use of the word "invisible" to describe the chair in Mario's story—is it truly magic, or is it a tool for a lesson?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It is a tool for a lesson because the "magic" only works when true friends intervene. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks the chair is literally supernatural, missing the metaphorical theme of the story.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Use the word "relieved" in a sentence about a student who finally finds a lost pencil.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
"I felt relieved when I found my pencil under the books." A poor answer uses the word incorrectly, such as "I relieved my pencil," which shows a misunderstanding of the word as an emotion.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often guess the meaning of a word based on its "sound" or by picking the first synonym they find in a dictionary without checking if it fits the sentence. For example, if a text says, "The mahout was concerned about Gajaraj," they might choose "interested" instead of "worried," ignoring that the context mentions the elephant not eating.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to read the two sentences before and after the target word aloud. Second, ask them to identify one "clue" word in those sentences that describes a feeling or an action. Third, have them try replacing the target word with their guess; if the new sentence sounds strange or changes the meaning, encourage them to look for a different clue.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Analyze the word "munching" in the phrase "food he was munching."
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the word "munching" and the surrounding words "food" and "dog."
- step 2: Look for an action related to food.
- step 3: Infer that because the dog is hungry, he is eating the food.
- step 4: Define "munching" as chewing or eating noisily.
REASONING: By connecting the action to the object (food), we confirm the meaning.
ANSWER: Munching means to chew food.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Analyze "indigestion" in the sentence: "He may have slight indigestion. Let me not force him to eat."
STEPS:
- step 1: Note the context: Gajaraj is not eating his food.
- step 2: The mahout wonders why; "indigestion" is the reason for not eating.
- step 3: Relate "indigestion" to a physical problem with the stomach.
- step 4: Conclude that it means an upset stomach.
REASONING: The cause (not eating) and the context (tummy) define the medical term.
ANSWER: Indigestion is a mild stomach issue.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Analyze "determined" in: "Mario, brave and determined, took the strange invisible chair."
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the character's goal: to test his friends.
- step 2: Look at the next actions: he keeps trying even after falling.
- step 3: Combine "brave" with "keep trying."
- step 4: Conclude "determined" means firm in his decision to succeed.
- step 5: Apply this to Mario's personality.
REASONING: Character traits are shown through repeated actions in context.
ANSWER: Determined means having a firm decision to do something despite obstacles.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If the text says, "The farmer tossed a rope round the neck of the dog," and the dog "yelped," why does "yelped" imply the dog was not happy?
COMPETENCY: Inferring emotion from descriptive verbs.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the action (rope tossed).
- step 2: Identify the sound (yelp).
- step 3: Connect the sound to the situation (being taken away).
- step 4: Conclude the dog is in distress.
ANSWER: "Yelped" is a high-pitched cry of pain or fear, and since he is being pulled away from his friend, the context confirms he is distressed.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare the word "caretaker" and "friend" as used in the story. How does the context differentiate them?
COMPETENCY: Analyzing character relationships through vocabulary.
STEPS:
- step 1: Define "caretaker" based on the mahout's actions (feeding, bathing).
- step 2: Define "friend" based on Gajaraj's wish to play.
- step 3: Contrast the two: one is a duty, the other is an emotional bond.
- step 4: Explain why the text calls the mahout a caretaker but not a friend.
- step 5: Summarize the difference.
ANSWER: A caretaker provides physical needs, while a friend provides emotional connection and joy; the text uses these words to show that Gajaraj needed more than just service.
DIFFICULTY: hard
