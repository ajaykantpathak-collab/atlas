## DEFINITION
Tone in poetry is the poet's emotional attitude toward the subject (e.g., playful, serious, or sad), while humour is a literary device that creates amusement by highlighting the gap between what is expected and what actually happens.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the tone of a short poem and explain how a specific action or detail creates humour by breaking a pattern of expectation.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the identification of tone and the mechanics of humour in poetry. It does not cover satire, irony, or the analysis of prose narratives.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever seen someone try to drink tea with a fork? You would laugh because it is silly and unexpected. To understand a "funny man" poem, start by listing what a "normal" person would do in that situation (like using a spoon). Then, identify what the character actually does (like using a fork). The humour lies in that difference. By noticing this gap, you can describe the poet’s tone as "lighthearted" or "playful" because they are pointing out the fun in being silly.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What do we mean by the "tone" of a poem?
Answer: The tone is the poet's attitude or mood toward the subject. A wrong answer suggests the student confuses "tone" with "topic," which means they aren't looking at the *feel* of the words.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Tone is the poet’s attitude toward the subject, such as being playful, serious, or excited. A student who says "the tone is about a funny man" is confusing the *subject* with the *attitude*; this reveals they need help distinguishing between *what* the poem is about and *how* the poet feels about it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a poem describes a man wearing shoes on his hands, why is this funny?
Answer: It is funny because it is the opposite of what we expect. A wrong answer suggests the student doesn't see the connection between "expectation" and "comedy."
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It is funny because it creates a surprise—we expect shoes on feet, not hands. If a student answers "because he is weird," they are missing the mechanism of humour (the gap between expectation and reality); they need to focus on identifying the "normal" expectation first.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A poem says a man went for a swim in the desert. How would you describe the tone?
Answer: The tone is absurd or humorous. To find this, compare "swimming" (needs water) with "desert" (has no water).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The tone is absurd or playful. Steps: 1. Identify the action (swimming). 2. Identify the setting (desert). 3. Compare them to see if it makes sense. 4. Label the resulting feeling as "absurd." If a student says "sad," they are misinterpreting the situation as a tragedy instead of a joke.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How does the "gap" between expectation and action create humour?
Answer: The gap creates a surprise that forces our brains to see the situation as silly. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks humour is just about words rather than the *logic* of the poem.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Humour happens when our brain expects a logical outcome but gets a surprising, illogical one instead. If a student says "it's just a funny word," they are failing to see the structural logic behind why we laugh at specific events.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it possible for a poem about a sad event to have a humorous tone?
Answer: Generally no, because the tone dictates the audience's reaction. A wrong answer shows a misunderstanding of how authors signal feelings to readers.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It is unlikely, as the tone is the "mood" the author sets. If a student says "yes," they might be confusing "dark humour" (which is advanced) with the basic concept of tone. Remind them that tone is the *guide* for how we should react to the poem.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a one-line scenario that would make a reader laugh.
Answer: A line like "The baker tried to bake a cake using a brick." The answer must show an expectation (baking a cake) being met with an absurd action (using a brick).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
"The chef tried to stir his soup with a giant ladder." This works because a ladder is clearly the wrong tool for soup. A poor answer would be "The chef was very funny," which is just an opinion and doesn't explain *why* it is funny through an action.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think humour is only about "funny words" (like adjectives) rather than the "funny action" (the plot or situation). For example, if a poem says "The silly man ate a hat," a student might focus on the word "silly" rather than the fact that eating a hat is an absurd action.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to draw a picture of the action described in the poem. If the picture shows something impossible (like a dog flying), ask them: "Would a real dog do this?" When they say no, explain that the *difference* between the real dog and the flying dog is where the humour lives, not just in the words used.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A poem describes a person trying to wash their face with sand. Analyze the humour.
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the action (washing face).
- 2: Identify the tool (sand).
- 3: Compare to normal expectation (using water).
- 4: Note the contrast (sand is dry/rough, not for cleaning).
REASONING: By comparing the tool to the intended goal, we see the absurdity.
ANSWER: The humour is created because sand is the opposite of a cleaning agent, making the action absurd and playful.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Identify the tone of a poem that describes a man walking his cat on a leash while wearing a tuxedo.
STEPS:
- 1: List the character's actions (walking cat, wearing tuxedo).
- 2: Contextualize (walking a cat is unusual, tuxedos are for parties).
- 3: Identify the poet's attitude (not mocking, just showing the oddity).
- 4: Conclude the tone (playful/light).
REASONING: The juxtaposition of formal wear and a cat creates a light, nonsensical vibe.
ANSWER: The tone is playful and slightly absurd because the actions are incongruous with normal daily life.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A poem shows a character trying to catch the moon in a bucket. Is this funny? Why?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify character goal (catch the moon).
- 2: Identify method (using a bucket).
- 3: Assess feasibility (moon is too far/too big).
- 4: Compare with reality.
- 5: Conclude why it's funny.
- 6: Identify tone.
REASONING: The gap between the character's ambition and their silly tool creates comedic failure.
ANSWER: Yes, it is funny because the character attempts an impossible task with a household item, creating an absurd and playful tone.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a poem describes a man who sleeps in a bathtub and takes a bath in his bed, how does this create humour?
COMPETENCY: Literary Analysis
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the two locations (bed and bathtub).
- 2: Identify the standard use of each (bed for sleep, tub for bath).
- 3: Identify the switch (tub for sleep, bed for bath).
- 4: Explain why the switch causes humour (it's the opposite of common sense).
ANSWER: The poem creates humour by swapping the purposes of the bed and the bathtub, which surprises the reader because the character is doing the exact opposite of what logic dictates.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare a poem about a character who is naturally clumsy with a poem about a character who does silly things on purpose. Which one relies more on "expectation vs. reality"?
COMPETENCY: Comparative Analysis
STEPS:
- 1: Define "clumsy" (accident).
- 2: Define "silly on purpose" (choice).
- 3: Analyze which breaks the "normal" pattern more effectively.
- 4: Explain the role of expectation.
- 5: Conclude.
ANSWER: The character who acts silly on purpose relies more on the gap between expectation and reality because the reader expects a normal person to make logical choices. The clumsy character creates humour through accidents, but the "funny man" creates humour by intentionally ignoring the rules of expected behaviour.
DIFFICULTY: hard
