## DEFINITION
Banks act as financial bridges by collecting money from people who have extra savings (savers) and lending that money to people who need it for specific purposes (borrowers). This system allows money to remain useful rather than sitting idle.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify who provides money to a bank and who receives it, explaining that the bank acts as a middleman that uses interest to manage this flow.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the basic flow of money between savers, banks, and borrowers. It does not cover technical credit scoring, complex investment products, or microfinance.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you have a piggy bank that is full, but your friend needs money to buy a school uniform. You could lend it directly, but what if you forget to track it? A bank solves this: you "deposit" your extra money there to keep it safe and earn a little reward called interest. The bank then takes that pool of money and lends it to people who need it, like a shopkeeper wanting to buy more stock. The key insight is that banks create a cycle where your idle money helps someone else grow, and you get paid for letting them use it.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The two groups are savers and borrowers. A wrong answer suggests the student hasn't grasped the basic "two-sided" nature of banking mentioned in the chapter.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why would someone choose to deposit money in a bank instead of keeping it in a metal box at home?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
A bank keeps money safe and pays interest, helping the savings grow. A wrong answer reveals the student thinks banks are just for hiding money, ignoring the growth aspect (interest).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If you deposit ₹1,000 at a 5% interest rate, how much interest do you earn in one year?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
You earn ₹50. The calculation is 1,000 * 0.05. A wrong answer shows the student struggles with basic percentage applications in financial contexts.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How does a bank benefit from the money deposited by savers?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The bank uses those deposits to provide loans (borrowing) to others. A wrong answer indicates the student views the bank as a static vault rather than an active financial institution.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it better for the economy if money sits in a jar or is deposited in a bank? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It is better in a bank because the money is put to work by being lent to borrowers who might use it for productive activities. A wrong answer reveals a failure to understand the "flow" of money in an economy.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
If you were a bank manager, why would you pay interest to the people who deposit their money?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
To encourage them to keep their money in the bank so the bank has enough funds to lend out. A poor answer fails to see the bank's need for a stable supply of money.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think banks generate money out of thin air to give to borrowers. They don't realize the money lent to borrowers is actually the money deposited by the savers.
## ON_FAILURE
Explain the "Water Tank" analogy: The bank is like a tank. Savers are the ones pouring water in (deposits), and borrowers are the ones using the tap (loans). The bank cannot let the tap run if the tank is empty, so they must attract "depositors" first.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Rahul has ₹5,000. He deposits it in a bank at 10% annual interest. How much does he have after one year?
STEPS:
- identify principal: ₹5,000.
- identify interest rate: 10% (0.10).
- calculate interest: 5,000 * 0.10 = 500.
- add interest to principal: 5,000 + 500 = 5,500.
REASONING: Interest is a reward for the bank using your money. Adding it to the principal shows total wealth growth.
ANSWER: He has ₹5,500.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Priya needs ₹2,000 to buy books. The bank offers a loan. Who is the borrower and who is the lender?
STEPS:
- identify the need for money: Priya needs ₹2,000.
- define 'borrower': The person taking the money.
- define 'lender': The institution providing the money.
- classify: Priya is the borrower, the bank is the lender.
REASONING: Banking roles are defined by the flow of cash; the receiver of the cash is the borrower.
ANSWER: Priya is the borrower; the bank is the lender.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Why do banks pay interest on savings but charge interest on loans?
STEPS:
- step 1: identify the saver's role (providing funds).
- step 2: identify the bank's role (middleman).
- step 3: identify the borrower's role (using funds).
- step 4: analyze the cost of money (bank pays to attract money, charges to make a profit).
- step 5: conclude the balance.
REASONING: This explains the "magic" of finance where the bank's profit lies in the difference between what they pay and what they charge.
ANSWER: Banks pay interest to attract deposits and charge interest to cover costs and earn profit for providing the service.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If all savers decided to take their money out of the bank at once, what would happen to the borrowers?
COMPETENCY: Systemic reasoning
STEPS:
- identify the source of loan money (savers).
- trace the impact of withdrawal.
- conclude that banks would have no money to lend.
ANSWER: Borrowers would not be able to get loans because the bank would have run out of funds to provide to them.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare the "Value of Work" (Chapter 13) with the "Value of Money" in banking. How does a bank act as a facilitator for people's work?
COMPETENCY: Synthesis of concepts
STEPS:
- define economic activity (work for money).
- define banking (storing/lending money).
- connect: money earned from work is saved in banks.
- analyze: saved money provides capital for others to start their own work.
ANSWER: Banks help people who work by providing a safe place for their earnings, and then they lend that money to others so they can start their own economic activities, effectively circulating the value created by work.
