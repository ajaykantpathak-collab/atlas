## DEFINITION
Constructing triangles using SSS (Side-Side-Side) and SAS (Side-Angle-Side) criteria involves using a ruler, a compass, and a protractor to draw a unique triangle when specific measurements are provided. SSS requires knowing the lengths of all three sides, while SAS requires knowing the lengths of two sides and the measure of the angle trapped between them.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to draw a triangle given three side lengths or two side lengths and an included angle using a compass and protractor accurately. Mastery is shown by producing a triangle that matches the given measurements within a margin of 1mm or 1 degree.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the SSS and SAS construction methods for triangles. It excludes ASA, AAS, and RHS constructions, as well as formal geometric proofs of congruency.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are a carpenter trying to build a triangular wooden frame for a window, but you only have three sticks of specific lengths. How do you ensure the frame fits perfectly? Start by laying down the longest stick as your base. Use a compass set to the exact length of the second stick to draw an arc from one end, then repeat with the third stick from the other end; where these arcs cross is your third corner! When you have two sides and a corner angle, draw the base first, use your protractor to mark the angle at one end, draw the second side, and finally connect the endpoints. The key insight is that these specific measurements "lock" the triangle into one unique shape.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
In an SSS construction, what tool do you use to ensure the third vertex is exactly the right distance from the two ends of your base line?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
You use a compass. A wrong answer like "a ruler" shows the student doesn't understand that the compass is used to maintain a fixed distance (radius) to find the intersection point, whereas a ruler only measures straight lines.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you are given two sides of 5cm and 6cm, why can you not construct a unique triangle without knowing the angle between them?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Because the two sides could be hinged at different angles, creating triangles of many different shapes and sizes. An answer suggesting "it's impossible" reveals they don't realize the sides can move (rotate) if the third side isn't fixed.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
To construct a triangle with sides 4cm, 5cm, and 6cm, what is the best first step?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Draw the longest side (6cm) as the base line. This makes the subsequent arcs from the ends (5cm and 4cm) easier to manage and keeps the drawing organized. An answer suggesting "drawing a 4cm line" is not wrong, but less efficient for keeping the drawing centered.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
In an SAS construction with sides 5cm and 4cm and an included angle of 60°, why is it important that the angle is "included"?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The angle must be between the two given sides so it acts as a "hinge" that fixes the distance between the two endpoints. If the angle were not included (e.g., opposite one of the sides), the triangle's shape would not be fixed.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If a student constructs a triangle using SSS but the arcs do not intersect, what does this tell you about the measurements given?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The measurements are impossible; the sum of the two shorter sides is likely less than or equal to the longest side. This shows the student understands the geometric constraint that a triangle's sides must satisfy the triangle inequality theorem.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a set of instructions for a friend to construct a triangle with sides 7cm, 8cm, and 9cm using only a ruler and a compass.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
1. Draw a 9cm line segment. 2. Set the compass to 7cm and draw an arc from one endpoint. 3. Set the compass to 8cm and draw an arc from the other endpoint. 4. Connect the intersection point to both ends. A poor answer would lack the step of using the compass to "fix" the lengths of the other two sides.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to draw the two shorter sides first without checking their intersection, or they measure the angle incorrectly by not aligning the protractor's baseline with the drawn line segment. This happens because they view construction as "sketching" rather than a precise geometrical process.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student fails, have them re-measure their base line with a ruler to ensure it is exactly the length required. Then, watch them set the compass: ensure they put the needle on 0 and the pencil on the required length mark on the ruler. Finally, guide them to keep the compass point firmly on the base-line endpoint while drawing the arc.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Construct a triangle with sides 5cm, 6cm, and 7cm.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Use a ruler to draw a line segment AB = 7cm.
- Step 2: Open the compass to 5cm. Place the needle at A and draw an arc above the line.
- Step 3: Open the compass to 6cm. Place the needle at B and draw an arc intersecting the first one. Label this point C.
- Step 4: Use the ruler to join AC and BC.
REASONING: The compass maintains the exact length of the sides, ensuring the distance from the endpoints to the third vertex is correct. This geometric process forces a unique triangle.
ANSWER: A triangle with sides 5cm, 6cm, and 7cm.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Construct a triangle with sides 4cm and 5cm, and an included angle of 60°.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw a line segment AB = 5cm.
- Step 2: Place the protractor center on A, align the base line, and mark 60°.
- Step 3: Draw a ray from A through the 60° mark.
- Step 4: Use a ruler and compass to mark a point C on this ray, exactly 4cm from A.
- Step 5: Join CB to complete the triangle.
REASONING: Setting the angle first fixes the direction of the second side, and measuring the distance on that ray fixes the length, creating a unique triangle.
ANSWER: A triangle with sides 4cm, 5cm, and a 60° angle.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student needs to build a triangular garden bed. The sides are 3m, 4m, and 5m. They draw it on paper using a scale of 1cm = 1m.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define the scale: 3m=3cm, 4m=4cm, 5m=5cm.
- Step 2: Draw the longest side (5cm) as the base AB.
- Step 3: Set compass to 3cm, draw arc from A.
- Step 4: Set compass to 4cm, draw arc from B.
- Step 5: Join intersection C to A and B.
- Step 6: Verify: measure AC (3cm) and BC (4cm).
REASONING: Using a scale allows us to represent large real-world objects on paper. The SSS construction ensures the angles of the garden bed will be accurate to the design.
ANSWER: A 3cm, 4cm, 5cm triangle on paper.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are given two sides, 6cm and 8cm, and an angle of 90°. Does it matter if you draw the 6cm side or the 8cm side as the base? Explain.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Step 1: Consider the SAS construction steps.
- Step 2: Test drawing the 6cm side as the base vs the 8cm side.
- Step 3: Observe that the final triangle shape remains the same (congruent).
- Step 4: Justify why the order doesn't change the outcome.
ANSWER: No, it does not matter. As long as the 90° angle is placed between the two sides, the resulting triangle will be the same regardless of which side is drawn first as the base.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A friend says that for a triangle with sides 2cm, 3cm, and 6cm, the arcs will never meet. Explain why they are correct using the concept of triangle construction.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- Step 1: Visualize the construction: base is 6cm.
- Step 2: Arcs are 2cm and 3cm from the endpoints.
- Step 3: Calculate the reach: 2cm + 3cm = 5cm.
- Step 4: Compare reach to base: 5cm < 6cm.
- Step 5: Conclude that the arcs cannot meet.
ANSWER: Your friend is correct. The sum of the two shorter sides (2cm + 3cm = 5cm) is less than the third side (6cm). In construction, the arcs represent all possible points for the third vertex, but they stay 1cm apart, making it impossible to form a closed triangle.
DIFFICULTY: hard
