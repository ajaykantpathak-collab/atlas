## DEFINITION
A periscope is an optical instrument that uses two plane mirrors placed at 45-degree angles to allow an observer to see objects that are outside their direct line of sight. It works by reflecting light twice, effectively "bending" the path of the light to the observer's eye.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to describe the path light travels through a periscope and correctly place two mirrors at 45-degree angles inside a tube to observe an object hidden behind an obstacle.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the basic construction and reflection principle of a cardboard-tube periscope. It excludes prism-based periscopes, magnification, and modern submarine optics.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever tried to see over a tall crowd at a school parade or look around a corner without being spotted? A periscope helps you do exactly that by using two mirrors. Imagine a hollow cardboard tube shaped like a 'Z'. If you place a mirror at the top bend and another at the bottom bend, each at a 45-degree angle, the light hits the first mirror, bounces down, hits the second mirror, and bounces into your eye. You are essentially "catching" the light from above and bringing it down to your eye level.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the primary function of a periscope?
[1-2 sentence response]
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A periscope allows a person to see objects that are not in their direct line of sight. An answer claiming it "makes things bigger" or "sees through walls" shows a misunderstanding of reflection vs. magnification or transparency.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you place the two mirrors in a periscope parallel to each other but at 30-degree angles instead of 45-degree angles, will you be able to see the object clearly? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, you will not see the object clearly because the light will not be reflected at the correct angle to reach your eye. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks mirrors always work regardless of their orientation, failing to grasp that the angle of incidence must equal the angle of reflection to redirect light precisely.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A student is building a periscope. They place the first mirror at the top. If the light hits the mirror at a 45-degree angle to the surface of the mirror, at what angle will it reflect?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
It will reflect at a 45-degree angle. This is based on the law of reflection where the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. A wrong answer indicates they do not know the basic rule of reflection for plane mirrors.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why is a periscope often used in submarines that are underwater?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
It allows the crew to observe the surface of the water while the submarine remains submerged and protected. An answer suggesting it is for "seeing through the hull" reveals a failure to understand the device’s purpose of redirecting light from an outside source.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If you removed one of the two mirrors from a periscope, could you still see the hidden object? Defend your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, because one mirror can only change the direction of light once, which would not lead the light to your eye after clearing the obstacle. You need the second mirror to "reset" the direction of the light back to your line of sight.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple modification: If you wanted to see around a corner to your right, how would you orient the mirrors?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
You would rotate the entire periscope tube so that the top mirror faces the corner while the bottom mirror remains aligned with your eye. A poor answer ignores the need for physical alignment of the mirrors toward the light source.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think the light "travels" through the cardboard tube as if it were a clear pipe. They fail to realize that without the mirrors positioned at precise angles, the light would just hit the inside wall of the tube and be absorbed.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to trace the path of a laser pointer or flashlight beam onto a single mirror. Ask, "Where does the light go?" Then, place a second mirror in the path of the reflected light and ask, "How can we make this light hit your eye?" This demonstrates that the mirrors are not "windows" but "reflectors" that must be aimed like a game of billiards.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student needs to build a periscope where the distance between the two mirrors is 50 cm. If the object is at eye level above the top mirror, describe the path of light.
STEPS:
- Identify light source: The object reflects light.
- First reflection: Light hits the top mirror at 45 degrees.
- Path of light: Light travels 50 cm downward parallel to the tube.
- Second reflection: Light hits the bottom mirror at 45 degrees.
- Final destination: Light enters the student's eye.
REASONING: Since the mirrors are at 45 degrees, the light turns exactly 90 degrees at each mirror, moving the beam from horizontal to vertical and back to horizontal.
ANSWER: The light travels in a 'Z' path, turning 90 degrees twice.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A student has a tube that is 30 cm long. They place mirrors at the ends. If they want to see an object 10 cm above the top of the tube, does the length of the tube matter for the image quality?
STEPS:
- Recognize the function: The tube length determines the distance between the mirrors.
- Analyze reflection: The distance does not change the angle of reflection (45 degrees).
- Conclusion: The image remains the same; only the physical distance is spanned.
- Verification: The law of reflection is independent of the distance between mirrors.
REASONING: As long as the mirrors are parallel and at 45 degrees, the light will reach the eye regardless of the tube length, assuming the tube is wide enough.
ANSWER: No, the image quality does not change based on tube length.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student uses a mirror that is slightly tilted at 40 degrees instead of 45 degrees. Will the light reach the eye?
STEPS:
- Identify the law: Angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
- Calculate: If the mirror is at 40 degrees, the light turns by 80 degrees (not 90).
- Trace the path: The light will hit the second mirror at an incorrect angle.
- Check final path: The light will miss the eye or be reflected into the tube wall.
- Conclusion: The image will not be seen.
REASONING: Precision in the 45-degree angle is required for the light to turn exactly 90 degrees, ensuring the path remains parallel to the tube's axis.
ANSWER: No, the light will not reach the eye.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are in a submarine and the periscope is stuck. You can see the reflection of the sky, but it looks distorted and cut off. What is the most likely issue with your mirrors?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction.
STEPS:
- Analyze the symptom: You see "some" image, but it is "cut off."
- Identify the cause: Mirrors are likely misaligned or one is not at 45 degrees.
- Correction: Re-align the mirror to the 45-degree position.
ANSWER: The mirrors have likely shifted from their 45-degree alignment, causing the light to reflect at the wrong angle and miss the center of the second mirror.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why can you not use a plain cardboard surface instead of a mirror to build a periscope? Explain using the concept of reflection.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- Define reflection: Smooth surfaces reflect light regularly.
- Contrast materials: Cardboard is rough (diffuse reflection).
- Apply to periscope: Rough surfaces scatter light in all directions.
- Conclude: Mirrors are needed for regular reflection to maintain the image.
ANSWER: Mirrors provide regular reflection, which keeps the light rays organized to form an image; cardboard would scatter the light in many directions, resulting in no clear image.
DIFFICULTY: hard
