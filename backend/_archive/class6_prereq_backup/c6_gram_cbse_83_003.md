## DEFINITION
Conjunctions are joining words — and/but/or join equals, because/so joins reason and result. 'I was tired, SO I slept' — 'so' carries the result. Wrong joiner = wrong logic: 'I was tired BUT I slept' makes the reader ask 'but?'.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student picks the joiner matching the logic (addition=and, contrast=but, choice=or, reason=because, result=so), combines two sentences with the right conjunction, and spots logic-flips.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the five core conjunctions. Does NOT cover subordinators in complex sentences (although, when, while — class 7+) or correlative pairs (either…or).

## WALKTHROUGH
The logic test: read the two halves — same direction (and), opposite direction (but), choice (or), cause→effect (because/so). 'He studied hard, SO he passed' — cause then result. 'He passed BECAUSE he studied hard' — result then cause. Same fact, different joiner, different emphasis.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students default to 'and' for everything, and swap because/so — writing 'He passed, so he studied hard' which reverses cause and effect.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Name the logic between the halves — add, contrast, choice, cause, result. Step 2: Pick the joiner that names that logic. Step 3: Because goes before the CAUSE; so goes before the RESULT — never swap.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Join correctly: 'He was tired. He kept working.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'He was tired, but he kept working' — contrast logic (expectation vs reality). A wrong answer 'and' misses that keeping-working contradicts the tiredness expectation.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Join: 'She was hungry. She ate two sandwiches.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'She was hungry, so she ate two sandwiches' — cause → result. A wrong answer 'because she ate' reverses causality — the sandwiches didn't cause the hunger.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Which is wrong and why: 'I wanted tea, but I drank tea.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The 'but' promises contrast that never arrives — should be 'and'. A wrong answer accepting it shows the student hears 'but' as a pause-word, not a contrast-signal.
