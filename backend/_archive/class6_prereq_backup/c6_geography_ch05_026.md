## DEFINITION
India’s neighbours are the countries that share land borders with India, located in South Asia, along with island nations situated in the nearby Indian Ocean. These neighbouring countries form the immediate geopolitical circle around India’s landmass.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to label all seven land-bordering countries and two major maritime island neighbours on a blank map of India. They must also correctly identify the cardinal direction (North, East, West, South) for each neighbour.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the identification and cardinal location of India's seven land neighbours and two primary island neighbours. It excludes detailed border disputes, specific maritime laws, and international foreign policy.
## WALKTHROUGH
If you were to invite all your friends to a party, you would start by looking at who lives right next door to your house, wouldn't you? Think of India as a large house on a street; our "neighbours" are the countries whose land touches ours or who live just across the water. To begin, imagine standing in the center of India: look up to the North to see mountains, look to the East and West for plains, and look South into the blue ocean. We use a compass-like approach to group these countries by their direction from us. By identifying these neighbours, you begin to see how India fits into the larger map of Asia and the world.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Name the two countries that share a border with India to the North.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The two countries are China and Nepal. A student who misses one or names a country in a different direction (like Pakistan) has not yet memorized the directional placement of the neighbours on the map.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why Sri Lanka is described as an "island neighbour" rather than a land neighbour.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Sri Lanka is an island neighbour because it is separated from India by a body of water (the Palk Strait) and has no connected land border. A student who says "it is too small" misses the point that it is the presence of water, not the size, that defines the maritime boundary.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
You would encounter Pakistan. If a student says China or Bangladesh, they are confusing the cardinal directions of the Indian map.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
**Correct Answer:** If you are traveling from the westernmost part of India (Gujarat), you are most likely to encounter Pakistan, which shares a long international land border with the state. 

**Explanation:** A wrong answer reveals that the student has a fundamental misconception regarding India’s political geography and map orientation, likely confusing the cardinal directions or misidentifying India's neighboring countries. It may also suggest the student is relying on rote memorization of a list of neighbors without understanding their specific geographical placement relative to Indian states.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the location of Bangladesh to that of Bhutan; how do their positions relative to India differ?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Bangladesh is located to the northeast and shares a long border with India's eastern states, while Bhutan is situated further north, nestled in the Himalayas between India and China. Confusing these two suggests the student is not paying attention to the unique "shape" of India’s northeastern region on the map.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it accurate to say that all of India’s neighbours share a land border? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it is inaccurate because countries like Sri Lanka and the Maldives are separated from India by the ocean. A student who says "yes" fails to distinguish between land-based connectivity and maritime proximity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a mnemonic or a simple sentence to help a classmate remember the countries that border India.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer would be a creative sentence like "China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Pakistan, and Afghanistan are our land friends." A poor answer simply lists them without a structure, which does not demonstrate a creative grasp of the material.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the placement of the "Seven Sisters" states in the Northeast with the borders of Myanmar and Bangladesh. They often think Myanmar is to the North instead of the East. This happens because the map of Northeast India looks complex, and students struggle to orient themselves when the land curves.
## ON_FAILURE
First, have the student trace the outline of India with their finger, pausing at the "neck" of the country. Then, ask them to place a dot for each neighbour, starting from the West (Pakistan) and moving clockwise. Say: "Imagine you are drawing a circle around India; put a sticker on every country you touch on the way."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You are planning a road trip from Delhi to the border of a northern neighbour. Which one would you choose, and what direction are you heading?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify northern neighbours (China, Nepal).
- 2: Check map for proximity to Delhi.
- 3: Determine cardinal direction (North).
- 4: Conclude the destination.
REASONING: By narrowing down the neighbours by direction, we confirm that Nepal is the most accessible northern border from Delhi.
ANSWER: You would head North to reach the border of Nepal.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A ship leaves the port of Kochi and travels south. Which island nation is it likely to reach?
STEPS:
- 1: Locate Kochi on the map of India.
- 2: Identify direction of travel (South).
- 3: Identify island neighbours in the Indian Ocean.
- 4: Determine which is further south from the Indian coast.
REASONING: The Maldives is situated in the Indian Ocean, directly south of the Indian peninsula.
ANSWER: It is likely to reach the Maldives.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: List India's neighbours by grouping them into those that share land borders and those that are island nations.
STEPS:
- 1: List all seven land neighbours: Pakistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Afghanistan.
- 2: List the two main island neighbours: Sri Lanka, Maldives.
- 3: Verify no overlap.
- 4: Organize into a clear list.
REASONING: Categorization helps in memorizing the physical relationship India has with each country.
ANSWER: Land Neighbours: Pakistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Afghanistan. Island Neighbours: Sri Lanka, Maldives.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you were walking along India's eastern border, which two countries would you be walking between at the point where they meet India?
COMPETENCY: Spatial Reasoning
STEPS:
- 1: Look at the Northeast map.
- 2: Locate Bangladesh.
- 3: Locate Myanmar.
- 4: Note their positions relative to the Indian state of Mizoram/Manipur.
ANSWER: You would be walking near the borders of Bangladesh and Myanmar, which both lie to the East/Northeast of India.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why does the map show a "disputed" border with Afghanistan in the North?
COMPETENCY: Critical Thinking
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the northernmost point of India.
- 2: Locate the area where India touches Afghanistan.
- 3: Recognize that political control in that region is complex.
- 4: Explain that maps reflect these complexities as "disputed."
ANSWER: The border is marked as disputed because the region is currently involved in political and territorial disagreements that prevent a clear, universally recognized boundary line.
DIFFICULTY: hard
