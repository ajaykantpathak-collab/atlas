## DEFINITION
The water cycle is the continuous movement of water on Earth through the atmosphere, land, and oceans, powered by the Sun's energy. It involves heat transfer processes—radiation, convection, and evaporation—that change water between liquid, vapour, and solid states.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wondered how a puddle disappears on a sunny day, or how rain clouds form high up in the sky? Start by imagining the Sun as a giant heater; its rays (radiation) warm up the water in lakes and oceans, giving the water molecules enough energy to turn into invisible vapour (evaporation). Because warm air is lighter than cold air, it naturally rises into the sky in a process called convection. As this air travels higher and gets colder, the vapour turns back into liquid droplets to form clouds, eventually falling back to earth as rain or snow. The key insight is that nature uses heat transfer to recycle water over and over again.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the primary source of energy that drives the entire water cycle?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The Sun. A wrong answer here suggests the student does not understand that the water cycle requires a constant energy input to move water from the liquid phase to the vapour phase.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why does the moist air containing water vapour rise into the atmosphere?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Because warm air is less dense than the surrounding cooler air, it rises through the process of convection. A student saying "because it is light" is partially correct but missing the formal concept of convection currents.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If you place a bowl of water in the shade versus in direct sunlight, which will evaporate faster? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The bowl in direct sunlight will evaporate faster because it receives more radiation from the Sun, increasing the temperature of the water molecules. A wrong answer indicates a failure to link heat energy (radiation) to the rate of evaporation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How does the cooling of air high in the atmosphere affect the water vapour within it?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Cooling causes the water vapour to lose energy and condense into liquid water droplets, which form clouds. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks water vapour stays a gas regardless of temperature.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Could the water cycle occur if the Earth were not receiving any heat from the Sun?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, because without the Sun's radiation, there would be no energy to cause evaporation, and therefore no water vapour to rise and form clouds. This tests the student’s ability to judge the necessity of energy input in a physical system.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple experiment using a glass of ice water on a hot day to represent part of the water cycle.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The student should describe how water droplets form on the outside of a cold glass. This represents condensation, the opposite of evaporation, showing the phase change triggered by cooling.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that water "disappears" or vanishes into thin air during evaporation, rather than changing state into an invisible gas (vapour). They also frequently confuse the cooling of air in the atmosphere with the air simply "holding" less water, rather than the phase change (condensation) occurring due to the loss of heat.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to place a lid on a pot of boiling water and observe the droplets on the underside. Explain that the steam is the water vapour, and when it hits the cooler lid, it turns back into liquid water, proving that water does not disappear but merely changes state based on heat.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A farmer in a village has a small pond. On a hot summer day (40°C), he notices the water level dropping. If the pond receives 500 units of solar radiation energy per hour, what is happening to the water?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the source of energy (Sun's radiation).
- Step 2: Identify the process (evaporation caused by heat gain).
- Step 3: Explain the movement (water turns to vapour).
- Step 4: Explain the rise (convection carries the warm vapour upwards).
REASONING: The solar radiation provides the thermal energy needed for molecules to break free from the liquid surface. This increase in energy causes the water level to decrease as it transitions to gas.
ANSWER: The water is evaporating due to solar radiation and rising via convection.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In a coastal town, warm air over the sea rises and moves inland. It cools down over the mountains. What happens to the water vapour in this air?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the state of water (vapour in air).
- Step 2: Identify the change in condition (cooling).
- Step 3: Determine the result of cooling (condensation).
- Step 4: Predict the outcome (cloud formation/rain).
REASONING: When air cools, it can no longer hold as much water vapour, leading to condensation. This is the stage where the cycle produces precipitation.
ANSWER: The water vapour condenses into clouds and eventually falls as rain.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Compare the energy source for heating a cup of tea (by a stove) and heating the ocean (by the Sun). Are they the same mode of heat transfer?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the stove's heat transfer (conduction/convection).
- Step 2: Identify the Sun's heat transfer (radiation).
- Step 3: Compare the two mechanisms.
- Step 4: Conclude if they are the same.
REASONING: The stove heats the cup through direct contact (conduction) and the air/liquid through movement (convection). The Sun heats the ocean across the vacuum of space, meaning it must be radiation.
ANSWER: No, they are different. The stove uses conduction/convection, while the Sun uses radiation.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Why does the water cycle not stop at night? Think about the energy stored in the Earth's surface.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating and reasoning about continuous processes.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Acknowledge that the Sun is not present at night.
- Step 2: Consider the heat absorbed by water bodies during the day.
- Step 3: Recognize that the water cycle is a continuous, large-scale process.
- Step 4: Conclude that heat already in the system keeps evaporation going, though at a slower rate.
ANSWER: The water cycle continues because large bodies of water act as heat reservoirs, storing energy from the day and releasing it slowly at night, allowing evaporation to persist even in the absence of direct sunlight.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: If you were to cover an entire lake with a giant plastic sheet, how would the water cycle in that specific area change? Explain using heat transfer concepts.
COMPETENCY: Applying concepts to hypothetical scenarios.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the role of radiation.
- Step 2: Identify the barrier to evaporation.
- Step 3: Analyze the role of convection.
- Step 4: Synthesize the impact on the local water cycle.
ANSWER: The plastic sheet would block radiation from reaching the water and physically prevent water vapour from escaping into the atmosphere, effectively stopping the evaporation and convection steps of the water cycle in that area.
DIFFICULTY: hard
