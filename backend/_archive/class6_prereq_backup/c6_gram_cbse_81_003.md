## DEFINITION
Numbers: singular means one, plural means more than one — but English pluralizes in five ways: +s (books), +es (boxes), y→ies (cities), f→ves (leaves), irregular (children, feet, mice), and some never change (sheep, news).

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
remember

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student forms plurals by the five rules, produces the irregular set from memory, and catches double-plural errors ('childs', 'sheeps', 'mices').

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers regular rules, the common irregular set, and unchanged plurals. Does NOT cover foreign plurals (criterion/criteria) — class 9+.

## WALKTHROUGH
Sort objects on the desk by ending: book+s, box+es (hiss-sounds s/x/ch/sh need es), city→cities (consonant+y drops y), leaf→leaves (f→v+es). Then the rebels that must be memorized: child→children, foot→feet, mouse→mice, sheep→sheep.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students apply +s universally → 'childs', 'mouses', 'foots' — and add -s to uncountables → 'waters', 'furnitures', 'homeworks'.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Check the ending letter — s/x/ch/sh → es; consonant+y → ies; f/fe → ves. Step 2: Check the irregular list — is it a memorized rebel? Step 3: Check countability — uncountables take no plural at all.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Pluralize: 'box', 'baby', 'knife', 'child', 'sheep'.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
boxes, babies, knives, children, sheep. Wrong answers 'boxs/childs/sheeps' show rule-gaps at the hiss-sound, irregular, and unchanged steps respectively.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Fix: 'I have many homeworks and two luggages.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'much homework and two pieces of luggage' — both uncountable, no plurals. A wrong answer keeping the -s shows the student doesn't yet sort nouns into countable vs uncountable.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'The ___(mouse/mice) ate the cheese' vs 'All the ___ ran away.' Which form in each?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
First: mouse (one). Second: mice (many). A wrong answer 'mouses' or singular-for-plural shows the irregular form isn't yet automatic.
