## DEFINITION
Simplifying an algebraic expression means using mathematical operations to group "like terms" together, reducing the expression to its most compact and readable form. A "like term" is a term that shares the exact same variable part (e.g., $3x$ and $5x$ are like, but $3x$ and $5y$ are not).
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify variable terms vs. constants and successfully combine all coefficients of like variables into a single expression. Mastery is demonstrated by reducing expressions like $4a + 3b + 2a - b$ to $6a + 2b$.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the identification and combination of like terms and the use of the distributive property to remove basic parentheses. It excludes factoring, polynomial division, and expressions involving exponents (e.g., $x^2$).
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are sorting a basket containing 3 apples, 2 bananas, 5 more apples, and 1 more banana. You wouldn't count them as "11 fruit" if you want to know exactly what you have; you would say "8 apples and 3 bananas." Algebra works exactly the same way! When you look at an expression like $3x + 5 + 2x - 3$, think of '$x$' as one type of item (apples) and the plain numbers as another type (bananas). You "sort" the $x$ terms together ($3x$ and $2x$) and the plain numbers together ($5$ and $-3$). By grouping these "like" items, you simplify the clutter into a clear, short answer: $5x + 2$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the definition of a "like term" in an algebraic expression?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Like terms are terms that have the exact same variable part (e.g., $2y$ and $7y$). A student who says "all numbers are like terms" confuses constants with coefficients, failing to recognize that variables define the "type" of the term.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Can we simplify $4x + 3y$ into $7xy$? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, it cannot be simplified because $x$ and $y$ are different variables. A student who adds them to get $7xy$ or $7$ has failed to understand that variables act as labels that cannot be combined unless they match.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Simplify the expression: $7a - 2b + 3a + 5b$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
First, group the 'a' terms ($7a + 3a = 10a$) and the 'b' terms ($-2b + 5b = 3b$). The result is $10a + 3b$. A student who calculates $13ab$ or $10a - 3b$ has missed the sign of the constant or attempted to multiply the variables instead of adding the coefficients.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why is $3x + 5$ already in its simplest form?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
It is simplest because one term has a variable ($x$) and the other is a constant ($5$), which are "unlike" terms. A student who says "it equals $8x$" ignores the difference between a variable term and a standalone constant.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the following: $2(x + 4) + 3x$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Distribute the $2$ to get $2x + 8$, then add $3x$ to get $5x + 8$. If a student gets $2x + 4 + 3x = 5x + 4$, they have forgotten to multiply the second term inside the parentheses by the coefficient outside.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create an expression that has three terms, where two are like terms and one is a constant. Then, simplify it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An example is $5m + 3 + 2m$. The simplification is $7m + 3$. A poor answer would be $5m + 3 + 2n$, which contains no like terms, showing the student doesn't grasp how to construct a solvable expression.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to add variables as if they were just numbers, leading them to claim $5x + 2 = 7x$. They ignore the fact that the '2' does not have an 'x' attached, treating the constant as if it belongs to the variable family.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student: "If you have 5 bags of sugar and 2 kilograms of rice, do you have 7 bags of sugar?" They will say no. Explain that $5x$ is like 5 bags of sugar and $2$ is like 2 kilograms of rice. You can't combine them because they aren't the same "unit" or "type."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper sells $5$ kg of rice bags at $x$ rupees each and $2$ kg of wheat bags at $y$ rupees each. Later, he sells $3$ more rice bags at $x$ rupees each and $4$ more wheat bags at $y$ rupees each. Write and simplify an expression for the total cost.
STEPS:
- identify: Rice cost = $5x + 3x$; Wheat cost = $2y + 4y$.
- formula: Total = $(5x + 3x) + (2y + 4y)$.
- apply: Grouping like terms: $(5+3)x + (2+4)y$.
- compute: $8x + 6y$.
- verify: The total quantity of rice is 8 bags and wheat is 6 bags.
REASONING: By sorting the items by type (rice vs wheat), we can consolidate the quantities, making the calculation easier to handle for the shopkeeper.
ANSWER: $8x + 6y$ rupees.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Simplify $10a - 3b + 4 - 2a + 5b - 1$.
STEPS:
- identify: $a$ terms: $10a, -2a$; $b$ terms: $-3b, 5b$; constants: $4, -1$.
- formula: $(10a - 2a) + (-3b + 5b) + (4 - 1)$.
- apply: Combine coefficients: $(10-2)a + (-3+5)b + (4-1)$.
- compute: $8a + 2b + 3$.
- verify: All groups have been combined once; no terms overlap.
REASONING: We move terms to be adjacent to their like counterparts, which prevents us from accidentally adding an 'a' to a 'b'.
ANSWER: $8a + 2b + 3$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Simplify $3(2x + 1) - 4x + 5$.
STEPS:
- identify: Need to distribute the 3 across the parentheses first.
- formula: $(3 \times 2x) + (3 \times 1) - 4x + 5$.
- apply: $6x + 3 - 4x + 5$.
- compute: $(6x - 4x) + (3 + 5)$.
- compute: $2x + 8$.
- verify: Check distribution ($6x+3$) and addition ($2x+8$).
REASONING: The parentheses act as a container; we must multiply everything inside before we can combine it with the terms outside.
ANSWER: $2x + 8$.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Rahul says $3x + 2x = 5x^2$. Is he correct? Explain why or why not.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction.
STEPS:
- understand: Rahul added the coefficients but also multiplied the variable $x \cdot x$.
- identify: Simplification involves addition of coefficients, not powers.
- apply: $3x + 2x$ is like $3$ apples plus $2$ apples, which is $5$ apples ($5x$).
- verify: $5x^2$ would mean $5$ times $x$ times $x$.
ANSWER: Rahul is incorrect. When we add like terms, we only add the coefficients. The variable stays exactly the same because it represents the "type" of item we are counting.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: You are given two expressions: $A = 4x + 2y - 1$ and $B = x - 3y + 5$. Find $A + B$.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions.
STEPS:
- understand: We need to combine all like terms from both expressions.
- identify: Gather $x$ terms ($4x + x$), $y$ terms ($2y - 3y$), and constants ($-1 + 5$).
- apply: $(4+1)x + (2-3)y + (-1+5)$.
- compute: $5x - 1y + 4$.
- verify: Simplify $-1y$ to $-y$.
ANSWER: The expression is $5x - y + 4$. We added the $x$'s to get $5x$, combined $2y$ and $-3y$ to get $-y$, and calculated the total constant as $4$.
DIFFICULTY: hard
