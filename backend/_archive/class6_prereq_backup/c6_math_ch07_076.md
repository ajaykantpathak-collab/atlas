## DEFINITION
A circle is a closed plane figure where every point on the edge is at an equal distance from a fixed center point; the line segments describing this shape include the radius (center to edge), diameter (edge to edge through center), and chord (any two points on the edge).
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to draw, label, and distinguish between a radius, diameter, chord, and arc on a given circle and calculate the diameter when the radius is provided.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the identification and basic properties of radii, diameters, chords, and arcs for 2D circles. It does not cover tangents, secants, inscribed angles, or 3D spherical geometry.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever looked at a bicycle wheel or a round dinner plate and wondered how we describe its parts? To begin, imagine a single dot in the middle of a paper—that is your center. If you use a compass to draw around it, every point you create is exactly the same distance from that center. We start by drawing a line from the center to any edge (the radius), then extend it all the way across through the center (the diameter) to see that the diameter is just two radii joined together. Finally, we look at any two points on the edge and connect them with a straight line (a chord) or follow the curve between them (an arc) to see the full "anatomy" of the circle.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the name of the line segment that connects two points on a circle and passes through the center?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The line segment is called the diameter. A student who answers "radius" confuses the line from the center to the edge with the line stretching across the entire circle.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a circle has a radius of 5 cm, why is it incorrect to say the diameter is 5 cm as well?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It is incorrect because the diameter must stretch from one side to the other, covering two radii; thus, it is 10 cm. A student who says it remains 5 cm fails to understand that the diameter represents the full width, not just the distance from the center.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A circular park has a diameter of 20 meters. If a path is built from the center to the edge, how long will the path be?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The path (radius) will be 10 meters. We find this by dividing the diameter (20m) by 2 (20 ÷ 2 = 10). A student who answers 40m has mistakenly multiplied instead of dividing.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why is the diameter considered a special type of chord?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The diameter is a chord because it connects two points on the circle, but it is special because it is the longest possible chord as it passes through the center. A student who cannot explain this does not see the relationship between a general chord and the diameter.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If you draw a chord that does not pass through the center, is it still a diameter? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it is not a diameter. A diameter must pass through the center to be the longest line segment; any other chord is shorter. A student who says "yes" does not understand the unique requirement of the diameter passing through the center.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Draw a circle and mark a point A on the edge and point B on the edge. Draw a chord AB and an arc AB. How do they differ?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The chord AB is a straight line segment, while the arc AB is the curved path along the edge of the circle. A poor answer would fail to distinguish between the straight distance and the curved boundary.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that any line segment drawn inside a circle is a diameter or a radius. They fail to realize that the radius MUST start at the center and the diameter MUST pass through the center. For example, a student might label a random short chord as a radius because they see it "inside" the circle.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student misidentifies parts, have them use a compass to draw a circle. First, ask them to identify the center point they poked with the metal tip. Tell them: "Any line starting from that specific hole is a radius." Then, have them draw a line through that hole to the other side to create a diameter. This physical action reinforces that the center point is the "anchor" for these measurements.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A circular garden in a park has a radius of 7 meters. A gardener wants to place a rope across the widest part of the garden. How long should the rope be?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify that the "widest part" refers to the diameter of the circle.
- step 2: Identify the given radius (r = 7m).
- step 3: Use the formula for diameter: d = 2 × r.
- step 4: Calculate: d = 2 × 7 = 14m.
- step 5: Conclude that the rope must be 14 meters long.
REASONING: Since the diameter is twice the length of the radius, and the diameter represents the widest span of the circle, doubling the radius gives us the correct length.
ANSWER: 14 meters
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An athlete runs along the curved edge of a semi-circular track. If the total distance across the straight part of the track (the diameter) is 100 meters, what is the length of the radius from the center of the track?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the diameter (d = 100m).
- step 2: Identify that we need to find the radius (r).
- step 3: Use the formula relating radius to diameter: r = d / 2.
- step 4: Calculate: r = 100 / 2 = 50m.
- step 5: Verify that two radii (50 + 50) equal the diameter (100).
REASONING: The radius is always half of the diameter because the diameter consists of two radii extending in opposite directions from the center.
ANSWER: 50 meters
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A circular metal plate has a chord that is 8 cm long, but this chord does not pass through the center. If the radius of the plate is 5 cm, is this chord a diameter? Explain.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the length of the given chord (8 cm).
- step 2: Identify the radius of the circle (5 cm).
- step 3: Calculate the theoretical diameter of the circle (d = 2 × 5 cm = 10 cm).
- step 4: Compare the chord length (8 cm) with the diameter length (10 cm).
- step 5: Determine that since the chord is not the same length as the diameter and does not pass through the center, it cannot be a diameter.
REASONING: A diameter is defined as the longest chord passing through the center; here, the longest possible chord is 10 cm. Since 8 cm < 10 cm and the problem states it doesn't pass through the center, it fails the definition of a diameter.
ANSWER: No, it is not a diameter.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Imagine you have a circular pizza. Your friend says that if they cut a straight line from the edge to the center, they have created a diameter. Is your friend correct? Explain your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and justification
STEPS:
- step 1: Define what a diameter is (a line segment passing through the center from one edge to the other).
- step 2: Compare the friend's cut (edge to center) to the definition.
- step 3: Identify that the friend only described a radius.
- step 4: Formulate the explanation.
ANSWER: No, the friend is incorrect. Cutting from the edge to the center creates a radius. A diameter must continue from that center point all the way to the opposite edge.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: You are given a circle and asked to draw the longest possible line segment within it. Where must this line pass, and what is the name of this segment?
COMPETENCY: Translating geometric properties to definitions
STEPS:
- step 1: Recognize that the longest line in a circle must span the entire width.
- step 2: Relate the width of a circle to the center point.
- step 3: Identify that the line must pass through the center.
- step 4: Name the segment.
ANSWER: This line must pass through the center of the circle, and it is called the diameter. It is the longest segment because any other chord not passing through the center is restricted by the geometry of the circle to be shorter.
DIFFICULTY: hard
