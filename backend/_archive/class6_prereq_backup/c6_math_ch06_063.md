## DEFINITION
Equivalent ratios are two or more ratios that represent the same relative value, like simplifying a fraction, while a proportion is an equation stating that two ratios are equal. A proportion is written as $a:b = c:d$, meaning the relationship between $a$ and $b$ is identical to the relationship between $c$ and $d$.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify if two ratios form a proportion using the cross-product rule and generate equivalent ratios by multiplying or dividing both terms by the same non-zero number.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers identifying equivalent ratios and verifying proportions using the cross-product rule. It excludes continued proportions, inverse proportions, and compound proportions.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are making lemonade with a specific recipe: 2 cups of sugar for every 3 cups of water. If you want to make a larger batch, would 4 cups of sugar and 6 cups of water taste the same? To find out, you can check if these ratios are "equivalent" by seeing if one can be turned into the other by multiplying both numbers by the same value. A proportion simply puts an equal sign between these two ratios, like $2:3 = 4:6$. To quickly check if any two ratios are a proportion, we use the "cross-product rule": multiply the outer numbers (extremes) and the inner numbers (means); if the results are equal, the ratios are in proportion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What does it mean for two ratios to be "equivalent"?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Equivalent ratios represent the same comparison or relationship between two quantities. A wrong answer like "the numbers are the same" reveals the student confuses equality of numbers with equality of ratios.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you have the ratio 3:5, is 6:10 an equivalent ratio? Explain why.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Yes, because both terms of 3:5 are multiplied by 2 to get 6:10 ($3 \times 2 = 6$ and $5 \times 2 = 10$). A student who says "no" likely fails to see that ratios can be scaled up or down while maintaining the same relationship.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Use the cross-product rule to check if 2:4 and 5:10 are in proportion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
$2 \times 10 = 20$ and $4 \times 5 = 20$. Since $20 = 20$, they are in proportion. A wrong answer suggests the student is either multiplying the wrong terms or does not understand the equality requirement.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If $a:b = c:d$, why must $a \times d$ equal $b \times c$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Because $a/b = c/d$ is equivalent to $a \times d = b \times c$ when you clear the denominators. A student who struggles here lacks the algebraic understanding of how equations work.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is 2:3 equivalent to 4:7? Defend your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No. Using the cross-product rule: $2 \times 7 = 14$ and $3 \times 4 = 12$. Since $14 \neq 12$, they are not equivalent. A student claiming they are equivalent is likely just guessing without using a mathematical rule.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create your own proportion using the ratio 1:4 and show why it is correct.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A correct answer would be 1:4 = 2:8, because $1 \times 8 = 8$ and $4 \times 2 = 8$. A poor answer would show random numbers that don't satisfy the cross-product rule.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to add the same number to both parts of a ratio to find an equivalent ratio (e.g., changing 2:3 to 3:4). This is incorrect because addition changes the proportional relationship, whereas only multiplication or division preserves it.
## ON_FAILURE
Explain that a ratio represents a "multiplicative" relationship, not an "additive" one. Show them that 2/3 is not 3/4 by checking the cross-products: $2 \times 4 = 8$ but $3 \times 3 = 9$. Remind them that they must use a multiplication table to find equivalent values.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper sells 5 pencils for ₹10. If a student buys 15 pencils, how much should they pay if the ratio of pencils to price remains constant?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given ratio: 5 pencils : ₹10.
- step 2: Set up proportion: 5/10 = 15/x, where x is the cost for 15 pencils.
- step 3: Apply cross-product rule: $5 \times x = 10 \times 15$.
- step 4: Compute: $5x = 150$, so $x = 150 / 5$.
- step 5: Result: x = 30.
REASONING: By maintaining the same ratio, we ensure the unit price of the pencils stays constant regardless of the quantity purchased.
ANSWER: ₹30
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A recipe for 4 people uses 2 cups of rice. How much rice is needed for 10 people using the same proportion?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify ratios: 4 people : 2 cups = 10 people : x cups.
- step 2: Set up proportion: $4/2 = 10/x$.
- step 3: Apply cross-product: $4 \times x = 2 \times 10$.
- step 4: Compute: $4x = 20$, so $x = 20 / 4$.
- step 5: Result: x = 5.
REASONING: The proportion ensures that the amount of rice per person remains consistent across different group sizes.
ANSWER: 5 cups
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Two garden beds have dimensions. Bed A is 3m by 4m. Bed B is 9m by 12m. Are these beds proportional in shape?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify ratios of length and width: 3:9 and 4:12.
- step 2: Set up proportion: 3/9 = 4/12.
- step 3: Apply cross-product rule: $3 \times 12 = 36$ and $9 \times 4 = 36$.
- step 4: Compare products: $36 = 36$.
- step 5: Verify: Since the cross-products are equal, the ratios are equivalent.
REASONING: This confirms that Bed B is a perfect scaled-up version of Bed A.
ANSWER: Yes, the ratios are in proportion.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a map uses a scale of 1cm : 5km, how many kilometres does 7cm represent? Explain your reasoning using proportions.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions.
STEPS:
- step 1: Establish the ratio 1cm/5km.
- step 2: Set up the proportion 1/5 = 7/x.
- step 3: Use cross-multiplication: $1 \times x = 5 \times 7$.
- step 4: Solve for x.
ANSWER: 35km. The ratio 1:5 means for every 1cm on the map, there are 5km in reality; thus 7cm must represent $7 \times 5 = 35$km.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students, Ravi and Priya, argue about ratios. Ravi says 2:5 is the same as 4:10. Priya says 2:5 is the same as 5:8. Who is correct and why?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- step 1: Check Ravi’s proportion: $2 \times 10 = 20$, $5 \times 4 = 20$.
- step 2: Check Priya’s proportion: $2 \times 8 = 16$, $5 \times 5 = 25$.
- step 3: Compare results.
ANSWER: Ravi is correct because his cross-products are equal (20=20), meaning the ratios are equivalent. Priya is incorrect because $16 \neq 25$.
DIFFICULTY: hard
