## DEFINITION
Poetic devices are tools like acrostic form (using first letters to spell a word), imagery (using words to paint pictures), and repetition (repeating sounds or words) that help poets make their writing more vivid and meaningful.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify these three devices in a short poem and explain how they contribute to the poem's theme or mood.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers Acrostic form, Imagery, and Repetition. It excludes meter, rhyme schemes, and free verse analysis.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever tried to hide a secret message in a card you made for a friend? An acrostic poem does exactly that by using the first letter of each line to spell a hidden word. When we read, our minds act like projectors—imagery helps us see the "movie" in our heads, like imagining a soldier on a snowy mountain. Finally, repetition is like a drumbeat in a song; it keeps our attention fixed on the most important feelings. To start, find the pattern of the letters, picture the scene described, and listen for the words that keep coming back.

What is an acrostic poem?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
An acrostic poem is one where the first letter of each line spells out a word vertically. A wrong answer suggests the student is confusing it with a regular paragraph, indicating they need to practice looking at the left-hand margin of poems.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a poem describes a "scorching, dry, golden desert," which poetic device is the author using?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The author is using imagery. A wrong answer (like "repetition") shows the student does not yet distinguish between descriptive sensory language and word patterns.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Find the repetition in this line: "The wind sings, the wind calls, the wind dances." How does it change the feeling?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The repetition is "the wind." It emphasizes that the wind is the main character or focus of the scene. A wrong answer suggests the student is looking for rhyming words rather than repeated phrases.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze this: Why might a poet choose to spell the word "BRAVE" in an acrostic poem about soldiers?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The poet chooses "BRAVE" to reinforce the poem's theme and make the reader think about the qualities of a soldier immediately. A wrong answer shows the student sees the acrostic as just a "puzzle" rather than a tool for meaning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Critique this: "A poem without imagery is just as good as a poem with imagery." Do you agree?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
I disagree because imagery allows the reader to visualize the experience, making the poem personal and emotional. A student who agrees likely struggles to connect with the sensory details in text.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a 3-line acrostic poem about a "PET" using the letters P-E-T.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
P-Playing in the sun, E-Eating all my food, T-Tail wags at me. A poor answer uses words that don't relate to a pet, showing a lack of focus on the theme.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think "repetition" means the same thing as "rhyme." They confuse the sound of the end of a line (rhyme) with the actual repeating of a word or phrase throughout the poem (repetition).
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to read the poem aloud and tap their desk only when they hear the *exact same word* repeated. Explain that rhyme is about matching sounds at the end, while repetition is about bringing back a word to make it "loud" or important.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Analyze the imagery in the phrase "The wind-whipped sky."
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the key nouns and adjectives (wind, whipped, sky).
- step 2: Think about what "whipped" suggests (movement, speed, force).
- step 3: Visualize the scene (a sky where wind is moving clouds or kites quickly).
- step 4: Connect to the feeling (the scene feels energetic and wild).
REASONING: By breaking down the verbs used as adjectives, we see how the poet creates a specific visual.
ANSWER: The phrase creates an image of a fast, powerful, and energetic sky, helping the reader feel the movement of the wind.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Identify the acrostic in a poem where lines start with S-O-N-G.
STEPS:
- step 1: Write down the first letter of each line.
- step 2: Read them vertically (S-O-N-G).
- step 3: Identify the theme word (SONG).
- step 4: Conclude that the poem is about music or melody.
REASONING: Vertical reading extracts the hidden theme.
ANSWER: The hidden word is SONG, indicating the poem is likely about a melody or singing.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Combine imagery and repetition to describe a sunrise.
STEPS:
- step 1: Select a repeated word for emphasis (e.g., "Gold").
- step 2: Add imagery for the sky (e.g., "burning orange").
- step 3: Draft lines: "Gold light wakes the world, Gold colors the clouds, Gold shines on the river."
- step 4: Check if imagery is vivid (Yes: burning, waking, shining).
- step 5: Check if repetition is clear (Yes: "Gold" is repeated).
REASONING: Repeating a strong visual word makes the image "pop" for the reader.
ANSWER: "Gold light wakes the world, Gold paints the clouds, Gold warms the river."
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: In a poem about a mountain, if the poet repeats the word "High" three times, what is the effect?
COMPETENCY: Literary Analysis
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the repeated word.
- step 2: Consider what "High" describes (the height of the mountain).
- step 3: Interpret the effect (emphasizing scale).
- step 4: Explain why (repetition makes the mountain seem taller to the reader).
ANSWER: The repetition emphasizes the vast height of the mountain, making the reader feel small in comparison.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Read a stanza and pick an image that appeals to your sense of sight. Explain why it is imagery.
COMPETENCY: Critical Thinking
STEPS:
- step 1: Scan the poem for descriptive adjectives.
- step 2: Select a phrase (e.g., "coloured birds").
- step 3: Explain the visual (kites looking like birds).
- step 4: State why it is imagery (it paints a picture in the mind).
- step 5: Reflect on the impact (it helps us imagine the sky).
ANSWER: The phrase "coloured birds" is imagery because it uses a visual comparison to help us see the kites flying in the sky.
