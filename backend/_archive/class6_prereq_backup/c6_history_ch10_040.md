## DEFINITION
The Battle of Plassey (1757) was a pivotal event where the East India Company shifted from being a business group to a political power in India by defeating the Nawab of Bengal, Siraj-ud-Daulah.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to explain that the Battle of Plassey was won through political trickery rather than military force and identify the two main figures involved: Robert Clive and Siraj-ud-Daulah.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the transition of the East India Company to a political power and the role of treachery in the Battle of Plassey; it excludes military strategy, economic policies, or administrative structures.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are playing a team game, and instead of winning by playing better, someone on your team secretly agrees to help the other side win in exchange for a prize. This is exactly how the East India Company, led by Robert Clive, managed to win the Battle of Plassey. They didn't win because they were stronger; they won because they bribed the Nawab's general, Mir Jafar, to switch sides. By doing this, they removed the ruler of Bengal and started controlling the region. The key insight is that political power was gained through influence and betrayal, not just battlefield skill.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Who were the two main leaders facing each other during the Battle of Plassey in 1757?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The leaders were Robert Clive (for the Company) and Siraj-ud-Daulah (the Nawab of Bengal). A wrong answer suggests the student is mixing up historical figures, likely confusing them with later events like the 1857 Revolt.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Was the Battle of Plassey won primarily because of the Company’s superior weapons and training?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, it was won through treachery, specifically the bribery of Mir Jafar. A "Yes" answer reveals the student missed the core lesson about how the Company gained power through manipulation rather than raw military might.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If you were a historian, why would you say Mir Jafar’s decision to switch sides was the most important moment of the battle?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps: 1. Acknowledge Mir Jafar was the Nawab's general. 2. Note he held a large portion of the army. 3. Observe that his betrayal left the Nawab unprotected. 4. Conclude the battle's outcome was decided by this choice. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks battles are only about who has more soldiers.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How did the victory at Plassey change the status of the East India Company in India?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
It changed them from a purely trading group into a political power that could install its own choice of ruler. A wrong answer shows a failure to grasp the transition from "business" to "government."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it fair to call the victory at Plassey a "military" victory in the traditional sense?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, because a traditional military victory implies a fair fight of skill and strength, whereas this was a result of political betrayal. Justifying this shows the student understands the difference between a skirmish and a political takeover. A wrong answer indicates the student equates "winning a battle" only with "killing the enemy."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
If the Company had not bribed Mir Jafar, what do you think might have happened to their influence in Bengal?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
They likely would have remained simple traders, as they lacked the force to defeat the Nawab's army alone. A poor answer ignores the power gap between the small Company force and the vast Bengal army.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think the British won because they were "more modern" or "smarter" than the Indians. This is incorrect; the British won because they used local political instability and greed to their advantage. They didn't win by being better; they won by being more manipulative.
## ON_FAILURE
1. Ask the student: "If I bribe your goalie to let the ball in, did I win because I’m a better football player?" 2. Explain that the Company acted like the person who bribed the goalie. 3. Clarify that in 1757, the Company was small and needed local allies to gain power.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Why did the Company want the Nawab of Bengal defeated?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that the Nawab held the power in Bengal.
- Step 2: Note that the Company wanted to control trade without interference.
- Step 3: Recognize that the Nawab was a barrier to their influence.
- Step 4: Explain the move to replace him with a puppet ruler (Mir Jafar).
REASONING: This connects the "why" (power/trade) to the "how" (the battle).
ANSWER: The Company wanted to remove a ruler who restricted them and install someone who would listen to them.
