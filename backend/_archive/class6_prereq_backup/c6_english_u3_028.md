## DEFINITION
An adjective is a word that describes a noun (person, place, or thing), while an adverb is a word that describes an action (verb) by telling us how, when, or where it happens.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify adjectives and adverbs in a given sentence and correctly replace or add them to change the intensity or detail of a description.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the identification and function of adjectives and adverbs. Excludes adjective clauses, adverbial phrases, and comparative/superlative forms.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are describing your favorite tree, like a Neem tree. If you just say "The tree is big," it is a simple fact, but adding an adjective like "The *ancient* tree is big" creates a clearer picture. Now, if you want to describe how the leaves move, you add an adverb: "The leaves move *gently*." To begin, look for words that name things (nouns) and ask, "What kind of thing is it?" for adjectives, or look for action words (verbs) and ask, "How is the action done?" for adverbs. You will find that these words act like "detail-adders" to make your writing come alive.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the difference between an adjective and an adverb based on the words they describe?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
An adjective describes a noun (like 'green' leaf), whereas an adverb describes an action or verb (like 'slowly' falling). A wrong answer suggests the student confuses parts of speech; they might think both describe nouns, which ignores the 'action' aspect of adverbs.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
In the sentence, "The clever girl spoke softly," identify the adjective and the adverb and explain what each is describing.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'Clever' is the adjective describing the noun 'girl', and 'softly' is the adverb describing the verb 'spoke'. A wrong answer shows the student cannot distinguish between who is performing the action and how the action is performed.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Complete the sentence with an appropriate adverb: "The farmer planted the neem seeds _________."
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Possible answers: 'carefully', 'neatly', 'quickly'. To solve this, identify the verb 'planted' and choose a word ending in '-ly' that explains the manner of planting. A wrong answer suggests the student is choosing an adjective (like 'careful') instead of an adverb.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze this sentence: "The bright sun shone brightly." Why is 'bright' an adjective and 'brightly' an adverb?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
'Bright' modifies the noun 'sun', while 'brightly' modifies the action 'shone'. A wrong answer indicates the student does not understand that changing the word form allows it to perform a different grammatical function.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Critique the sentence: "The medicine worked quick." Is this grammatically correct? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It is incorrect because 'quick' is an adjective and should be the adverb 'quickly' to describe the verb 'worked'. A wrong answer reveals the student thinks adjectives can always describe actions, which is a common grammatical error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Write two sentences about a kitchen spice: one using an adjective to describe the spice and one using an adverb to describe how you use it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: "The 'yellow' turmeric is healthy." (Adjective). "I 'carefully' add the spice to the pot." (Adverb). A poor answer would lack the noun-adjective or verb-adverb relationship.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think all words ending in '-ly' are adverbs, or they try to use adjectives where adverbs are needed (e.g., "He ran fastly" or "He ran quick"). They confuse the form of the word with its function in the sentence.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to underline the verb in their sentence. Then, ask them: "Does the word describe the object, or does it describe the action?" If it describes the action, remind them to look for the '-ly' suffix or use a word that explains 'how' the action happened.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Identify the adjective and adverb in: "The hungry birds chirped loudly."
STEPS:
- 1: Find the noun (the person/thing): 'birds'.
- 2: Find the word describing the birds: 'hungry'. (Adjective)
- 3: Find the action word (verb): 'chirped'.
- 4: Find the word describing how they chirped: 'loudly'. (Adverb)
- 5: Verify: Does 'hungry' describe birds? Yes. Does 'loudly' describe how they chirped? Yes.
REASONING: Following the noun-verb path ensures every word is categorized by its function.
ANSWER: Adjective: 'hungry', Adverb: 'loudly'.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Turn the adjective 'soft' into an adverb and use it in a sentence about a breeze.
STEPS:
- 1: Recognize 'soft' is an adjective.
- 2: Apply the rule: add '-ly' to change it to an adverb: 'softly'.
- 3: Create an action: 'the breeze blew'.
- 4: Combine: 'The breeze blew softly.'
- 5: Check: 'Softly' describes how the breeze blew.
REASONING: By converting the adjective, the student learns how to change the focus from the object to the action.
ANSWER: "The breeze blew softly."
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Select the correct words to complete: "The (careful/carefully) gardener worked (steady/steadily)."
STEPS:
- 1: Analyze first gap: describes 'gardener' (noun). Use adjective: 'careful'.
- 2: Analyze second gap: describes 'worked' (verb). Use adverb: 'steadily'.
- 3: Combine: "The careful gardener worked steadily."
- 4: Review: Do the words fit their grammatical roles? Yes.
REASONING: Distinguishing between modifying a person (noun) vs. modifying an action (verb) is key to mastery.
ANSWER: "The careful gardener worked steadily."
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you change "The rain fell hard" to "The hard rain fell," how does the meaning change?
COMPETENCY: Semantic Analysis
STEPS:
- 1: Locate 'hard' in the first sentence (describing 'fell').
- 2: Locate 'hard' in the second sentence (describing 'rain').
- 3: Compare: The first is an adverb (how it fell), the second is an adjective (what kind of rain).
ANSWER: The meaning shifts from describing the action of the rain falling (adverb) to describing the quality of the rain itself (adjective).
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why can't we say "The neem tree grows healthily" if we want to describe the tree? How should we rephrase it?
COMPETENCY: Syntactic Correction
STEPS:
- 1: Identify 'healthily' as an adverb describing 'grows'.
- 2: Identify the intent: describe the 'tree' (noun).
- 3: Replace adverb with adjective: 'healthy'.
- 4: Rephrase: "The healthy neem tree grows."
ANSWER: 'Healthily' describes the action of growing, not the tree itself. To describe the tree, use the adjective 'healthy' before the noun.
DIFFICULTY: hard
