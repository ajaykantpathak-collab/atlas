## DEFINITION
A fraction represents a part of a whole. A **Proper fraction** has a numerator smaller than the denominator (value < 1), an **Improper fraction** has a numerator greater than or equal to the denominator (value ≥ 1), and a **Mixed number** expresses an improper fraction as a whole number combined with a proper fraction.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to classify any given fraction as proper, improper, or mixed, and convert between improper fractions and mixed numbers with 100% accuracy.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers identifying, classifying, and converting fractions greater than zero. It excludes negative fractions, complex fractions, and decimal conversions.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you have 5 rotis to share among 3 friends. How do you describe each person's share? You might say each gets "one and two-thirds" (a mixed number) or "five-thirds" (an improper fraction). To start, look at the top number (numerator) and the bottom number (denominator). If the top is smaller, it’s a "proper" slice of a single whole; if the top is bigger, you have more than one whole, making it "improper" or "mixed." Remember that improper fractions and mixed numbers are just two different ways of writing the same amount!
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the difference between a proper fraction and an improper fraction?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A proper fraction has a numerator smaller than the denominator, while an improper fraction has a numerator equal to or greater than the denominator. A student who says "proper is always smaller" may not realize that improper fractions can also represent values equal to 1 (like 4/4).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Can a mixed number ever be a proper fraction? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, a mixed number consists of a whole number plus a proper fraction, meaning its total value is always greater than 1. Answering "yes" shows confusion between the "fractional part" of the mixed number and the mixed number as a whole.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Convert 7/3 into a mixed number.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The answer is 2 1/3. Steps: Divide 7 by 3 to get 2 with a remainder of 1. The quotient 2 becomes the whole number, the remainder 1 becomes the numerator, and the denominator stays 3. Answering 3 1/2 shows the student tried to flip the numbers randomly rather than performing division.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If you have 1 3/4 kg of rice, why is this equivalent to 7/4 kg?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Each 1 whole contains 4/4, so 1 3/4 is (4/4 + 3/4) = 7/4. A wrong answer suggests the student added the whole number to the numerator (1+3=4) instead of converting the whole into the fractional unit.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Which is greater: 5/4 or 1 1/4? Justify your choice.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
They are equal. 1 1/4 means 1 + 1/4, which is 4/4 + 1/4 = 5/4. Answering that one is greater suggests the student sees different notation as proof of a different value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a scenario involving 2 whole chikkis and 1/3 of another chikki. How would you write this as both an improper fraction and a mixed number?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The mixed number is 2 1/3. The improper fraction is 7/3. A poor answer fails to realize that 2 wholes contain 6/3, leading to the total of 7/3.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to convert improper fractions to mixed numbers by simply placing the smaller number over the larger number regardless of which was originally the numerator. For example, converting 8/3 to 3 2/3 instead of 2 2/3. This happens because they focus on "fitting the numbers together" rather than performing the division of the total parts by the denominator.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student gets a conversion wrong, ask them to "draw it out." Have them draw circles to represent the denominator (e.g., 3 circles for thirds) and shade them until they reach the numerator (8). They will visually see 2 full circles and 2/3 of a third circle, which reinforces the division method (8 ÷ 3 = 2 remainder 2).

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Meena has 11/2 litres of milk. How much is this as a mixed number?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the numerator (11) and denominator (2).
- step 2: Divide 11 by 2.
- step 3: 11 ÷ 2 = 5 with a remainder of 1.
- step 4: Place the quotient (5) as the whole number, and the remainder (1) over the divisor (2).
- step 5: Result is 5 1/2 litres.
REASONING: Since 2/2 = 1 litre, dividing by 2 tells us how many "wholes" are hidden inside the 11 halves. The remainder shows the fractional part left over.
ANSWER: 5 1/2 litres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A tailor uses 2 3/4 metres of cloth for a shirt. Write this as an improper fraction.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the whole (2), numerator (3), and denominator (4).
- step 2: Multiply the whole by the denominator: 2 × 4 = 8.
- step 3: Add this to the existing numerator: 8 + 3 = 11.
- step 4: Place this sum over the original denominator: 11/4.
- step 5: The fraction is 11/4.
REASONING: Each whole consists of 4/4 parts, so 2 wholes consist of 8/4. Adding the extra 3/4 gives the total count of fourths.
ANSWER: 11/4 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: You have 3 2/5 kg of sugar and you receive 4/5 kg more. How much do you have total as a mixed number?
STEPS:
- step 1: Convert 3 2/5 to an improper fraction: (3 × 5 + 2) / 5 = 17/5.
- step 2: Add the extra sugar: 17/5 + 4/5 = 21/5.
- step 3: Convert the sum back to a mixed number by dividing: 21 ÷ 5 = 4 remainder 1.
- step 4: Write as 4 1/5.
REASONING: Converting to improper fractions first simplifies addition when denominators are the same, and the final division converts it back to the requested mixed format.
ANSWER: 4 1/5 kg
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Rahul says 5/3 is the same as 1 2/3. Is he correct? Explain your reasoning using a drawing of chocolate bars.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- step 1: Recognize 5/3 means five 1/3-pieces.
- step 2: Divide 5 by 3 to get 1 whole and 2/3 remainder.
- step 3: Draw two rectangles (bars) divided into thirds. Shade 5 pieces total.
- step 4: Observe 1 full bar is shaded and 2/3 of the second bar is shaded.
ANSWER: Yes, he is correct. Since 3/3 makes one whole, five 1/3-pieces equal one whole (3/3) and two extra 1/3-pieces, which is 1 2/3.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare 7/2 and 3 1/2. Which is larger? Show your work.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Convert 3 1/2 to an improper fraction: (3 × 2 + 1) / 2 = 7/2.
- step 2: Compare 7/2 to 7/2.
- step 3: Conclude they represent the same value.
ANSWER: Neither is larger; they are equal. Converting 3 1/2 to 7/2 shows they represent the exact same quantity.
DIFFICULTY: medium
