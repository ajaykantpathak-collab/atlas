## DEFINITION
The Delhi Sultanate refers to the period between 1206 and 1526 CE when five successive dynasties ruled parts of India from their capital, Delhi. These dynasties were the Mamluk (Slave), Khalji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, and Lodi dynasties.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are building a tower made of five different colored blocks, where each block represents a different ruling family. Just as you need to place the bottom block first, history requires us to know which family ruled first in Delhi. We start with the Slave dynasty, founded by Qutbuddin Aibak, and move forward through the Khalji dynasty. Think of these dynasties as shifts in power where the "boss" of the empire changes, but the capital remains the same. By memorizing the order of these five families, you gain a map of 300 years of Delhi's history.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Who was the founder of the Slave (Mamluk) dynasty?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Qutbuddin Aibak founded the Slave dynasty. A wrong answer here suggests the student is mixing up the founders of the different dynasties, likely confusing Aibak with the later Khalji rulers.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is Razia Sultan considered a unique figure in the history of the Delhi Sultanate?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
She was the only woman to ever rule the Delhi Sultanate. If a student answers incorrectly, it shows they are not distinguishing between the individual rulers and are perhaps ignoring the significance of gender roles in medieval governance.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If you were living in the year 1300 CE, which dynasty would you be living under?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
You would be under the Khalji dynasty. To solve this, students should compare the date 1300 against the provided ranges (Slave: 1206-1290; Khalji: 1290-1320). A wrong answer suggests a failure to correlate dates with the dynasty timeline.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the roles of Jalaluddin Khalji and Alauddin Khalji. How do their positions differ in the history of their dynasty?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Jalaluddin Khalji was the founder, while Alauddin Khalji was the most famous ruler who expanded the empire. A wrong answer shows a misunderstanding of the difference between "founding" an organization and "expanding" its influence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Does the Delhi Sultanate represent a single, unchanging family line? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it consisted of five different dynasties that replaced one another over 300 years. A wrong answer reveals the student thinks the "Sultanate" was one family tree rather than a sequence of different ruling houses.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a mnemonic or a short story to help remember the names of the five dynasties in order: Mamluk, Khalji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good response uses a memory trick like "My Kite Takes Some Light" (M-K-T-S-L). A poor answer simply lists the names without any structural memory aid, indicating difficulty in retention.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think the Delhi Sultanate was a single "kingdom" ruled by one king for 300 years. They fail to realize that a "dynasty" change often meant a violent or political shift where one royal family was replaced by another. This leads to confusion when they see multiple names like "Aibak" and "Khalji" as if they were related.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to draw a timeline with five boxes. Label each box with the name of a dynasty. Explain that each box represents a new family taking charge, much like changing the captain of a sports team. Have them read the names aloud to distinguish the distinct "sounds" of each dynasty.

PROBLEM: A student has to place the rulers Qutbuddin Aibak, Razia Sultan, and Alauddin Khalji in the correct chronological order.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the dynasty for each ruler (Aibak: Slave; Razia: Slave; Alauddin: Khalji).
- step 2: Check the dynasty dates (Slave: 1206-1290; Khalji: 1290-1320).
- step 3: Determine the relative position of the dynasties (Slave comes before Khalji).
- step 4: Within the Slave dynasty, order the rulers (Aibak founded it, Razia followed later).
- step 5: Sequence: Aibak -> Razia -> Alauddin.
REASONING: By grouping rulers by dynasty first, the timeline becomes logical. This helps organize historical figures into manageable blocks.
ANSWER: Qutbuddin Aibak, then Razia Sultan, then Alauddin Khalji.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Why would it be harder for a new dynasty to take over Delhi than for a king from the same dynasty to inherit the throne?
COMPETENCY: Historical Analysis
STEPS:
- step 1: Define what inheriting the throne means (keeping the same family).
- step 2: Define what a new dynasty means (a complete change in ruling family).
- step 3: Consider the impact on the existing army and nobles who supported the previous family.
- step 4: Conclude that changing a dynasty involves shifting loyalties and potential conflict, whereas inheriting does not.
ANSWER: Changing a dynasty involves a complete shift in power, often requiring the support of different groups, whereas inheriting is a continuation of the same family's rule.
DIFFICULTY: medium
