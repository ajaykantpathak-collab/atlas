## DEFINITION
Integers are a set of numbers that include all positive counting numbers (1, 2, 3...), their negative counterparts (-1, -2, -3...), and zero. They represent values on either side of zero, used to describe quantities like amounts below ground level or financial debt.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify integers on a number line, compare their values (knowing that -5 is smaller than -2), and represent real-world situations (like temperature or bank balances) using positive and negative signs.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the definition of integers, their representation on a number line, and their use in simple real-world comparisons. It excludes rational numbers, operations (addition/subtraction) on integers, and absolute value calculations.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are in an elevator of a building with a basement. When you press the '0' button, you are at the ground level (the Welcome Hall). If you press the button to go to the parking level in the basement, you have gone "below" the ground. We represent these basement levels with a minus sign, like -1 or -2. To start, think of zero as your reference point: numbers to the right are "positive" (above ground/credits), and numbers to the left are "negative" (below ground/debits). The key insight is that as you move further to the left of zero, the numbers actually represent smaller and smaller values.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the three components that make up the set of integers?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Integers consist of positive whole numbers, negative whole numbers, and zero. A wrong answer (like saying they include fractions) reveals a confusion between integers and rational numbers.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If the temperature in a city is -5°C and in another city it is -2°C, which city is colder? Explain why.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The city at -5°C is colder. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks -5 is "more" than -2 because 5 is greater than 2, failing to realize that negative numbers represent values further from zero in the "less than" direction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A bank account has a balance of 0. If you withdraw 500 rupees, how would you represent your new balance using an integer?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The balance is -500 rupees. The steps involve identifying 0 as the start and "withdrawal" as a decrease, requiring a negative sign. A wrong answer shows a misunderstanding of how negative signs represent "below zero" or debt.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the two numbers -10 and -1. Which is greater, and how does their position on the number line prove it?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
-1 is greater than -10. On a number line, any number to the right of another is greater; since -1 is closer to zero and to the right of -10, it is the larger value. A wrong answer indicates the student is ignoring the negative sign when comparing magnitudes.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is zero a positive or a negative integer? Defend your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Zero is neither positive nor negative; it is the neutral reference point. A wrong answer indicates a misunderstanding of the "boundary" nature of zero in the number system.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-life scenario where you would need to use a negative number, and explain what zero would represent in that specific scenario.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An example is measuring altitude: a mountain peak is +500m, while a valley is -100m; zero represents the sea level. A poor answer would be one that describes a quantity that cannot be negative, such as "number of people in a room," showing a lack of understanding of what negative numbers represent (opposite directions/states).
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that -10 is "bigger" than -1 because 10 is bigger than 1. This happens because they focus only on the numeral (the magnitude) and ignore the negative sign, which flips the relationship of the numbers.
## ON_FAILURE
Draw a horizontal number line on paper with 0 in the middle. Ask the student to place a marker at -1 and another at -10. Ask, "If you are walking from the negative side toward zero, which number do you hit first?" Explain that the number you reach first (the one closer to zero) is the larger one because it is further to the right.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A thermometer in a cold storage unit shows 0°C. The temperature drops by 3 degrees, then drops another 2 degrees. What integer represents the final temperature?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the starting point as 0.
- step 2: Identify "drops" as movement to the left (negative direction).
- step 3: The first drop of 3 degrees moves us to -3.
- step 4: The second drop of 2 degrees from -3 moves us further left to -5.
REASONING: Since the temperature is decreasing below the zero-degree freezing point, each drop represents a move into the negative integer range on the number line.
ANSWER: -5°C
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A diver is swimming at 5 meters below sea level. A submarine is at 15 meters below sea level. Represent their positions as integers and determine which one is deeper.
STEPS:
- step 1: Set sea level as the reference point (0).
- step 2: Assign -5 to the diver and -15 to the submarine.
- step 3: Compare -5 and -15 on a number line.
- step 4: Note that -15 is further to the left of 0 than -5.
REASONING: In vertical measurements, "below" indicates a negative value; the further the number is from zero in the negative direction, the greater the depth.
ANSWER: -5 and -15; the submarine is deeper.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A company starts with 0 rupees. It gains 200 rupees (credit), then spends 500 rupees (debit). What is the integer balance?
STEPS:
- step 1: Start at 0.
- step 2: A credit of 200 is represented as +200.
- step 3: A debit of 500 is a decrease, represented as -500.
- step 4: Combining 0 + 200 - 500 results in -300.
REASONING: Credits add to the balance (positive), while debits subtract from it (negative). A balance below zero represents debt.
ANSWER: -300 rupees
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student says, "Since -2 is smaller than -1, then -200 must be smaller than -100." Is this statement correct? Justify your answer using the number line.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand the student's comparison logic.
- step 2: Visualize the number line where -200 is much further to the left than -100.
- step 3: Confirm that numbers further to the left are smaller.
ANSWER: Yes, the statement is correct. On a number line, as you move left from zero, the values decrease. Since -200 is located further to the left of zero than -100, it represents a much smaller value.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: You are given three numbers: -8, 2, and -15. Arrange them in order from smallest to largest and explain your reasoning based on their distance from zero.
COMPETENCY: Ordering and sequencing
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the negative numbers (-8, -15) and the positive number (2).
- step 2: Place positive numbers as the largest.
- step 3: Compare the negative numbers by their distance from zero (-15 is further left than -8).
- step 4: Sequence as -15, -8, 2.
ANSWER: The order is -15, -8, 2. The smallest is -15 because it is furthest to the left on the number line. -8 is greater than -15 but less than 2, which is the only positive number and therefore the largest.
