## DEFINITION
A cylinder is a 3D solid object with two identical circular bases connected by a smooth, curved surface. Its volume measures the total space inside the cylinder, while its surface area measures the total area covering its curved side and two circular ends.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to calculate the volume and total surface area of a cylinder given its radius and height. They must demonstrate the ability to distinguish between the curved surface area and the total surface area in a word problem.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the volume, curved surface area, and total surface area of standard solid cylinders. It excludes cones, spheres, hollow cylinders, and any complex geometric combinations outside Class 6 NCERT expectations.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are buying a tin of energy drink. How much liquid can it hold, and how much metal sheet is needed to make the tin? To find the space inside (volume), we start with the area of the circular base (pi × radius × radius) and multiply it by how tall the tin is (height). If you want to cover the tin with a label, you look at the curved surface (circumference × height), and if you include the top and bottom lids, you add the area of those two circles to get the total surface area. Always remember: the base is a flat circle, and the height is the distance between the two circles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the three components you must calculate to find the total surface area of a cylinder?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The three components are the area of the top circle, the area of the bottom circle, and the area of the curved side. Answering only "the volume" or just "the circumference" shows the student is confusing area with volume or linear measurement.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you double the height of a cylinder but keep the radius the same, what happens to the volume?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The volume doubles. If a student says it quadruples, they are incorrectly applying the "square" from the radius formula to the height, showing a misunderstanding of how variables interact in the formula.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A cylinder has a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm. Using 3.14 for pi, calculate its volume.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Calculate base area = 3.14 × 3 × 3 = 28.26. Step 2: Multiply by height (10) = 282.6. Answer = 282.6 cm³. A wrong answer here likely stems from not squaring the radius (e.g., just doing 3.14 × 3 × 10).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why do we multiply the circumference of the base by the height to get the curved surface area?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Because the curved surface of a cylinder, if "unrolled," forms a rectangle where the width is the circumference and the length is the height. A student struggling here likely views the curved surface as a single line rather than a 2D shape.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it possible for two cylinders to have the same volume but different surface areas? Explain.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, because volume depends on the product of the base area and height, while surface area depends on the sum of these parts. A student who says "no" fails to realize that different combinations of radius and height can produce the same product (volume) but different individual values (surface area).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a container that holds exactly 1540 cm³ of water using a radius of 7 cm. What height must you choose?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The student should set up 1540 = (22/7) × 7 × 7 × h, simplify to 1540 = 154 × h, and find h = 10 cm. A poor answer ignores the formula and guesses numbers, showing a lack of algebraic connection to the geometry.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse "squaring" the radius (r × r) with "multiplying by two" (r × 2). For a radius of 3, they calculate 3 × 2 = 6 instead of 3 × 3 = 9. This happens because they over-generalize the "double" rule from the perimeter of a rectangle.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to draw a square on graph paper with a side of 3 and count the squares to find the area (9). Then ask them to draw a rectangle with sides 3 and 2 and count those (6). Show them that the area of a square is "side times side," which is why r × r is used for the circle base area, not r × 2.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A cylindrical water tank has a radius of 7m and a height of 5m. How much water can it hold?
STEPS:
- Identify: r = 7m, h = 5m, formula V = πr²h.
- Use 22/7 for π to make calculation easier.
- Square the radius: 7 × 7 = 49.
- Multiply: (22/7) × 49 × 5.
- Simplify: 22 × 7 × 5 = 770.
REASONING: Multiplying the base area (πr²) by the height (h) gives the total capacity, as the base area is stacked through the height.
ANSWER: 770 m³
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Calculate the curved surface area of a pipe with radius 2cm and height 10cm. (Use π = 3.14).
STEPS:
- Identify: r = 2cm, h = 10cm, formula CSA = 2πrh.
- Multiply 2 × 3.14 × 2 × 10.
- Multiply 2 × 2 = 4.
- Multiply 4 × 10 = 40.
- Multiply 40 × 3.14 = 125.6.
REASONING: The curved surface area is essentially a flattened rectangle with a width equal to the circumference (2πr) and a height equal to the pipe's height.
ANSWER: 125.6 cm²
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A company makes a soda can (cylinder) with r = 3cm and h = 12cm. They want to cover it entirely with a label (top, bottom, and side). What is the total surface area? (Use π = 3.14).
STEPS:
- CSA = 2 × 3.14 × 3 × 12 = 226.08.
- Base Area (one circle) = 3.14 × 3 × 3 = 28.26.
- Two bases = 28.26 × 2 = 56.52.
- Total = 226.08 + 56.52.
- Sum = 282.6.
REASONING: To cover the entire object, we must account for the curved middle piece plus the two flat circular ends.
ANSWER: 282.6 cm²
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you have two cylinders, one is tall and thin (r=2, h=10) and one is short and wide (r=5, h=2), which one has a larger base area? Explain.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Calculate base area of cylinder 1: π × 2 × 2 = 4π.
- Calculate base area of cylinder 2: π × 5 × 5 = 25π.
- Compare 4π and 25π.
- Conclude that the short, wide cylinder has a significantly larger base area because radius is squared.
ANSWER: The short, wide cylinder has a larger base area. This is because the radius is squared in the area formula, meaning the wider base grows much faster than the taller height.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A carpenter has a wooden cylindrical log of height 20cm and radius 10cm. He cuts it into two equal smaller cylinders. Does the total surface area increase, decrease, or stay the same?
COMPETENCY: Analyzing relationships
STEPS:
- Calculate initial surface area: 2πrh + 2πr².
- Imagine the cut: The cut creates two new circular faces that weren't there before.
- Recognize these two new faces add to the surface area.
- Conclude the total surface area increases.
ANSWER: The total surface area increases. When the log is cut, two new circular surfaces are exposed (the "cross-sections" at the cut), adding to the total area while the curved surface area remains constant.
