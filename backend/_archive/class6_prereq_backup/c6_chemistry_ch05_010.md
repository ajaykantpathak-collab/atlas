## DEFINITION
Cooking, spoilage, and preservation are processes that involve changes in food; cooking is often a combination of physical (softening) and chemical changes (altering composition), while spoilage is a chemical change caused by microbes, and preservation is the method of preventing these changes.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can identify whether a specific food change (like milk souring or water boiling) is primarily physical or chemical and suggest a practical way to preserve common food items.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers identifying changes in food (cooking/spoilage) and basic preservation methods. It does not cover complex nutrition science, industrial food regulations, or chemical reaction balancing.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever noticed how a fresh piece of bread turns moldy after a few days, or how a raw egg changes completely when you fry it? To begin, think about whether you can turn the fried egg back into a raw, liquid state—if you cannot, it is a chemical change. We look at food changes by observing if the substance remains the same (physical) or turns into something entirely new (chemical). Cooking, like boiling rice, involves physical softening and chemical starch changes, while spoilage is a permanent chemical breakdown. The key insight is that while physical changes can often be reversed, chemical changes in food (like spoilage or burning) are usually permanent.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the difference between a physical change and a chemical change in food?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A physical change alters the state or shape of food without changing its identity, while a chemical change creates new substances. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks all changes in food are the same; they likely miss that chemical changes are usually permanent.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If milk turns into yogurt (curd), is this a physical or chemical change? Explain why.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It is a chemical change because the bacteria change the composition of the milk, and you cannot turn the yogurt back into liquid milk. A wrong answer reveals they focus only on the visual state (it is still "white and soft") rather than the fundamental change in properties.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
You have cooked rice. If you leave it on the counter for two days, it spoils. Is this spoilage a physical or chemical change? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
It is a chemical change because the bacteria have broken down the food, creating new substances that smell and taste different. If the student answers "physical," they likely believe it is just "old," failing to realize the chemical decay process is irreversible.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why do we put pickles or jams in jars and seal them tightly?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
We seal them to stop air and microbes from entering, which prevents the chemical process of spoilage. A wrong answer suggests they think it is just to keep the food "clean," missing the link between air exposure and chemical spoilage.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If you boil water to make tea, the steam is a physical change, but the brewing of tea leaves is a chemical change. Do you agree? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, boiling water only changes the state of water (liquid to gas), which is reversible, while tea leaves release new chemicals into the water that change its color and taste permanently. A wrong answer shows confusion between phase changes and chemical composition changes.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple experiment to prove that leaving an apple slice out is a chemical change.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The student should suggest slicing an apple, leaving one half exposed to air, and observing the color change to brown. A poor answer would involve just "looking at it" without mentioning the observation of the irreversible color change or the role of air.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that "cooking" is only a physical change because the food just gets "softer" or "warmer." They ignore that heat causes the internal proteins and starches to break down into new forms, which is why a cooked potato can never become raw again.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student: "If you take a piece of raw dough and bake it into bread, can you turn the bread back into dough by adding water?" When they say no, explain that the heat caused a chemical reaction. Then, ask them to list one change they can reverse (like freezing water) and one they cannot (like burning toast) to distinguish between the two types.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You have 500g of raw potatoes. You boil them, and they gain 50g of weight due to water absorption. You then mash them. Identify the changes.
STEPS:
- 1: Analyze boiling; the potatoes absorb water (physical) and heat alters starches (chemical).
- 2: Analyze mashing; the shape changes (physical).
- 3: Determine if any part is reversible; the softening and starch change are irreversible.
- 4: Conclude that the process is a chemical change due to the heat-induced starch alteration.
REASONING: Boiling involves two processes: physical water absorption and chemical starch transformation, making it a chemical change.
ANSWER: Chemical change (due to irreversible starch alteration).
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A student leaves a glass of milk in the sun for 6 hours. It smells sour. What kind of change is this, and why?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the starting material: fresh milk.
- 2: Identify the condition: heat and light exposure for 6 hours.
- 3: Identify the result: sour smell (chemical change).
- 4: Conclude that bacteria caused an irreversible chemical reaction.
REASONING: The sour smell indicates a new substance has been formed, which is a hallmark of a chemical change.
ANSWER: Chemical change because the milk's composition has changed irreversibly.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: To preserve 2kg of mangoes, you slice them and add salt and oil. Why does this work?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the purpose of preservation: stopping chemical spoilage.
- 2: Identify the agents: salt and oil.
- 3: Explain their role: they create an environment where spoilage bacteria cannot grow.
- 4: Conclude the result: the chemical change (decay) is delayed.
REASONING: Preservation methods like pickling use substances that inhibit the chemical processes of decay.
ANSWER: This works by inhibiting the growth of bacteria, preventing the chemical breakdown of the fruit.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have two glasses: one with ice melting (Glass A) and one with milk left in the sun (Glass B). Classify each and justify your answer.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- 1: Analyze Glass A: ice to water is just a state change.
- 2: Analyze Glass B: milk to sour milk involves a change in substance.
- 3: Apply definitions: A is physical, B is chemical.
- 4: Justify based on reversibility.
ANSWER: Glass A is a physical change because it is a phase change that can be reversed by freezing. Glass B is a chemical change because the souring is an irreversible change in the milk's chemical identity.
DIFFICULTY: easy
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A chef says, "Cooking is just a physical change because I am only changing the shape and temperature of the food." Is the chef correct? Use evidence from the Maillard reaction (browning of bread) to support your answer.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the chef's claim: cooking is only physical.
- 2: Evaluate the claim against the Maillard reaction.
- 3: Note that browning is a change in color and flavor caused by new chemical products.
- 4: Construct the argument against the chef.
ANSWER: The chef is incorrect. The Maillard reaction is a chemical process where heat changes the sugars and proteins in bread, creating new flavors and colors that cannot be reversed. Therefore, cooking involves chemical changes, not just physical ones.
DIFFICULTY: hard
