## DEFINITION
The unitary method is a problem-solving technique where you first find the value of a single unit of an item, and then use that value to calculate the total for any number of units. It acts as a bridge between a known total and an unknown total by using "one" as the middle step.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the given relationship, calculate the value of one unit through division, and then scale that value up through multiplication to solve for the target quantity.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the basic unitary method for direct relationships (more items = more cost/quantity). It excludes inverse variation (e.g., more workers = less time) and complex compound proportions.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you go to the market to buy mangoes and the shopkeeper says 5 mangoes cost 75 rupees. How do you figure out the cost of 8 mangoes? You can’t guess! Instead, "zoom in" to find the cost of just 1 mango by dividing the total price by the number of items (75 ÷ 5 = 15 rupees). Once you know 1 mango costs 15 rupees, you can "zoom out" to find the cost of 8 mangoes by multiplying (8 × 15 = 120 rupees). This "divide to find one, multiply to find many" approach is the essence of the unitary method.

Step 1: Cost of 1 kg = 135 ÷ 3 = 45 rupees. Step 2: Cost of 7 kg = 7 × 45 = 315 rupees. If a student adds or subtracts, it shows they are failing to see the proportional relationship between quantity and price.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer would be: "If 2 buses carry 100 passengers, how many do 5 buses carry?" The first step is: "1 bus carries 100 ÷ 2 = 50 passengers." An answer failing to isolate a single unit shows a lack of understanding of the core concept.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse which number to divide by which. For example, if 4 pens cost 20 rupees, they might calculate 4 ÷ 20 = 0.2 instead of 20 ÷ 4 = 5. They treat the numbers as isolated digits rather than "Total Cost" and "Total Quantity."
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to label the units: "20 rupees" and "4 pens." Ask: "If you want the price of one pen, should you divide the rupees by the pens or the pens by the rupees?" Guide them to see that "rupees per pen" requires dividing the total rupees by the total pens.

## THIS_FORMULA
Value of 1 unit = Total Value ÷ Total Quantity; Target Value = (Value of 1 unit) × (Target Quantity)
## FORMULA_VARIABLES
Total Value: The initial cost/amount given; Total Quantity: The number of items given; Target Quantity: The number of items you need to find.
## FORMULA_UNITS
Rupees, metres, kilograms, litres, or "items."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: If 7 kg of potatoes cost 140 rupees, what is the cost of 12 kg of potatoes?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given (7 kg = 140 rupees) and target (12 kg = ?).
- step 2: Find cost of 1 kg (unit value) by dividing total cost by quantity.
- step 3: 140 ÷ 7 = 20 rupees per kg.
- step 4: Multiply unit value by target quantity (20 × 12).
- step 5: Result is 240 rupees.
REASONING: Dividing the total price by the weight tells us how much one single kilogram costs. Once we have that, we can calculate the price for any amount by scaling up that unit price.
ANSWER: 240 rupees.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A printer prints 9 pages in 3 minutes. How many pages will it print in 10 minutes?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given (9 pages = 3 minutes) and target (10 minutes = ?).
- step 2: Find how many pages are printed in 1 minute.
- step 3: 9 ÷ 3 = 3 pages per minute.
- step 4: Multiply unit value by target time (3 × 10).
- step 5: Result is 30 pages.
REASONING: By finding the rate (pages per minute), we turn a complex problem into a simple multiplication task. This works because the printing speed is assumed to be constant.
ANSWER: 30 pages.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A cyclist travels 45 km in 3 hours. How far will he travel in 7 hours? If he has a 100 km journey, how many hours will it take (assuming constant speed)?
STEPS:
- step 1: Find speed (km per 1 hour). 45 ÷ 3 = 15 km/hr.
- step 2: For 7 hours: 15 × 7 = 105 km.
- step 3: For 100 km: divide distance by speed (100 ÷ 15).
- step 4: 100 ÷ 15 = 6.66 hours (or 6 hours and 40 minutes).
REASONING: This combines the unitary method (finding distance per hour) with solving for time. It shows that the unit rate allows us to solve for either distance or time easily.
ANSWER: 105 km in 7 hours; 6 hours 40 minutes for 100 km.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are buying notebooks. Shop A sells 5 notebooks for 100 rupees. Shop B sells 3 notebooks for 66 rupees. Which shop offers a cheaper price per notebook?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate unit price for Shop A (100 ÷ 5).
- step 2: Calculate unit price for Shop B (66 ÷ 3).
- step 3: Compare 20 rupees/notebook vs 22 rupees/notebook.
- step 4: Conclude Shop A is cheaper.
ANSWER: Shop A is cheaper because each notebook costs 20 rupees, whereas Shop B charges 22 rupees per notebook.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A factory produces 500 toys in 5 days. The manager wants to know if they can produce 1200 toys in 10 days. Using the unitary method, explain if this is possible.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- step 1: Find the daily production rate (500 ÷ 5 = 100 toys/day).
- step 2: Calculate expected output for 10 days (100 × 10 = 1000 toys).
- step 3: Compare 1000 with the target of 1200.
- step 4: Conclude it is not possible.
ANSWER: The factory produces 100 toys per day. In 10 days, they would produce 1000 toys. Therefore, producing 1200 toys is not possible at the current rate.
DIFFICULTY: hard
