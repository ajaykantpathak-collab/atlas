## DEFINITION
Adding integers is the process of combining two numbers that include positive values, negative values, or zero, often visualized as movement along a number line or floor changes in a building. A positive integer represents movement in a forward or upward direction, while a negative integer represents movement in a backward or downward direction.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to accurately calculate the sum of any two integers (positive or negative) using a number line or the "zero pair" token method. They must also be able to represent a real-world scenario (like building floors or bank balances) as an addition expression.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the basic addition of integers on the number line and using the token/building floor model. It excludes algebraic symbolic addition (like variables) and advanced property proofs.
## WALKTHROUGH
If you are at the ground floor of a building and someone tells you to go up 3 floors and then down 5 floors, where do you end up? To solve this, start at 0 on a number line. Moving "up" or "forward" is a positive move, and moving "down" or "backward" is a negative move. First, draw an arrow from 0 to +3; then, from that spot, draw an arrow 5 units to the left. You will land on -2, which is your new position. The key insight is that adding a negative number is the same as taking a step backward, regardless of where you start.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the result of adding (-3) + (-4) on a number line?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The result is -7. A wrong answer like +7 or +1 reveals that the student is ignoring the negative signs and treating them as regular addition or failing to move in the correct direction on the number line.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you are at Floor +2 and you press the "-3" button in the lift, explain why you end up at Floor -1.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Starting at +2, moving down 3 floors takes you past 0 (the ground) to -1. A student who says the answer is +1 or -5 has not grasped that negative movement from a positive starting point crosses the zero barrier.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Evaluate: (+5) + (-8). Show your steps using the token method.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Place 5 green (positive) tokens and 8 red (negative) tokens. Pair each green with a red to form 5 "zero pairs" (which equal 0). You are left with 3 red tokens, so the answer is -3. A common error is to just subtract the numbers and guess the sign; the token method proves the sign must be negative because more negatives exist.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the addition of (+3) + (+5) and (+3) + (-5). How does the "direction" of the second number change the final result?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Adding +5 moves you further away from zero in the positive direction (reaching +8), while adding -5 moves you backward, crossing zero and landing in the negative side (reaching -2). A wrong answer suggests the student thinks the sign of the second number doesn't matter for the final direction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it possible for the sum of a positive integer and a negative integer to be zero? Provide an example.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, when the numbers are additive inverses, such as (+4) + (-4) = 0. Students who say "no" do not yet understand that positive and negative quantities can perfectly cancel each other out.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-world word problem that results in the expression (-10) + (+15).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A valid problem would be: "I owed a shopkeeper ₹10 (represented as -10), but then I earned ₹15 and paid him back (represented as +15). What is my new balance?" A poor answer would be a problem that doesn't involve a starting debt or negative state.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the sign of the sum when adding a positive and a negative number. They might calculate (+3) + (-7) and get +4, simply subtracting the smaller from the larger and keeping the first number's sign, or they might ignore the negative sign entirely. This happens because they are still thinking in terms of primary school "take away" subtraction rather than directional movement.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student gets this wrong, have them draw a number line. Ask them to put their finger on the first number (+3). Then, tell them: "The second number is -7, which means move 7 steps to the left." Physically counting the steps backward past 0 to -4 corrects the mental error of just "subtracting" numbers.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A submarine is at a depth of 50 metres below sea level (-50). It then rises 20 metres. What is its new depth?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify current position as -50.
- step 2: Identify "rising" as a positive movement of +20.
- step 3: Set up the expression: (-50) + (+20).
- step 4: On a number line, start at -50 and move 20 units to the right.
- step 5: Landing point is -30.
REASONING: Since the submarine is already below sea level, we use a negative integer. Rising indicates a positive change in position, so we add a positive value to the negative starting point.
ANSWER: -30 metres (or 30 metres below sea level).
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A bank account has a balance of ₹200. You make a withdrawal of ₹300. What is your new balance?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify current balance as +200.
- step 2: Identify "withdrawal" as a negative movement of -300.
- step 3: Set up the expression: (+200) + (-300).
- step 4: Start at +200 on the number line and move 300 steps to the left.
- step 5: After moving 200 steps to reach 0, move 100 more to the left to reach -100.
REASONING: Withdrawal represents money leaving the account, which is a negative change. Moving from a positive balance past zero results in a negative balance (debt).
ANSWER: -₹100.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student is playing a game where they start at position -5. In the first round, they gain 10 points. In the second round, they lose 7 points. What is their final position?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify initial state: -5.
- step 2: Identify first move: +10.
- step 3: Identify second move: -7.
- step 4: Combine: (-5) + (+10) = +5.
- step 5: Combine result with final move: (+5) + (-7) = -2.
- step 6: Verify: -5 + 10 = 5; 5 - 7 = -2.
REASONING: We treat the gain as addition of a positive integer and the loss as the addition of a negative integer. The sequence of moves happens one after another, resulting in a final coordinate.
ANSWER: -2.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If (a) + (b) = 0, what can you tell me about the relationship between (a) and (b)? Explain using the number line.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Recognize the sum is zero.
- step 2: Understand that to return to 0 from any point (a), the movement (b) must be the exact opposite.
- step 3: Explain that (b) is the additive inverse of (a).
- step 4: Conclude they are equidistant from zero on opposite sides.
ANSWER: They are additive inverses. If you start at (a), you must travel the same distance in the opposite direction to land exactly on 0.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students are debating: "Does adding (-5) to (-10) make the number smaller or larger?" One says it gets smaller because it becomes -15. The other says it gets larger because the total size of the debt is bigger. Who is right and why?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the values: -15 is to the left of -10 on the number line.
- step 2: Recall that numbers to the left are "less than" numbers to the right.
- step 3: Explain that -15 is a smaller value than -10.
- step 4: Evaluate the language used by the students.
ANSWER: The first student is mathematically correct. On the number line, -15 is to the left of -10, so the value has decreased. The second student is confused by the word "larger," likely thinking about the distance from zero (magnitude), but in mathematics, moving further left is moving to a smaller value.
DIFFICULTY: hard
