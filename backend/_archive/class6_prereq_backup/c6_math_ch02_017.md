## DEFINITION
A number line is a straight line that represents integers as points at equal distances from a center point called zero; numbers to the right are positive, and numbers to the left are negative. It visually demonstrates that numbers increase as you move right and decrease as you move left.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to correctly plot any given integer on a number line and use the position of numbers to compare their values (e.g., identifying that -5 is smaller than -2).
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the visual representation and comparison of integers on a number line. It excludes rational numbers, fractions, coordinates, and absolute value calculations.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are standing in an elevator in a building with underground parking. If you are at the ground floor (0) and go up to the first floor, you are at +1; if you go down to the basement, you are at -1. To understand the number line, draw a horizontal line and place 0 in the center. Mark equal gaps moving right for positive numbers (1, 2, 3...) and moving left for negative numbers (-1, -2, -3...). The key insight is that the line never stops, and no matter how large a negative number looks (like -100), its position to the left of 0 makes it smaller than any positive number or zero.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the name of the central point on a number line that separates positive and negative numbers?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The central point is called zero (0). A wrong answer suggests the student has not yet internalized the reference point of the number system.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you have two numbers, -3 and -7, which one is further to the right on the number line, and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
-3 is further to the right because it is greater than -7. A student who says -7 is greater shows they are treating negative numbers like positive numbers, ignoring the leftward direction of negative value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Plot the integers -4, 2, and 0 on a number line and list them in increasing order.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The order is -4, 0, 2. A student who lists them as 2, 0, -4 is failing to recognize that "increasing" means moving from the smallest (leftmost) to the largest (rightmost) value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the position of 5 and -5 on the number line. Are they the same distance from zero?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Yes, they are the same distance from zero in opposite directions. A wrong answer suggests the student does not understand that positive and negative integers are symmetrical reflections across the zero point.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If an elevator is at -2 and moves 3 units to the right, where does it end up?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It ends at +1. A student answering -5 indicates they are moving the wrong direction (left) instead of the positive direction (right).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Draw a number line and show where -6 and -1 are. If you were a person walking from -6 toward -1, would you be moving toward a larger or smaller number?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
You would be moving toward a larger number. An answer of "smaller" shows the student is misled by the digit size (6 vs 1) rather than the actual position on the line.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that since 5 is bigger than 2, then -5 must be bigger than -2. They focus on the magnitude of the digit and ignore the negative sign, failing to realize that on the number line, -5 is to the left of -2, making it smaller.
## ON_FAILURE
Direct the student to draw two separate lines: one for positive (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5) and one for negative (0, -1, -2, -3, -4, -5). Ask them: "If you have 5 rupees debt (-5) and 2 rupees debt (-2), which situation is better for your pocket?" Once they identify that -2 is "better" (larger), explain that "better" or "more" always corresponds to the position further to the right on the number line.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A thermometer in Leh shows -4°C in the morning. By noon, the temperature rises by 6 degrees. What is the new temperature?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the starting point: -4 on the number line.
- step 2: Identify the movement: "rise" means moving to the right (positive direction).
- step 3: Count 6 units to the right from -4: -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2.
- step 4: The result is 2.
REASONING: Since the temperature rose, we move in the positive direction along the number line. Starting from -4, moving 6 steps right lands us at +2, which is the new temperature.
ANSWER: 2°C
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A deep-sea diver is at -10 meters (below sea level). He swims down 3 meters further to reach a cave. What is his new depth?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the starting point: -10 on the number line.
- step 2: Identify the movement: "down" means moving to the left (negative direction).
- step 3: Count 3 units to the left from -10: -11, -12, -13.
- step 4: The result is -13.
REASONING: Moving deeper below sea level is equivalent to moving further left on the number line. Since we start at -10 and go deeper, we move 3 units away from zero to reach -13.
ANSWER: -13 meters
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student has a bank balance of -50 rupees (a debt). They deposit 80 rupees. What is their new balance?
STEPS:
- step 1: Start at -50 on the number line.
- step 2: A deposit is an increase, so move to the right by 80 units.
- step 3: First, move 50 units to reach 0.
- step 4: Subtract 50 from 80 to see how many units are left to move: 80 - 50 = 30.
- step 5: Move the remaining 30 units right from 0 to reach +30.
REASONING: To find the final position, we bridge the gap to zero first. Once the debt is cleared (at zero), the remaining deposit amount represents the new positive balance.
ANSWER: +30 rupees
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Imagine a town where the ground level is 0. A shop is at -3, a school is at 1, and a library is at -5. If you are standing at the library, are you closer to the shop or the school? Explain your reasoning using the number line.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Plot -5 (library), -3 (shop), and 1 (school) on the number line.
- step 2: Measure the distance from -5 to -3 (it is 2 units).
- step 3: Measure the distance from -5 to 1 (it is 6 units).
- step 4: Compare the distances.
ANSWER: You are closer to the shop because the distance from -5 to -3 is only 2 units, whereas the distance from -5 to 1 is 6 units.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students are arguing. Student A says -100 is greater than -1 because it has a larger number. Student B says -1 is greater because it is to the right of -100. Who is correct and why?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand the definition of 'greater' on a number line (further to the right).
- step 2: Analyze Student A's logic (confusing magnitude with value).
- step 3: Analyze Student B's logic (correctly using the number line rule).
- step 4: Formulate the explanation.
ANSWER: Student B is correct. On a number line, any number to the right is greater. Since -1 is closer to zero and further to the right than -100, -1 is the larger number. Student A is mistakenly looking at the digit value rather than the position on the line.
DIFFICULTY: hard
