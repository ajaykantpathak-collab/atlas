## DEFINITION
Animals are classified into three main groups based on their dietary habits: herbivores (plant-eaters), carnivores (flesh-eaters), and omnivores (eaters of both plants and animals). This classification is based on the primary source of nutrition an animal consumes to survive and gain energy.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to correctly categorize a variety of animals into herbivore, carnivore, or omnivore groups and explain the reasoning behind each classification based on their observed diet.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers only the classification of animals as herbivores, carnivores, or omnivores. It does not cover decomposers, saprophytic nutrition, or internal physiological processes like digestion.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wondered why a cow spends all day chewing grass while a tiger only hunts for meat? Start by looking at the food source: if an animal eats only plants (like grains, leaves, or fruits), it is a herbivore. If it hunts or scavenges to eat other animals, it is a carnivore. If it enjoys a "mixed diet" of both plants and animal products, it is an omnivore. To begin, list three animals you see in your neighbourhood and track what they eat for a day. The key insight is that an animal's body and behavior are naturally adapted to the type of food it needs to survive.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the primary food source for an animal classified as a herbivore?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Herbivores eat only plant material. An answer stating they eat both plants and meat reveals a failure to distinguish between herbivores and omnivores.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If an animal eats both fruits and small insects, how would you classify its dietary habit?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It is an omnivore. A student who calls it a herbivore ignores the fact that insects are animal matter, showing a misunderstanding of what constitutes an animal-based food source.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A deer only eats grass, and a lion only eats deer. If we removed all the grass from the forest, what would happen to the deer and the lion?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
First, the deer would lose its food source and perish. Second, because the lion relies on the deer for food, it would eventually starve as well. A wrong answer suggests the student does not understand that these dietary habits create a chain of dependency.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the diet of a sparrow and a cow; why are they in different categories?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
A cow is a herbivore because it eats only plant parts. A sparrow is an omnivore because it eats seeds (plants) and insects (animals). A wrong answer reveals the student failed to notice the sparrow's multi-source diet.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is a human being who eats both vegetables and eggs an omnivore? Explain your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, because they consume both plant-based foods (vegetables) and animal-derived foods (eggs). If a student says "no," they likely define omnivores too narrowly, perhaps only thinking of wild animals like bears.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple "Food Sort" experiment: How would you determine if a local lizard is an omnivore or a carnivore?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Observe the lizard for several days, noting if it eats plant matter (like flower petals or fruit) alongside insects. If it only catches flies or spiders, it is a carnivore; if it shows interest in both, it is an omnivore. A poor answer would suggest guessing without observation.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think "herbivores only eat grass" or "carnivores only hunt large mammals." They forget that plant material includes fruits, seeds, and nectar, and that small animals like insects count as animal protein. For example, they might argue a bird eating seeds and worms is just a "herbivore" because they focus only on the seeds.
## ON_FAILURE
Review your list of foods. Ask yourself: "Is this from a plant (like wheat, rice, or leaves) or from an animal (like meat, egg, or milk)?" If the animal consumes both types, label it 'Omnivore.' Practice by sorting five local animals into three columns—'Plant Only,' 'Animal Only,' and 'Both'—to visualize the categories clearly.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A bird eats seeds from a sunflower (plant) and also catches small worms (animal) in the soil. Categorize the bird.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the food sources mentioned (seeds and worms).
- step 2: Determine if seeds are plant-based (Yes) and if worms are animal-based (Yes).
- step 3: Apply the rule: Plant only = Herbivore; Animal only = Carnivore; Both = Omnivore.
- step 4: Since both are present, classify as Omnivore.
REASONING: Dietary classification is based on the variety of sources; consuming both types of matter is the definition of an omnivore.
ANSWER: The bird is an omnivore.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A rabbit eats only carrots and lettuce. A fox eats only the rabbit. Categorize both animals.
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the rabbit's diet (carrots and lettuce = plants).
- step 2: Classify the rabbit (Herbivore).
- step 3: Analyze the fox's diet (rabbit = animal).
- step 4: Classify the fox (Carnivore).
REASONING: Following the food source leads to the classification; carrots are plants, and rabbits are animals.
ANSWER: Rabbit is a Herbivore; Fox is a Carnivore.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: You have a pet that eats rice, milk, and small fish. Categorize it and explain why it is not a herbivore.
STEPS:
- step 1: List the food: Rice (plant), Milk (animal), Fish (animal).
- step 2: Identify the categories: It eats both plant and animal products.
- step 3: Use the definition: Eating both makes it an omnivore.
- step 4: Compare with herbivore definition: Herbivores only eat plants.
- step 5: Conclude: It is an omnivore because it eats animal products too.
REASONING: By cross-checking each food item against the definition of a herbivore, we prove the animal belongs to the omnivore group.
ANSWER: It is an omnivore because it consumes both plant and animal-based foods.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Imagine an animal that was a herbivore but now, due to a lack of plants, has started eating small insects. Does this change its category? Why or why not?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Define current category (Herbivore).
- step 2: Define new behavior (Eating insects).
- step 3: Check against definitions (Omnivore definition = eats both).
- step 4: Justify the transition.
ANSWER: Yes, it changes to an omnivore. A category is based on what an animal actually eats to survive, not what it "used to" eat. Since it now consumes both plants and animals, the label must shift.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why might an omnivore have an advantage over a herbivore if a sudden drought kills all the plants in a region?
COMPETENCY: Analyzing and evaluating
STEPS:
- step 1: Define the primary food source of a herbivore (plants).
- step 2: Define the primary food source of an omnivore (plants and animals).
- step 3: Predict the consequence of plant loss for both.
- step 4: Evaluate the survival chance of the omnivore.
ANSWER: The omnivore has an advantage because it can switch its diet to rely entirely on other animals, whereas the herbivore is completely dependent on plants and would likely starve. This shows that omnivores have more flexible survival strategies in changing environments.
DIFFICULTY: hard
