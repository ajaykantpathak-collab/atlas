## DEFINITION
A diamante poem is a seven-line poem structured in the shape of a diamond, which uses specific parts of speech to show the transition between two contrasting or related ideas. It serves as a compact way to organize descriptive language around a central theme.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the correct part of speech for each of the seven lines and construct a poem that follows the 1-2-3-4-3-2-1 word count and structural pattern.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the structural rules, word counts, and part-of-speech requirements for diamante poems. It excludes free verse, visual poetry (calligrams), and other non-structured forms of poetry.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever tried to describe a "win" and a "loss" using only a few words? To start a diamante, pick two opposites like 'Summer' and 'Winter' or 'Victory' and 'Defeat'. You begin by naming your first subject, then add adjectives and actions to describe it, before making a "turn" in the middle to describe the second subject. Think of it like a bridge where the middle line (Line 4) acts as the connection between your two ideas. By following the 1-2-3-4-3-2-1 structure, you transform simple words into a balanced, diamond-shaped piece of art.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
How many lines are in a standard diamante poem?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A diamante poem has seven lines. A wrong answer suggests the student has not memorized the basic structural constraint of the form.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
What does "Line 3" of a diamante poem require?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Line 3 requires three "-ing" words (participles) describing the first subject. A wrong answer shows confusion between adjectives and action-based participle verbs.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If your diamante starts with "Badminton" and ends with "Chess," what should the nouns in Line 4 represent?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Line 4 should have two nouns related to Badminton and two nouns related to Chess. A wrong answer indicates the student missed the "bridge" purpose of the middle line.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze this line: "Running, sweating, winning." Does this fit the requirement for a diamante?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Yes, it fits if it is in Line 3 or 5, as it consists of three "-ing" words. A wrong answer suggests the student is struggling to identify the grammatical category of present participles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it better to choose two completely unrelated subjects for a diamante, like "Pen" and "Planet"? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it is better to choose related or contrasting subjects (like "Win" and "Loss") because the diamante structure is designed to show a transformation or relationship between them. A wrong answer shows a lack of understanding regarding the poem's thematic purpose.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a one-line start for a diamante using the subject "Sports."
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
"Sports." Any other word would be incorrect because Line 1 must be exactly one noun.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often mix up the word count or the part of speech for the middle (4th) line. They may write adjectives instead of nouns or use the wrong number of words, usually because they prioritize the rhyme (which is not required) over the strict structural "formula" of the diamante.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Create a diamante poem for "Sun" and "Moon."
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify Line 1 (Noun): Sun
- Step 2: Identify Line 2 (2 Adjectives): Bright, hot
- Step 3: Identify Line 3 (3 -ing words): Shining, warming, glowing
- Step 4: Identify Line 4 (4 Nouns: 2 for Sun, 2 for Moon): Rays, light, shadows, night
- Step 5: Identify Line 5 (3 -ing words): Orbiting, cooling, sleeping
- Step 6: Identify Line 6 (2 Adjectives): Pale, cold
- Step 7: Identify Line 7 (1 Noun): Moon
REASONING: Following the count ensures the diamond shape, while the parts of speech create a logical flow.
ANSWER: Sun / Bright, hot / Shining, warming, glowing / Rays, light, shadows, night / Orbiting, cooling, sleeping / Pale, cold / Moon.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you are asked to write a diamante about "Victory" and "Defeat," and you write "Sad, Angry" for the middle line, why is this incorrect?
COMPETENCY: Structural Analysis
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the line mentioned (Line 4).
- Step 2: Identify the requirement for Line 4 (4 Nouns).
- Step 3: Identify the student's input ("Sad, Angry" = Adjectives).
- Step 4: Compare requirement with input.
ANSWER: It is incorrect because Line 4 must consist of four nouns, while "Sad" and "Angry" are adjectives. A diamante requires specific parts of speech for each line.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why does the word count matter in a diamante? What would happen if Line 3 had four words?
COMPETENCY: Logical Reasoning
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the constraint (Line 3 must have 3 words).
- Step 2: Observe the "diamond shape" outcome of the fixed count.
- Step 3: Determine the visual/structural impact.
- Step 4: Conclude that the structure would lose its symmetry and diamond shape.
ANSWER: The word count creates the physical diamond shape. If Line 3 had four words, the poem would bulge in the middle, breaking the visual pattern and failing to follow the standard diamante formula.
