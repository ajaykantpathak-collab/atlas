## DEFINITION
Problem solving with integers involves using positive and negative numbers to represent real-world changes, such as gains/losses, temperatures above/below zero, or movements in a building or mine. We use the relationship: Starting Position + Movement = Target Position to calculate the final state.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to translate a word problem into an integer expression, correctly apply addition or subtraction based on the context (direction of movement), and identify the final position on a number line.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers single-step or simple multi-step integer movements in real-world contexts like temperature, elevation, and bank balances. It excludes complex multi-step problems requiring algebraic variables or optimization.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are in an elevator in a building that has floors below ground level. If you are on the ground floor (0) and go down two floors, you are at -2. If you then go up five floors, you add (+5) to your position. To solve these, always ask: "Where am I starting?" and "What is the change happening to me?" Write these down as an expression: (Starting Floor) + (Movement) = (Target Floor). The key insight is that "going down" or "losing" is always a negative movement, while "going up" or "gaining" is a positive movement.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If a submarine is at -50 metres and it descends another 20 metres, what is the expression to find its new depth?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The expression is (-50) + (-20). A student who answers (-50) + (+20) likely confuses "descending" (a negative change) with the distance value itself, failing to assign the correct sign to the movement.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why -5 degrees is colder than -2 degrees using the idea of a number line or a thermometer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
-5 is colder because it is further to the left of zero on the number line. A student who says -5 is "bigger" because 5 is bigger than 2 is ignoring the negative sign, which indicates a position below the reference point of zero.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A bank account has a balance of ₹100. You make a withdrawal (a debit) of ₹150. What is your new balance, and how do you represent it?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The balance is -₹50. Steps: 1. Identify starting balance (+100). 2. Identify movement (-150 for withdrawal). 3. Expression: (+100) + (-150) = -50. A student who calculates 150 - 100 = 50 ignores the direction of the transaction (a loss from a smaller amount).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If you are at floor -3 and you reach floor +2, what was the movement? Compare your result to the target.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The movement was +5 floors. Expression: Target - Start = Movement, so (+2) - (-3) = +5. A student who answers -1 likely added the numbers incorrectly or failed to realize that subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive (moving toward the positive direction).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Critique the statement: "Subtracting a negative number always makes the result smaller."
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
This is false. Subtracting a negative number is equivalent to adding a positive number, which makes the result larger. A student who thinks it makes the result smaller is likely treating the two minus signs as a single negative outcome rather than an inverse operation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-world scenario involving a temperature change that results in a final value of 0 degrees Celsius.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good response would be: "The temperature started at -4 degrees and rose by 4 degrees." A poor response would involve non-integer values or fail to demonstrate the "inverse" relationship needed to reach zero.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the "sign of the number" with the "operation to perform." For example, if asked to subtract -5 from 2, they may write 2 - 5 instead of 2 - (-5). They do this because they see the minus sign in front of the 5 and assume it is the only sign that matters, forgetting that the context of "subtracting" a debt is fundamentally an addition of value.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student struggles, draw a vertical number line (like a thermometer). Ask them to place a finger on the starting number. Ask: "If you move down/debit, which way does your finger go?" (Down). "If you move up/credit, which way?" (Up). Explicitly write the expression using brackets like (+2) - (-5) and show that the "minus-minus" turns into a "plus" movement because taking away a loss is a gain.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A merchant has a bank balance of ₹500. He makes a deposit of ₹200, then pays a bill of ₹800. What is his final balance?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given values: Start = +500, Deposit = +200, Bill/Debit = -800.
- Step 2: Set up the expression: (+500) + (+200) + (-800).
- Step 3: Perform first movement: 500 + 200 = 700.
- Step 4: Perform second movement: 700 + (-800) = -100.
- Step 5: Verify: The balance is -100, meaning he is in debt by ₹100.
REASONING: We sum the credits (positive) and subtract/add the debits (negative) sequentially to track the balance change.
ANSWER: -₹100
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A mountain climber is at an elevation of -20 metres (a cave entrance). She climbs up 150 metres to reach a plateau. What is the elevation of the plateau?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Start = -20m, Movement = +150m.
- Step 2: Expression: (-20) + (+150).
- Step 3: Use the rule for adding different signs: Subtract smaller from larger (150 - 20 = 130).
- Step 4: Keep the sign of the larger number (+).
- Step 5: Result is +130 metres.
REASONING: Since we are moving upward from a negative elevation, we add a positive value, effectively reducing the depth until we cross zero.
ANSWER: +130 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: At 2:00 AM, the temperature in a town was -8°C. By 10:00 AM, it rose by 12°C. By 4:00 PM, it fell by 5°C. What was the temperature at 4:00 PM?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Start = -8, First movement = +12, Second movement = -5.
- Step 2: Set up expression: (-8) + (+12) + (-5).
- Step 3: Combine first two: (-8 + 12) = +4.
- Step 4: Combine result with last movement: (+4) + (-5) = -1.
- Step 5: Interpret: The final temperature is -1°C.
REASONING: We treat the temperature changes as additions of positive or negative integers to the initial state.
ANSWER: -1°C
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are designing a lift system for a building. If the basement levels are -1 to -5 and the ground floor is 0, and you are currently at -3, write an expression to show the movement required to get to the 2nd floor (+2). If you accidentally press -2 instead, where do you end up?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Start = -3, Target = +2.
- Step 2: Movement = Target - Start = (+2) - (-3) = +5.
- Step 3: If you press -2, new position = Start + Movement = (-3) + (-2) = -5.
ANSWER: The expression for the correct movement is (+2) - (-3) = +5. If you press -2, you end up at floor -5.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A company tracks its stock value. On Monday, the value changes by -₹10. On Tuesday, it changes by +₹25. On Wednesday, it changes by -₹40. If the stock started at ₹100 on Sunday, what is the net change, and what is the final value on Wednesday?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate net change: (-10) + (+25) + (-40).
- Step 2: Solve net change: (-10 + 25) = +15; (+15 - 40) = -25.
- Step 3: Calculate final value: Start + Net change = 100 + (-25) = 75.
ANSWER: The net change is -₹25. The final value is ₹75. The net change represents the total loss or gain over the three days.
DIFFICULTY: hard
