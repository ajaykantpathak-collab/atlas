## DEFINITION
The Bahmani Kingdom was an independent state established in the Deccan in 1347, which later split into five regional powers known as the Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Berar, Bidar, and Golconda) in 1527.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the transition from a single kingdom to five smaller sultanates and correctly name the five Deccan Sultanates.

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever seen a large piece of clay split into several smaller, separate pieces? The Bahmani Kingdom is similar: it began as one large, unified power in the Deccan in 1347, but by 1527, it broke apart into five distinct smaller kingdoms. To learn this, imagine a map of the Deccan region changing from one big shaded area into five smaller, different colored sections. Each of these new sections—Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Berar, Bidar, and Golconda—became a "Deccan Sultanate." By understanding this split, you realize how regional kingdoms often transformed over time.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
In what year was the Bahmani Kingdom founded, and who was its founder?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
It was founded in 1347 by Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah. A wrong answer here suggests the student is mixing up dates or names with other historical periods, indicating they need to re-read the timeline introduction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
What is the relationship between the Bahmani Kingdom and the five Deccan Sultanates?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The Bahmani Kingdom broke up, and the five Deccan Sultanates were the smaller kingdoms that formed from that break. A wrong answer reveals the student thinks these were five separate, unrelated groups that never belonged to a single kingdom.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If you were drawing a map of the year 1530, would you draw one Bahmani Kingdom or five separate sultanates? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
You would draw five because the Bahmani Kingdom ended in 1527. A wrong answer shows the student failed to note the end date of the original kingdom.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the structure of the Deccan region before 1347 and after 1527.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Before 1347, the region was part of the Delhi Sultanate; after 1527, it was divided into five independent sultanates. A wrong answer reveals confusion about the transition of power in the Deccan.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it accurate to say the Deccan Sultanates existed throughout the entire time the Bahmani Kingdom existed?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, the Sultanates only emerged after the Bahmani Kingdom collapsed in 1527. A wrong answer implies the student does not understand the chronological order of these events.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a mnemonic or a simple list to remember the five Deccan Sultanates.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer would be a memory hook like "B-A-B-B-G" (Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Berar, Bidar, Golconda). A poor answer lists incorrect names or misses several, showing a lack of engagement with the specific regional names provided.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think the Bahmani Kingdom and the Deccan Sultanates existed at the same time as separate competing countries. This is incorrect because the Sultanates are the *result* of the Bahmani Kingdom's breakup, not its rivals.
