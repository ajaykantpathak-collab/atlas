## DEFINITION
A bank is a financial institution that acts as a safe storage place for money, accepting deposits from people who have extra cash and lending that money to people who need it for productive purposes. It serves as a bridge between those who have surplus funds and those who need capital to grow.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to explain that a bank is a safe place for money and describe its dual role of accepting deposits and issuing loans. They should be able to contrast keeping money at home versus keeping it in a bank.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the basic definition and the core function of banks (accepting deposits and lending). It does not cover banking regulations, investment banking, or government monetary policy.
## WALKTHROUGH
Think about a time you saved money in a piggy bank—where did you keep it? If you have a large amount of money hidden under a mattress, it is not earning anything, and it might get lost or stolen. A bank is like a giant, highly secure "community piggy bank." First, people deposit their extra savings into the bank for safekeeping. Second, the bank takes these collected savings and lends them to someone who needs to buy a tractor for a farm or start a small shop. This makes the money work for the economy instead of just sitting still.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the two main functions of a bank?
(Recall)
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The two main functions are accepting deposits from people with extra money and lending money to those who need it. A wrong answer suggests the student confuses a bank with a simple storage locker, failing to recognize the "lending" or "circulating" aspect of finance.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Keeping money in a bank keeps it safe and prevents it from sitting idle, whereas money under a bed earns nothing and faces risks like theft. If a student says "because it gives you more money," they may be over-generalizing; focus on safety and the ability to access it as the primary immediate benefit.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
**Correct Answer:**
Keeping money in a bank is better because it is safer from theft or accidental loss and earns interest, which allows your money to grow over time. Unlike a box under your bed, a bank also provides secure digital access to your funds for easier transactions.

**Explanation:**
A wrong answer—such as claiming that banks are worse because they "keep your money" or "might lose it"—reveals a **misconception regarding financial security and the role of banking institutions.** It suggests the student does not yet understand the concept of deposit insurance (like FDIC) or the fundamental purpose of a bank as a secure, interest-bearing repository versus a high-risk, stagnant storage method.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If your neighbour needs money to open a small bakery, where can they go to get it, and what is that process called?
(Apply)
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
They can go to a bank to request a loan. The process is: 1) Identify the need, 2) Approach the bank, 3) Request the loan, 4) Bank provides the funds from its pool of deposits. A wrong answer suggests the student doesn't see the bank as a source of capital for economic activity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How do banks help "connect" two different types of people in a town?
(Analyze)
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Banks connect people with "extra money" (depositors) to people who "need money" (borrowers). By acting as a middleman, the bank ensures money is used effectively rather than being hidden away. A wrong answer shows a failure to see the bank as a social/economic bridge.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it safer to lend your own savings directly to a stranger for their business, or to deposit it in a bank? Why?
(Evaluate)
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It is safer to use a bank because banks have systems to manage risk and security that an individual does not. Lending to a stranger directly carries a high risk of losing your money if they cannot pay you back.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Imagine you are the manager of a new bank. How would you explain to a villager why they should open an account?
(Create)
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer would highlight safety, the prevention of theft, and the idea that their money helps the community grow. A poor answer would focus purely on profit or ignore the "safe place" aspect.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think a bank is just a "vault" or a big safe that holds cash. They fail to understand the "lending" part of the business, believing the bank's only job is to protect their bills.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student: "If the bank just hid your money in a box, how would they pay the people who work there?" Guide them to realize that the bank uses the money to help others (loans), which makes the money "active" in the economy.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Mr. Sharma has ₹5,000 extra. Mrs. Gupta needs ₹5,000 for a sewing machine. How does the bank help?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify Mr. Sharma as the depositor.
- 2: Identify Mrs. Gupta as the borrower.
- 3: Mr. Sharma deposits his money in the bank for safekeeping.
- 4: The bank takes that money and lends it to Mrs. Gupta.
- 5: The bank acts as the bridge between both parties.
REASONING: This shows the bank's role in moving money from someone who has it to someone who needs it.
ANSWER: The bank keeps Mr. Sharma's money safe and provides the necessary capital to Mrs. Gupta.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Why is keeping ₹10,000 in a tin box in the kitchen risky?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the risk of physical damage (fire, moisture).
- 2: Identify the risk of theft (burglary).
- 3: Identify the lack of economic usage (it stays idle).
- 4: Compare this to a bank's security features.
REASONING: Physical storage is vulnerable; banks provide protection and institutional security.
ANSWER: Keeping it in a tin box is risky because it can be stolen or lost, whereas a bank vault is protected.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A local farmer needs to buy a new tractor but has no cash. How does the bank help him, and where does that money come from?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the farmer's need (capital for equipment).
- 2: Explain the bank's role as a lender.
- 3: Explain the source (deposits from other savers).
- 4: Conclude that the farmer can now start his work.
REASONING: This connects the borrower's needs to the collective pool of money provided by savers.
ANSWER: The bank provides a loan to the farmer, funded by the collective savings of other people in the town.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If all the people in a village decided to keep their money under their beds instead of using a bank, what would happen to people who want to start a new business?
COMPETENCY: Critical Thinking/Systems Analysis
STEPS:
- 1: Recognize that money would be "trapped" in homes.
- 2: Realize that there is no central place for borrowers to find money.
- 3: Conclude that new businesses would likely fail to start.
ANSWER: Business owners would struggle to find people willing to lend them money, making it very difficult for the village to grow or improve.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare the "Value of Work" (economic activity) to the "Bank's Role." How does the bank support the value of work?
COMPETENCY: Synthesis
STEPS:
- 1: Define economic activity (people doing work for money).
- 2: Define the bank's role (managing the money earned from work).
- 3: Connect the two: Bank allows money to be saved and invested in more work.
- 4: Explain that banks help "work" continue by providing money for new equipment.
ANSWER: The bank supports the "value of work" by ensuring that the money earned from one person's work can be safely used to help another person start their own work, keeping the economy moving.
