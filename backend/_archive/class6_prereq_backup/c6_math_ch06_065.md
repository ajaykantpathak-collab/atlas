## DEFINITION
Two quantities are in direct proportion if they change in the same direction—when one increases, the other increases, or when one decreases, the other decreases—while maintaining a constant ratio between them.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to calculate an unknown value in a proportion by setting up an equivalent ratio equation (a/b = c/d) and solving for the missing term.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers identifying and solving simple direct proportion problems involving two quantities. It excludes inverse proportion, compound proportion, and graphical representations of proportion.
## WALKTHROUGH
If you go to a shop to buy notebooks, have you noticed that buying more notebooks costs you more money? Start by asking: "If 2 notebooks cost 40 rupees, what will 5 notebooks cost?" To solve this, first find the cost of one notebook (the unit rate) or set up the ratio: notebooks/cost = notebooks/cost. This ensures the relationship between the quantities stays balanced. Always remember that in direct proportion, the "what you do to one side, you must do to the other" rule applies to keep the ratio identical.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If 4 pens cost 20 rupees, what is the ratio of pens to cost?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The ratio is 4:20, which simplifies to 1:5. A wrong answer (like 5:1) shows the student confused the order of quantities, putting cost before quantity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you travel 10 km in 2 hours, how far will you travel in 4 hours? Explain why.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
You will travel 20 km. This is because the time doubled, so the distance must also double to maintain the same speed (ratio). A wrong answer suggests the student doesn't see the multiplicative relationship between the two quantities.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If 3 kg of apples cost 150 rupees, how much will 7 kg of apples cost?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Find cost of 1 kg (150/3 = 50 rupees). Step 2: Multiply by 7 kg (50 * 7 = 350 rupees). A wrong answer usually comes from adding numbers instead of multiplying/dividing by the ratio.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Two quantities A and B are in direct proportion. If A doubles, what happens to B?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
B must also double. An incorrect answer shows the student does not understand that "direct" means they change by the same factor.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If 5 workers can finish a job in 10 hours, is this direct proportion? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, because as the number of workers increases, the time taken decreases (inverse proportion). A student who says "yes" assumes all related quantities are in direct proportion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-world scenario where two things are in direct proportion and explain your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: "The number of pages I read vs. the time taken." Reasoning: The more time I spend reading, the more pages I finish, and the rate (pages/minute) stays roughly constant. A poor answer would describe something where one does not affect the other or they change inversely.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to use addition instead of multiplication. For example, if 2 pens cost 10 rupees, they might think 4 pens cost 12 rupees (adding 2 to the price) instead of 20 rupees (multiplying by 2). This happens because they view the relationship as a simple sequence rather than a proportional ratio.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student adds instead of multiplying, ask them: "If 2 pens cost 10 rupees, and you want to buy 0 pens, would you pay 8 rupees?" Guide them to see that 0 pens must cost 0 rupees. Then, show that 4 pens is just 2 sets of "2 pens," so you must pay 2 sets of "10 rupees."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A recipe for 4 people requires 200g of flour. How much flour is needed for 10 people?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given (4 people = 200g) and target (10 people = ?g).
- step 2: Set up proportion: 4/200 = 10/x.
- step 3: Simplify the first ratio: 4/200 = 1/50.
- step 4: Solve 1/50 = 10/x, so x = 10 * 50 = 500g.
- step 5: Verify: 500/10 = 50, which matches 200/4 = 50.
REASONING: Maintaining the ratio of people to flour ensures the recipe tastes the same regardless of how many people are served.
ANSWER: 500g
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A car travels 120 km in 3 hours at a constant speed. How far will it go in 7 hours?
STEPS:
- step 1: Given: 120 km in 3 hours. Find distance for 7 hours.
- step 2: Calculate speed (unit rate): 120 km / 3 hours = 40 km per hour.
- step 3: Apply the rate to the new time: 40 km/h * 7 hours.
- step 4: Multiply: 40 * 7 = 280.
- step 5: Interpret: The car covers 280 km in 7 hours.
REASONING: By finding the distance per 1 hour, we can scale it to any number of hours proportionally.
ANSWER: 280 km
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper sells 5 notebooks for 125 rupees. If a student has 250 rupees, how many notebooks can they buy?
STEPS:
- step 1: Given: 5 notebooks = 125 rupees. Target: ? notebooks = 250 rupees.
- step 2: Set up proportion: 5/125 = x/250.
- step 3: Simplify 5/125 to 1/25.
- step 4: Set 1/25 = x/250. Multiply both sides by 250: x = 250 / 25.
- step 5: Solve: x = 10.
REASONING: Since 250 is double 125, the number of notebooks must also be double the original amount to keep the price per notebook constant.
ANSWER: 10 notebooks
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have two piles of gold coins. Pile A has 10 coins weighing 50g. Pile B has 20 coins weighing 110g. Are the piles in direct proportion to their weight? Explain.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Find ratio of Pile A (10/50 = 1/5).
- step 2: Find ratio of Pile B (20/110 = 2/11).
- step 3: Compare 1/5 (which is 2/10) with 2/11.
- step 4: Since 2/10 is not equal to 2/11, they are not in direct proportion.
ANSWER: No, they are not in direct proportion because the ratio of coins to weight is not the same for both piles.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A printer prints 15 pages in 3 minutes. If the printer starts slowing down and the ratio of pages to minutes changes, can we still use direct proportion to predict future printing?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand that direct proportion relies on a constant ratio.
- step 2: Recognize that if the speed changes, the ratio is no longer constant.
- step 3: Conclude that the prediction would be inaccurate.
ANSWER: No, we cannot use direct proportion because the condition of a "constant ratio" is broken; the printer is no longer working at a consistent speed.
DIFFICULTY: hard
