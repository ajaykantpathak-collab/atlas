## DEFINITION
Word problems leading to equations involve translating real-world scenarios into mathematical expressions where an unknown quantity is represented by a variable, allowing us to find the missing value by solving the resulting equation.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to translate a narrative description of a relationship between two quantities into a single-variable algebraic equation and solve it correctly.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers single-variable linear word problems involving basic operations (sum, difference, multiples/times). It excludes systems of equations, multi-variable problems, and complex optimization.
## WALKTHROUGH
If your friend has 5 rupees more than you, and together you have 25 rupees, how would you find out how much money each of you has? Start by picking one person (usually the smaller amount) as your "x". If you are "x", your friend is "x + 5", and together they add up to the total: "x + (x + 5) = 25". Always identify the unknown, write the relationship as an expression, set it equal to the total, and solve for x step-by-step.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the first step when you see a word problem asking for an unknown number?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The first step is to assign a variable (like 'x') to the unknown quantity. A wrong answer like "adding the numbers in the problem" suggests the student is guessing operations without understanding the relationship between quantities.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a pen costs 'x' rupees and a notebook costs 'x + 10' rupees, what does the expression 'x + (x + 10)' represent?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It represents the total cost of one pen and one notebook combined. If a student says it represents the cost of one item, they are failing to recognize how the grouping of terms works in an equation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A basket has apples and oranges. There are 4 more apples than oranges. If there are 12 oranges, how do you express the number of apples using 'x' if 'x' is the number of oranges?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The number of apples is x + 4. Substituting 12 for x, we get 12 + 4 = 16 apples. If a student answers "x - 4", they are confusing "more than" with "less than."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If 3 times a number increased by 5 equals 20, how do you write this as an equation?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
3x + 5 = 20. If a student writes 3(x + 5) = 20, they have misunderstood the order of operations, thinking the "times" applies to the whole sum rather than just the number.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it always better to let 'x' be the larger number in a word problem? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It is not always better; it is usually easier to let 'x' be the smaller quantity to avoid subtraction. If a student says "yes" without justification, they lack the strategic thinking needed to simplify their own algebra.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a simple word problem that results in the equation 2x + 10 = 30.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: "A box has 30 marbles. There are two identical bags of marbles and 10 loose marbles. How many marbles are in one bag?" A poor answer would be a random sentence that doesn't logically connect to the numbers provided.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse "3 times more than x" (which is 3x + x or 4x) with "3 times x" (3x). They also struggle with "less than," often writing 5 - x instead of x - 5 when told a number is 5 less than x.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student struggles, have them draw a bar model. For "5 less than x," draw a long bar for 'x' and a shorter bar for '5', showing clearly that the result is x - 5. Practice translating small phrases like "double of a number" or "half of a number" into expressions before attempting full equations.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Rahim has 12 rupees more than Sita. Together they have 60 rupees. How much money does Sita have?
STEPS:
- step 1: Let Sita's money = x. Then Rahim's money = x + 12.
- step 2: Total money = x + (x + 12) = 60.
- step 3: Combine like terms: 2x + 12 = 60.
- step 4: Subtract 12 from both sides: 2x = 48.
- step 5: Divide by 2: x = 24.
REASONING: By defining the smaller amount as 'x', we create a simple relationship. Combining terms allows us to isolate the unknown.
ANSWER: 24 rupees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A notebook costs 3 times as much as a pen. If the total cost of one notebook and one pen is 40 rupees, what is the cost of the pen?
STEPS:
- step 1: Let pen = x. Then notebook = 3x.
- step 2: Equation: x + 3x = 40.
- step 3: Combine terms: 4x = 40.
- step 4: Divide by 4: x = 10.
REASONING: The "3 times" relationship creates a multiplication term (3x). Adding the two parts together gives the total cost.
ANSWER: 10 rupees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A vegetable seller has 50 kg of potatoes in two sacks. One sack has 8 kg more than the other. Find the weight of the lighter sack.
STEPS:
- step 1: Let lighter sack = x. Heavier sack = x + 8.
- step 2: Equation: x + (x + 8) = 50.
- step 3: Simplify: 2x + 8 = 50.
- step 4: Subtract 8: 2x = 42.
- step 5: Divide by 2: x = 21.
REASONING: The difference of 8 kg is added to one variable to represent the weight difference. Solving for x gives the lighter sack's weight.
ANSWER: 21 kg
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Meena says: "If I double my age and add 5, I get 25." Her friend says: "If I add 5 to my age and then double it, I get 25." Are they the same age? Explain using equations.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- step 1: Meena's equation: 2x + 5 = 25.
- step 2: Friend's equation: 2(x + 5) = 25.
- step 3: Solve Meena: 2x = 20, x = 10.
- step 4: Solve Friend: x + 5 = 12.5, x = 7.5.
ANSWER: No, they are not the same age. Meena is 10 and her friend is 7.5. The order of operations changes the result significantly.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A carpenter has a wooden plank of 100 cm. He cuts it into two pieces such that one piece is 20 cm longer than the other. Write the equation and find the length of the shorter piece. If he makes one piece 3 times longer than the other, how would the equation change?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: First scenario: x + (x + 20) = 100. 2x = 80, x = 40 cm.
- step 2: Second scenario: x + 3x = 100. 4x = 100, x = 25 cm.
- step 3: Compare results.
ANSWER: The shorter piece is 40 cm in the first case. In the second case, the equation is x + 3x = 100 and the shorter piece is 25 cm.
DIFFICULTY: hard
