## DEFINITION
A simple sentence contains one independent clause (a complete thought), while a complex sentence combines one independent clause with a dependent clause (a group of words that needs the independent clause to make sense).
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify a simple vs. complex sentence and write one example of each correctly. Mastery is shown by correctly labeling sentence types in a short paragraph.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers simple and complex sentence structures. It excludes compound-complex sentences, phrases, and technical syntax analysis.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are telling your friend about your day at school. If you say, "I played badminton," that is a simple, complete thought. But if you want to explain *why* or *when* it happened, you might say, "Because I practiced hard, I won the match." The second part is a complex sentence because it adds a piece of information that cannot stand alone. To begin, look for the "main" action in your sentence. If there is only one, it is likely simple; if you see words like "because," "when," or "if" connecting two ideas, you are likely looking at a complex sentence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the main difference between a simple sentence and a complex sentence?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A simple sentence has one independent clause, while a complex sentence includes at least one dependent clause. A wrong answer suggests the student confuses them with compound sentences or does not recognize what a "complete thought" is.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Read this: "Although Prabhat felt nervous, he played the game." Is this a simple or complex sentence? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It is a complex sentence because "Although Prabhat felt nervous" cannot stand alone as a sentence. A wrong answer reveals the student thinks the presence of two verbs automatically makes it a "simple" sentence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Turn the simple sentence "Surya smiled" into a complex sentence by adding a dependent clause.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Example: "When the match ended, Surya smiled." This requires the student to attach a dependent thought (using a subordinate conjunction) to the main clause. A wrong answer shows the student does not understand how to create a dependent clause.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the sentence: "Because it was raining, we stopped the game." Identify the dependent clause and the independent clause.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Dependent: "Because it was raining"; Independent: "we stopped the game." A wrong answer shows the student cannot identify which part of the sentence provides a complete meaning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Critique this sentence: "I went home because." Is this a complete complex sentence? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it is incomplete because "because" requires a reason to follow it to make it a proper dependent clause. A wrong answer indicates the student does not understand that dependent clauses must contain both a subject and a verb.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a complex sentence about playing a sport in your school playground.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Acceptable answers follow the pattern: [Dependent clause] + [Independent clause] or vice versa (e.g., "If we finish our homework, we will play cricket"). Poor answers lack a dependent clause or are just two simple sentences joined incorrectly.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that any long sentence is "complex." They confuse length with structure, often misidentifying a simple sentence with a long prepositional phrase (e.g., "Prabhat played a long match with his friend in the field") as complex because it contains many words.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to underline the verbs in the sentence. Then, identify if there is more than one clause that has its own subject and verb. If one part cannot stand alone (e.g., "because he was tired"), explain that it is a dependent clause, making the whole sentence complex.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Identify if the sentence "Surya practiced every day" is simple or complex and explain why.
STEPS:
- step 1: Find the subject (Surya).
- step 2: Find the verb (practiced).
- step 3: Check if there is another clause starting with a conjunction (None found).
- step 4: Conclude it is a simple sentence because there is only one independent clause.
REASONING: By breaking down the sentence into its components, we see it expresses a single complete thought without any extra dependent parts.
ANSWER: It is a simple sentence.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Identify the sentence "If you win, you feel happy" as simple or complex and explain the structure.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the two parts: "If you win" and "you feel happy."
- step 2: Test "you feel happy"—it is a complete thought (independent).
- step 3: Test "If you win"—it cannot stand alone (dependent).
- step 4: Classify the structure (Dependent + Independent = Complex).
REASONING: A sentence with a dependent clause is complex by definition.
ANSWER: It is a complex sentence.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Combine "The game ended" and "The sun set" into a complex sentence.
STEPS:
- step 1: Choose a conjunction (e.g., "when" or "as").
- step 2: Attach it to one clause to make it dependent ("When the game ended").
- step 3: Place it next to the independent clause ("The sun set").
- step 4: Arrange: "When the game ended, the sun set."
- step 5: Verify: Does it have one independent and one dependent clause? Yes.
REASONING: Linking clauses with a conjunction creates a relationship of time or cause, forming a complex structure.
ANSWER: "When the game ended, the sun set."
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Rewrite "I enjoy sports. I am healthy" into a single complex sentence.
COMPETENCY: Sentence transformation
STEPS:
- step 1: Recognize that the two sentences are independent.
- step 2: Add a subordinating conjunction to one.
- step 3: Join them with a comma if the dependent clause comes first.
- step 4: Finalize the structure.
ANSWER: "Because I enjoy sports, I am healthy."
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why does adding "Because" to the start of a sentence change the whole structure?
COMPETENCY: Structural Analysis
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the function of "Because."
- step 2: Note that it turns an independent clause into a dependent one.
- step 3: Explain that this forces the reader to look for a second, independent clause to complete the thought.
- step 4: Conclude that it forces the sentence to become complex.
- step 5: Provide an example.
ANSWER: Adding "Because" turns an independent thought into a dependent one, meaning the sentence is no longer complete on its own; it requires an independent clause to finish the meaning, creating a complex sentence (e.g., "Because I studied, I passed.").
DIFFICULTY: hard
