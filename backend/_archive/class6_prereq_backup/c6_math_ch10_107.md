## DEFINITION
A letter or number has a line of symmetry if it can be folded along a straight line such that the two halves perfectly overlap, like a mirror image. This line acts as a "fold" or "divider" that splits the figure into two identical, matching parts.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify, sketch, and count the lines of symmetry for any given letter or digit by mentally or physically "folding" it. They must accurately distinguish between vertical, horizontal, and no symmetry.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers identifying line symmetry in English capital letters and digits (0-9). It excludes rotational symmetry, 3D shapes, and font-specific variations (e.g., assuming standard sans-serif block print).
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever looked at your own reflection in a mirror? Imagine holding a piece of paper with the letter 'A' written on it up to a mirror; it looks exactly the same, doesn't it? To start, take a piece of paper, write a large 'A' or 'B', and try to fold the paper so the two sides match perfectly—this fold is your line of symmetry. If you can't make them match no matter how you fold, the letter has no symmetry. Always check for vertical (up-down) and horizontal (left-right) folds separately. The key insight is that the line of symmetry acts like a mirror: if you put a mirror on the line, the reflection should complete the original shape.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which of these letters has a vertical line of symmetry: 'B' or 'A'?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'A' has a vertical line of symmetry. A student who says 'B' likely confuses the shape of the letter with its position or forgets that the top and bottom of 'B' are usually different sizes, preventing a perfect vertical overlap.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Does the number '3' have a line of symmetry? If so, is it horizontal or vertical?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The number '3' has a horizontal line of symmetry (if drawn with a flat top and bottom). A student who says "vertical" or "no" may not have visualized the "fold" along the middle of the digit.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Draw the letter 'H' and identify all its lines of symmetry.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'H' has two lines of symmetry: one vertical (down the center) and one horizontal (across the middle). Students often miss the horizontal line because they only look for the most obvious vertical split.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the symmetry of the letter 'M' and the letter 'O'. Why is one more symmetrical than the other?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
'O' has both vertical and horizontal symmetry (and more), while 'M' has only vertical symmetry. A student struggling here might only see "balance" rather than specific "mirror-overlap" properties.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If you fold a paper with the letter 'N' vertically, do the halves match? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, they do not match because the diagonal bar of the 'N' runs in the opposite direction on the reflected half. Students who think it is symmetric are likely focusing on the two vertical pillars rather than the internal structure.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a list of three numbers that have absolutely no line of symmetry and explain why.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Examples: 2, 5, 7. These have no symmetry because their curves and corners do not align when folded in any direction (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal). An incorrect answer (e.g., including '8') shows a failure to test for both horizontal and vertical axes.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse "balance" with "symmetry." They might think a letter like 'S' is symmetric because it looks "balanced" or "pretty," but it doesn't have a line of symmetry because the top half (when folded horizontally) is not a mirror image of the bottom. They often try to rotate the letter to see if it matches, rather than folding it.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to cut the letter out of paper. Tell them: "If you cannot fold the cutout so that the edges touch perfectly on both sides of the crease, it is not a line of symmetry." Practice with a 'B'—fold it horizontally, then vertically—to show them why only one of those lines works.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student writes the letters "CODE" on a piece of paper. Which of these letters have at least one line of symmetry?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine 'C'. Fold it horizontally. The top and bottom match. It has horizontal symmetry.
- Step 2: Examine 'O'. Fold it horizontally, then vertically. It matches in both. It has symmetry.
- Step 3: Examine 'D'. Fold it horizontally. The top and bottom match. It has horizontal symmetry.
- Step 4: Examine 'E'. Fold it horizontally. The top and bottom match. It has horizontal symmetry.
- Step 5: Verify results. All four letters have at least one line of symmetry.
REASONING: By testing each letter for a potential fold, we confirm if it can be divided into mirror-halves.
ANSWER: C, O, D, and E all have at least one line of symmetry.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Identify the lines of symmetry for the number "808".
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine '8'. It has vertical and horizontal symmetry.
- Step 2: Examine '0'. It has vertical and horizontal symmetry.
- Step 3: Examine the sequence "808". A vertical line can be drawn through the center of the whole sequence.
- Step 4: Check for horizontal lines. The whole sequence can be folded horizontally through the middle.
- Step 5: Conclusion. The whole sequence "808" possesses both vertical and horizontal symmetry.
REASONING: Symmetry can apply to a single character or a set of characters if the entire arrangement maintains a mirror-image property.
ANSWER: The sequence "808" has both vertical and horizontal lines of symmetry.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student has a 2-digit number. The first digit is '1' and the second has vertical symmetry but no horizontal symmetry. Find two possible numbers.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify possible digits for the second position. Digits with vertical symmetry are 0, 1, 8.
- Step 2: Filter for "no horizontal symmetry". 0 has horizontal, 8 has horizontal, 1 (in some fonts) is vertical only.
- Step 3: Let's test 'A' (not a number, but check digits). Wait, digit '1' works. What about 'M' (not a digit)?
- Step 4: Since we need digits: 11 is a candidate. 1- (Wait, looking at 0, 1, 8). 11 is the primary candidate.
- Step 5: Verify: '1' has vertical symmetry, '11' has vertical symmetry and no horizontal symmetry (top of 1 is different from bottom).
REASONING: We must isolate the properties of each digit to satisfy the combined condition of the 2-digit number.
ANSWER: 11 (Note: In standard block fonts, 1 is the only digit satisfying this).
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have a word "MOM". Is the entire word symmetrical? Draw the line of symmetry and explain.
COMPETENCY: Visualizing and mapping symmetry in composite shapes.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze individual letters: M, O, M.
- Step 2: Check for a vertical line through the center of the word.
- Step 3: Reflect the left M to the right M. They align perfectly.
- Step 4: Reflect the O to itself. It aligns perfectly.
ANSWER: Yes, the word is symmetrical. The line of symmetry is a vertical line passing through the center of the letter 'O'. This works because the M on the left is a mirror image of the M on the right.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why does the number '8' have more lines of symmetry than the number '1'?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying geometric properties.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define symmetry for '8': It has horizontal and vertical (and diagonal) symmetry.
- Step 2: Define symmetry for '1': It has only vertical symmetry.
- Step 3: Compare the complexity of the shapes. '8' is a closed loop, '1' is a line.
- Step 4: Conclude that the closed loop structure of '8' allows for multiple axes of reflection.
ANSWER: '8' has more lines of symmetry because its structure is a closed, balanced loop that can be folded in multiple directions (horizontally and vertically) while still overlapping. '1', being a single straight line, only allows for vertical symmetry.
DIFFICULTY: hard
