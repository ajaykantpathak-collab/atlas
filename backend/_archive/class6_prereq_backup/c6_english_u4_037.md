## DEFINITION
Vocabulary in context is the skill of using clues within a sentence or paragraph to determine the meaning of an unfamiliar word without relying solely on a dictionary. It involves looking at the "neighborhood" of the word—the surrounding sentences—to see how the word functions to create a specific setting or emotion.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to define an unfamiliar word by identifying at least two context clues (such as examples, synonyms, or descriptive details) in a provided paragraph. The student should then be able to explain how the word contributes to the overall meaning of the text.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers general vocabulary acquisition strategies using context clues within the unit "Sports and Wellness." It excludes etymology (word origins), non-contextual dictionary memorization, and technical railway terminology.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever been reading a story and hit a word you didn't know, like 'phenomenon', but you kept reading and still understood the story? To begin, don't stop at the hard word; instead, look at the sentences right before and after it. Ask yourself: "Is the author describing what the person is doing?" or "Is there a word that means the opposite?" By looking at these 'clues', you can make an educated guess about the word's meaning, much like being a detective solving a mystery. The key insight is that words rarely travel alone; they are always accompanied by clues that reveal their secrets.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is a 'context clue' in a reading passage?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A context clue is a piece of information, such as a definition, synonym, or example, found near an unknown word that helps explain what it means. A wrong answer, like saying it's a dictionary definition, reveals the student believes vocabulary must be learned in isolation rather than through reading.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
In the sentence, "Surya was a real phenomenon; he won points again and again, laughing and joking," what does 'phenomenon' likely mean, and what clue tells you?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It means someone who is exceptionally good or talented. The clue is "won points again and again," which describes his special performance. If a student guesses it means "a bad player," they are ignoring the positive context clues provided by the author.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If you read the sentence, "The athlete was hopeless at the high jump, failing every attempt," how would you define 'hopeless' using context?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps: 1. Identify the unknown word: 'hopeless'. 2. Locate the descriptive phrase: 'failing every attempt'. 3. Link the phrase to the word. 4. Define it as 'lacking skill'. If a student thinks it means 'sad', they are confusing the word's common emotional meaning with its contextual meaning here.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze this: "Prabhat felt terrible about losing, on the contrary, Surya felt relaxed." Why does the phrase "on the contrary" help you define the two words?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
It signals that Prabhat and Surya have opposite feelings. Since we know Prabhat felt 'terrible', 'relaxed' must mean the opposite of 'terrible' (calm or at peace). If the student misses this, they are failing to recognize transition words as logic clues.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
"The coach conducted the match with great care." If you change 'conducted' to 'organized', does the sentence meaning stay the same? Why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, because in this context, 'conducted' refers to the act of setting up or running an event. If a student says no, they might be stuck on a different meaning of 'conduct' (like electricity), showing they aren't looking at the subject (the coach) and object (the match).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a sentence using the word 'significant' where the context makes its meaning clear without using a dictionary.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
"The last Friday of the month was significant; it was the only day we held our big tournament and handed out trophies." A good answer uses the example of the tournament to show 'significant' means 'important'. A poor answer would be "The day was significant," which lacks descriptive context.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to guess a word's meaning by looking only at the word itself rather than the full sentence. They might assume 'hopeless' always means "sad," whereas in the sports unit, it specifically refers to "lack of skill." This happens because they are accustomed to memorizing single definitions rather than analyzing the author's intent.
## ON_FAILURE
If the student struggles, ask them to use the "Cover-Up Method." Tell them to cover the unknown word with their finger and read the sentence using a placeholder word like "blank." Then, ask them to list three words that could fit in that "blank" based on the rest of the sentence. This forces them to focus on the sentence's logic rather than the word's dictionary definition.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Define 'sensed' in: "If Prabhat sensed that he would lose a game, he would not play it."
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the word 'sensed'.
- step 2: Read the 'if-then' structure of the sentence.
- step 3: Look for the result: he avoids playing if he feels defeat is coming.
- step 4: Conclude that 'sensed' means 'felt' or 'knew beforehand'.
- step 5: Check if 'knew' makes sense in the sentence.
REASONING: By looking at the cause-and-effect relationship, we see that the character is predicting an outcome. This leads to the definition of 'sensed' as a mental awareness.
ANSWER: 'Sensed' here means to feel or become aware of something before it happens.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Define 'phenomenon' in: "Surya was a real phenomenon. He won points again and again."
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the word 'phenomenon'.
- step 2: Note the following sentence as an example.
- step 3: Observe that winning repeatedly is a sign of high skill.
- step 4: Determine that a phenomenon is a person with special, rare talent.
- step 5: Verify the definition fits the context of a sports star.
REASONING: The sentence immediately following the target word provides a direct example of the word's meaning.
ANSWER: A phenomenon is an extraordinary or highly talented person.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Define 'harmony' in: "Yoga symbolises the harmony between human and nature."
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the word 'harmony'.
- step 2: Look for the surrounding keywords: 'unity', 'mind and body', 'nature'.
- step 3: Recall that 'unity' often means things working well together.
- step 4: Connect 'harmony' to the idea of balance or peaceful agreement.
- step 5: Verify the definition by substituting 'balance' into the sentence.
REASONING: Using existing knowledge of synonyms like 'unity' helps define abstract nouns like 'harmony'.
ANSWER: Harmony means a state of balance or peaceful agreement between different parts.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: In the phrase "hopeless at basketball," if the context also says he "could not score a basket," what does 'hopeless' mean here?
COMPETENCY: Inferential understanding
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the phrase 'hopeless at'.
- step 2: Link it to the provided evidence ('could not score').
- step 3: Determine the level of skill described.
- step 4: State the definition.
ANSWER: It means having no skill or talent in that specific activity, proven by the fact he couldn't score.
DIFFICULTY: easy
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why does the author use the word "significant" for the last Friday of the month? Use context to justify your answer.
COMPETENCY: Textual analysis
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify why that specific Friday is mentioned.
- step 2: Note that it is the day for "friendly matches."
- step 3: Compare it to other days where no matches occur.
- step 4: Explain that it is an important, turning-point day for the plot.
- step 5: Conclude the definition of 'significant' as 'important'.
ANSWER: It is significant because it is the day the coach organizes the games, which is the turning point of the story, making it important.
DIFFICULTY: hard
