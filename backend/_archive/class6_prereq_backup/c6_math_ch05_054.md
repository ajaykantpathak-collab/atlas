## DEFINITION
Forming algebraic expressions is the process of representing relationships between numbers and unknown quantities (variables) using mathematical operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. It allows us to write general rules that apply to any value of the unknown.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to translate a written sentence describing a relationship between quantities into a correct algebraic expression and identify the variable and the operations used within that expression.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers translating simple word descriptions into expressions involving one variable and basic operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division). It excludes the formation of equations (equalities), function notation, and complex multi-variable expressions.
## WALKTHROUGH
If your friend is 2 years younger than you, how do you find their age if you only know your age is 'y'? You start by identifying the unknown (y) and the action (subtracting 2), leading to the expression 'y - 2'. To form expressions, first, pick a letter for the unknown, then look for keywords: 'more' or 'sum' means add, 'less' or 'difference' means subtract, 'times' or 'product' means multiply, and 'divided' means division. Remember that in algebra, '3 times x' is simply written as '3x' without the multiplication sign. The key insight is that an expression acts like a "recipe"—it tells you exactly what to do with the unknown value once you know it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If a notebook costs $n$ rupees, how would you write the cost of 5 such notebooks?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is $5n$. An answer of $n+5$ reveals the student confuses multiplication (repeated addition of items) with addition (increasing the value by a constant amount).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a boy is $x$ years old today, what was his age 3 years ago? Explain why you chose your operation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The answer is $x - 3$. If a student answers $3 - x$, they have reversed the order of subtraction, failing to realize that the current age ($x$) is the starting point from which we take away 3 years.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A number is increased by 7. Write an expression for this. If the number is 10, what is the result?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The expression is $n + 7$. If $n=10$, the result is $10 + 7 = 17$. A student who writes $7n$ does not understand that "increased by" represents addition, not multiplication.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the expressions $x + 4$ and $4x$. How are they different in meaning?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
$x + 4$ means 4 is added to the value of $x$, while $4x$ means $x$ is multiplied by 4. A student who says they are the same ignores the fundamental difference between scaling a value and shifting a value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A farmer has $y$ mangoes and divides them equally into 6 baskets. Write the expression. If he has 30 mangoes, is your expression valid? Justify.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The expression is $y/6$. It is valid because for $y=30$, $30/6 = 5$ mangoes per basket. A student who writes $6/y$ has failed to identify the dividend (total) and the divisor (number of groups).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create an expression for a situation where a shopkeeper gives a discount of 5 rupees on any item costing $p$ rupees.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The expression is $p - 5$. A good answer identifies the variable $p$ and the discount operation (subtraction). A poor answer like $5 - p$ shows the student does not understand that the discount is taken away from the original price.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the placement of terms in subtraction and division. For example, when asked for "5 less than x", they write $5 - x$ instead of $x - 5$. This happens because they read left-to-right exactly as the words appear, ignoring that the "starting" quantity must come first in the expression.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to replace the variable with a real number. Say: "If you have 10 apples ($x=10$) and I take 5 away, do you have $5-10$ (which is impossible here) or $10-5$?" This helps them see that the total quantity must be the first term in the subtraction expression.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A tailor uses $c$ centimetres of cloth to stitch one shirt. He needs to stitch 8 shirts and then he uses an extra 20 centimetres for a decorative border. Write an expression for the total cloth used.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the unknown ($c$) and the number of shirts (8).
- step 2: Recognize "8 shirts" means multiplying the cloth per shirt by 8 ($8 \times c$ or $8c$).
- step 3: Recognize "extra 20 cm" means adding 20 to the result ($8c + 20$).
- step 4: Combine into the final expression: $8c + 20$.
- step 5: Verify by testing: if $c=100$ cm, total is $8(100) + 20 = 820$ cm.
REASONING: Multiplying the unit cost/length by the quantity gives the total for the shirts, and adding the fixed border amount completes the total.
ANSWER: $8c + 20$ cm
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A bucket contains $w$ litres of water. A family uses half of the water for cooking. Write an expression for the water remaining.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the total water as $w$.
- step 2: Identify "half of the water" as division by 2 or multiplication by 1/2 ($w/2$ or $0.5w$).
- step 3: Recognize "remaining" implies subtracting the used portion from the total ($w - w/2$).
- step 4: Simplify the expression: $w - w/2 = w/2$.
- step 5: Verify: if $w=10$, used is 5, remaining is $10-5=5$.
REASONING: Since half is used, the other half remains; the expression represents the subtraction of the part used from the whole.
ANSWER: $w/2$ litres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A taxi charges a fixed base fare of 50 rupees, plus 12 rupees for every kilometre ($k$) travelled. Write an expression for the total fare, and find the cost if the passenger travels 10 km.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify fixed cost (50) and variable cost ($12 \times k$).
- step 2: Combine them into an expression: $50 + 12k$.
- step 3: Substitute $k=10$ into the expression: $50 + 12(10)$.
- step 4: Calculate: $50 + 120 = 170$.
- step 5: Interpret: The total cost is 170 rupees.
REASONING: The fixed cost is constant regardless of distance, while the variable cost depends on the distance travelled; both must be added to get the final fare.
ANSWER: $50 + 12k$; 170 rupees
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A school library has $b$ books. The librarian buys 15 more books and then divides all books equally among 3 shelves. Write the expression. If $b=60$, how many books are on each shelf?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- step 1: Add the new books to the existing total ($b + 15$).
- step 2: Apply the division to the entire new total: $(b + 15) / 3$.
- step 3: Substitute $b=60$: $(60 + 15) / 3$.
- step 4: Calculate: $75 / 3 = 25$.
ANSWER: The expression is $(b+15)/3$. With 60 books, there are 25 books on each shelf.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two friends, Arav and Sara, have $x$ and $y$ rupees respectively. Arav gives 10 rupees to Sara. Write the new amounts for both. Is the sum of their money still the same as the original sum?
COMPETENCY: Analyzing relationships between variables
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify changes: Arav loses 10 ($x-10$), Sara gains 10 ($y+10$).
- step 2: New amounts: Arav $= x-10$, Sara $= y+10$.
- step 3: Original sum: $x + y$.
- step 4: New sum: $(x-10) + (y+10) = x + y$.
ANSWER: Arav has $x-10$ and Sara has $y+10$. The sum is still $x+y$ because the money was just transferred between them, not added or removed from the system.
DIFFICULTY: hard
