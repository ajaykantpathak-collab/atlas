## DEFINITION
Integers are a collection of numbers that include all natural numbers (1, 2, 3...), their negative opposites (-1, -2, -3...), and zero, which serves as the neutral reference point. They are used to represent quantities that have direction or status relative to a fixed zero point, such as temperature, altitude, or bank balances.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to represent real-world situations (e.g., depths, debts, temperatures) as positive or negative integers and correctly compare their values on a number line.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the identification and basic comparison of integers in real-life contexts like temperature, elevation, and finance. It excludes complex numbers, scientific notation, and advanced algebraic financial modeling.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wondered how a lift knows to go to "Floor -1" in a basement, or how a weather reporter says it is "minus 5 degrees"? Think of zero as the "starting line"—like the ground floor of a building or sea level. When you move above or forward from this line, you use positive numbers (+); when you move below or backward, you use negative numbers (-). To begin, draw a line and place zero in the middle. Practice marking movements: "up" or "deposit" as jumps to the right, and "down" or "withdrawal" as jumps to the left. The key insight is that the sign tells you the direction of the movement relative to zero.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the difference between a positive integer and a negative integer?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A positive integer is greater than zero, while a negative integer is less than zero. A wrong answer here often shows the student thinks negative numbers are just "small" numbers rather than numbers representing an opposite direction or "debt."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you are at sea level (0m) and dive to a depth of 50m, how would you represent your position as an integer?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
You would represent it as -50m. A student answering +50m ignores the "depth" or "below" aspect, showing they haven't grasped that negative signs indicate direction below a reference point.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Compare -10 and -2. Which is greater and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
-2 is greater than -10. On a number line, -2 is to the right of -10, and in real life, being 2 rupees in debt is better than being 10 rupees in debt. A wrong answer suggests the student is only looking at the digits (10 vs 2) and ignoring the negative sign's impact on value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If a bank account has a balance of -200 rupees, what does that mean in real terms?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
It means the account holder owes the bank 200 rupees. A student who says "they have 200 rupees" fails to understand that negative signs in finance represent debits or debt.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it possible for a negative number to be larger than a positive number? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, because every negative number is to the left of zero and every positive number is to the right of zero on the number line. A wrong answer reveals a fundamental misunderstanding of the order of integers on the number line.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a scenario where the number -5 is used.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A valid answer describes a temperature, a floor, or a financial situation below zero. A poor answer (like "the number of apples") is incorrect because objects cannot be counted in negative quantities in this context; the scenario must imply a direction or status relative to a zero point.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that -10 is "larger" than -2 because 10 is a larger number than 2. This happens because they see the negative sign as just a symbol and focus only on the magnitude (the digit).
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to draw a vertical number line (like a thermometer). Have them place 0 at the middle, then -2 and -10. Ask: "Which number is higher up on the thermometer?" When they see -2 is higher, explain that "higher" on a thermometer means a warmer (greater) temperature.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A submarine is at 200m below sea level. It rises 150m. What is its new position?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify starting position: 200m below sea level = -200.
- step 2: Identify the change: "rise" means move towards positive, so +150.
- step 3: Set up the expression: -200 + 150.
- step 4: Calculate: Moving from -200 towards 0 by 150 steps leaves you at -50.
- step 5: Interpret: The submarine is at 50m below sea level.
REASONING: We use negative for "below" and positive for "upward movement." Adding these integers represents the net change in depth.
ANSWER: -50m (or 50m below sea level)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Your bank balance is 500 rupees. You pay a bill of 700 rupees. What is your new balance?
STEPS:
- step 1: Starting balance: +500.
- step 2: The bill payment is a withdrawal: -700.
- step 3: Set up: 500 - 700.
- step 4: Calculate: 500 - 500 = 0, then we still need to pay 200 more, so 0 - 200 = -200.
- step 5: Interpret: You are now 200 rupees in debt.
REASONING: Payments are subtractions from your balance. When the payment exceeds your balance, the result enters the negative range.
ANSWER: -200 rupees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A city is at 300m altitude. A mine is 400m below ground level. What is the difference in elevation between them?
STEPS:
- step 1: City altitude = +300.
- step 2: Mine depth = -400.
- step 3: Difference means "Target - Starting" or the gap between them.
- step 4: Gap = 300 - (-400).
- step 5: Subtracting a negative is like adding: 300 + 400 = 700.
REASONING: The difference is the total vertical distance. We combine the distance from city to sea level (300) and sea level to mine (400).
ANSWER: 700m
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Rahul says, "Since 15 is bigger than 5, -15 must be bigger than -5." Is he correct? Explain your reasoning using a number line.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand Rahul's logic (focusing on magnitude only).
- step 2: Visualize the number line with 0, -5, and -15.
- step 3: Identify that -5 is closer to zero (to the right) than -15.
- step 4: Conclude that -5 > -15.
ANSWER: No, Rahul is incorrect. On a number line, numbers to the right are always greater. Since -5 is to the right of -15, -5 is the greater number.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: You have three bank accounts: A (-500 rupees), B (0 rupees), and C (100 rupees). Rank them from "most money" to "least money."
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the values: A = -500, B = 0, C = 100.
- step 2: Compare the values: 100 > 0 > -500.
- step 3: Map back to the account names.
- step 4: Write the final order.
ANSWER: Account C (100) > Account B (0) > Account A (-500). Account C has the most money, and Account A is in debt (least money).
DIFFICULTY: hard
