## DEFINITION
A triangle is a closed shape with three sides where the interior angles always add up to 180 degrees, while a quadrilateral is a closed shape with four sides where the interior angles always add up to 360 degrees.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to calculate the missing interior angle of a triangle given two angles, and a quadrilateral given three angles, by using the sum properties.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers finding missing angles in triangles and quadrilaterals using the sum property; it excludes circle theorems, formal geometric proofs, and general polygon angle rules.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are building a paper kite (quadrilateral) and a triangular flag. If you cut the paper for the flag and know two of its corners measure 60° and 70°, how do you find the third? Start by thinking of the total "budget" of degrees: 180° for a triangle. Subtract the angles you know (60 + 70 = 130) from the total (180 - 130 = 50). For a quadrilateral, your "budget" is 360°. Always remember that these shapes are like closed loops; the angles must "close" perfectly to meet the specific degree sum.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the sum of all interior angles in any triangle?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The sum is 180 degrees. If a student answers "360 degrees," they are confusing it with the quadrilateral property.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you have a triangle and a quadrilateral, which one has a larger sum of interior angles and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The quadrilateral has a larger sum (360°) because it has four sides instead of three, requiring more total rotation to close the shape. An incorrect answer suggests the student hasn't internalized the relationship between the number of sides and the total angle sum.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A triangle has two angles of 45° and 55°. Find the third angle.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The third angle is 80°. Calculation: 45 + 55 = 100; 180 - 100 = 80. A wrong answer likely results from miscalculating the sum or subtracting from 360 instead of 180.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
In a quadrilateral, three angles are 100°, 110°, and 70°. Find the fourth angle.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The fourth angle is 80°. Calculation: 100 + 110 + 70 = 280; 360 - 280 = 80. Errors here usually stem from forgetting the "360" total or simple addition mistakes.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Can a triangle have two angles of 90° each? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, because 90 + 90 = 180, leaving 0° for the third angle, which is impossible for a triangle. A "Yes" answer reveals a lack of understanding that all three angles must add exactly to 180°.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a quadrilateral where all four angles are equal. What is the measure of each angle?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Each angle must be 90° because 360 / 4 = 90. If the student picks random numbers, they fail to apply the "sum of 360" rule to a division problem.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often apply the "180-degree rule" to quadrilaterals or the "360-degree rule" to triangles. They treat the magic number (180 or 360) as a guess rather than a property of the shape's complexity.
## ON_FAILURE
If the student is confused, first draw a triangle and a square. Ask them to tear the corners off the triangle and see if they form a straight line (180°). Then, show that a square is just two triangles joined together, making it 180 + 180 = 360°. Have them write the "180" and "360" labels clearly on their notebook cover as a reference.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A triangular park has two corners marked with angles of 50° and 65°. A gardener needs to place a bench at the third corner. What is the angle at the third corner?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify given angles: 50° and 65°.
- 2: Recall triangle property: sum = 180°.
- 3: Add known angles: 50 + 65 = 115°.
- 4: Subtract from total: 180 - 115 = 65°.
- 5: The third angle is 65°.
REASONING: Since the three internal angles must form a complete set reaching 180°, subtracting the known parts from the whole identifies the missing piece.
ANSWER: 65 degrees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A floor tile is a quadrilateral. Three of its corner angles are 90°, 90°, and 80°. What is the fourth angle?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify known angles: 90°, 90°, and 80°.
- 2: Recall quadrilateral property: sum = 360°.
- 3: Add known angles: 90 + 90 + 80 = 260°.
- 4: Subtract from total: 360 - 260 = 100°.
- 5: The fourth angle is 100°.
REASONING: Quadrilaterals require a larger total angle sum to close four sides; subtracting the existing corners reveals the remaining gap.
ANSWER: 100 degrees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A surveyor is checking a trapezoid-shaped plot of land. Two angles are 60° each. The third angle is 120°. Find the fourth angle.
STEPS:
- 1: Identify known angles: 60°, 60°, 120°.
- 2: Recall property: quadrilateral sum = 360°.
- 3: Sum the known angles: 60 + 60 + 120 = 240°.
- 4: Find the difference: 360 - 240 = 120°.
- 5: The fourth angle is 120°.
REASONING: By applying the sum property of 360°, we confirm the last angle required to close the boundary of the four-sided plot.
ANSWER: 120 degrees
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are given a triangle with angles A, B, and C. If angle A is 90° and angle B is 45°, determine the value of angle C. If you increase angle A to 100°, what must happen to the other angles for it to remain a triangle?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- 1: Calculate angle C: 180 - (90 + 45) = 45°.
- 2: Identify the relationship: The total sum must remain 180°.
- 3: If A becomes 100°, the new sum of B + C must be 180 - 100 = 80°.
- 4: Conclude: B and C must decrease in total to balance the increase in A.
ANSWER: Angle C is 45°. If angle A increases, the sum of B and C must decrease to keep the total at 180°.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A friend claims that a quadrilateral can have three angles of 100° each. Is this possible? If so, what is the fourth angle? If not, why?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- 1: Check the sum: 100 + 100 + 100 = 300°.
- 2: Subtract from the quadrilateral total: 360 - 300 = 60°.
- 3: Analyze: A 60° angle is physically possible for a fourth corner.
- 4: Confirm: Yes, it is possible; the fourth angle is 60°.
ANSWER: It is possible, and the fourth angle is 60°. The student must show the calculation 360 - 300 = 60.
DIFFICULTY: hard
