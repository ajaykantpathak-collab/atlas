## DEFINITION
Time is a physical quantity used to measure the duration of events or the interval between them, with the 'second' serving as the basic SI unit. Larger and smaller units are derived from the second to allow for convenient measurement of durations ranging from microseconds to centuries.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to convert between time units (e.g., hours to minutes, minutes to seconds) and identify which unit is appropriate for measuring specific durations, such as the time taken to blink versus the time taken to grow up.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers standard time units (seconds, minutes, hours, days, years) and sub-units (milliseconds, microseconds, nanoseconds) and their conversion relationships. It excludes leap years, time zones, calendar systems, and atomic time standards.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are waiting for your favorite television show to start; do you check the clock in hours or minutes? To understand time, we start with the 'second' as our building block. Just like building a tower with bricks, we stack 60 seconds to make a minute, and 60 of those minutes to make an hour. When you need to measure something very fast, like a camera shutter clicking, we look at smaller parts of a second, such as milliseconds. The key insight is that because time is measured in consistent intervals, we can always use multiplication or division to switch between these units.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often assume that all time conversions use a factor of 10, similar to the metric system for length (10 mm = 1 cm). They might incorrectly calculate 100 seconds in a minute because they are applying the decimal system rules to time, which uses a base-60 system for minutes and hours.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that time is special and does not follow the base-10 pattern. Have them write out a table: "1 minute = 60 seconds", "1 hour = 60 minutes". Ask them to physically count 60 seconds with a stopwatch to see that it takes a "full minute," reinforcing that the conversion factor is 60, not 10.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student takes 2 hours and 15 minutes to complete their homework. How many minutes did they spend in total?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given time: 2 hours and 15 minutes.
- step 2: Convert hours to minutes using the rule 1 hour = 60 minutes.
- step 3: Calculate: 2 hours × 60 minutes/hour = 120 minutes.
- step 4: Add the extra minutes: 120 minutes + 15 minutes = 135 minutes.
- step 5: Conclude the total time is 135 minutes.
REASONING: We convert all parts of the time into the same unit (minutes) before adding them to ensure the total is accurate.
ANSWER: 135 minutes
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An express train travels from one city to another in 300 minutes. How many hours is this?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given time: 300 minutes.
- step 2: Recall the rule that 60 minutes = 1 hour.
- step 3: Set up division: 300 ÷ 60.
- step 4: Perform the calculation: 300 / 60 = 5.
- step 5: The total duration is 5 hours.
REASONING: Since we are moving from a smaller unit (minutes) to a larger unit (hours), we divide by the conversion factor.
ANSWER: 5 hours
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A light flashes every 2 seconds. How many times will it flash in 10 minutes?
STEPS:
- step 1: Convert the total time (10 minutes) into seconds: 10 × 60 = 600 seconds.
- step 2: Identify the interval: 2 seconds per flash.
- step 3: Divide total time by interval: 600 ÷ 2.
- step 4: Compute: 300 flashes.
- step 5: Verify if the interval logic holds: If it starts at 0, it flashes 301 times, but usually, we calculate intervals in a duration.
REASONING: By converting the duration to the same unit as the interval (seconds), we can easily calculate the frequency of the event.
ANSWER: 300 flashes

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: If a clock is broken and loses 5 minutes every hour, how many minutes does it lose in a full day (24 hours)?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the rate of loss: 5 minutes per hour.
- step 2: Identify the total time: 24 hours.
- step 3: Multiply the rate by the total time: 5 minutes/hour × 24 hours.
- step 4: Calculate: 5 × 24 = 120 minutes.
- step 5: Convert to hours for context: 120 minutes = 2 hours.
ANSWER: The clock loses 120 minutes, which is equal to 2 hours over the course of a full day.
