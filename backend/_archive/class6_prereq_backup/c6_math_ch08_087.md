## DEFINITION
Area is the measure of the total surface region covered by a closed two-dimensional shape. It represents how many unit squares (like 1 cm × 1 cm) can fit inside the boundary of the figure.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to calculate the area of any given rectangle or square when dimensions are provided and explain why area is measured in square units.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the calculation of the area of rectangles and squares using dimensions. It excludes the calculation of areas for parallelograms, triangles, composite shapes, or surface areas of 3D objects.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are asked to cover the floor of your bedroom with square tiles. How would you know how many tiles to buy? You would start by measuring how long and wide the floor is. If you place a grid of 1 cm squares over a shape, you are counting how much "flat space" it takes up. For a rectangle, you don't need to count every single square; you can simply multiply the number of rows (width) by the number of squares in each row (length). This multiplication is the shortcut to finding the area.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the formula to calculate the area of a rectangle if you are given its length and width?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The formula is Area = length × width. A student who says "length + width" is confusing the formula for perimeter with area; they need to revisit the difference between boundary length and interior space.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you have a square with a side of 5 cm, why do we call the result "25 cm²" instead of just "25 cm"?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
We use cm² because area measures two-dimensional space, and we are multiplying centimeters by centimeters (cm × cm). An answer of "25 cm" reveals a misunderstanding that area is a length, rather than a measure of surface coverage.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A rectangular garden is 8 meters long and 3 meters wide. How much space does it cover?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
8 meters × 3 meters = 24 square meters (m²). A student who answers "22 meters" has likely calculated the perimeter [2 × (8 + 3)] instead of the area, showing they have not distinguished between walking around a shape and filling it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If a square has an area of 36 cm², what is the length of one of its sides?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The side length is 6 cm, because 6 × 6 = 36. If a student answers "18" or "9," they are incorrectly dividing or guessing, showing they do not yet realize the area of a square is a number multiplied by itself.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Can two different rectangles have the same area but different side lengths? Give an example.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes. A rectangle of 4 cm × 3 cm has an area of 12 cm², and a rectangle of 6 cm × 2 cm also has an area of 12 cm². A student who says "no" thinks that area is tied to a specific set of dimensions, ignoring that different factors can produce the same product.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a rectangle that has an area of 20 cm². What are the possible dimensions?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Possible answers include 4 cm × 5 cm, 2 cm × 10 cm, or 1 cm × 20 cm. An answer that doesn't multiply to 20 shows a failure to grasp the relationship between dimensions and total area.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse Perimeter (the distance around) with Area (the space inside). They tend to apply the perimeter formula (2 × (l + w)) when asked for area, or vice-versa, because both involve length and width measurements. For example, for a 5 cm by 4 cm rectangle, they might answer 18 cm² (thinking of perimeter) instead of 20 cm².
## ON_FAILURE
First, draw the shape on a piece of grid paper. Count the individual 1 cm² squares inside the shape to see the area. Then, show that perimeter is only the outer "fencing" of the shape. Ask the student to calculate both for a small rectangle to see that the numbers are different, reinforcing that Area = filling, and Perimeter = edging.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A farmer wants to cover a rectangular shed floor with concrete. The shed is 6 meters long and 4 meters wide. If one bag of concrete covers 3 square meters, how many bags are needed?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify length (6 m) and width (4 m).
- Step 2: Use Area = l × w to find the total floor area.
- Step 3: 6 m × 4 m = 24 m².
- Step 4: Divide total area (24 m²) by coverage per bag (3 m²/bag).
- Step 5: 24 ÷ 3 = 8 bags.
REASONING: By finding the total area, we know exactly how much space requires concrete. Dividing by the coverage per bag tells us how many units of concrete are required to fill that space.
ANSWER: 8 bags
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A square patio has a side length of 9 meters. The owner wants to put a decorative border, but also needs to know the area for painting. What is the area?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the shape as a square and the side length (9 m).
- Step 2: Use the formula Area = s × s.
- Step 3: Substitute the value: 9 m × 9 m.
- Step 4: Calculate the product: 81 m².
REASONING: A square is a special rectangle where length and width are equal, so multiplying the side by itself gives the total surface coverage.
ANSWER: 81 m²
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A rectangular hall is 10 m long and 8 m wide. A rug that is 5 m × 4 m is placed in the center. What is the area of the floor not covered by the rug?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate the area of the hall (10 m × 8 m = 80 m²).
- Step 2: Calculate the area of the rug (5 m × 4 m = 20 m²).
- Step 3: Identify that "not covered" means subtracting the rug area from the hall area.
- Step 4: Subtract: 80 m² - 20 m² = 60 m².
REASONING: The rug takes up a portion of the total area; removing that portion from the total leaves the uncovered floor space.
ANSWER: 60 m²
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you double the length and width of a rectangle, does the area also double? Explain using a 2x3 rectangle as an example.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate area of original 2x3 rectangle (6 m²).
- Step 2: Double the dimensions to 4x6.
- Step 3: Calculate area of new 4x6 rectangle (24 m²).
- Step 4: Compare 6 m² and 24 m².
ANSWER: No, the area becomes four times larger, not double. 24 is four times 6. Doubling both dimensions means multiplying by 2 twice (2 × 2 = 4).
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A rectangular playground has an area of 100 m². List two different pairs of dimensions this playground could have, and explain which one requires more fencing (perimeter).
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify pairs of numbers that multiply to 100 (e.g., 10x10 and 20x5).
- Step 2: Calculate the perimeter for 10x10 [2 × (10+10) = 40 m].
- Step 3: Calculate the perimeter for 20x5 [2 × (20+5) = 50 m].
- Step 4: Compare results.
ANSWER: The playground could be 10m x 10m or 20m x 5m. The 20m x 5m playground requires more fencing (50 m) than the 10m x 10m one (40 m), showing that shapes with the same area can have different perimeters.
DIFFICULTY: hard
