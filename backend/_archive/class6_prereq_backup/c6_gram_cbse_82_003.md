## DEFINITION
Past and future have matching pairs: simple past for finished actions ('She danced yesterday'), past continuous for ongoing past ('She was dancing when I arrived'); simple future = will + base ('She will dance'), and 'going to' for planned future ('She is going to dance tomorrow').

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student forms and chooses between simple past and past continuous (the interrupted-action pattern), and between will and going-to for predictions vs plans.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the four forms with regular verbs plus the common irregulars (go→went, eat→ate, is→was). Does NOT cover past perfect or future perfect (class 9+).

## WALKTHROUGH
The interrupted-action pattern is the heart: 'While she WAS DANCING, the music STOPPED' — long background action (continuous) cut by a short event (simple past). Future split: 'will' for decisions made now and predictions; 'going to' for plans already made — 'I am going to visit Agra this summer.'

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students learn irregular pasts as lists without patterns, write 'didn't went' (double past — after 'did' the verb returns to base form), and use will/going-to interchangeably.

## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: For interrupted past — background gets was/were+V-ing, the interrupter gets simple past. Step 2: After 'did/didn't', verb returns to base — never 'did went'. Step 3: Future — decided already? going to. Deciding now/predicting? will.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
'While we ___(play) cricket, it started to rain.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
'were playing' — background action in progress when the rain interrupted. A wrong answer 'played' flattens both events into one instant — missing the while-pattern entirely.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Fix: 'He didn't went to school yesterday.'

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
'He didn't go' — after 'did', the main verb returns to base form; the past is already carried by 'did'. A wrong answer 'didn't goed' or keeping 'went' shows double-past marking.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
'I have decided — I ___ visit Agra this summer.' will or going to?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
'am going to' — the plan already exists ('I have decided'). A wrong answer 'will' misses the will/going-to split: will = fresh decisions/predictions, going to = existing plans.
