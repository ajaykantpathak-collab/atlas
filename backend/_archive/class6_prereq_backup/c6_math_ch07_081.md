## DEFINITION
A perpendicular line is a line that meets another at a right angle (90°), while an angle bisector is a ray that divides an angle into two equal parts. Together, these tools allow us to divide space and shapes into precise, symmetrical sections using only a compass and a straightedge.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to use a compass and ruler to construct a perpendicular bisector of a line segment and a perpendicular line from a point on a line. The student must demonstrate accuracy by checking that the resulting angles are 90° or that the segments are divided equally.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the geometric construction of perpendicular lines and angle bisectors using a compass and straightedge. It excludes formal geometric proofs, construction of complex polygons, and circle-related loci.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are trying to find the exact middle of a long garden path so you can place a fountain right in the center. How do you find that perfect middle point without guessing? To begin, you place your compass point on one end of the path (the line segment) and draw an arc that reaches past the middle, doing the same from the other end. Where those arcs cross above and below the path, you draw a line; that line is the perpendicular bisector, and it cuts your path exactly in half at a perfect 90-degree angle. This works because the compass keeps the distance consistent, creating a balance between the two sides.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the measure of the angle formed by two perpendicular lines?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The angle is exactly 90 degrees. If a student answers "180 degrees" or "any angle," they likely confuse perpendicular lines with parallel lines or general intersecting lines.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you have an angle of 60° and you draw its angle bisector, what is the measure of the two new angles created?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Each new angle is 30°. If a student answers "60°" or "15°," they do not understand that "bisect" means to divide into two equal parts.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
To construct a perpendicular bisector of a line segment AB, where do you place the compass point first?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
You place the compass point on endpoint A (or B) and open it to more than half the length of the segment. A wrong answer suggests the student is guessing or lacks a systematic approach to using the compass.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why do we draw arcs both above and below the line segment when constructing a perpendicular bisector?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
We need two points to define a unique line; one arc alone provides only a single point, but two intersection points allow us to draw the perpendicular line that passes through the exact center of the segment. If the student says "it looks better," they fail to see the geometric requirement for two points to define a line.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If you are asked to construct a perpendicular to a line from a point ON the line, why is it necessary to first mark two equidistant points on the line?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Those two points act as the new centers for the final arcs, ensuring the resulting line is perfectly vertical (perpendicular) to the original line at that specific point. An incorrect answer indicates the student thinks the construction is arbitrary rather than based on symmetry.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a plan to divide a 12cm line segment into four equal parts using only a compass and straightedge.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
First, construct the perpendicular bisector to split the 12cm segment into two 6cm parts. Then, construct the perpendicular bisectors of those two new segments. A poor answer would suggest measuring with a ruler, which ignores the geometric construction requirement.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often fail to change the compass width after drawing the first arc, or they change the width mid-construction, which prevents the arcs from intersecting correctly. They mistakenly believe the compass width must be the same for all parts of the construction, whereas only the arcs from the two centers must share the same radius.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student's arcs do not cross, guide them to: 1) Increase the compass opening to more than half the segment length. 2) Ensure the point of the compass is exactly on the endpoint. 3) Remind them that the same compass width must be used for both the 'above' and 'below' arcs to maintain symmetry.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You have a line segment AB that is 10cm long. Construct its perpendicular bisector to find the center point.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Place the compass point at A and open it to a width greater than 5cm (e.g., 7cm).
- Step 2: Draw an arc above and below the line segment.
- Step 3: Without changing the compass width, move the compass point to B.
- Step 4: Draw two more arcs that intersect the first two arcs above and below the line.
- Step 5: Use your ruler to draw a line connecting the two intersection points; this line passes through the 5cm mark.
REASONING: By using the same compass width from both ends, you create two points that are equidistant from A and B, ensuring the line passing through them is the perpendicular bisector.
ANSWER: A line crossing segment AB at the 5cm mark at a 90° angle.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Construct a perpendicular line to a line 'l' passing through a point 'P' located on 'l'.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Place the compass point on P and draw two arcs on line 'l', one to the left and one to the right, marking two points (X and Y).
- Step 2: Open the compass slightly wider than the distance XP.
- Step 3: Place the compass on X and draw an arc above line 'l'.
- Step 4: Without changing the width, place the compass on Y and draw an arc above line 'l' that intersects the first arc.
- Step 5: Draw a line from the intersection point down to point P.
REASONING: Point P is equidistant from X and Y, so the intersection point of the arcs creates a path that is perfectly vertical to the baseline.
ANSWER: A perpendicular line passing through P.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: You have a 90° angle (∠ABC). Divide it into two 45° angles.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Place the compass point on vertex B and draw an arc that cuts both arms of the angle at points D and E.
- Step 2: Open the compass to a width greater than half the distance DE.
- Step 3: Place the compass point on D and draw an arc inside the angle.
- Step 4: Without changing the width, place the compass on E and draw an arc intersecting the previous arc at point F.
- Step 5: Use a ruler to draw a line from B to F.
REASONING: By creating points equidistant from B on both arms, and then finding a point F equidistant from D and E, line BF acts as the axis of symmetry for the angle.
ANSWER: Two 45° angles.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you were given a line segment and asked to find the midpoint, but you only had a compass and no ruler, how would you ensure your construction is correct?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and justification
STEPS:
- Step 1: Perform the perpendicular bisector construction.
- Step 2: Observe where the bisector crosses the segment.
- Step 3: Verify symmetry by checking if the distance from the midpoint to each end is visually identical or by using the compass to "walk" the distance.
ANSWER: You would perform the perpendicular bisector construction. The intersection point is the midpoint; you confirm it by checking that the compass distance from the center to A is equal to the compass distance from the center to B.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student tries to construct a perpendicular bisector, but their arcs do not intersect. List two possible errors they might have made and how to fix them.
COMPETENCY: Reasoning and troubleshooting
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that intersection requires the "reach" of the compass to be greater than half the length.
- Step 2: Identify that moving the compass point away from the endpoints causes the intersection to miss.
- Step 3: Explain the fix for each.
ANSWER: Error 1: The compass width is too small; fix by opening it wider than half the segment. Error 2: The compass points were not placed exactly on the endpoints; fix by ensuring the point is secure at A and B.
DIFFICULTY: hard
