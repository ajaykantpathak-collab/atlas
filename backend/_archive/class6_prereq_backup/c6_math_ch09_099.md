## DEFINITION
A double bar graph is a graphical display that uses two bars placed side-by-side for each category to compare two different sets of related data at a glance. By using different colors or patterns for the two bars, we can easily observe the differences and similarities between two data groups.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to correctly plot two distinct sets of data on a single graph and accurately interpret the differences between the side-by-side bars.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the representation and interpretation of two related data sets on one graph. Excludes grouped bar graphs, stacked bar graphs, and error bars.
## WALKTHROUGH
If your school wanted to compare the number of students who played Cricket versus Hockey in Class 6A and 6B, how would you show all that information at once? A single bar graph can only show one thing at a time, but a double bar graph allows you to draw two bars side-by-side—one for Cricket and one for Hockey—for each class. To start, draw your base line (x-axis) and your number line (y-axis), then decide on a scale that fits both sets of data. Create a "key" or "legend" using different colors (e.g., Red for Cricket, Blue for Hockey) so anyone reading the graph knows which bar belongs to which sport. By keeping the bars for each class next to each other, you can immediately see which sport is more popular in each class.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the main purpose of using a double bar graph instead of two separate single bar graphs?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The main purpose is to compare two related sets of data side-by-side. A student who says "to show more numbers" misses the core concept of comparison; they need to understand that the proximity of the two bars is what makes the analysis easier.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a double bar graph has bars for 'Rainfall in 2023' and 'Rainfall in 2024' for different months, why is it important to use a key or legend?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The key identifies which color represents 2023 and which represents 2024. A wrong answer like "it looks pretty" shows they don't grasp that the key is a functional tool for data identification.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
In a double bar graph showing marks of two students (Amit and Priya) in 5 subjects, if Amit’s bar is consistently taller than Priya's in all subjects, what does this tell you?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
It tells you that Amit scored higher than Priya in every subject represented. A student who answers only "Amit is smart" is not interpreting the data; they must focus on the relative heights of the bars.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If you are drawing a double bar graph to show the price of apples and oranges over 4 months, why must the width of all bars be the same?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Uniform width ensures that the comparison is based purely on the height (the numerical value) of the bars. If bars had different widths, the eye might be tricked into thinking a wider bar represents a larger quantity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If the gap between the two bars of a pair is inconsistent (e.g., one pair is touching, another pair has a wide gap), does it affect the accuracy of the data?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
It does not change the numerical data, but it makes the graph confusing and unprofessional. A student who says "it's fine" fails to see that graphs are communication tools that require consistency for clarity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a scenario where a double bar graph would be more useful than a pictograph.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer would be "comparing the population of two states over 10 years," because the numbers are too large for pictures. A poor answer would be "counting toys," as that is better suited for a simple list or pictograph.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to add the values of the two side-by-side bars together. They confuse the double bar graph with a "stacked" bar graph and assume the total height of both bars combined is the value they need to report.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to point to just ONE bar and read its height against the vertical axis. Explain that each bar represents an independent value. Say: "We don't add them; we look at them side-by-side like two friends standing next to each other to see who is taller."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Two shops, A and B, sold the following number of notebooks in 3 months:
Jan: A=50, B=40; Feb: A=60, B=70; Mar: A=80, B=60. Draw and interpret the data.
STEPS:
- 1: List the data pairs for each month.
- 2: Choose a scale for the y-axis (e.g., 1 unit = 10 notebooks).
- 3: Draw pairs of bars for each month (Jan, Feb, Mar) on the x-axis.
- 4: Use two different colors to represent Shop A and Shop B.
- 5: Compare the heights: In Jan A>B, Feb B>A, Mar A>B.
REASONING: By using a consistent scale, we accurately translate counts into heights. Side-by-side placement allows for immediate visual comparison of monthly performance.
ANSWER: A total of 3 pairs of bars showing the sales performance.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Compare the height of 3 boys and 3 girls in a group (cm):
Boys: 120, 130, 140; Girls: 125, 125, 145.
STEPS:
- 1: Set the y-axis to start at 100 cm (use a break symbol if needed) to show differences clearly.
- 2: Create a legend: Blue for Boys, Pink for Girls.
- 3: Plot the pairs side-by-side for each of the 3 pairs of students.
- 4: Identify which pair has the taller girl and which has the taller boy.
REASONING: Because the data points are close in value, using a zoomed-in scale makes the comparison easier.
ANSWER: 3 pairs of bars on the graph.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Imran has 200, 300, 400 rupees in 3 days. His sister has 100, 350, 450 rupees. Create a graph and find the total difference.
STEPS:
- 1: Plot pairs (200,100), (300,350), (400,450).
- 2: Calculate difference for Day 1: 200-100 = 100.
- 3: Calculate difference for Day 2: 350-300 = 50.
- 4: Calculate difference for Day 3: 450-400 = 50.
- 5: Total difference of heights = 100+50+50 = 200.
REASONING: We use the graph to visualize the gaps, then perform arithmetic to find the specific numeric difference between the two sets.
ANSWER: 200 rupees total difference.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have a double bar graph showing temperature in Delhi and Mumbai for one week. The bars for Delhi are consistently higher. Does this mean Delhi is always hotter?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- 1: Look at the y-axis for temperature.
- 2: Compare the height of the 'Delhi' bar and 'Mumbai' bar for every day.
- 3: Conclude that if Delhi's bar is higher every day, it indicates a higher temperature record for those days.
ANSWER: Yes, if the Delhi bar is consistently higher, it indicates that the recorded temperature for Delhi was higher than Mumbai on those specific days shown in the graph.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student draws a double bar graph where the 'Boys' bars are all 5cm wide and 'Girls' bars are 2cm wide. Is this a good way to compare the data? Why or why not?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction.
STEPS:
- 1: Recall that bar graphs represent value through height.
- 2: Recognize that width affects visual perception of "size."
- 3: Explain that different widths make the graph misleading.
ANSWER: No, it is not a good way. The width of bars must be uniform because the eye might incorrectly perceive the wider bar as having a larger quantity, even if the heights are the same.
DIFFICULTY: hard
