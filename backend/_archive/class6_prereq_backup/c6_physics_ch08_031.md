## DEFINITION
Non-uniform motion is the movement of an object where its speed changes over time, meaning it does not cover equal distances in equal intervals of time. In this type of motion, an object may speed up, slow down, or stop momentarily during its journey.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify non-uniform motion in everyday scenarios (like a car in traffic) and distinguish it from uniform motion by describing why the speed is not constant.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the concept of changing speed and its qualitative description. It excludes acceleration formulas, vector quantities, and calculus-based motion analysis, adhering strictly to Class 6 physics foundations.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever watched a car driving through a busy market street? It doesn't move at the same speed the whole time; it slows down for a turn, speeds up on an empty road, and stops at a red light. To start, imagine your own journey to school: you walk quickly, perhaps stop to talk to a friend, and then run because you are late. This is non-uniform motion because your "pace" or speed is always changing. By observing that you cover different distances in the same amount of time (like one minute), you can see that motion is rarely perfectly steady. The key insight is that most things we see in nature—birds, cyclists, or running water—are moving in a non-uniform way.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If a cyclist travels 5 meters in the first second and 3 meters in the second second, is the cyclist’s motion uniform or non-uniform?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The motion is non-uniform. A wrong answer here suggests the student confuses "total distance" with "constant speed." If they think it is uniform because the cyclist is "still moving," they have missed the core definition that distance covered must be equal in every equal time interval.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Can you describe a situation where a ball thrown into the air shows non-uniform motion?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
As the ball rises, it slows down until it stops for a tiny fraction of a second at the peak, then it speeds up as it falls back down. Students who answer "it moves at the same speed" are failing to observe the influence of gravity, which naturally causes non-uniformity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A train leaves a station and increases its speed steadily for 5 minutes. Is this non-uniform motion? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Yes, it is non-uniform. Step 1: Identify that the speed is changing. Step 2: Recognize that changing speed means the distance covered per minute is increasing. Step 3: Conclude that since distance per interval is not equal, the motion is non-uniform. A wrong answer shows the student incorrectly believes "speeding up" is not "motion."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If you draw a distance-time graph for a car stuck in a traffic jam, why would the line be curved instead of straight?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The line is curved because the car's distance from the start point is not increasing at a steady rate. If they say "it's straight because it's a road," they are confusing the shape of the road with the representation of motion on a graph.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is a person walking at a steady pace of 1 meter per second for 10 seconds an example of non-uniform motion? Defend your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it is uniform motion because the speed is constant. If the student argues it is non-uniform, they likely do not realize that "steady pace" is the exact opposite of "changing speed."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a scenario where a toy car changes from uniform to non-uniform motion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer involves a car moving on a smooth floor (uniform) and then hitting a rug or a rough surface (slowing down, making it non-uniform). A poor answer just lists random movements without explaining the transition in speed.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that if an object is moving, its motion must be "uniform" simply because it is traveling a straight path. They conflate "linear motion" (the path) with "constant speed." For example, they might say a car accelerating on a straight highway is uniform because it isn't turning, failing to realize that "increasing speed" makes it non-uniform.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to count how many steps they take in the first 5 seconds of walking, then in the next 5 seconds. If the numbers are different, explain that their speed changed. Define uniform motion as "doing the exact same thing every single second," and non-uniform as "everything else." Use the car-in-traffic example to show that even on a straight road, changing speed makes the motion non-uniform.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A cyclist travels 10 metres in the first 2 seconds and 6 metres in the next 2 seconds. Is this uniform? Calculate the speed for each interval.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify interval 1 (10m, 2s) and interval 2 (6m, 2s).
- Step 2: Use Speed = Distance / Time.
- Step 3: Interval 1 Speed = 10 / 2 = 5 m/s.
- Step 4: Interval 2 Speed = 6 / 2 = 3 m/s.
- Step 5: Compare 5 m/s and 3 m/s. They are not equal, so motion is non-uniform.
REASONING: By splitting the journey into equal time slices, we prove the speed is not constant, confirming non-uniformity.
ANSWER: Non-uniform; 5 m/s and 3 m/s.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A marble rolls down a slope. It travels 2 cm in the first second, 4 cm in the second, and 6 cm in the third. Is this uniform?
STEPS:
- Step 1: List distances: 2cm, 4cm, 6cm.
- Step 2: List times: 1s, 1s, 1s.
- Step 3: Observe that for every 1 second, the distance is increasing.
- Step 4: Since distance is not the same for every interval, the speed is changing.
- Step 5: Conclude it is non-uniform motion.
REASONING: Uniform motion requires the exact same distance every second. Since the distance increases every second, the speed is increasing, which fits the definition of non-uniform motion.
ANSWER: Non-uniform.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A bus travels 20 km in the first hour and gets stuck in a jam, traveling only 5 km in the second hour. Calculate the total distance, total time, and if the speed is uniform.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Total distance = 20 km + 5 km = 25 km.
- Step 2: Total time = 1 hour + 1 hour = 2 hours.
- Step 3: Check speed in Hour 1 (20 km/h) and Hour 2 (5 km/h).
- Step 4: Since 20 km/h ≠ 5 km/h, the motion is non-uniform.
- Step 5: Note that speed changes due to external factors like traffic.
REASONING: We apply the definition of non-uniform motion by checking if the speed remains constant throughout the two-hour duration.
ANSWER: Non-uniform; 25 km total distance.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are watching a leaf fall from a tree. It flutters and changes speed as the wind blows. Why can you not use a single "speed" number to describe its entire trip?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to physical concepts.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Observe that the leaf changes its path and speed.
- Step 2: Recognize that speed is distance divided by time for a specific interval.
- Step 3: Explain that because the leaf's speed changes at every moment, one number only describes an average, not the actual motion.
ANSWER: The leaf is in non-uniform motion because wind makes it speed up and slow down; therefore, a single speed number would be inaccurate for the whole trip.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare the motion of a ball dropped from a height and a clock's pendulum. Both are non-uniform in their own way. How do they differ?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that the ball speeds up continuously as it falls.
- Step 2: Identify that the pendulum speeds up and slows down repeatedly as it swings.
- Step 3: Compare the "repeating" nature of the pendulum versus the "one-way" nature of the falling ball.
ANSWER: Both are non-uniform, but the pendulum's non-uniform motion is periodic (it repeats the same pattern), whereas the ball's motion is a continuous increase in speed until it hits the ground.
DIFFICULTY: hard
