## DEFINITION
A quadrilateral is a closed two-dimensional shape formed by four straight line segments (sides) and four interior angles, where the sum of these interior angles is always 360 degrees.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify, name, and categorize quadrilaterals based on their specific properties (parallel sides and equal angles) and calculate a missing angle using the sum-of-angles rule.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the classification of quadrilaterals (parallelogram, rectangle, square, rhombus, trapezium) and their defining properties. It excludes area calculations, proofs of theorems, and cyclic quadrilaterals.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever looked at a school playground or a window frame and noticed how many different four-sided shapes there are? To begin, think of a quadrilateral as any "four-sided box"—if you draw four connected lines that close up, you have made one. To identify the specific type, start by asking, "Are the opposite sides parallel?" If yes, it’s a parallelogram family; if only one pair is, it’s a trapezium. Next, check if the corners are square (90 degrees) or if all sides are the same length. The key insight is that these shapes are like a family tree: a square is just a very "perfect" rectangle, and a rectangle is a "perfect" parallelogram.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the sum of all interior angles of any quadrilateral?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The sum is 360 degrees. A wrong answer like "180 degrees" shows the student is confusing quadrilaterals with triangles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Can a quadrilateral be a rectangle if it does not have four right angles? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, a rectangle must have all four angles equal to 90 degrees by definition. Answering "yes" indicates the student does not understand that properties define the name of the shape.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A quadrilateral has three angles measuring 80°, 100°, and 80°. What is the measure of the fourth angle?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The fourth angle is 100° (360 - 260 = 100). If a student gets this wrong, they likely failed to subtract correctly from 360 or forgot the total sum rule.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How is a rhombus different from a square, even though both have four equal sides?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
A square must have 90-degree angles, whereas a rhombus does not require its angles to be 90 degrees. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks side length is the only defining property of these shapes.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is it correct to call a square a type of rectangle? Defend your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, because a square satisfies all properties of a rectangle (opposite sides parallel, all angles 90°), plus it has the extra property of equal sides. If a student says "no," they don't understand the hierarchical nature of geometric classifications.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Draw a figure that is a quadrilateral but is neither a parallelogram nor a trapezium. Describe its properties.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A kite or a generic irregular quadrilateral. The answer should explain that it has four sides but lacks parallel sides. A poor answer would be drawing a triangle or a shape with more than four sides.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that if a shape has four equal sides, it MUST be a square. They forget about the rhombus, which has four equal sides but lacks the 90-degree corner property.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to draw two four-sided shapes with equal sides: one with square corners and one tilted like a diamond. Label the square corners on the first and point out the "leaning" corners on the second. Explain that the "leaning" one is called a rhombus because it lacks the 90-degree constraint.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A park in the shape of a quadrilateral has three angles measuring 70°, 110°, and 70°. Find the fourth angle to ensure the fence is closed properly.
STEPS:
- step 1: identify knowns (70°, 110°, 70°) and total sum (360°).
- step 2: add the known angles: 70 + 110 + 70 = 250°.
- step 3: subtract the sum of known angles from 360°: 360 - 250 = 110°.
- step 4: the fourth angle is 110°.
REASONING: Since all quadrilaterals must close at 360°, the missing piece of the puzzle must be the difference between the total and the sum of the parts already present.
ANSWER: 110°
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A carpenter is building a tabletop. He says it is a parallelogram with one angle of 90°. What must the other three angles be?
STEPS:
- step 1: recall that in a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal and adjacent angles sum to 180°.
- step 2: if one angle is 90°, the adjacent angle must be 180 - 90 = 90°.
- step 3: since opposite angles are equal, the other two angles must also be 90°.
- step 4: conclude that all four angles are 90°.
REASONING: By applying the properties of a parallelogram, we see that a parallelogram with one right angle forces all angles to become right angles, turning it into a rectangle.
ANSWER: All four angles are 90°.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A window frame is a quadrilateral. It has one pair of parallel sides of lengths 5m and 8m, and the sides connecting them are not parallel. What type of quadrilateral is this, and what is the minimum information needed to define its shape?
STEPS:
- step 1: identify the property of one pair of parallel sides (trapezium).
- step 2: identify that the other sides are not parallel (confirms it is not a parallelogram).
- step 3: note the lengths are unequal (5m and 8m).
- step 4: categorize as a trapezium.
- step 5: determine that to define it fully, we need the distance between the parallel sides (height) and the angles.
REASONING: Defining a shape requires understanding its constraints; since it is a trapezium, we need height and angles to fix the geometry.
ANSWER: It is a trapezium; we need height and angles to define it.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you cut a square along its diagonal, you get two triangles. If you cut a rectangle in the same way, you get two triangles. Are these triangles the same? Explain.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: visualize the diagonal cut.
- step 2: identify properties of the resulting triangles (right-angled).
- step 3: compare square-based triangles (isosceles right triangles) with rectangle-based triangles (scalene right triangles).
ANSWER: No. The triangles from a square are isosceles right triangles (two sides equal), while the triangles from a rectangle are generally scalene right triangles (sides depend on length/width), unless the rectangle is a square.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A design teacher claims that a rhombus is just a square that has been "pushed over." Is this mathematically true? Justify using the properties of angles and sides.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- step 1: identify properties of a square (equal sides, 90° angles).
- step 2: identify properties of a rhombus (equal sides, not necessarily 90° angles).
- step 3: compare the "pushing" (changing angles) to the fixed property (sides).
ANSWER: Yes, it is true. "Pushing" the square changes the angles (they are no longer 90°) but the sides remain equal. The result is a rhombus, which retains the property of equal sides but loses the right-angle property.
DIFFICULTY: hard
