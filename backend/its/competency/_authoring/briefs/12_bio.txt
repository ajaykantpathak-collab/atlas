DEFINITION: The genetic code is the set of rules by which information encoded in mRNA sequences (codons, three-nucleotide units) is translated into amino acid sequences during protein synthesis; the wobble hypothesis explains how a single tRNA anticodon can recognize multiple codons due to flexible base pairing at the third (5') position of the anticodon.
WALKTHROUGH: Imagine you receive a WhatsApp message written in a language where every word is exactly three letters long, there are no spaces, and the same word can mean slightly different things depending on context — that is essentially what the ribosome reads on mRNA. Begin by recalling that transcription (c12_bio_cbse_06_005) produces mRNA with a sequence of A, U, G, C. Next, observe that 4³ = 64 possible three-letter combinations (codons) must specify only 20 amino acids plus start/stop signals — this forces degeneracy. Then, use the standard codon table to translate a short mRNA fragment (e.g., AUG UUU UCU UAA) into Met-Phe-Ser-Stop. Finally, introduce Crick's wobble hypothesis: the third base of the codon (3' end) pairs loosely with the first base of the anticodon (5' end), allowing one tRNA to read multiple codons — this reduces the number of tRNAs needed and buffers against mutations.
