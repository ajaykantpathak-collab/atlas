DEFINITION: Uniform motion is motion in which an object covers equal displacements in equal intervals of time, implying constant velocity (both magnitude and direction remain unchanged). Non-uniform motion is motion in which an object covers unequal displacements in equal intervals of time, or changes direction, resulting in a change in velocity (magnitude, direction, or both).
WALKTHROUGH: Observe a ceiling fan rotating at constant speed – the blades cover equal angles in equal time but their direction changes continuously. Now compare with a bicycle moving on a straight road at a steady 10 km/h. Ask: "In which case is the velocity constant?" Measure the bicycle's position every 2 seconds using a tape measure and stopwatch; record displacements. If displacements are equal, motion is uniform. Connect to prior concept: velocity (c9_physics_ch2_3) is displacement/time. If velocity is constant, motion is uniform; if velocity changes, motion is non-uniform.

  Q: A velocity-time graph shows a straight line from (0, 5) to (10, 25). Find the acceleration, initial velocity, and distance covered in 10 seconds.
  A: Initial velocity = 5 m/s (y-intercept). Final velocity = 25 m/s at t = 10. Acceleration = slope = (25-5)/(10-0) = 20/10 = 2 m/s^2. Distance = area under v-t graph = trapezium area = 1/2 x (5 + 25) x 10 = 1/2 x 30 x 10 = 150 m.

DEFINITION: A distance-time graph is a graphical representation plotting distance travelled (on the y-axis) against time elapsed (on the x-axis) for an object moving along a straight line. The slope of the graph at any point gives the speed of the object at that instant, with SI units of metres per second (m/s).
WALKTHROUGH: Imagine you are standing at a railway crossing in Delhi and watch a train pass by. You note its distance from you every 10 seconds: at 0 s it is 0 m, at 10 s it is 200 m, at 20 s it is 400 m, at 30 s it is 600 m. Plot these points on graph paper with time on the horizontal axis and distance on the vertical axis. You will see the points lie on a straight line passing through the origin. This straight line tells you the train moves with constant speed — a concept you studied in Class 7 as uniform motion (c7_physics_ch04_008). Now calculate the slope: rise over run = (600-0) m / (30-0) s = 20 m/s. This slope is the speed. If the train had slowed down, the line would curve, becoming less steep. The steeper the line, the greater the speed. This visual connection between slope and speed is the key principle of distance-time graphs.
D1 Q: What physical quantity does the slope of a distance-time graph represent, and what are its SI units?
D1 A: The slope represents speed, with SI units of metres per second (m/s). A wrong answer such as "velocity" reveals the student confuses scalar distance with vector displacement; a wrong answer such as "acceleration" reveals confusion with velocity-time graphs where slope gives acceleration.
D2 Q: A distance-time graph for a car shows a straight line passing through the origin with a slope of 15 m/s. After 20 seconds, the line becomes horizontal. Explain what the horizontal section means physically and why the graph cannot tell us whether the car is facing north or south.
D2 A: The horizontal section means the car is at rest (speed = 0 m/s) because distance does not change with time. The graph cannot indicate direction because distance is a scalar quantity — it only measures path length, not displacement. A wrong answer stating "the car moves backwards" reveals the student thinks a flat line means negative speed, confusing distance-time with displacement-time graphs.
D3 Q: Two cyclists, A and B, start from the same point at the same time. Cyclist A's distance-time graph is a straight line with slope 5 m/s. Cyclist B's graph is a curve that starts steeper than A's but becomes less steep after 10 s. At t = 15 s, both have travelled 75 m. Calculate the average speed of each cyclist over 15 
D3 A: Average speed = total distance / total time = 75 m / 15 s = 5 m/s for both. Cyclist B had higher initial speed because the curve starts steeper than A's straight line (slope > 5 m/s initially). A wrong answer giving different average speeds reveals the student does not understand average speed depends only on total distance and total time, not the path taken.
D4 Q: A student draws a distance-time graph for a ball thrown vertically upward and claims the graph goes up, then down, forming an inverted V shape. Identify the error in this graph and explain what the correct graph should look like.
D4 A: The error: distance travelled never decreases — it always increases or stays constant. The graph cannot go down. The correct graph curves upward with decreasing slope (ball slows down), becomes horizontal at the top (momentarily at rest), then curves upward with increasing slope (ball speeds up downward). A wrong answer accepting the inverted V reveals the student confuses distance with displacement or height.
D5 Q: Evaluate this statement: "If two objects have the same distance-time graph, they must have the same velocity." Is it true or false? Justify your answer.
D5 A: False. Distance-time graphs show speed (magnitude only), not velocity (magnitude and direction). Two objects moving in opposite directions at the same speed will have identical distance-time graphs but opposite velocities. A student answering "true" reveals confusion between speed and velocity, and between distance and displacement.
