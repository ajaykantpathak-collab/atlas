DEFINITION: A rechargeable cell (secondary cell) uses a reversible chemical reaction that allows electrical energy to be restored by passing a current through it in the opposite direction, whereas a non-rechargeable cell (primary cell) uses an irreversible chemical reaction that cannot be practically reversed, making it a single-use energy source.
WALKTHROUGH: Observe a TV remote (uses AA alkaline cells that you throw away) and a mobile phone (uses a Li-ion battery you recharge daily). Both convert chemical energy to electrical energy, but the phone's battery can be "refilled" with electricity while the remote's cannot. Recall from Class 6 (c6_physics_ch03_016) that a cell has two electrodes and an electrolyte; the key difference is whether the chemical changes at the electrodes can be undone by forcing current backwards. We will examine the reaction reversibility, then compare cost, waste, and performance to decide which cell suits which job.

  Q: Three identical bulbs are connected in series across a battery. If one bulb fuses, what happens to the other two? What if they were in parallel?
  A: Series: If one bulb fuses (breaks the circuit), the other two go OUT. In series, the same current flows through all components -- a break anywhere stops all current. Parallel: If one bulb fuses, the other two STAY ON. In parallel, each bulb has its own independent path to the battery -- a break in one path doesn't affect the others.
VETTED-DIAG cfq_7_1 (fit 0.23, src c8_physics_diagnostics.marker)
  Q: A bar magnet is cut into two equal pieces perpendicular to its length. How many poles does each piece have? Can a magnetic monopole be created by cutting a magnet?
  A: Each piece has TWO poles (N and S). Cutting a magnet creates two smaller magnets, each with a north and south pole. A magnetic monopole CANNOT be created by cutting -- no matter how many times you cut, each piece always has both poles. This is a fundamental difference from electric charges (which can be isolated as positive or negative).

  Q: A 6V battery is connected to a 3Omega resistor. Find the current. If an ammeter (very low resistance) is connected in series and a voltmeter (very high resistance) in parallel across the resistor, do they affect the circuit?
  A: I = V/R = 6/3 = 2 A. The ammeter (low resistance in series) has negligible effect on current. The voltmeter (high resistance in parallel) draws negligible current. Ideally, ammeters have zero resistance and voltmeters have infinite resistance, so they don't affect the circuit.
