DEFINITION: A chemical equation is a symbolic representation of a chemical reaction where reactants (starting substances) are written on the left side and products (new substances formed) on the right side, separated by an arrow (→), with physical states indicated in parentheses. Balancing by inspection is the method of adjusting stoichiometric coefficients (numbers placed before formulas) to ensure the number of atoms of each element is identical on both sides, obeying the Law of Conservation of Mass.
WALKTHROUGH: Observe a magnesium ribbon burning in air — it produces a blinding white light and leaves a white ash (magnesium oxide). This is a chemical change you saw in Class 6 (c6_chemistry_ch05_004) and Class 9 (c9_chem_ch3_1). To represent it chemically, we first write the word equation: Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium oxide. Then we convert to formulas using valency rules from Class 9 (c9_chem_ch3_6): Mg + O₂ → MgO. Notice oxygen atoms are unbalanced (2 on left, 1 on right). We balance by placing coefficient 2 before MgO, then 2 before Mg: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. This step-by-step inspection method is the core skill for all reaction types in this chapter.
D1 Q: Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction: Hydrogen gas burns in oxygen gas to form liquid water. Include physical states.
D1 A: Correct answer: 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l). A wrong answer like H₂ + O₂ → H₂O reveals the student recalls the reaction but fails to apply the Law of Conservation of Mass — they have not balanced oxygen atoms (2 on left, 1 on right). Another error: writing H₂O(g) instead of (l) shows confusion about water's state at room temperature, a common board exam marking penalty.

### NODE c10_chem_cbse_01_003  class=10 sub=chem ch=1 verdict=PARTIAL action=REWRITE [5]
DEFINITION: A combination reaction is a chemical reaction in which two or more reactants (elements or compounds) combine to form a single product. The general form is A + B → AB, where A and B can be elements or compounds, and AB is always a compound.
WALKTHROUGH: Begin with a real-world observation: a student lighting a magnesium ribbon in the school laboratory sees an intense white flame and a white powdery ash (MgO). Ask: "What happened to the magnesium and the oxygen from the air?" Connect to prior node c10_chem_cbse_01_001 (identifying chemical change) — new substance formed, energy released. Then use c10_chem_cbse_01_002 (balancing equations) to write the skeletal equation Mg + O₂ → MgO and balance it step-by-step. Highlight that two reactants (Mg, O₂) form ONE product (MgO) — this is the defining pattern. Extend to other examples: burning coal (C + O₂ → CO₂), quicklime reacting with water (CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂). Emphasize that combination reactions are often exothermic (release heat), linking to the flame observed.
D1 Q: Define a combination reaction and give the general symbolic representation. Provide one example with a balanced chemical equation including physical states.
D1 A: A combination reaction is a reaction where two or more substances combine to form a single product. General form: A + B → AB. Example: 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s). 
Wrong answer insight: If a student writes "A + B → C + D" or gives an example like 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂, they confuse combination with decomposition. If they omit physical states, they lose board exam marks (1 mark for states in 3-marker questions).
D2 Q: Why is the reaction CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) classified as a combination reaction, even though one reactant is a compound and the product is a solution? Explain the criterion that defines the reaction type.
D2 A: It is a combination reaction because two distinct reactants (calcium oxide and water) combine to form only ONE product (calcium hydroxide). The physical state of the product (aqueous) does not change the fact that a single compound is formed from two reactants. The defining criterion is the number of products (exactly one), not the states or complexity of reactants.

VETTED-MIS misconception_1 (fit 0.65)
  M: A combination reaction always produces a compound from two elements only.
  C: A combination reaction is any reaction in which two or more reactants (which may be elements or compounds) join to form a single product. The defining feature is "many reactants -> one product", not the type of reactants.
VETTED-MIS misconception_12 (fit 0.40)
  M: When two substances combine, the product always has properties that are an average of the reactants' properties.
  C: A chemical combination produces a new substance with its own characteristic properties. These properties are not predictable by averaging the properties of the reactants; they must be measured for the new compound.
VETTED-MIS misconception_9 (fit 0.39)
  M: In a combination reaction, mass is created because one product is formed from two reactants.
  C: In any chemical reaction, including combination reactions, mass is conserved. The atoms of the reactants rearrange to form the product, so the mass of the product equals the combined mass of the reactants.
