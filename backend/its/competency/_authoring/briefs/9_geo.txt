DEFINITION: Coastal landforms are natural features shaped by the dynamic interaction of sea waves, currents, tides, and wind along the shoreline, classified as erosional (cliffs, wave-cut platforms, sea caves, arches, stacks) or depositional (beaches, spits, bars, tombolos, barrier islands, lagoons). Human-landform relationships refer to how coastal communities adapt to, modify, and depend on these features for settlements, ports, fisheries, tourism, and protection against hazards like erosion and storm surges.

The key principle: coastal landforms exist in a dynamic equilibrium where wave energy, sediment supply, and sea level determine whether a coast builds up or wears away.
