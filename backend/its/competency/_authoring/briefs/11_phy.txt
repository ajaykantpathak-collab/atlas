DEFINITION: The equation of trajectory of a projectile is the mathematical relationship between the vertical displacement (y) and horizontal displacement (x) of an object projected with initial velocity \(v_0\) at an angle \(\theta_0\) to the horizontal, under constant gravitational acceleration \(g\) and negligible air resistance. It is given by \(y = x \tan\theta_0 - \frac{g x^2}{2 v_0^2 \cos^2\theta_0}\), which describes a parabolic path in the vertical plane.
WALKTHROUGH: Watch a cricket ball hit for a six — it follows a curved path. That curve is not circular; it is a parabola. We already know the horizontal motion is uniform (\(x = v_0 \cos\theta_0 \, t\)) and vertical motion is uniformly accelerated (\(y = v_0 \sin\theta_0 \, t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2\)). To find the *shape* of the path, we need a direct relation between \(y\) and \(x\) without time. Solve the first equation for \(t = \frac{x}{v_0 \cos\theta_0}\) and substitute into the second. The result is a quadratic in \(x\), confirming the parabolic trajectory. This equation is the "fingerprint" of the projectile — it tells us the height at any horizontal distance.

DEFINITION: Aristotle's fallacy is the incorrect assertion that a continuous external force is required to maintain a body in uniform motion; Galileo's experiments with inclined planes demonstrated that in the absence of friction, a body in uniform motion continues indefinitely without any external force, establishing the law of inertia: a body remains in its state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line unless compelled by a net external force to change that state.
WALKTHROUGH: Observe a marble rolling on a smooth floor versus a rough carpet—on the smooth floor it travels farther before stopping. Now imagine a perfectly frictionless horizontal surface (like an air hockey table with the air on): the marble would never stop. This thought experiment mirrors Galileo's insight: he asked what happens to a ball rolling down one smooth incline and up another; as the second incline flattens to horizontal, the ball rolls farther to reach the same height, implying infinite distance on a frictionless horizontal plane. Start by identifying the hidden force (friction) in everyday experience, then trace Galileo's logical extrapolation to the idealised frictionless case, and conclude with the law of inertia as the correct principle.

DEFINITION: The law of conservation of momentum states that for an isolated system (no net external force), the total vector momentum remains constant in time: \(\sum \vec{p}_{\text{initial}} = \sum \vec{p}_{\text{final}}\). Momentum \(\vec{p} = m\vec{v}\) is a vector quantity measured in kg·m/s. The law follows directly from Newton's second and third laws and holds for all collisions and explosions in isolated systems, regardless of whether kinetic energy is conserved.
WALKTHROUGH: Watch a cricket bowler deliver a ball: the ball gains forward momentum while the bowler experiences a tiny backward recoil. Begin by identifying the system boundaries: are external forces (friction, gravity component along motion) negligible during the brief interaction? If yes, apply \(\sum \vec{p}_i = \sum \vec{p}_f\) as a vector equation. Break into x and y components. For 1D problems, choose a sign convention. Always verify isolation assumption before writing the conservation equation.

DEFINITION: Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by a body due to its motion, defined as the work needed to accelerate a body of mass \( m \) from rest to velocity \( \vec{v} \). For a particle of mass \( m \) moving with speed \( v \), its kinetic energy \( K \) is given by \( K = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 \). It is a scalar quantity, always non-negative, with SI unit joule (J) and dimensions \([ML^2T^{-2}]\).
WALKTHROUGH: Observe a cricket ball and a tennis ball thrown at the same speed — the cricket ball hurts more because it has more mass. Now imagine the same cricket ball thrown at different speeds — the faster one does more damage. This shows kinetic energy depends on both mass and speed. In Class 9 you learned \( v^2 - u^2 = 2as \) and multiplied by \( m/2 \) to get \( \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2 = mas = Fs = W \). The quantity \( \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \) is kinetic energy, and the change in it equals the net work done. Start by measuring mass (kg) and speed (m/s), then apply \( K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \).

DEFINITION: Forced oscillations occur when an external periodic force drives a damped oscillator at a frequency different from its natural frequency; resonance is the phenomenon where the driving frequency matches the natural frequency of the system, causing the amplitude to reach a maximum limited only by damping.
