DEFINITION: The Age of Colonialism from the 15th century refers to the era of global history when European maritime powers initiated extensive overseas exploration and established colonies. Driven by mercantilism and the search for direct sea routes to Asia after traditional overland routes were blocked, this era marked the transition from isolated regional kingdoms to a highly competitive, European-dominated global trade network.
WALKTHROUGH: Imagine opening a spice box in an Indian kitchen and finding black pepper, cardamom, and cloves. Did you know that in 15th-century Europe, these exact spices were so rare and valuable that they were literally worth their weight in gold? To understand why European ships suddenly began arriving on India's western coast, we must first look at Europe in 1453, when the Ottoman Empire captured Constantinople and closed the traditional overland Silk Route. This created a massive crisis for European merchants who desperately wanted Indian spices but could no longer travel safely by land. By asking ourselves, "How else could they reach India?", we connect this geographical block to the sudden explosion of shipbuilding and deep-sea navigation in Portugal and Spain. Thus, the quest for a kitchen spice became the driving force behind the global Age of Discovery and the colonization of India.
