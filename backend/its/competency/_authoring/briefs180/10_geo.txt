DEFINITION: Food security in India is ensured through a dual mechanism: the Buffer Stock — a reserve of foodgrains (wheat and rice) procured by the Food Corporation of India (FCI) at Minimum Support Price (MSP) to stabilize prices and meet emergencies — and the Public Distribution System (PDS) — a network of fair price shops that distribute these foodgrains to economically vulnerable populations at subsidized issue prices under the National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013.
WALKTHROUGH: Imagine you see a Fair Price Shop (ration shop) in your neighbourhood with a board displaying "Wheat: ₹2/kg, Rice: ₹3/kg" — this is the visible endpoint of a massive logistical chain. Begin by asking: *Where does this grain come from?* Trace it back to the farmer in Punjab or Haryana selling wheat to FCI at MSP (₹2,275/quintal for 2023-24). Then follow the grain to FCI godowns where it becomes part of the buffer stock — held against norms like 411 lakh tonnes (April 1) for wheat. Finally, see how the Centre allocates this stock to states, which then distribute it through 5.3 lakh fair price shops to ration cardholders. The key principle: procurement at remunerative prices → strategic storage → targeted distribution converts production surplus into consumption security.

DEFINITION: Contribution of agriculture to the national economy refers to the sector's share in India's Gross Domestic Product (GDP), its role in generating employment for nearly half the workforce, its supply of raw materials to agro-based industries, and its contribution to export earnings and food security — collectively making it the primary sector backbone of the Indian economy despite a declining GDP share.
WALKTHROUGH: Observe a news headline: "Agriculture GDP share falls to 18% but still employs 42% of workers" — this apparent contradiction is the entry point. They connect to prior nodes on cropping patterns (c10_geo_cbse_04_003) and major crops (c10_geo_cbse_04_004-006) to see which crops drive value vs. which absorb labor. They then examine how Green Revolution (c10_geo_cbse_04_008) boosted GDP share temporarily but not employment elasticity. The key principle: Agriculture's economic significance lies not in its GDP percentage alone, but in its irreplaceable role as the primary employer and wage-goods supplier for the bottom 60% of the population.
