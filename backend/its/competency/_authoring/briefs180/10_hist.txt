DEFINITION: Allegories of the nation are symbolic female figures — Marianne for France and Germania for Germany — that personified the abstract idea of the nation-state in 19th-century Europe, using specific visual attributes (Phrygian cap, tricolour, oak leaves, sword, crown) to represent liberty, unity, strength, and popular sovereignty, making the intangible concept of nationalism tangible and emotionally resonant for ordinary citizens.
WALKTHROUGH: Think of the image of Bharat Mata — a mother figure draped in a saffron sari, holding the national flag, standing on a map of India — that you see in school assemblies or Republic Day parades. Start by recalling the French Revolution (node 001) which replaced the king's body with the nation as the source of sovereignty. The key insight: these were not just art; they were political tools that turned "the people" into "the nation" by giving them a shared visual language.
