DEFINITION: A delta is a depositional landform formed at a river's mouth where sediment-laden water enters a standing water body (sea/lake), loses velocity, and drops its load, creating a branching distributary network; an estuary is a funnel-shaped, tidal river mouth where freshwater mixes with seawater in a drowned valley, characterized by strong tidal currents that prevent deltaic buildup and maintain a single channel.
WALKTHROUGH: Stand at the Sundarbans ferry ghat in West Bengal: the water is brown with silt, the channel splits into three, mangroves fringe mudbanks — you are on a delta. Both rivers carry Himalayan/Deccan sediment, yet one builds land, the other stays open. The difference lies in what the river brings (sediment volume), what the sea does (tidal range, wave energy), and the coastal geometry (shelf slope). We already know from c11_geo_cbse_07_003 that lower-course rivers deposit on floodplains; here deposition meets marine forces. The key principle: delta forms when river deposition > marine removal; estuary forms when marine removal ≥ river deposition.
