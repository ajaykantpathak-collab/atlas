DEFINITION: A Smart City is an urban area that uses digital technology, information and communication networks (ICT), and data sensor systems to optimize resource allocation, reduce operational costs, and improve the quality of life and environmental sustainability for its residents. Economically, it represents a shift from capital-intensive physical infrastructure expansion to data-driven efficiency improvements of existing assets.
WALKTHROUGH: Imagine walking down an urban street in India in the middle of a hot afternoon. You look up and notice all the heavy sodium streetlights are glowing brightly, wasting electricity because the manual timer is broken. This represents a waste of municipal taxpayers' money that could have been spent on schools or clinics. 

To solve this, a Smart City does not just build *more* power plants; instead, it installs smart light-emitting diode (LED) streetlights with light sensors that automatically dim or turn off depending on natural daylight. 

In this topic, we will analyze how cities use technology as an economic tool. In Class 7, you learned about basic utility infrastructure like water pipelines and electricity networks. Now, we will look at how adding a layer of digital technology (like sensors and data networks) allows municipal governments to monitor resource use in real-time. This reduces resource wastage, lowers municipal operating budgets, and makes cities more sustainable without requiring massive, destructive construction projects.

D2 Q: Why does a smart water grid with acoustic leak-detection sensors improve municipal finances more than simply increasing the water tariff (price) to cover losses?
D2 A: Simply increasing the water tariff forces consumers to pay for wasted water (Non-Revenue Water lost through leakages), which reduces consumer welfare and can lead to non-payment. In contrast, smart acoustic sensors locate invisible underground leaks immediately. This reduces physical water loss, allowing the municipality to sell more of the water it already treats without raising rates, thereby improving municipal revenue while preserving consumer trust.
