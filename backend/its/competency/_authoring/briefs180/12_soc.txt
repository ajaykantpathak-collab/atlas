DEFINITION: The distinctive features of Indian society refer to its plurality (coexistence of multiple cultural, religious, linguistic, and ethnic groups), diversity (the vast range of variations in customs, beliefs, practices, and social structures across regions and communities), and stratification (the hierarchical arrangement of groups into ranked layers — primarily through the caste system, but also via class, gender, tribe, and religion — that systematically allocate power, prestige, and resources). These three features are interlocked: plurality and diversity provide the raw material of difference, while stratification converts difference into structured inequality.
WALKTHROUGH: Imagine you are standing in a bustling railway station in Mumbai: you hear Marathi, Hindi, Gujarati, Tamil, and Bengali; you see people dressed in kurta-pyjama, jeans, sarees, hijabs, and business suits; you notice some passengers being served tea in glass tumblers while others order coffee in paper cups. Start by asking: "What makes this scene uniquely Indian?" Guide students to list the visible differences (languages, dress, food, rituals) → that's plurality. Then ask: "Are these differences just random variety, or do they follow patterns?" → they cluster into regional, religious, occupational groups → that's diversity. Finally ask: "Who gets the best seats, who cleans the platforms, who owns the stalls?" → roles are not randomly assigned but follow caste, class, gender lines → that's stratification. The key insight: Indian society is not just a mosaic of differences (plurality/diversity) but a structured hierarchy (stratification) that gives meaning and consequence to those differences.
