DEFINITION: Cyber safety refers to the practices, measures, and awareness required to protect individuals, systems, and networks from cyber threats such as unauthorised access, data theft, malware, phishing, and identity theft, while ensuring ethical, legal, and responsible use of digital technologies.
WALKTHROUGH: Imagine you receive an email claiming to be from your bank asking you to "verify your account" by clicking a link and entering your PIN. The logo looks perfect, the language is professional, but the sender address is "security@bank-verification-services.xyz" instead of your bank's actual domain. This is a classic phishing attempt — but how do you prove it? Start by examining the URL structure: legitimate banks use their primary domain (e.g., hdfcbank.com), not subdomains on unrelated domains. Next, check for HTTPS and valid security certificates. Then, recall that no legitimate bank ever asks for PIN via email. This scenario connects your prior knowledge of URLs (c11_cs_cbse_11_007) and HTTPS (c11_cs_cbse_11_009) to cyber safety. The key insight: cyber threats exploit trust and urgency; defence requires verifying technical indicators (domain, protocol, certificate) before taking action.

Passive digital footprint: data collected without explicit user action (e.g., website recording IP address and browsing history via cookies).
