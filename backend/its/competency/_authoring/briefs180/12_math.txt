DEFINITION: The Mean Value Theorem (MVT) states: If a function \( f \) is continuous on the closed interval \([a, b]\) and differentiable on the open interval \((a, b)\), then there exists at least one point \( c \in (a, b) \) such that \( f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a} \). DEFINITION: The shortest distance between two skew lines in three-dimensional space is the length of the unique line segment that is perpendicular to both lines. Skew lines are non-parallel, non-intersecting lines that lie in different planes. If the lines are given in vector form as \(\vec{r} = \vec{a_1} + \lambda \vec{b_1}\) and \(\vec{r} = \vec{a_2} + \mu \vec{b_2}\), the shortest distance \(d\) is \(d = \frac{|(\vec{b_1} \times \vec{b_2}) \cdot (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})|}{|\vec{b_1} \times \vec{b_2}|}\), provided \(\vec{b_1} \times \vec{b_2} \neq \vec{0}\).
WALKTHROUGH: Imagine you are an architect designing a modern building in Mumbai. These pipes are skew — they don't intersect and aren't parallel. To avoid collision, you need the minimum clearance between them. Start by writing the vector equations of both lines from their endpoints. Identify \(\vec{a_1}, \vec{b_1}, \vec{a_2}, \vec{b_2}\). Compute \(\vec{b_1} \times \vec{b_2}\) (the direction perpendicular to both pipes). Then find \(\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1}\) (vector connecting a point on each pipe). The shortest distance is the absolute projection of this connecting vector onto the perpendicular direction: \(d = \frac{|(\vec{b_1} \times \vec{b_2}) \cdot (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})|}{|\vec{b_1} \times \vec{b_2}|}\).
