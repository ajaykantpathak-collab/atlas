DEFINITION: Women's political representation refers to the presence and participation of women in elected legislative bodies (Parliament, State Assemblies, Local Governments). In India, despite universal adult franchise (Article 326), women remain severely underrepresented due to structural barriers like patriarchal norms, sexual division of labour, and lack of access to political networks. Reservation is a constitutional affirmative action policy that mandates a minimum percentage of seats (currently one-third in Panchayats and Municipalities under Articles 243D and 243T via the 73rd and 74th Amendments, and one-third in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies under the 106th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023) to be reserved exclusively for women candidates, ensuring their descriptive and substantive representation.

This creates a structural barrier where formal equality (Article 326) does not translate into actual representation. Next, examine the data: women constitute ~14% of Lok Sabha (2019) vs. 49% of the population. Then, study the solution already working at the grassroots: the 73rd/74th Amendments reserved 1/3 seats in Panchayats/Municipalities, leading to 1.4 million elected women representatives. Analyze how this reservation works (rotation of seats, intersection with SC/ST quotas). Finally, evaluate the 106th Amendment: it extends this logic to higher legislatures but faces implementation delays (delimitation/census). The key civic principle: Democracy requires not just the right to vote, but the right to be represented; reservation is a temporary corrective measure to transform formal equality into substantive equality.
