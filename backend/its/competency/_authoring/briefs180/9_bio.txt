DEFINITION: Classification is the scientific process of grouping living organisms into hierarchical categories based on shared characteristics and evolutionary relationships, enabling systematic study, identification, and communication about the vast diversity of life.
WALKTHROUGH: Imagine walking into a massive library in Delhi where millions of books are piled randomly on the floor with no shelves, no labels, and no catalog — finding a specific book on Indian medicinal plants would take months. This chaos mirrors the challenge biologists face with over 8.7 million estimated species on Earth. In the previous node (c9_bio_ch4_1), you learned that biodiversity refers to this immense variety of life forms. Now, consider how a botanist in the Western Ghats identifies a new frog species, or how a doctor in Mumbai communicates about a pathogen to a researcher in London. Without a universal, logical system to organize this diversity, scientific progress would stall. Classification provides that system: it imposes order on chaos, allows precise identification, enables global communication through standardized names, reveals evolutionary history, and guides conservation efforts. The key biological principle is that classification transforms an overwhelming list of organisms into a structured framework that reflects their natural relationships.

D5 Q: Evaluate this statement: "Modern DNA sequencing has made traditional classification based on physical characteristics obsolete." Provide two biological arguments for and against this view.
D5 A: Correct evaluation: The statement is *false* — both approaches are complementary, not mutually exclusive.
