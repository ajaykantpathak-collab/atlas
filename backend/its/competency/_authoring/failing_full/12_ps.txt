D4 Q: Compare the Syndicate's role in the 1967 presidential election (Dr. Zakir Husain) with the 1969 presidential election (V.V. Giri vs. Neelam Sanjiva Reddy). How did the shift from consensus to open conflict reflect the breakdown of the Congress system's faction-management mechanism?
D4 A: In 1967, the Syndicate *consulted* Indira Gandhi and reached consensus on Dr. Zakir Husain — the faction-management mechanism (informal negotiation among factions) still worked. In 1969, after the 1967 electoral setback, Indira Gandhi nationalized banks, abolished privy purses, and projected a left-leaning agenda *against Syndicate wishes*. The Syndicate nominated Neelam Sanjiva Reddy (their loyalist) as official Congress candidate; Indira Gandhi supported V.V. Giri as an independent, asking Congress MPs to vote "conscience." Giri won. This shifted faction-management from *accommodation within party forums* to *open electoral combat using state machinery* — the "Congress System" (c12_ps_cbse_09_002) collapsed because the Syndicate tried to impose organizational discipline on a PM who commanded a popular mandate, and the PM responded by bypassing party institutions entirely. A wrong answer treating 1969 as merely a "personality clash" misses the structural rupture: the organizational wing lost its veto over the parliamentary leader.
