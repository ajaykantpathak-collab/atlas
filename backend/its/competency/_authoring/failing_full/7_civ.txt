DEFINITION: Fundamental Rights are the basic human rights enshrined in Part III of the Indian Constitution (Articles 12 to 35) that guarantee civil liberties to all citizens, protecting them against state infringement and ensuring equal dignity and development. They are enforceable by courts, meaning a citizen can directly approach the Supreme Court or High Courts if these rights are violated.
WALKTHROUGH: Imagine walking into school and noticing that the teacher allows only certain students to speak during a debate based on their family background. Immediately, you feel that something is deeply unfair and goes against basic fairness. In Class 6, you learned general concepts of human rights and simple equality; now in Class 7, we examine how the Indian Constitution legally guarantees these protections as "Fundamental Rights" divided into six specialized categories. To begin exploring this, ask yourself: what makes a right "fundamental" instead of just a regular legal rule? We connect this to our prior study of the key features of the Constitution (c7_civ_ch02_007), which established that the Constitution limits government power to protect individuals. The key civic principle here is that Fundamental Rights act as a shield for every individual against the arbitrary use of state authority.
D3 Q: A local government authority denies a group of citizens permission to hold a peaceful, unarmed meeting to discuss civic issues, claiming it might cause inconvenience. Which category of Fundamental Rights is primarily being challenged here?
D3 A: This challenges the Right to Freedom (specifically freedom of assembly under Article 19). While reasonable restrictions can be placed for public order, a total or arbitrary denial of peaceful assembly infringes upon this guaranteed right. 
A wrong answer reveals the misconception that the government has absolute control over public spaces and citizen gatherings without needing constitutional justification.
