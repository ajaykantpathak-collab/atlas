DEFINITION: Pregnancy is the physiological state in a female mammal beginning with the implantation of the blastocyst in the uterine endometrium and ending with parturition, during which the embryo and later the fetus develops inside the uterus. Embryonic development encompasses the sequential, highly regulated processes of cleavage, gastrulation, neurulation, and organogenesis that transform the zygote into a multicellular embryo with distinct germ layers and rudimentary organ systems by the end of the eighth week.
WALKTHROUGH: Begin by asking the student to recall the journey of the zygote from the fallopian tube to the uterus — a journey they studied in the fertilization and implantation nodes. Then, have them observe a timeline diagram of human prenatal development (available in the NCERT textbook Fig. 3.11). Point out that the first two weeks are "pre-embryonic" (cleavage to blastocyst), weeks 3–8 are the embryonic period proper (gastrulation to organogenesis), and week 9 to birth is the fetal period. Connect this to their Class 9 knowledge (c9_bio_ch3_10) where they learned the trimesters. The key biological principle is that development is a continuous cascade of cell division, migration, differentiation, and induction, all orchestrated by genetic programs and maternal–fetal hormonal dialogue.
D3 Q: A pregnant woman at 6 weeks gestation experiences severe nausea and vomiting (hyperemesis gravidarum). Her serum hCG level is markedly elevated. Explain the physiological basis for the correlation between high hCG and nausea, and predict which hormone's rising levels will likely bring relief by the end of the first trimester.
D3 A: hCG peaks around weeks 8–10 and shares structural similarity with TSH, potentially overstimulating the thyroid and the chemoreceptor trigger zone in the area postrema, contributing to nausea. As hCG declines, progesterone and estrogen from the placenta rise steadily; progesterone's smooth-muscle relaxant effect on the gastrointestinal tract and the establishment of placentation correlate with symptom resolution. A wrong answer that attributes nausea solely to "progesterone" misses the specific temporal correlation with hCG dynamics.
D4 Q: Compare and contrast the roles of the yolk sac and the allantois in early human embryonic development. Why is the human yolk sac non-nutritive despite being well-developed?
D4 A: The yolk sac is the first site of hematopoiesis (blood island formation), produces primordial germ cells, and contributes to the gut tube; it is non-nutritive because placental nutrition is established early via the chorionic villi. The allantois emerges as an endodermal diverticulum from the yolk sac, expands into the connecting stalk, forms the umbilical vessels, and its proximal part becomes the urachus (later median umbilical ligament). A wrong answer that assigns nutritive function to the yolk sac confuses human development with avian/reptilian models.
