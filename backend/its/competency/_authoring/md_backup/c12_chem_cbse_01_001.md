## DEFINITION
A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more chemically non-reacting substances whose composition can be varied within certain limits. Based on the physical states of solute and solvent, binary solutions are classified into nine types: gaseous solutions (gas in gas, liquid in gas, solid in gas), liquid solutions (gas in liquid, liquid in liquid, solid in liquid), and solid solutions (gas in solid, liquid in solid, solid in solid). The component present in larger amount is the solvent and determines the physical state of the solution; the other component(s) is the solute.

## WALKTHROUGH
Look around your kitchen: air (gas in gas), soda water (gas in liquid), brass utensil (solid in solid), dental amalgam (liquid in solid). All are solutions but in different physical states. Recall from Class 9 (c9_chem_ch1_1) that mixtures can be homogeneous (solutions) or heterogeneous. Now we categorise homogeneous mixtures by the states of solute and solvent. Start by identifying the solvent — the component in excess that sets the solution's state. Then place the solute-solvent pair into the 3×3 grid (gas/liquid/solid × gas/liquid/solid). Notice that solid solutions like alloys are technologically crucial (steel, brass), while gas-in-liquid solutions like oxygen in water sustain aquatic life. This classification underpins all subsequent quantitative work.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Name the type of solution formed when oxygen gas dissolves in water. Identify the solute and solvent.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Correct answer: Liquid solution — gas in liquid. Solute: oxygen (gas); Solvent: water (liquid). A wrong answer (e.g., "gas in gas" or swapping solute/solvent) reveals the student cannot map a real example to the 3×3 classification grid and confuses which component determines the physical state.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is a mixture of hydrogen gas in palladium metal classified as a solid solution and not a liquid solution? Explain using the definition of solvent.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Correct answer: Palladium is the solvent (present in larger amount) and is a solid, so the solution's physical state is solid. Hydrogen is the solute (gas). A wrong answer (e.g., "because hydrogen is a gas") shows the student thinks the solute's state decides the solution type, violating the definition that solvent determines the physical state.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A dentist uses an amalgam of mercury (liquid) and silver (solid) for fillings. Classify this solution, identify solute and solvent, and state the physical state of the resulting solution.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Correct answer: Solid solution — liquid in solid. Solute: mercury (liquid); Solvent: silver (solid); Physical state: solid. Steps: (1) Recognise amalgam is a solid solution. (2) Mercury is the minor component (solute), silver is the major component (solvent). (3) Solvent is solid → solution is solid. A wrong classification (e.g., "liquid solution") indicates the student cannot apply the solvent-dominance rule to a familiar real-world example.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the three gaseous solutions in Table 1.1 (gas in gas, liquid in gas, solid in gas). What common feature allows all three to be homogeneous mixtures despite different solute states?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Correct answer: In all three, the solvent is a gas. Gas molecules have large inter-particle spaces and weak intermolecular forces (recall Class 11 kinetic theory, c11_chem_cbse_05_009), allowing solute particles (gas, liquid, or solid) to disperse uniformly at molecular level. A wrong answer focusing only on "all are gases" misses the key role of the gaseous solvent's properties in achieving homogeneity.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims: "Brass (copper + zinc) is a liquid solution because both metals melt at high temperatures." Evaluate this statement and correct the error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Correct evaluation: False. Brass is a solid solution (solid in solid) at room temperature. The physical state of a solution is determined by the state of the solvent at the given temperature, not by melting points. Copper is the solvent (major component), zinc is the solute; both are solids at room temperature → solid solution. The error reveals confusion between the solution's state at a specific temperature and the components' melting behaviour.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple home experiment to demonstrate that air is a gaseous solution (gas in gas). List materials, procedure, and how you would prove homogeneity.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Expected answer: Materials: incense stick, matchbox, room with still air. Procedure: Light incense stick in one corner; observe fragrance spreading uniformly throughout the room within minutes. Homogeneity proof: Fragrance (solute gas) mixes uniformly with air (solvent gas) without separation, showing single-phase homogeneous mixture. A poor answer (e.g., "mix two gases in a jar") lacks a practical demonstration of homogeneity and does not use observable diffusion.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think the solute's physical state decides the solution type. For example, they classify "carbon dioxide in water" as a gas solution because CO₂ is a gas, instead of liquid solution (gas in liquid). They also confuse "solid in liquid" (sugar in water) with "liquid in solid" (amalgam) by focusing on the solute's state. The root error: not identifying which component is the solvent (the one in excess that sets the phase).

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A cold drink bottle contains carbon dioxide gas dissolved in water under pressure. When opened, bubbles escape. Classify this solution before and after opening, identify solute and solvent in each case, and explain the change.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Before opening, the bottle contains CO₂ dissolved in water — the solvent is water (liquid, major component), solute is CO₂ (gas). Classification: liquid solution, gas in liquid.
- Step 2: After opening, pressure drops; CO₂ comes out of solution as bubbles. The remaining liquid is mostly water with some dissolved CO₂ — still a liquid solution (gas in liquid) but with lower solute concentration.
- Step 3: The escaping bubbles are a separate gas phase (pure CO₂), not a solution. The system now has two phases: a gas phase (bubbles) and a liquid solution phase.
- Step 4: The classification of the liquid phase does not change; only the amount of solute changes. The solvent remains water (liquid) throughout.
- Step 5: Conclusion: Opening changes concentration, not solution type. Solvent (water) determines the phase of the solution phase.

REASONING: The solution type is defined by the state of the solvent in the solution phase. Water remains the solvent and is liquid before and after opening. The escape of CO₂ is a solubility change (Henry's law), not a change in solution classification.

ANSWER: Before opening: liquid solution (gas in liquid), solute = CO₂, solvent = water. After opening: liquid phase remains liquid solution (gas in liquid) with less CO₂; gas phase (bubbles) is pure CO₂, not a solution.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In a dental clinic, an amalgam filling is prepared by mixing liquid mercury with solid silver-tin alloy powder. The final filling is a hard solid. Classify the amalgam, identify solute and solvent, and justify why it is not a liquid solution.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Observe the components: mercury (liquid at room temperature) and silver-tin alloy (solid). The final product is a solid.
- Step 2: Determine which component is in excess. In dental amalgam, the silver-tin alloy powder is the major component (≈70% by mass), mercury is the minor component (≈30%).
- Step 3: Apply the rule: solvent = component in larger amount = silver-tin alloy (solid). Solute = mercury (liquid).
- Step 4: Since solvent is solid, the solution is a solid solution — specifically, liquid in solid (solute liquid, solvent solid).
- Step 5: It is not a liquid solution because the solvent is not liquid; the physical state of the solution follows the solvent, not the solute.

REASONING: Many students mistakenly think mercury being liquid makes it a liquid solution. The definition is clear: solvent determines the phase. Here the solid alloy is the solvent, so the amalgam is a solid solution. This is crucial for its function — a liquid filling would not stay in the tooth.

ANSWER: Solid solution (liquid in solid). Solute: mercury (liquid). Solvent: silver-tin alloy (solid). Not a liquid solution because solvent is solid.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student collects three samples: (A) air in a syringe, (B) iodine crystals dissolved in carbon tetrachloride, (C) hydrogen gas absorbed in palladium metal. For each, (i) classify the solution type, (ii) identify solute and solvent, (iii) state the physical state of the solution.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Sample A — Air is a mixture of gases (mainly N₂, O₂). The major component is nitrogen (≈78%), so solvent = nitrogen (gas). Solute = oxygen, argon, etc. (gases). Classification: gaseous solution, gas in gas. Physical state: gas.
- Step 2: Sample B — Iodine (solid) dissolves in carbon tetrachloride (liquid). CCl₄ is the solvent (liquid, major). Solute = iodine (solid). Classification: liquid solution, solid in liquid. Physical state: liquid.
- Step 3: Sample C — Hydrogen (gas) absorbed in palladium (solid). Palladium is the solvent (solid, major). Solute = hydrogen (gas). Classification: solid solution, gas in solid. Physical state: solid.
- Step 4: Verify each follows the 3×3 grid: (gas,gas), (solid,liquid), (gas,solid) — all distinct types.
- Step 5: Note that in all cases, the solution's physical state matches the solvent's state.

REASONING: This example tests the ability to apply the classification grid to diverse real systems. The key is always identifying the solvent (major component) first, then reading off the solution type from the (solute state, solvent state) pair.

ANSWER: (A) Gaseous solution, gas in gas; solute = O₂/Ar/etc., solvent = N₂; state = gas. (B) Liquid solution, solid in liquid; solute = I₂, solvent = CCl₄; state = liquid. (C) Solid solution, gas in solid; solute = H₂, solvent = Pd; state = solid.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A laboratory technician prepares a solution by dissolving 5 g of camphor (solid) in 100 mL of nitrogen gas at high pressure. The resulting mixture is a single homogeneous phase. Classify this solution, identify solute and solvent, and explain why this is possible despite the solvent being a gas. Relate your explanation to the kinetic molecular theory of gases.

COMPETENCY: Applying kinetic theory to justify solution formation across states
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify components: camphor (solid), nitrogen (gas). Nitrogen is the major component (100 mL gas vs 5 g solid) → solvent = nitrogen (gas). Solute = camphor (solid).
- Step 2: Classification: gaseous solution, solid in gas.
- Step 3: Explain homogeneity: Gas molecules (N₂) have large inter-particle spaces and weak intermolecular forces (kinetic theory). Camphor molecules disperse into these spaces, forming a uniform mixture at molecular level.
- Step 4: Note that this requires high pressure to achieve significant solubility (Henry's law), but the classification holds regardless of concentration.
- Step 5: Conclude that gas solvents can dissolve solids because gas particles are far apart and moving rapidly, allowing solute particles to occupy interstitial spaces.

ANSWER: Gaseous solution (solid in gas). Solute: camphor (solid). Solvent: nitrogen (gas). Possible because gas solvent has large inter-particle space and weak forces (kinetic theory), allowing solid solute molecules to disperse uniformly.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: In the steel industry, carbon (solid) is dissolved in iron (solid) to make steel. At room temperature, steel is a solid solution. However, during manufacturing, the mixture is heated to 1600°C where both iron and carbon are liquid. Classify the solution (i) at room temperature, (ii) at 1600°C. In each case, identify solute and solvent. Discuss whether the solution type changes with temperature and why this matters for processing.

COMPETENCY: Analysing phase-dependent classification and its industrial implications
STEPS:
- Step 1: Room temperature: Iron is solid (major), carbon is solid (minor). Solvent = iron (solid), solute = carbon (solid). Classification: solid solution, solid in solid. State = solid.
- Step 2: At 1600°C: Iron melts at 1538°C, carbon sublimes at ≈3900°C but dissolves in liquid iron. Both are liquid? Actually carbon remains solid but dissolves in liquid iron. Solvent = liquid iron (major), solute = carbon (solid). Classification: liquid solution, solid in liquid. State = liquid.
- Step 3: The solution type CHANGES with temperature because the solvent's physical state changes. Classification is defined at a given temperature.
- Step 4: Industrial relevance: Processing (mixing, homogenising) is done in liquid state (easier diffusion), then solidified to obtain solid solution (steel) with desired properties. The phase change enables manufacturing.

ANSWER: (i) Room temp: solid solution (solid in solid), solute = carbon, solvent = iron. (ii) 1600°C: liquid solution (solid in liquid), solute = carbon, solvent = liquid iron. Type changes because solvent state changes. Processing uses liquid state for mixing; final product is solid solution.

DIFFICULTY: hard
