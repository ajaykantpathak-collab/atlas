## DEFINITION
Goods sent on approval basis (or sale on approval) refers to a transaction where goods are delivered to a customer with the understanding that the sale is complete only when the customer approves/accepts them; until approval, ownership remains with the seller and the transaction is not a sale. At the end of the accounting period, if such goods are still with the customer awaiting approval, the original sale entry must be reversed and the goods must be included in closing stock at cost price.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can independently identify goods-on-approval transactions from a trial balance and additional information, calculate the cost price of unapproved goods, pass the two required adjustment journal entries, and correctly show the effects in the Trading Account (reduced sales, increased closing stock) and Balance Sheet (debtors reduced, stock increased) without guidance.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the adjustment for goods sent on approval basis when they have been incorrectly recorded as actual sales in the books. It includes: (1) reversing the sale at invoice price, (2) adding the goods to closing stock at cost price, (3) the impact on Trading Account and Balance Sheet. It does NOT cover: (a) goods sent on consignment (separate chapter), (b) hire purchase transactions, (c) situations where goods are returned after the balance sheet date (subsequent events), or (d) GST implications (beyond Class 11 scope).

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you run a boutique and send a lehenga worth ₹15,000 (cost ₹10,000) to a bride-to-be on "try before you buy" terms on March 28. Your accountant records it as a sale immediately. On March 31 (year-end), the bride hasn't decided. If you leave it as a sale, your profit is inflated by ₹5,000 and the lehenga is missing from your stock. To fix this, we must ask: "Has the sale actually happened?" No. So we undo the sale entry and put the lehenga back in our stock at what it cost us. This adjustment ensures we follow the accrual concept — revenue is recognized only when earned (approval received), not when goods are dispatched.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
A trader sends goods costing ₹8,000 to a customer on approval basis at a sale price of ₹10,000 on March 25. The accountant records it as a regular sale. On March 31 (year-end), the customer has not yet approved. What is the correct adjustment entry to reverse the sale?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
**Correct Answer:**  
Sales A/c Dr. ₹10,000  
 To Debtors A/c ₹10,000  
(Being sale on approval reversed as approval not received by year-end)

**Why wrong answers reveal misunderstanding:**  
If a student debits Debtors and credits Sales, they confuse the direction of reversal. If they use ₹8,000 (cost) instead of ₹10,000 (sale price), they don't understand that the original entry was at sale price, so reversal must be at the same amount. If they omit this entry entirely, they fail to recognize that unapproved goods do not constitute revenue.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
In the same scenario (goods costing ₹8,000 sent at ₹10,000 on approval, recorded as sale, unapproved at year-end), a student passes only the first entry (reversing sale) and forgets the second entry for stock. Explain why the financial statements will still be incorrect.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
**Correct Answer:**  
Without the second entry (Stock on Approval A/c Dr. ₹8,000 to Trading A/c ₹8,000), the Trading Account will not include these goods in closing stock. This understates gross profit by ₹8,000 (since closing stock is credited to Trading Account) and understates current assets in the Balance Sheet by ₹8,000. The reversal of sale reduces revenue by ₹10,000, but without adding back the cost, the net effect is an artificial reduction of profit by ₹10,000 instead of the correct reduction of only the unrealized profit of ₹2,000.

**Why wrong answers reveal misunderstanding:**  
If the student thinks the first entry alone is sufficient, they don't grasp the dual effect of adjustments (every adjustment hits two places). If they add the stock at sale price (₹10,000), they violate the cost concept (stock valued at cost, not sale price). If they debit Trading A/c and credit Stock, they reverse the correct direction.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Goods costing ₹12,000 were sent on approval at ₹15,000 on March 20 and recorded as sales. On March 31, the customer approves the goods. No adjustment is needed. True or False? Justify.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
**Correct Answer:**  
True. No adjustment is needed because the sale is now confirmed (approval received before year-end). The original entry (Debtors Dr. ₹15,000 to Sales ₹15,000) is correct. The goods are no longer the seller's stock; they belong to the customer. The profit of ₹3,000 is rightly recognized in the current year.

**Why wrong answers reveal misunderstanding:**  
If the student says "False, we still need to adjust," they confuse "goods sent on approval" with "goods awaiting approval at year-end." They fail to distinguish between the *transaction type* (which is fine) and the *year-end status* (which triggers adjustment). If they say we need to reverse and re-record, they don't understand that approval converts the conditional sale into a final sale retroactively.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the treatment of "Goods sent on approval" with "Goods sent on consignment" at year-end when goods remain unsold/unapproved. Identify two key differences in accounting treatment.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
**Correct Answer:**  
1. **Ownership:** In approval, ownership transfers only on approval; in consignment, ownership remains with consignor throughout.  
2. **Valuation of unsold stock:** Approval stock is valued at *cost* (since it's the seller's own stock). Consignment stock is valued at *cost + proportionate non-recurring expenses* (e.g., freight, insurance) because the consignor incurs extra costs to send goods to the consignee's location.  
3. **Revenue recognition:** Approval sale recognized at approval date; consignment sale recognized only when consignee sells to third party.

**Why wrong answers reveal misunderstanding:**  
If the student values approval stock at invoice price, they treat it like consignment stock (which is sometimes valued at invoice price for memorandum purposes). If they say both require a "stock reserve" for unrealized profit, they confuse approval (where unrealized profit is eliminated by reversing sale) with consignment (where stock reserve is created on consignment stock). If they think both are the seller's stock, they miss the legal distinction.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A company sends goods on approval frequently. At year-end, 20 such transactions are pending approval. The accountant proposes: "Instead of reversing each sale individually, let's just reduce Sales by total invoice price and increase Closing Stock by total cost." Evaluate this proposal.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
**Correct Answer:**  
The proposal is **correct in effect but incomplete in procedure**. The net impact on Trading Account and Balance Sheet is identical: Sales reduced by total invoice price, Closing Stock increased by total cost. However, proper accounting requires individual journal entries (or a combined entry with narration) to maintain audit trail, allow customer-wise tracking (for follow-up), and ensure the Debtors control account agrees with the subsidiary ledger. Skipping individual reversals makes it impossible to identify which specific customers still need to approve/return goods.

**Why wrong answers reveal misunderstanding:**  
If the student says "It's wrong because we must reverse each sale separately," they prioritize form over substance and don't understand that combined entries are acceptable if supported by a schedule. If they say "It's perfectly fine, no need for journal entries," they neglect internal control and reconciliation requirements. If they suggest reducing Debtors by cost instead of invoice price, they confuse the two accounts affected.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a new adjustment problem (with numbers) involving goods sent on approval where: (a) some goods are approved before year-end, (b) some are returned after year-end, and (c) some are still pending. Provide the additional information note as it would appear in a board exam question.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
**Expected Answer (example):**  
*Additional Information:*  
1. Goods costing ₹25,000 were sent on approval at ₹30,000 on March 15 and recorded as sales. Of these:  
   - Goods costing ₹10,000 (invoice ₹12,000) were approved by the customer on March 28.  
   - Goods costing ₹5,000 (invoice ₹6,000) were returned by the customer on April 5.  
   - The remaining goods are still with the customer awaiting approval.  

**Criteria for good answer:**  
- Clearly states cost and invoice price for each category.  
- Specifies dates showing approval/return before/after year-end.  
- Only the "awaiting approval" portion requires adjustment.  
- The returned goods (after year-end) do NOT require adjustment at year-end (they were still unapproved on March 31).  

**Why poor answers reveal misunderstanding:**  
If the student includes the returned goods in the adjustment, they confuse subsequent events with year-end status. If they exclude the approved goods correctly but forget to mention them, the problem is incomplete. If they set all goods at same cost/invoice ratio, they oversimplify and miss the need to calculate cost per transaction.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that "goods sent on approval" requires creating a **provision for unrealized profit** (like in consignment) instead of **reversing the sale entry entirely**. They write: "Stock Reserve A/c Dr. To Profit & Loss A/c" for the difference between invoice and cost. This is wrong because in approval, the sale itself is not recognized until approval; there is no "sale" to have unrealized profit on. The correct treatment is to delete the sale (reverse at invoice price) and bring back the stock at cost. The difference (unrealized profit) automatically disappears because revenue is reduced by the full invoice price, and stock is increased only by cost.

## ON_FAILURE
1. **Clarify the legal point:** "On approval, ownership does NOT transfer until the customer says 'I accept'. Until then, it's YOUR stock lying in THEIR premises."  
2. **Walk through the original wrong entry:** "You debited Debtors ₹15,000, credited Sales ₹15,000. That entry assumed a sale happened. But it didn't. So what do you do to an incorrect entry? Reverse it."  
3. **Show the two-step fix on a T-account:**  
   - Step 1: Sales A/c Dr. 15,000 → Debtors A/c 15,000 (sale gone, debtor gone)  
   - Step 2: Stock on Approval A/c Dr. 10,000 → Trading A/c 10,000 (your stock back at cost)  
4. **Prove the profit effect:** "Original wrong profit: +15,000 (sales). After fix: -15,000 (sales reversed) +10,000 (closing stock credit in Trading) = net -5,000. That 5,000 is exactly the unrealized profit you wrongly booked. No separate 'provision' needed."

## THIS_FORMULA
Cost Price of Approval Goods = Invoice Price × (100 / (100 + Profit % on Cost))  
OR  
Cost Price = Invoice Price - (Invoice Price × (Profit % on Sales / 100))

## FORMULA_VARIABLES
- Invoice Price: Sale price at which goods were sent on approval (recorded as sales)
- Profit % on Cost: Profit margin expressed as percentage of cost price (e.g., 25% on cost)
- Profit % on Sales: Profit margin expressed as percentage of sale price (e.g., 20% on sales)

## FORMULA_UNITS
All amounts in Indian Rupees (₹). Percentages are dimensionless ratios.

## COMPUTATION_TYPE
arithmetic

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: On March 20, 2024, M/s Sharma Traders sent goods costing ₹40,000 to a customer on approval basis at a sale price of ₹50,000 (profit 25% on cost). The transaction was recorded as a regular credit sale. On March 31, 2024 (year-end), the customer had not yet conveyed approval. Pass the necessary adjustment entries and show the effect on Trading Account and Balance Sheet.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the original wrong entry: Debtors A/c Dr. ₹50,000 To Sales A/c ₹50,000 (recorded at invoice price).
- Step 2: Since approval not received by year-end, reverse the sale at the same invoice price: Sales A/c Dr. ₹50,000 To Debtors A/c ₹50,000.
- Step 3: The goods are still the property of Sharma Traders and must be included in closing stock at cost price: Stock on Approval A/c Dr. ₹40,000 To Trading A/c ₹40,000.
- Step 4: In Trading Account, show Sales reduced by ₹50,000 (on credit side) and Closing Stock increased by ₹40,000 (on credit side). Gross Profit decreases by ₹10,000 (the unrealized profit).
- Step 5: In Balance Sheet, show Debtors reduced by ₹50,000 (Current Assets) and add "Stock with Customers on Approval" ₹40,000 under Current Assets (or include in Closing Stock with a note).

REASONING: The reversal removes the unearned revenue. Adding stock at cost restores the asset to the books at its historical cost. The net effect on profit is a reduction of ₹10,000 (₹50,000 - ₹40,000), which is exactly the profit that was prematurely recognized.

ANSWER:  
Journal Entries:  
(1) Sales A/c Dr. 50,000 To Debtors A/c 50,000  
(2) Stock on Approval A/c Dr. 40,000 To Trading A/c 40,000  
Trading Account: Sales ₹50,000 less on credit side; Closing Stock ₹40,000 more on credit side.  
Balance Sheet: Debtors ₹50,000 less; Stock on Approval ₹40,000 added to Current Assets.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: M/s Gupta Enterprises sends goods on approval at a profit of 20% on sale price. On March 28, goods invoiced at ₹36,000 were sent to a client and recorded as sales. The client approved goods invoiced at ₹12,000 on March 30. The remaining goods were still pending approval on March 31. Calculate the adjustment amount for Sales reversal and Stock inclusion. Show the journal entries.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Determine the portion still unapproved: Total invoice ₹36,000 - Approved ₹12,000 = ₹24,000 (invoice price of unapproved goods).
- Step 2: Calculate cost price of unapproved goods. Profit is 20% on sale price, so cost = 80% of sale price. Cost = ₹24,000 × 80% = ₹19,200.
- Step 3: Pass reversal entry for unapproved portion only: Sales A/c Dr. ₹24,000 To Debtors A/c ₹24,000.
- Step 4: Pass stock inclusion entry at cost: Stock on Approval A/c Dr. ₹19,200 To Trading A/c ₹19,200.
- Step 5: Verify: Approved goods (₹12,000 invoice, ₹9,600 cost) remain as valid sale. Unapproved goods reversed. Net effect correct.

REASONING: Only the unapproved portion requires adjustment. The approved portion is a valid sale. Profit margin is given on sale price (20%), so cost is 80% of sale price. This is a common variation where profit % is on sales, not cost.

ANSWER:  
Adjustment for Sales Reversal: ₹24,000  
Adjustment for Stock Inclusion: ₹19,200  
Journal Entries:  
Sales A/c Dr. 24,000 To Debtors A/c 24,000  
Stock on Approval A/c Dr. 19,200 To Trading A/c 19,200

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: (Combined with Bad Debts) M/s Verma & Co. sends goods on approval at 25% profit on cost. On March 25, goods costing ₹60,000 were sent to Mr. X and recorded as sales. On March 31, Mr. X has not approved. Additionally, Mr. X's total debt (including this approval) is ₹1,20,000. The firm maintains a provision for doubtful debts at 5% on debtors. Pass all adjustment entries for (a) goods on approval, (b) provision for doubtful debts, and show the combined effect on Debtors in Balance Sheet.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate invoice price of approval goods: Cost ₹60,000 + 25% profit = ₹75,000.
- Step 2: Reverse sale for unapproved goods: Sales A/c Dr. ₹75,000 To Debtors A/c ₹75,000.
- Step 3: Include stock at cost: Stock on Approval A/c Dr. ₹60,000 To Trading A/c ₹60,000.
- Step 4: Calculate revised Debtors before provision: Original Debtors ₹1,20,000 - ₹75,000 (reversal) = ₹45,000.
- Step 5: Create provision for doubtful debts at 5% on revised debtors: Provision = ₹45,000 × 5% = ₹2,250. Entry: P&L A/c Dr. ₹2,250 To Provision for Doubtful Debts A/c ₹2,250.
- Step 6: Show Debtors in Balance Sheet: Sundry Debtors ₹45,000 Less Provision ₹2,250 = Net ₹42,750. Stock on Approval ₹60,000 shown separately under Current Assets.

REASONING: The goods on approval adjustment reduces debtors, which in turn reduces the base for provision for doubtful debts. This interlinkage is a classic board exam combination. The provision is calculated on debtors *after* all adjustments (including approval reversal).

ANSWER:  
Journal Entries:  
(1) Sales A/c Dr. 75,000 To Debtors A/c 75,000  
(2) Stock on Approval A/c Dr. 60,000 To Trading A/c 60,000  
(3) P&L A/c Dr. 2,250 To Provision for Doubtful Debts A/c 2,250  
Balance Sheet:  
Sundry Debtors 45,000  
Less Provision 2,250 → 42,750  
Stock on Approval 60,000 (Current Assets)

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A firm sends goods on approval at a profit of 33⅓% on cost. The accountant, unaware of the approval terms, records all dispatches as sales. At year-end, goods invoiced at ₹80,000 are with customers awaiting approval. The firm also has a policy of maintaining a provision for doubtful debts at 10% on debtors. The total debtors (including the approval amount) are ₹5,00,000.  
(a) Calculate the correct amount of Sales, Debtors, Closing Stock, and Provision for Doubtful Debts after adjustments.  
(b) Explain why the provision for doubtful debts is calculated on the adjusted debtors figure, not the original.

COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and justifying the sequence of adjustments.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand the problem: Goods on approval recorded as sales need reversal. Profit is 33⅓% on cost, so cost = 3/4 of invoice price.
- Step 2: Calculate cost of approval goods: ₹80,000 × (3/4) = ₹60,000.
- Step 3: Adjust Sales: Reduce by ₹80,000.
- Step 4: Adjust Debtors: Reduce by ₹80,000 → Adjusted Debtors = ₹5,00,000 - ₹80,000 = ₹4,20,000.
- Step 5: Adjust Closing Stock: Increase by ₹60,000 (cost).
- Step 6: Calculate Provision for Doubtful Debts: 10% on ₹4,20,000 = ₹42,000.
- Step 7: Justify sequence: Provision is on *recoverable* debts. Unapproved goods are not a debt (sale not complete), so they must be removed first.

ANSWER:  
(a) Adjusted Sales: Reduced by ₹80,000; Adjusted Debtors: ₹4,20,000; Adjusted Closing Stock: Increased by ₹60,000; Provision for Doubtful Debts: ₹42,000.  
(b) Provision for doubtful debts is an estimate of irrecoverable amounts from *confirmed* debtors. Since the sale on approval is not complete, the customer has no obligation to pay until approval. Including it in the debtors base would overstate the provision and misrepresent the expected realizable value of debts.

DIFFICULTY: medium

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: In the audit of M/s Jain & Co., you find that goods worth ₹1,00,000 (cost ₹70,000) were sent on approval on March 28 and recorded as sales. The customer approved them on April 5. The financial statements for the year ended March 31 are under preparation. The accountant argues: "Since the customer approved the goods within a few days, and the amount is material, we should recognize the sale in the current year to show true performance."  
As an auditor, evaluate this argument. What adjustment, if any, would you insist on? Cite the relevant accounting concept.

COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction, and applying accounting concepts to judge accounting treatment.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the core issue: Revenue recognition timing for conditional sales.
- Step 2: Recall the accounting concept: Accrual concept / Revenue Recognition Principle (AS 9 / Ind AS 115) — revenue recognized when significant risks and rewards of ownership transfer, which happens on approval, not dispatch.
- Step 3: Evaluate the argument: The accountant's view violates the principle because approval occurred *after* the reporting date (April 5 vs March 31). Subsequent events (approval after year-end) do not change the year-end status.
- Step 4: Determine required adjustment: Full reversal of sale (₹1,00,000) and inclusion of stock at cost (₹70,000) as on March 31.
- Step 5: Note disclosure: The approval after year-end may be disclosed as a non-adjusting event if material, but the year-end financials must reflect the unapproved status.

ANSWER:  
The accountant's argument is incorrect. The sale cannot be recognized in the current year because the condition (approval) was not satisfied by the balance sheet date (March 31). The accrual concept and revenue recognition principle require that revenue be recognized only when earned — i.e., when the customer approves. Since approval occurred on April 5 (next financial year), the sale belongs to the next year. The auditor must insist on the adjustment: reverse sale of ₹1,00,000 and add stock of ₹70,000. The subsequent approval may be disclosed in the notes as a non-adjusting event.

DIFFICULTY: hard
