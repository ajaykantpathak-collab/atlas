## DEFINITION
Minimum Subscription is the SEBI-mandated threshold (90% of issued capital) that a company must receive in cash before making any allotment of shares; Over-subscription occurs when applications for shares exceed the number offered; Pro-rata Allotment is the proportional distribution of shares among applicants when over-subscription happens, where each applicant receives shares in proportion to their application.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Student can independently: (1) calculate minimum subscription amount for any given issue size and determine if allotment can proceed; (2) compute pro-rata allotment ratios and adjust excess application money towards allotment/calls/refund for any over-subscription scenario; (3) pass complete journal entries for over-subscription with pro-rata allotment including money received in multiple instalments.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers: SEBI minimum subscription rule (90%), over-subscription types (full rejection, partial/pro-rata, combination), pro-rata calculation methodology, adjustment of excess application money to allotment and calls, refund of rejected applications, and complete journal entries. Does NOT cover: forfeiture of shares (next node), calls in arrears/advance (next node), issue for consideration other than cash, or debenture-related concepts.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine a popular IPO like Zomato or Paytm where millions of investors apply but the company only offered 10 crore shares. The company receives applications for 100 crore shares — that's over-subscription! How does the company fairly decide who gets shares? They can't give everyone what they asked for. SEBI says they must use pro-rata allotment — if you applied for 100 shares and the issue is oversubscribed 10 times, you get 10 shares. But what about the money you paid for 100 shares? The extra money gets adjusted against your future allotment and call payments. Let's learn the exact calculation steps and journal entries so you can handle any board or CUET question on this.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
A company issues 50,000 shares of ₹10 each. Applications are received for 45,000 shares. Can the company proceed with allotment? State the SEBI rule and calculate the minimum subscription amount.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
No, the company cannot proceed with allotment. SEBI requires minimum subscription of 90% of issued capital. Issued capital = 50,000 × ₹10 = ₹5,00,000. Minimum subscription = 90% of ₹5,00,000 = ₹4,50,000. Applications received = 45,000 × ₹10 = ₹4,50,000. Since applications equal exactly 90%, allotment CAN proceed (90% is the minimum threshold, not "more than 90%"). A wrong answer of "cannot proceed" reveals the student thinks 90% is exclusive rather than inclusive, or doesn't know the exact SEBI percentage.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain in your own words why excess application money in over-subscription is first adjusted towards allotment and then towards calls, rather than being refunded immediately. What accounting principle supports this treatment?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Excess application money belongs to applicants who will become shareholders; refunding it immediately creates unnecessary cash flow and administrative burden. The company adjusts it towards amounts due from the same applicants (allotment, then calls) because those payments are legally owed once shares are allotted. This follows the "Substance over Form" principle — the economic substance is that the applicant has prepaid their future obligations. A wrong answer focusing only on "convenience" misses the legal obligation aspect; an answer saying "it's a rule" without explaining the matching principle shows rote memorisation without understanding.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A company issues 1,00,000 shares of ₹10 each at a premium of ₹2 per share, payable as ₹3 on application, ₹5 on allotment (including premium), and ₹4 on first & final call. Applications received for 1,50,000 shares. Allotment is made pro-rata. Calculate: (a) Pro-rata ratio; (b) Excess application money received; (c) Amount due on allotment after adjustment; (d) Journal entry for allotment money due.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
(a) Pro-rata ratio = Shares issued : Shares applied = 1,00,000 : 1,50,000 = 2:3. (b) Application money received = 1,50,000 × ₹3 = ₹4,50,000. Application money required for 1,00,000 shares = 1,00,000 × ₹3 = ₹3,00,000. Excess = ₹1,50,000. (c) Allotment due = 1,00,000 × ₹5 = ₹5,00,000. Less excess application adjusted = ₹1,50,000. Net allotment due = ₹3,50,000. (d) Debenture Allotment A/c Dr. 5,00,000 To Share Capital A/c 3,00,000 To Securities Premium A/c 2,00,000. A wrong answer forgetting to include premium in allotment due, or adjusting excess against call instead of allotment first, reveals misunderstanding of adjustment priority.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare and contrast: (i) Full rejection of excess applications vs (ii) Pro-rata allotment vs (iii) Combination of both (some rejected, some pro-rata). For each, state the journal entry for refund/adjustment of excess application money. Which scenario is most common in real IPOs and why?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
(i) Full rejection: Excess applicants get full refund. Entry: Share Application A/c Dr. → Bank A/c (refund). (ii) Pro-rata: All applicants get shares proportionally; excess money adjusted to allotment/calls. Entry: Share Application A/c Dr. → Share Allotment A/c / Share Call A/c. (iii) Combination: Some applications rejected fully (refund), others allotted pro-rata (adjustment). Entry: Share Application A/c Dr. → Bank A/c (rejected) + Share Allotment A/c (pro-rata). Real IPOs use combination: retail portion often has full rejection for small oversubscription, while institutional/HNI portions use pro-rata. Wrong answer saying "pro-rata is always used" ignores SEBI regulations for different investor categories.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A company issued 2,00,000 shares of ₹10 each at par. Applications received for 3,00,000 shares. The board decides to: reject applications for 50,000 shares; make pro-rata allotment to remaining applicants. Application money: ₹3 per share. Allotment: ₹4 per share. Call: ₹3 per share. Evaluate if this decision complies with SEBI minimum subscription. Calculate the pro-rata ratio for the accepted applicants and the amount of application money refunded vs adjusted.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Total applications = 3,00,000 shares. Rejected = 50,000 shares. Remaining applicants = 2,50,000 shares applied for 2,00,000 shares issued. Minimum subscription check: 2,00,000 shares issued × ₹10 = ₹20,00,000. 90% = ₹18,00,000. Application money received from all 3,00,000 applicants = 3,00,000 × ₹3 = ₹9,00,000. But rejected applicants' money (50,000 × ₹3 = ₹1,50,000) is refunded. Net from accepted = ₹7,50,000. This is LESS than ₹18,00,000 minimum subscription — DECISION VIOLATES SEBI RULE. Pro-rata ratio for accepted = 2,00,000 : 2,50,000 = 4:5. Refunded = ₹1,50,000. Adjusted = (2,50,000 × ₹3) - (2,00,000 × ₹3) = ₹7,50,000 - ₹6,00,000 = ₹1,50,000. A student who misses the minimum subscription check fails the evaluation competency.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a new over-subscription problem where: (a) shares are issued at a discount; (b) application money exceeds the nominal value of shares issued; (c) the pro-rata adjustment results in zero allotment money due from applicants. Provide the complete journal entries for application, allotment, and first call.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: Issue 50,000 shares of ₹10 each at 10% discount (issue price ₹9). Payable: ₹4 application, ₹3 allotment, ₹2 call. Applications for 1,00,000 shares. Pro-rata 1:2. Application received = 1,00,000 × ₹4 = ₹4,00,000. Required for 50,000 shares = 50,000 × ₹4 = ₹2,00,000. Excess = ₹2,00,000. Allotment due = 50,000 × ₹3 = ₹1,50,000. Excess covers allotment fully (₹1,50,000) with ₹50,000 carried to call. Call due = 50,000 × ₹2 = ₹1,00,000. Less excess = ₹50,000. Net call due = ₹50,000. Journal: Bank Dr. 4,00,000 → Share Application 4,00,000; Share Application Dr. 4,00,000 → Share Capital 2,00,000, Share Allotment 1,50,000, Share Call 50,000; Share Allotment Dr. 1,50,000 → Share Capital 1,50,000; Bank Dr. 0 → Share Allotment 1,50,000 (no cash received); Share Call Dr. 1,00,000 → Share Capital 1,00,000; Bank Dr. 50,000 → Share Call 50,000. A poor answer would miss the discount on share capital or misallocate excess between allotment and call.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students believe that in pro-rata allotment, the excess application money is automatically refunded to applicants. In reality, the excess is FIRST adjusted against allotment money due, THEN against calls, and ONLY refunded if any balance remains after all calls are paid. This error stems from confusing "rejected applications" (where full refund happens) with "pro-rata allotment" (where applicants get shares but fewer than applied). For example, if you apply for 100 shares and get 50 pro-rata, you don't get refund for 50 shares' application money — that money pays for your allotment/call dues on the 50 shares you actually received. This confusion costs 2-3 marks in board exams on journal entries.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: XYZ Ltd. issued 2,00,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at a premium of ₹2 per share, payable as ₹3 on application, ₹5 on allotment (including premium), and ₹4 on first and final call. Applications were received for 3,00,000 shares. The directors decided to allot shares pro-rata to all applicants. Excess application money was adjusted towards allotment and the balance towards call. All money was duly received. Pass journal entries in the books of XYZ Ltd.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate minimum subscription: 90% of (2,00,000 × ₹12) = 90% of ₹24,00,000 = ₹21,60,000. Application money received = 3,00,000 × ₹3 = ₹9,00,000. Since this is only application money, we check if total issue can proceed — SEBI checks total subscription including allotment/calls, but at application stage we proceed if applications ≥ 90% of issue size in shares. 3,00,000 shares applied ≥ 1,80,000 (90% of 2,00,000) — condition satisfied.
- Step 2: Determine pro-rata ratio: Shares issued : Shares applied = 2,00,000 : 3,00,000 = 2:3. Each applicant gets 2 shares for every 3 applied.
- Step 3: Calculate application money accounting: Total received = 3,00,000 × ₹3 = ₹9,00,000. Required for 2,00,000 allotted shares = 2,00,000 × ₹3 = ₹6,00,000. Excess application money = ₹9,00,000 - ₹6,00,000 = ₹3,00,000.
- Step 4: Calculate allotment due and adjustment: Allotment due = 2,00,000 × ₹5 = ₹10,00,000. Less excess application adjusted = ₹3,00,000. Net allotment due = ₹7,00,000. Excess after allotment = ₹0 (fully adjusted).
- Step 5: Calculate call due: Call due = 2,00,000 × ₹4 = ₹8,00,000. No excess remaining, so full ₹8,00,000 receivable.
- Step 6: Pass journal entries:
  Bank A/c Dr. 9,00,000 → Share Application A/c 9,00,000 (Application money received)
  Share Application A/c Dr. 9,00,000 → Share Capital A/c 6,00,000; Share Allotment A/c 3,00,000 (Transfer to capital, excess to allotment)
  Share Allotment A/c Dr. 10,00,000 → Share Capital A/c 6,00,000; Securities Premium A/c 4,00,000 (Allotment due with premium)
  Bank A/c Dr. 7,00,000 → Share Allotment A/c 7,00,000 (Allotment money received)
  Share First & Final Call A/c Dr. 8,00,000 → Share Capital A/c 8,00,000 (Call due)
  Bank A/c Dr. 8,00,000 → Share First & Final Call A/c 8,00,000 (Call money received)
- Step 7: Verify: Total cash received = 9,00,000 + 7,00,000 + 8,00,000 = ₹24,00,000 = 2,00,000 × ₹12. Correct.

REASONING: The pro-rata ratio determines how many shares each applicant receives. Excess application money is not refunded but adjusted against future dues (allotment first, then calls) because the applicant becomes a shareholder for the allotted shares and owes those amounts. Premium is credited to Securities Premium Reserve at allotment stage when it becomes due.

ANSWER: Journal entries as above. Total cash collected ₹24,00,000. Share Capital ₹20,00,000, Securities Premium ₹4,00,000.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: ABC Ltd. invited applications for 1,50,000 shares of ₹10 each at a discount of ₹1 per share, payable as ₹2 on application, ₹3 on allotment, and ₹4 on first and final call. Applications received for 2,00,000 shares. The company rejected applications for 20,000 shares and made pro-rata allotment to the remaining applicants. Excess application money was adjusted towards allotment. All money due was received except the call money on 5,000 shares. Pass journal entries.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Minimum subscription check: 90% of 1,50,000 = 1,35,000 shares. Valid applications after rejection = 2,00,000 - 20,000 = 1,80,000 ≥ 1,35,000. Proceed.
- Step 2: Categorise applications: Rejected = 20,000 shares (full refund). Pro-rata pool = 1,80,000 shares applied for 1,50,000 shares issued. Pro-rata ratio = 1,50,000 : 1,80,000 = 5:6.
- Step 3: Application money: Received = 2,00,000 × ₹2 = ₹4,00,000. Refund rejected = 20,000 × ₹2 = ₹40,000. Required for 1,50,000 allotted = 1,50,000 × ₹2 = ₹3,00,000. Excess from pro-rata pool = (1,80,000 × ₹2) - ₹3,00,000 = ₹3,60,000 - ₹3,00,000 = ₹60,000.
- Step 4: Allotment due = 1,50,000 × ₹3 = ₹4,50,000. Less excess adjusted = ₹60,000. Net allotment due = ₹3,90,000.
- Step 5: Call due = 1,50,000 × ₹4 = ₹6,00,000. Call not received on 5,000 shares = 5,000 × ₹4 = ₹20,000 (Calls in Arrears). Call received = ₹5,80,000.
- Step 6: Journal entries:
  Bank A/c Dr. 4,00,000 → Share Application A/c 4,00,000
  Share Application A/c Dr. 4,00,000 → Share Capital A/c 3,00,000; Share Allotment A/c 60,000; Bank A/c 40,000 (Refund rejected)
  Share Allotment A/c Dr. 4,50,000 → Share Capital A/c 4,50,000 (Discount already in issue price, no separate entry)
  Bank A/c Dr. 3,90,000 → Share Allotment A/c 3,90,000
  Share First & Final Call A/c Dr. 6,00,000 → Share Capital A/c 6,00,000
  Bank A/c Dr. 5,80,000; Calls in Arrears A/c Dr. 20,000 → Share First & Final Call A/c 6,00,000
- Step 7: Verify cash: 4,00,000 - 40,000 + 3,90,000 + 5,80,000 = ₹13,30,000. Capital: 1,50,000 × ₹9 = ₹13,50,000. Arrears ₹20,000. Matches.

REASONING: Combination of rejection and pro-rata requires separate handling: rejected applications get full refund; pro-rata applicants get shares proportionally with excess adjusted. Discount on issue means issue price is ₹9 (₹10 - ₹1), so share capital credited at ₹9 per share. Allotment and call amounts are based on issue price instalments.

ANSWER: Journal entries as above. Calls in Arrears ₹20,000 shown in Balance Sheet as deduction from Share Capital.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: PQR Ltd. issued 5,00,000 shares of ₹10 each at a premium of ₹3 per share. Payment terms: ₹4 on application, ₹5 on allotment (including premium), ₹4 on first call. Applications received for 8,00,000 shares. The board allotted as follows: (i) 1,00,000 shares to applicants of 2,00,000 shares (pro-rata); (ii) Full allotment to applicants of 1,50,000 shares; (iii) Rejected applications for 50,000 shares; (iv) Remaining applicants received pro-rata allotment for the balance shares. Excess application money adjusted towards allotment and calls. Pass journal entries for application and allotment. Show working for pro-rata categories.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Total applications = 8,00,000 shares. Minimum subscription = 90% of 5,00,000 = 4,50,000 shares. Condition met.
- Step 2: Categorise all 8,00,000 applications:
  Category A: 2,00,000 shares applied → 1,00,000 allotted (pro-rata 1:2)
  Category B: 1,50,000 shares applied → 1,50,000 allotted (full)
  Category C: 50,000 shares applied → 0 allotted (rejected)
  Category D: Remaining = 8,00,000 - 2,00,000 - 1,50,000 - 50,000 = 4,00,000 shares applied
  Shares left to allot = 5,00,000 - 1,00,000 - 1,50,000 = 2,50,000 shares
  Category D pro-rata ratio = 2,50,000 : 4,00,000 = 5:8
- Step 3: Application money received = 8,00,000 × ₹4 = ₹32,00,000
- Step 4: Application money required for allotted shares = 5,00,000 × ₹4 = ₹20,00,000
- Step 5: Excess application money = ₹32,00,000 - ₹20,00,000 = ₹12,00,000
  Breakdown:
  Cat A: Applied 2,00,000 × ₹4 = ₹8,00,000; Required 1,00,000 × ₹4 = ₹4,00,000; Excess = ₹4,00,000
  Cat B: Applied 1,50,000 × ₹4 = ₹6,00,000; Required 1,50,000 × ₹4 = ₹6,00,000; Excess = ₹0
  Cat C: Applied 50,000 × ₹4 = ₹2,00,000; Required ₹0; Excess = ₹2,00,000 (refunded)
  Cat D: Applied 4,00,000 × ₹4 = ₹16,00,000; Required 2,50,000 × ₹4 = ₹10,00,000; Excess = ₹6,00,000
  Total excess = 4,00,000 + 0 + 2,00,000 + 6,00,000 = ₹12,00,000. Refund (Cat C) = ₹2,00,000. Adjustable excess = ₹10,00,000.
- Step 6: Allotment due = 5,00,000 × ₹5 = ₹25,00,000 (includes premium ₹3 × 5,00,000 = ₹15,00,000)
  Adjustable excess = ₹10,00,000. Net allotment due = ₹15,00,000.
- Step 7: Journal entries:
  Bank A/c Dr. 32,00,000 → Share Application A/c 32,00,000
  Share Application A/c Dr. 32,00,000 → Share Capital A/c 20,00,000; Share Allotment A/c 10,00,000; Bank A/c 2,00,000
  Share Allotment A/c Dr. 25,00,000 → Share Capital A/c 10,00,000; Securities Premium A/c 15,00,000
  Bank A/c Dr. 15,00,000 → Share Allotment A/c 15,00,000
- Step 8: Verify: Cash received so far = 32,00,000 - 2,00,000 + 15,00,000 = ₹45,00,000. Capital = 5,00,000 × ₹10 = ₹50,00,000. Premium = ₹15,00,000. Total due = ₹65,00,000. Call due = ₹20,00,000. Matches.

REASONING: Mixed allotment categories (pro-rata, full, rejection) require separate excess calculation per category. The key insight: total shares allotted must equal shares issued (5,00,000). Each category's excess is computed individually, then aggregated. Rejected category's excess is refunded; others' excess is adjusted. Premium is recognised at allotment when it becomes due.

ANSWER: Journal entries as above. Net allotment received ₹15,00,000. Securities Premium ₹15,00,000.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1

Applications were received for 6,00,000 shares. The directors decided to allot shares pro-rata to all applicants. However, one applicant, Mr. X, who applied for 12,000 shares, failed to pay the allotment and call money. His shares were subsequently forfeited. Pass journal entries up to forfeiture, showing the pro-rata calculation and adjustment of excess application money. (Assume all other money received.)

COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and applying multi-step accounting procedures

STEPS:
- Step 1: Verify minimum subscription (90% of 4,00,000 = 3,60,000 shares applied; 6,00,000 > 3,60,000 — OK)
- Step 2: Calculate pro-rata ratio = 4,00,000 : 6,00,000 = 2:3
- Step 3: Mr. X applied 12,000 shares → Allotted = 12,000 × 2/3 = 8,000 shares
- Step 4: Application money from Mr. X = 12,000 × ₹3 = ₹36,000; Required for 8,000 shares = 8,000 × ₹3 = ₹24,000; Excess = ₹12,000
- Step 5: Allotment due from Mr. X = 8,000 × ₹5 = ₹40,000; Less excess adjusted = ₹12,000; Net due = ₹28,000 (not paid)

- Step 2: Calculate applications in each category based on over-subscription:
  QIB: 6 × 5,00,000 = 30,00,000 shares applied
  NII: 4 × 1,50,000 = 6,00,000 shares applied
  RII: 2 × 3,50,000 = 7,00,000 shares applied
