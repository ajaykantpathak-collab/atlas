## DEFINITION
Mitosis is an equational cell division process in which a single diploid parent cell divides to produce two genetically identical diploid daughter cells, maintaining the chromosome number (2n) and DNA content (2C) across generations of somatic cells.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY

Does NOT cover meiosis significance (node c11_bio_cbse_10_008), detailed mechanics of mitotic phases (nodes c11_bio_cbse_10_003, 004), or cell cycle regulation (nodes c11_bio_cbse_10_001, 002).

## WALKTHROUGH
Observe a mango tree in your courtyard: it grows taller every year, heals bark wounds within weeks, and produces new leaves each spring. Ask: "How does a single zygote become this massive tree, and how does it replace damaged cells without changing its genetic identity?" Connect to prior knowledge: In Class 9 you learned meristematic tissues drive plant growth (c9_bio_ch2_2). Now trace the cellular mechanism: every new cell originates from mitosis. The key principle is that mitosis preserves genetic fidelity while enabling multiplication — the foundation of development, maintenance, and asexual propagation in eukaryotes.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
State the chromosome number and DNA content in daughter cells immediately after mitosis if the parent cell was diploid (2n) with 2C DNA content at G1.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Correct answer: Daughter cells are diploid (2n) with 2C DNA content. A wrong answer (e.g., 4C DNA or n chromosomes) reveals the student confuses mitosis with meiosis or misunderstands that DNA content halves only after cytokinesis completes, not during karyokinesis.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why a growing root tip cell must undergo mitosis after its cytoplasm expands during G1 and G2 phases.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Correct answer: Cytoplasmic expansion increases the nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio beyond the optimal limit for nuclear control of metabolism; mitosis restores this ratio by dividing the nucleus and cytoplasm proportionally. A wrong answer focusing only on "growth" or "DNA replication" reveals the student misses the homeostatic principle of nucleo-cytoplasmic balance.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A farmer notices that the sugarcane crop in his field regrows after harvesting the upper stalks. Identify the specific plant tissue and mitotic property enabling this regeneration.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Correct answer: Intercalary meristem at the nodes; mitosis in these retained meristematic cells allows continuous longitudinal growth. A wrong answer naming apical meristem or cambium reveals confusion between primary growth (apical), secondary growth (lateral), and regrowth from nodes (intercalary).

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the role of mitosis in human skin epithelium versus honeybee drone (male) development. What fundamental difference in ploidy exists?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Correct answer: Human skin mitosis occurs in diploid somatic cells (2n → 2n); honeybee drone develops from unfertilized haploid egg, so its somatic cells are haploid (n) and divide by mitosis (n → n). A wrong answer stating both are diploid reveals the misconception that mitosis only occurs in diploid cells.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate this statement: "Mitosis alone is sufficient for the long-term survival of a multicellular species." Justify your answer.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Correct answer: False. Mitosis enables growth and repair within an individual's lifetime, but without meiosis and sexual reproduction, there is no genetic variation for adaptation, no gamete formation, and no species continuity across generations. A wrong answer agreeing reveals confusion between individual maintenance and species survival.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple experiment using onion root tips to demonstrate that mitosis is restricted to specific regions of a plant.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Expected answer: Stain root tips with acetocarmine, squash, and observe under microscope. Count mitotic indices in 1 mm zones from tip upward. High index in apical 2-3 mm (meristem), near zero in mature zones. Poor answer: only staining without zonal comparison, revealing failure to link mitosis to spatial localization.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students believe mitosis occurs in all living cells of an organism throughout life. They fail to recognize that in animals, most somatic cells (neurons, cardiomyocytes) exit to G0 and never divide again, while in plants only meristematic cells retain mitotic capacity. Example: "Heart grows by mitosis" — actually cardiac muscle hypertrophies (cell enlargement) without division after birth.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A Class 11 student observes that a skin cut heals in 5-7 days but a spinal cord injury causes permanent paralysis. Using mitosis significance, explain this difference.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the tissues involved — skin epidermis (stratified squamous epithelium) vs. spinal cord (nervous tissue).
- Step 2: Recall mitotic capacity — epidermal basal layer cells are stem-like, continuously dividing (mitotically active); mature neurons are in G0, post-mitotic.
- Step 3: Apply nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio principle — skin cells divide to replace lost cells, restoring ratio; neurons cannot divide, so lost cells are not replaced.
- Step 4: Consider repair mechanism — skin heals by regeneration (mitosis); spinal cord heals by gliosis (glial scar, no neuronal mitosis).
- Step 5: Conclude — Mitosis enables repair only in tissues with retained mitotic capacity; permanent tissues lack this significance.
REASONING: This example connects the abstract significance "cell repair" to concrete tissue-level differences in mitotic activity, using the G0 concept from cell cycle and nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio homeostasis.
ANSWER: Skin heals by mitotic regeneration of epidermal stem cells; spinal cord cannot regenerate neurons because they are post-mitotic (G0), so injury leads to permanent functional loss.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In a biology lab, students count chromosomes in onion root tip cells. They find 16 chromosomes in each cell at metaphase. Predict the chromosome number and DNA content in: (a) G1 phase, (b) after S phase, (c) after mitosis (daughter cells).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recognize metaphase chromosome number = 2n = 16 (each chromosome has 2 chromatids).
- Step 2: G1 phase precedes S phase — chromosome number = 2n = 16, DNA content = 2C (unreplicated).
- Step 3: After S phase — DNA replicates, chromosome number remains 2n = 16 (each chromosome now has 2 sister chromatids), DNA content = 4C.
- Step 4: After mitosis — sister chromatids separate, each daughter cell receives 16 chromosomes (2n), each with one chromatid, DNA content = 2C.
- Step 5: Verify consistency — mitosis is equational: 2n → 2n, 2C → 4C (S) → 2C (post-M).
REASONING: This works through the quantitative changes in chromosome number (N) and DNA content (C) across the cell cycle, a classic board exam pattern (NCERT Exercise Q16).
ANSWER: (a) G1: 16 chromosomes, 2C DNA; (b) Post-S: 16 chromosomes, 4C DNA; (c) Post-mitosis daughter cells: 16 chromosomes, 2C DNA.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A horticulturist wants to propagate a prize-winning rose variety with identical traits. Explain why mitotic propagation (cuttings) is preferred over sexual reproduction (seeds), and identify one limitation.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define goal — genetic fidelity to parent phenotype (flower color, fragrance, disease resistance).
- Step 2: Sexual reproduction involves meiosis (recombination, independent assortment) + fertilization → genetic variation in offspring.
- Step 3: Mitotic propagation (stem cuttings) uses somatic cells → mitosis only → genetically identical clones (barring somatic mutations).
- Step 4: Connect to significance — mitosis preserves genetic complement; meiosis creates variation.
- Step 5: Identify limitation — clonal propagation accumulates somatic mutations and lacks genetic diversity, making entire crop vulnerable to new pathogens.
- Step 6: Synthesize — Mitosis significance (genetic fidelity) enables true-to-type propagation; meiosis significance (variation) is sacrificed.
REASONING: Combines mitosis significance (genetic fidelity, asexual reproduction) with meiosis significance (variation) from forward node c11_bio_cbse_10_008, applying to real-world horticulture decision-making.
ANSWER: Mitotic propagation via cuttings produces clones identical to parent, preserving desired traits; limitation is loss of genetic diversity and increased disease susceptibility.

COMPETENCY: Evaluating exceptions to biological generalizations with evidence

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A researcher treats plant root tips with a chemical that inhibits spindle fiber formation. Predict the effect on: (i) chromosome number in resulting cells, (ii) nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio, (iii) tissue growth. Justify each.
COMPETENCY: Predicting multi-level consequences of disrupting a cell cycle process
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify target — spindle fibers required for chromatid separation in anaphase (mitosis).
- Step 2: Predict karyokinesis failure — chromosomes cannot segregate → nuclei not formed properly → polyploid or multinucleate cells.
- Step 3: Chromosome number — if cytokinesis occurs without karyokinesis: single cell with 4C DNA, 2n chromosomes (unreduced); if neither occurs: binucleate 2n+2n.
- Step 4: Nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio — cytoplasm may divide (cytokinesis) but nucleus doesn't → ratio disturbed; or no division → ratio unchanged but cell enlarged.
- Step 5: Tissue growth — meristematic cells fail to produce viable daughter cells → root growth arrested.
- Step 6: Synthesize — Mitosis significance (growth, ratio maintenance, repair) all fail.
ANSWER: (i) Abnormal chromosome distribution (polyploidy/multinucleate); (ii) Nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio disrupted; (iii) Root growth stops due to failed cell production in meristem.
DIFFICULTY: hard
