## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the three core discussion themes explicitly listed in the textbook's vH;kl (exercise) section for "dkys es?kk ikuh ns"?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The three core discussion themes are: (1) Social hypocrisy and performative charity (vaèkfo'okl) — Question 1 asks about the basis of the name "Es<d&eaMyh" and why Banj Lsuk liked it; (2) Water as a survival right vs luxury — Question 3 asks why "ikuh ns, xqM+èkkuh ns" is demanded alongside water; (3) Systemic inequality and the narrator's complicity — Question 6 asks to analyze Thth's behavior toward Banj Lsuk in light of the story's message. A wrong answer listing generic themes like "poverty" or "water scarcity" without referencing the specific vH;kl questions reveals the student hasn't mapped textbook discussion prompts to oral preparation — they're preparing for written exams, not ASL.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain in your own words why the textbook includes the phrase "xxjh iwQVh cSy fi;klk" (the morning stars remain, the evenings remain) in the folk song refrain — what does this contrast with in the story's reality?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The refrain "xxjh iwQVh cSy fi;klk" symbolizes nature's eternal, indifferent cycles — stars rise and set regardless of human suffering. This contrasts sharply with the story's reality where human-made systems (caste, class, municipal neglect) disrupt the natural right to water. The folk song's rhythm ("ikuh ns, xqM+èkkuh ns") carries collective resilience, while the narrative shows individualized desperation: Banj Lsuk begs door-to-door, Thth performs charity only when watched, and the colony's taps run dry while the privileged waste water. A student who only explains the literal meaning of "iwQVh" and "fi;klk" misses the ironic juxtaposition — the oral exam expects you to contrast the song's cosmic permanence with the story's social fragility.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
During a group discussion, a peer claims: "Thth is actually kind — he gives Banj Lsuk water eventually, and even gives her better food than his own family eats." Using ONLY textual evidence from the narrator's internal monologue, refute this claim in 90 seconds of spoken Hindi.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Refutation structure: (1) Point: Thth's "kindness" is performative and conditional — "esjk lj viuh xksn eas ysdj cksyha" (my tongue moved in its place) shows reluctance, not generosity. (2) Evidence: He admits "ighys os Hkh rerekbZ, ysfdu ';knk nsj rd muls xqLlk ugha jgk x;k" (earlier they were polite, but until the end they didn't get arrogant) — he resents her assertiveness. (3) Evidence: He gives "gkFk ls vyx f[kldk" (different food from the cow) — not better food, but cattle feed, revealing dehumanization. (4) Explanation: His charity is "vaèkfo'okl" (hypocrisy) because he calculates "mls cqokbZ dgrs gSa" (this is called charity) while denying her dignity. (5) Link: The story ends with Thth invoking "Íf"k&eqfu" (sages) to justify withholding — proving his charity is self-serving theater. A weak answer praises Thth's actions without analyzing his internal monologue's revealing phrases — oral analysis demands reading what the narrator *thinks*, not just what he *does*.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare how the folk song's collective voice ("dkys es?kk ikuh ns" — plural demand) differs from Thth's individualistic charity in addressing water justice. Which approach does the story endorse, and what textual evidence supports your conclusion?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The folk song uses collective imperative ("ikuh ns" — give us water, "xqM+èkkuh ns" — give us light) representing community claim on resources as right. Thth's charity is individual, discretionary, and hierarchical — he decides *if*, *when*, and *what* to give ("ckYVh Hkj&Hkj dj ikuh bl xanh es<d&eaMyh ij" — he measures water drop by drop onto this helpless girl). The story endorses the collective approach: the final paragraphs shift from Thth's internal debate to systemic indictment — "dkys es?kk ny osQ ny meM+rs gSa] ikuh >ek>e cjlrk gS" (dark nights merge into nights, water drips drop by drop) — implying structural failure, not individual charity, is the crisis. Evidence: The narrator's own rhetorical questions ("D;k gekjs lkaLo`Qfrd eu osQ [kkyh&[kkyh gks tkus dk [krjk ugha gS?" — isn't there a danger of our civilizational conscience becoming callous?) implicate the entire social order. A student who says "both are needed" misses the story's satirical thrust — Parsai critiques charity as distraction from justice.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate this statement for an ASL discussion: "The story's ending — where Thth quotes sages to justify refusing water — proves that tradition and religion are the real enemies of social justice." Is this a fair reading of Parsai's intent? Defend your position with textual nuance.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, it's a reductive reading. Parsai targets the *misuse* of tradition, not tradition itself. Textual nuance: (1) Thth *selectively* invokes "Íf"k&eqfu" (sages) — "Íf"k&eqfu;ksa us nku dks lcls m¡Qpk LFkku fn;k gSA" — but ignores their actual teaching: "tc vkneh cw¡n&cw¡n ikuh dks rjls rc ikuh dhpM+ esa cgkvksA" (when humans release water drop by drop, water collects in the vessel). The sages advocated accumulation through consistent giving; Thth uses them to justify withholding. (2) The story contrasts "foKku" (science/rationality) with "fo'okl" (superstition/ritual) — "foKku dk viuk roZQ gS vkSj fo'okl dk viuk lkeF;ZA" — but ends questioning whether *educated* people like Thth are any better: "ij ;fn oqQN fo'okl foKkulEer u gksdj Hkh mDr rjg ls gkfudj u gksa rks muosQ lkFk Hkh mlh izdkj dk crkZo D;k mfpr gS?" (if ritualistic people act badly even without superstition, is their behavior also justified?). Parsai indicts *hypocrisy across both tradition and modernity*. A fair oral evaluation acknowledges this dual critique — saying "religion is the enemy" simplifies the satire and loses marks for lack of textual nuance.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a 2-minute ASL-style spoken response to this prompt: "Imagine you are a resident of that colony 20 years later. The water crisis has worsened. Record a voice message for your colony WhatsApp group proposing ONE concrete collective action inspired by the story's folk song — not charity, but justice."

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Expected response criteria: (1) Opens with folk song reference: "dkys es?kk ikuh ns — ye sirf geet nahi, hamara haq hai" (not just a song, our right). (2) Identifies systemic failure: "Thth jaise log aaj bhi 'cqokbZ' karke apna zimmedari chhupate hain" (people like Thth still hide behind 'charity'). (3) Proposes ONE collective action: "Main prastaav karta hoon ki colony ka har ghar ek 'ikuh nidhi' mein ₹50 mahine daale, jisse hum municipal tanker ka contract khud karein — bina kisi neta ya adhikari ke bharose" (every house contributes ₹50/month to a water fund to contract tankers directly). (4) Links to story: "Jaise geet mein 'cSy fi;klk' — sammukh taare rahte hain — waise hamari ekta bhi rahegi" (like stars remain in morning/evening, our unity will remain). (5) Closes with call: "Kal subah 8 baje park mein milte hain. Awaaz ek, haq ek." A poor answer proposes vague "awareness campaigns" or "writing to MLA" — missing the story's lesson that *collective self-reliance* (like the folk singers demanding directly) beats petitioning power.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students treat "Speaking and Listening" as reading aloud the written answers from nodes 012-014. They memorize paragraph answers and recite them monotonously in ASL, thinking fluency = speed. The actual error: ASL assesses *interactive competence* — initiating, turn-taking, responding to peers, using discourse markers ("ek minute," "main isse sehmat nahi," "is baat ko aage badhate hue"), and adjusting register (formal for examiner, conversational for peer discussion). Reciting written answers fails because: (a) written syntax (long subordinate clauses) chokes spoken breath groups, (b) no eye contact or prosodic signaling, (c) zero evidence of listening to others. The vH;kl questions are *discussion starters*, not speech scripts.

## ON_FAILURE
If a student recites written answers in mock ASL: (1) Stop them at 30 seconds. (2) Play back their recording — ask: "Where did you take a breath? Where did you look at your imaginary peer?" (3) Give them the PEEL Speaking Card: P=Point (1 sentence), E=Evidence (1 textual phrase + page reference), E=Explanation (2 sentences in *your* words), L=Link (1 sentence to today). (4) Practice with a timer: 45 sec prep → 2 min speak → 30 sec peer response. (5) Repeat with a *different* vH;kl question daily for 5 days. Mastery = spontaneous PEEL without notes.
