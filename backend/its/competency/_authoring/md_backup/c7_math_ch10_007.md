## DEFINITION
The commutative property of integer multiplication states that changing the order of two integers being multiplied does not change their product; mathematically expressed as $a \times b = b \times a$ for any integers $a$ and $b$.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can apply the commutative property to simplify integer multiplication calculations, verify equality across different orderings of positive and negative integers, and solve multi-step expressions without recomputing from scratch.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the commutative property for the multiplication of two or more integers, including sign rules for negative and positive integers. It does not cover the distributive property or matrix multiplication.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at a school sports event in Delhi, arranging chairs for the annual function. You have 5 rows of chairs, and each row has (-3) extra chairs allocated or removed due to spacing adjustments, giving you a quick calculation of $5 \times (-3)$. Alternatively, if you calculate (-3) rows of 5 units each, how does the product compare? To begin, write down both expressions side by side: $5 \times (-3)$ and $(-3) \times 5$. Recall from your earlier work with integer multiplication sign rules that a positive integer multiplied by a negative integer results in a negative product. Compute each side independently to find that both equal $-15$. The key insight is that just like whole numbers, integers are commutative under multiplication, meaning the flip in order never alters the final product.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which of the following mathematical statements correctly demonstrates the commutative property of multiplication for integers $-8$ and $4$?
A) $(-8) + 4 = 4 + (-8)$
B) $(-8) \times 4 = 4 \times (-8)$
C) $(-8) \div 4 = 4 \div (-8)$
D) $(-8) - 4 = 4 - (-8)$
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is B. Option B correctly uses the multiplication operation to show that flipping the order of $-8$ and $4$ maintains equality: $(-8) \times 4 = -32$ and $4 \times (-8) = -32$. Choosing Option A shows a confusion between the commutative property of *addition* and multiplication. Choosing options C or D indicates that the student does not recognize that commutativity applies specifically to multiplication (and addition), but not to division or subtraction.

A) Yes, because switching the order of negative numbers changes the sign of the product.
B) No, because the commutative property applies to integers regardless of their signs.
C) Yes, because multiplication is only commutative for positive whole numbers.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct answer is B. Rahul is incorrect because the commutative property of multiplication holds true for all integers, whether positive, negative, or zero. Both expressions evaluate to $+30$ ($(-5) \times (-6) = 30$ and $(-6) \times (-5) = 30$). Choosing Option A or C reveals a misconception that negative integers break fundamental arithmetic properties or that commutativity is restricted to natural numbers.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate the value of $14 \times (-9)$ using the commutative property of multiplication by first rewriting the expression with (-9) as the first multiplier. What is the final product?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $-126$. 
Steps:
1. Rewrite using commutativity: $(-9) \times 14$.
2. Identify the sign rules: negative integer $\times$ positive integer = negative product.
3. Multiply the absolute values: $9 \times 14 = 126$.
4. Apply the negative sign: $-126$.
A student who answers $+126$ has made a sign error, forgetting that multiplying a negative integer by a positive integer yields a negative product.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Examine the following chain of multiplications: $A = [(-2) \times 7] \times (-5)$ and $B = (-2) \times [7 \times (-5)]$. How does the commutative property relate to rewriting these expressions?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The correct answer is that while both $A$ and $B$ evaluate to $+70$, transitioning between them requires both the *associative* property (moving brackets) and the *commutative* property (swapping inner terms). A student who confuses commutativity with associativity will state that swapping brackets is an example of the commutative property. Commutativity strictly refers to changing the order (position) of factors, whereas associativity refers to changing their grouping.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer will present an expression such as $5 \times (-4) \times 0$, apply commutativity to rewrite it as $(-4) \times 5 \times 0$, and show that both evaluate to $0$. A poor answer uses only positive numbers, omits zero, or fails to demonstrate an actual change in the order of the factors.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that swapping the positions of negative numbers alters the sign of the final product, thinking that $(-a) \times b$ becomes positive simply because the negative number moved to the second position (e.g., believing that $(-4) \times 5 = -20$ but $5 \times (-4) = +20$). Another common error is applying the commutative property incorrectly to division, assuming that $a \div b = b \div a$ for integers.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that the commutative property is about order, not value alteration. Write out both forms side-by-side: $(-4) \times 5 = -20$ and $5 \times (-4) = -20$. Point out that the numbers simply traded places, but the sign rule (negative $\times$ positive = negative) remains identical because the numbers and their signs are just being reordered, not changed into their opposites.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A store in Jaipur records a daily stock loss of 8 items for 15 consecutive days, represented as $15 \times (-8)$. Use the commutative property to rewrite this expression with (-8) first, and calculate the total stock change.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given expression: $15 \times (-8)$.
- Step 2: Apply the commutative property of multiplication ($a \times b = b \times a$) to swap the factors: $(-8) \times 15$.
- Step 3: Determine the sign of the product using integer sign rules (negative $\times$ positive = negative).
- Step 4: Multiply the absolute values: $8 \times 15 = 120$.
- Step 5: Attach the negative sign to get the final product: $-120$ items.
REASONING: The commutative property guarantees that changing the order of multiplication does not change the result. Rewriting $15 \times (-8)$ as $(-8) \times 15$ makes it easier to compute while ensuring the mathematical value remains invariant.
ANSWER: -120 items
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Verify whether the commutative property holds for the two negative integers $-25$ and $-12$ by evaluating both $A = (-25) \times (-12)$ and $B = (-12) \times (-25)$.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write down the first expression: $A = (-25) \times (-12)$.
- Step 2: Evaluate expression A using the rule that the product of two negative integers is positive: $25 \times 12 = 300$, so $A = +300$.
- Step 3: Write down the second expression with reversed order: $B = (-12) \times (-25)$.
- Step 4: Evaluate expression B using the same sign rule: $12 \times 25 = 300$, so $B = +300$.
- Step 5: Compare the results: Since $A = 300$ and $B = 300$, $A = B$, confirming commutativity.
REASONING: This demonstrates that even with two negative integers, swapping the order preserves equality because multiplication of signed numbers follows strict absolute value multiplication combined with consistent sign rules.
ANSWER: Both expressions equal $+300$, verifying the commutative property.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Simplify the multi-step expression $4 \times (-7) \times 25$ by strategically applying the commutative property to group numbers that make mental math easier, then compute the final answer.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write the initial expression: $4 \times (-7) \times 25$.
- Step 2: Apply the commutative property of multiplication to reorder the factors so that $4$ and $25$ are adjacent: $4 \times 25 \times (-7)$.
- Step 3: Group the first two numbers using associativity: $(4 \times 25) \times (-7)$.
- Step 4: Compute the product inside the parentheses: $4 \times 25 = 100$.
- Step 5: Multiply the result by the remaining integer: $100 \times (-7) = -700$.
REASONING: The commutative and associative properties allow us to rearrange multiplication problems to form clean powers of 10 (like $4 \times 25 = 100$), drastically simplifying complex integer calculations.
ANSWER: -700

Explain step-by-step which properties of multiplication Priya used to move the numbers around in Arrangement 2, and verify that both arrangements yield the exact same final product.
COMPETENCY: Translating and justifying properties of integer multiplication in multi-factor expressions.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze Arrangement 1 and compute its step-by-step product.
- Step 2: Identify that Arrangement 2 involves swapping the positions of $8$ and $(-2)$, which is the commutative property, alongside regrouping brackets (associative property).
- Step 3: Compute Arrangement 2 step-by-step.
- Step 4: Compare final outputs to verify equality.
ANSWER: In Arrangement 2, Priya used the commutative property to swap $8$ and $(-2)$ and the associative property to change grouping.
