## DEFINITION
Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity states that when electrons occupy degenerate orbitals (orbitals of equal energy within the same subshell), they occupy separate orbitals singly with parallel spins before any pairing occurs. This arrangement maximizes the total spin multiplicity (2S+1) and minimizes electron-electron repulsion, leading to the lowest energy ground state.

## WALKTHROUGH
Observe a magnetic stirrer in the chemistry lab: the stir bar (iron) is strongly attracted to the rotating magnet beneath the beaker, while a ceramic stir bar is not. This macroscopic magnetism originates from unpaired electron spins in the iron atoms. Now consider the electron configuration of gaseous oxygen atoms (1s²2s²2p⁴). How do four electrons arrange in three degenerate 2p orbitals? If they paired up immediately, we would get one paired orbital and two empty orbitals — but experiments show oxygen atoms have two unpaired electrons (paramagnetic). This forces us to accept that electrons fill degenerate orbitals singly first with parallel spins (Hund's rule). Let's verify with carbon (2p²): two electrons occupy two different p orbitals with parallel spins. For nitrogen (2p³): three electrons singly occupy all three p orbitals (half-filled, extra stable). For oxygen (2p⁴): the fourth electron must pair in one orbital, leaving two unpaired. This pattern — singles first, parallel spins, then pairs — is Hund's rule, driven by exchange energy stabilization and reduced electron-electron repulsion.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
State Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity in your own words.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Correct answer: When filling degenerate orbitals (same subshell), electrons occupy separate orbitals singly with parallel spins before any orbital gets a second electron. This maximizes total spin multiplicity. A wrong answer (e.g., "electrons pair up as soon as possible" or "electrons fill orbitals in order of increasing energy") reveals confusion with the Aufbau principle or a misunderstanding that pairing lowers energy. The student likely thinks electrons "prefer" to be paired, ignoring exchange energy and repulsion minimization.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do electrons in degenerate orbitals prefer to remain unpaired with parallel spins rather than pair up in the same orbital? Explain using the concepts of electron-electron repulsion and exchange energy.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Correct answer: Parallel spins in different orbitals keep electrons farther apart on average (reducing Coulombic repulsion) and allow quantum mechanical exchange interactions that lower the energy (exchange energy). Pairing forces two electrons into the same orbital, increasing repulsion and losing exchange stabilization. A wrong answer focusing only on Pauli exclusion ("opposite spins required in same orbital") misses the energetic driving force. The student may not grasp that Hund's rule is about energy minimization, not just a filling order.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Draw the orbital diagram for the 3d subshell of a Fe²⁺ ion (Z=26). How many unpaired electrons does it have? Is Fe²⁺ paramagnetic or diamagnetic?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Correct working: Fe atomic config = [Ar]4s²3d⁶. Fe²⁺ loses 4s² electrons → [Ar]3d⁶. For 3d⁶: five degenerate d orbitals. Hund's rule: first 5 electrons go singly into each orbital with parallel spins (↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑). The 6th electron pairs in one orbital (↑↓). Unpaired electrons = 4. Paramagnetic (attracted to magnetic field). A wrong answer (e.g., 2 unpaired or 0 unpaired) shows the student paired electrons too early (violating Hund's rule) or miscounted d orbitals. They may also incorrectly remove 3d electrons before 4s for the ion.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the orbital diagrams of nitrogen (1s²2s²2p³) and oxygen (1s²2s²2p⁴). Explain why nitrogen has a half-filled p subshell and how this affects its ionization energy relative to oxygen.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Correct answer: Nitrogen (2p³): three electrons singly occupy all three p orbitals with parallel spins (↑ ↑ ↑) — half-filled, maximum exchange energy, symmetrical distribution, extra stable. Oxygen (2p⁴): one orbital has paired electrons (↑↓), two orbitals have single electrons (↑ ↑). The paired electrons in oxygen experience greater repulsion, making it easier to remove one (lower ionization energy) despite oxygen's higher nuclear charge. This explains the anomaly where IE(O) < IE(N). A wrong answer that doesn't mention exchange energy or symmetrical distribution fails to connect Hund's rule to the periodic trend anomaly.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse Hund's rule with the Aufbau principle, thinking Hund's rule governs the order of subshell filling (e.g., 4s before 3d). Another pervasive error: In orbital diagrams for p⁴, d⁶, etc., they draw the paired electrons correctly but assign opposite spins to the unpaired electrons in different orbitals (e.g., p⁴: ↑↓, ↑, ↓). This violates the "parallel spins" requirement of Hund's rule. They think "different orbitals → spins can be anything," not realizing parallel spins maximize exchange energy. Example: For carbon (2p²), they draw one orbital with ↑ and another with ↓ instead of ↑ and ↑.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Write the orbital diagram for the valence electrons of a manganese atom (Z=25) in its ground state. Determine the number of unpaired electrons and predict whether it is paramagnetic or diamagnetic.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Write the full electronic configuration of Mn (Z=25): 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d⁵.
- Step 2: Identify the valence subshells: 4s and 3d. The 4s subshell has one orbital, filled with 2 electrons (paired). The 3d subshell has five degenerate orbitals.
- Step 3: Apply Hund's rule to the 3d⁵ subshell: Place one electron in each of the five d orbitals with parallel spins (all ↑) before any pairing occurs.
- Step 4: Draw the orbital diagram: 4s: [↑↓]; 3d: [↑] [↑] [↑] [↑] [↑] (five boxes each with one up arrow).
- Step 5: Count unpaired electrons: 4s has 0, 3d has 5 unpaired electrons → total 5 unpaired electrons.
- Step 6: Conclude paramagnetic behavior due to presence of unpaired electrons.

REASONING: Hund's rule maximizes total spin multiplicity by keeping electrons unpaired with parallel spins in degenerate orbitals. The half-filled d⁵ configuration is especially stable due to maximum exchange energy and symmetrical distribution.

ANSWER: Orbital diagram: 4s: ↑↓; 3d: ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ (five separate orbitals). Number of unpaired electrons = 5. Paramagnetic.

Using Hund's rule, draw the orbital diagram for the valence electrons (4s and 3d) and explain why this configuration is more stable than the expected [Ar] 4s² 3d⁴.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Write the expected configuration from Aufbau: [Ar] 4s² 3d⁴.
- Step 2: Write the actual configuration: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵.
- Step 3: Draw orbital diagram for actual configuration: 4s: [↑] (one electron); 3d: five orbitals each with one electron [↑] [↑] [↑] [↑] [↑] (all parallel spins).
- Step 4: Draw orbital diagram for expected configuration: 4s: [↑↓]; 3d: four orbitals with one electron [↑] [↑] [↑] [↑] and one empty orbital.
- Step 5: Compare: Actual has half-filled 4s (1 electron) and half-filled 3d (5 electrons) — both subshells are half-filled. Expected has filled 4s but 3d⁴ (not half-filled).
- Step 6: Explain stability: Half-filled subshells have maximum exchange energy (more parallel spin pairs) and symmetrical electron distribution, lowering energy. The energy gain from half-filled 3d⁵ and half-filled 4s¹ outweighs the energy cost of promoting one 4s electron to 3d.

REASONING: Hund's rule favors maximum multiplicity. The actual configuration achieves half-filled 3d and half-filled 4s, maximizing exchange energy (number of favorable parallel-spin interactions) and symmetrical distribution, making it more stable.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A transition metal ion M²⁺ has the electronic configuration [Ar] 3d⁷. Determine the number of unpaired electrons in M²⁺ using Hund's rule. If the ion is placed in a magnetic field, will it be attracted or repelled? Identify the metal M (atomic number) given that it is in the first transition series.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the configuration of M²⁺: [Ar] 3d⁷. The neutral atom M would have configuration [Ar] 4s² 3d⁷ (since 4s fills before 3d and is lost first upon ionization). So neutral M has atomic number = 18 (Ar) + 2 + 7 = 27 → Cobalt (Co).
- Step 2: For Co²⁺, the valence subshell is 3d⁷. There are five degenerate d orbitals.
- Step 3: Apply Hund's rule: Fill each of the five d orbitals singly with parallel spins (5 electrons, all ↑). The remaining 2 electrons pair up in two of the orbitals (↑↓).
- Step 4: Draw orbital diagram: Five boxes: three with single ↑, two with ↑↓.
- Step 5: Count unpaired electrons: 3 unpaired electrons.
- Step 6: Magnetic behavior: Paramagnetic (attracted to magnetic field) because unpaired electrons > 0.
- Step 7: Identify metal: Co (Z=27).

REASONING: Hund's rule dictates single occupancy with parallel spins before pairing. For d⁷, maximum unpaired electrons = 3 (since 5 orbitals, 7 electrons → 5-2=3 unpaired). The ion is paramagnetic.

ANSWER: Unpaired electrons = 3. Paramagnetic (attracted). Metal M = Cobalt (Co, Z=27).

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: In the first transition series, the ionization energy generally increases across the period, but there are dips at Cr and Cu. Using Hund's rule and the concept of exchange energy, explain why the first ionization energy of chromium (Z=24) is lower than that of vanadium (Z=23) even though chromium has a higher nuclear charge.

COMPETENCY: Explaining periodic trends using electronic configuration and Hund's rule.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Write ground state configurations: V: [Ar] 4s² 3d³; Cr: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵.
- Step 2: Identify the electron removed in first ionization: For V, it's a 4s electron (from 4s²). For Cr, it's the single 4s electron (from 4s¹).
- Step 3: Analyze stability of resulting ions: V⁺ becomes [Ar] 4s¹ 3d³ (not particularly stable). Cr⁺ becomes [Ar] 3d⁵ (half-filled d subshell, extra stable due to exchange energy and symmetry).
- Step 4: Conclude: The Cr⁺ ion is stabilized by half-filled d⁵ configuration (maximum multiplicity), so less energy is required to remove the 4s electron from Cr compared to removing a 4s electron from V (which does not yield a half-filled d subshell). Hence IE of Cr < IE of V.

ANSWER: Cr has configuration [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵. Removing the 4s¹ electron yields Cr⁺ with stable half-filled 3d⁵ (extra exchange energy). V has [Ar] 4s² 3d³; removing a 4s electron gives V⁺ [Ar] 4s¹ 3d³, lacking half-filled stability. Thus, despite higher nuclear charge, Cr has lower IE due to stability of product ion.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student claims that for a p⁴ subshell, the two paired electrons must have opposite spins (Pauli), but the two unpaired electrons in the other two orbitals can have opposite spins to each other (one ↑, one ↓) because they are in different orbitals. Evaluate this claim using Hund's rule and the concept of exchange energy.

COMPETENCY: Evaluating electron spin arrangements in degenerate orbitals.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall Hund's rule: Electrons in degenerate orbitals occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins (same spin direction) before pairing.
- Step 2: For p⁴: three orbitals. First three electrons go into each orbital with parallel spins (↑, ↑, ↑). Fourth electron pairs in one orbital (↑↓). The remaining two unpaired electrons are in separate orbitals and must have parallel spins (both ↑) to satisfy Hund's rule.
- Step 3: If the two unpaired electrons had opposite spins (↑ and ↓), the total spin multiplicity would be lower (S = 0, multiplicity = 1) compared to parallel spins (S = 1, multiplicity = 3). Hund's rule maximizes multiplicity.
- Step 4: Exchange energy: Parallel spins in different orbitals allow exchange interaction, lowering energy. Opposite spins do not.
- Step 5: Therefore, the claim is false. The two unpaired electrons must have parallel spins.

ANSWER: The claim is incorrect. Hund's rule requires maximum multiplicity, meaning all unpaired electrons in degenerate orbitals must have parallel spins. For p⁴, the two unpaired electrons must both be spin-up (or both spin-down) to maximize total spin (S=1, multiplicity=3) and gain exchange energy stabilization. Opposite spins would reduce multiplicity and lose exchange energy.
