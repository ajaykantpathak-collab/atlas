## DEFINITION
Trend analysis is a technique of financial statement analysis that expresses each item of a financial statement for a series of years as a percentage of the same item in a chosen base year, thereby revealing the direction and magnitude of changes over time.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to select an appropriate base year, compute trend percentages for multiple financial statement items across at least five years, and interpret the resulting trends to comment on the enterprise's performance trajectory.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the meaning of trend analysis, criteria for base year selection, step-by-step computation of trend percentages, and basic interpretation. It does NOT cover comparative statements, common-size statements, ratio analysis, or cash flow analysis, which are separate techniques in the same chapter.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are a credit analyst at a bank reviewing a loan application from a manufacturing company. The borrower provides five years of revenue data: ₹50 lakh, ₹55 lakh, ₹48 lakh, ₹62 lakh, ₹70 lakh. At first glance, the numbers fluctuate — how do you quickly assess whether the business is growing, stagnating, or volatile? Trend analysis solves this by converting each year's figure into a percentage of a single base year (say, Year 1 = 100%). Suddenly the pattern becomes visible: 100, 110, 96, 124, 140. You can now see a dip in Year 3 but strong recovery thereafter. To begin, you must choose a base year that is 'normal' — free from one-time events like fire losses or asset sales. Then for every subsequent year, divide the current year's figure by the base year figure and multiply by 100. The key insight is that trend percentages remove the distraction of absolute rupee amounts and expose the underlying growth or decline pattern, enabling meaningful inter-period comparison even when the scale of operations changes.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Define trend analysis and write the formula for calculating the trend percentage of a financial statement item for a given year.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Trend analysis is a horizontal analysis technique where each year's financial statement figure is expressed as a percentage of the same figure in a selected base year. Formula: Trend Percentage = (Current Year Figure / Base Year Figure) × 100. A wrong answer (e.g., using the previous year as base instead of a fixed base year, or forgetting to multiply by 100) reveals the student confuses trend analysis with year-on-year growth rates or does not grasp the fixed-base concept.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why must the base year be a 'normal' year rather than a year with exceptional profits due to a one-time gain like sale of land? Explain in your own words.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
If the base year contains a one-time gain, its figure is artificially inflated. All subsequent trend percentages will be calculated against this inflated base, making normal years appear as declines (percentages below 100) even when performance is stable. This distorts the true trend. A wrong answer (e.g., 'any year can be base year' or 'the first year is always the base') shows the student memorises the procedure without understanding the purpose of comparability.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A company's Revenue from Operations for five years (2019-2023) is: 2019: ₹40,00,000; 2020: ₹44,00,000; 2021: ₹38,00,000; 2022: ₹50,00,000; 2023: ₹56,00,000. Taking 2019 as base year, compute the trend percentages for all five years. Show all steps.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3

Step 2: 2020: (44,00,000 / 40,00,000) × 100 = 110.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe the base year must always be the earliest year in the data set (e.g., Year 1). In reality, the base year should be a 'normal' year — free from strikes, fires, policy changes, or one-time gains/losses. If Year 1 was abnormal, using it as base distorts all trend percentages. Another error: calculating trend percentage as (Current Year - Base Year)/Base Year × 100 (which gives percentage change, not trend percentage). The correct trend percentage is (Current Year / Base Year) × 100, so the base year itself is 100, not 0.

## THIS_FORMULA
Trend Percentage = (Figure for Current Year / Figure for Base Year) × 100

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: The following data relates to the Revenue from Operations of Z Ltd. for five years ending 31st March. Compute trend percentages taking 2019 as base year.

| Year | Revenue from Operations (₹) |
|------|----------------------------|
| 2019 | 12,00,000 |
| 2020 | 13,80,000 |
| 2021 | 11,40,000 |
| 2022 | 15,60,000 |
| 2023 | 16,80,000 |

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the base year figure. Base year = 2019, Base figure = ₹12,00,000. By definition, trend percentage for base year = 100.
- Step 2: Write the formula: Trend % = (Current Year Revenue / Base Year Revenue) × 100.
- Step 3: Compute for 2020: (13,80,000 / 12,00,000) × 100 = 1.15 × 100 = 115.
- Step 4: Compute for 2021: (11,40,000 / 12,00,000) × 100 = 0.95 × 100 = 95.
- Step 5: Compute for 2022: (15,60,000 / 12,00,000) × 100 = 1.30 × 100 = 130.
- Step 6: Compute for 2023: (16,80,000 / 12,00,000) × 100 = 1.40 × 100 = 140.
- Step 7: Tabulate results and interpret: Revenue dipped in 2021 (95) but recovered strongly, reaching 140 in 2023 — a 40% increase over base year.

REASONING: Trend analysis uses a fixed base to eliminate the effect of scale. By dividing each year by the same base (2019), we isolate the proportional change. The base year is set to 100, so any value above 100 indicates growth relative to base, below 100 indicates decline. This is different from year-on-year growth rates which use a moving base.

ANSWER: Trend Percentages: 2019=100, 2020=115, 2021=95, 2022=130, 2023=140.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: The trend percentages for 'Trade Receivables' of M Ltd. (base year 2018=100) are: 2018=100, 2019=120, 2020=132, 2021=145, 2022=152. Also, determine the year in which the absolute increase over the previous year was the highest.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Use the trend percentage formula in reverse: Base Year Figure = (Current Year Figure × 100) / Trend % of Current Year. Here, 2022 figure = ₹76,00,000, Trend % 2022 = 152. So Base Year (2018) Figure = (76,00,000 × 100) / 152 = ₹50,00,000.
- Step 2: Verify: 2018 base = ₹50,00,000 → Trend % = 100. Correct.
- Step 3: Compute actual figures for other years using base figure:
  2019: (120/100) × 50,00,000 = ₹60,00,000
  2020: (132/100) × 50,00,000 = ₹66,00,000
  2021: (145/100) × 50,00,000 = ₹72,50,000
  2022: (152/100) × 50,00,000 = ₹76,00,000 (given)
- Step 4: Calculate year-on-year absolute increases:
  2019 over 2018: 60,00,000 - 50,00,000 = ₹10,00,000
  2020 over 2019: 66,00,000 - 60,00,000 = ₹6,00,000
  2021 over 2020: 72,50,000 - 66,00,000 = ₹6,50,000
  2022 over 2021: 76,00,000 - 72,50,000 = ₹3,50,000
- Step 5: Highest absolute increase = ₹10,00,000 in 2019 (first year after base).

REASONING: This problem combines forward and backward use of the trend formula. It tests whether the student understands that trend percentages are derived from a single base figure, and that absolute increases are not the same as trend percentage increases. The highest trend percentage jump (2018→2019: +20 points) coincides with the highest absolute increase because the base is smallest.

ANSWER: 
Actual Trade Receivables: 2018=₹50,00,000; 2019=₹60,00,000; 2020=₹66,00,000; 2021=₹72,50,000; 2022=₹76,00,000.
Highest absolute increase: 2019 (₹10,00,000).

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: (Assertion-Reason) 
Assertion (A): Trend analysis using a fixed base year is superior to year-on-year percentage change analysis for long-term strategic planning.
Reason (R): Year-on-year percentage changes use a shifting base, making it difficult to compare the magnitude of change over a long period, whereas a fixed base provides a common reference point.

Choose the correct option:
(i) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A.
(ii) Both A and R are true, but R does not correctly explain A.
(iii) A is true but R is false.
(iv) A is false but R is true.

COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying analytical methods; critical evaluation of financial analysis techniques.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Evaluate Assertion: Is trend analysis (fixed base) superior for long-term planning? Yes, because it shows cumulative change from a starting point, enabling vision of overall trajectory.
- Step 2: Evaluate Reason: Does year-on-year use shifting base? Yes. Does that make long-term comparison difficult? Yes. Does fixed base provide common reference? Yes.
- Step 3: Check if R explains A: The reason directly explains why fixed base is better for long-term planning — because shifting base obscures cumulative perspective. So R correctly explains A.
- Step 4: Select option (i).

ANSWER: (i) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A. Trend analysis with a fixed base year allows stakeholders to see the total percentage change from a common starting point, which is essential for assessing long-term growth, stability, and structural shifts. Year-on-year changes only show incremental shifts and require chaining to see the long-term picture, which can compound rounding errors and hide the big picture.
