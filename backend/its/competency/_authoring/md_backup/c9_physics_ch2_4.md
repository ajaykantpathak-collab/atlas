## DEFINITION
Uniform motion is motion in which an object covers equal displacements in equal intervals of time, implying constant velocity (both magnitude and direction remain unchanged). Non-uniform motion is motion in which an object covers unequal displacements in equal intervals of time, or changes direction, resulting in a change in velocity (magnitude, direction, or both).

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can distinguish uniform from non-uniform motion using displacement-time data, velocity-time data, and graphical representations, and can explain why a body moving at constant speed in a circular path is in non-uniform motion.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY

## WALKTHROUGH
Observe a ceiling fan rotating at constant speed – the blades cover equal angles in equal time but their direction changes continuously. Now compare with a bicycle moving on a straight road at a steady 10 km/h. Ask: "In which case is the velocity constant?" Measure the bicycle's position every 2 seconds using a tape measure and stopwatch; record displacements. If displacements are equal, motion is uniform. Connect to prior concept: velocity (c9_physics_ch2_3) is displacement/time. If velocity is constant, motion is uniform; if velocity changes, motion is non-uniform.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
A car moves on a straight highway. Its odometer readings at 10:00 AM, 10:05 AM, 10:10 AM, and 10:15 AM are 1200 km, 1205 km, 1210 km, and 1215 km respectively. Is the car in uniform motion? State the condition for uniform motion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Yes, the car is in uniform motion because it covers equal displacements (5 km) in equal time intervals (5 minutes). The condition for uniform motion is constant velocity: equal displacements in equal intervals of time along a straight line. A wrong answer (e.g., saying speed is constant but not checking direction) reveals the student confuses uniform speed with uniform velocity, ignoring the straight-line requirement.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
A boy runs around a square track of side 50 m at a constant speed of 5 m/s. Is his motion uniform or non-uniform? Explain why, referencing the definition of velocity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The motion is non-uniform. Although speed is constant (5 m/s), the direction changes at each corner of the square track. Velocity is a vector (magnitude + direction); since direction changes, velocity changes. Uniform motion requires constant velocity. A wrong answer (claiming uniform because speed is constant) reveals the misconception that uniform motion only requires constant speed, neglecting the vector nature of velocity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
An object moves along a straight line. Its displacements in successive 3-second intervals are: 6 m, 6 m, 6 m, 9 m, 6 m. Calculate the average velocity for the first 12 seconds and for the total 15 seconds. Is the motion uniform throughout? Justify.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
First 12 s: total displacement = 6+6+6+6 = 24 m (four intervals of 3 s each). Average velocity = 24 m / 12 s = 2 m/s. Total 15 s: total displacement = 24+9 = 33 m. Average velocity = 33 m / 15 s = 2.2 m/s. Motion is NOT uniform throughout because the displacement in the fourth interval (9 m) differs from the others (6 m), indicating changing velocity. A wrong calculation (e.g., averaging speeds) reveals inability to apply the definition of uniform motion to discrete data.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Two distance-time graphs are shown: Graph A is a straight line passing through origin with slope 4 m/s. Graph B is a curve that gets steeper with time. (a) Which graph represents uniform motion? (b) What does the slope of Graph A represent? (c) If Graph B represents a car accelerating from rest, what can you say about its velocity at different times?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
(a) Graph A represents uniform motion because a straight line on a distance-time graph indicates constant speed (equal distances in equal times). (b) The slope of Graph A represents the speed (magnitude of velocity) of the object, which is 4 m/s. (c) For Graph B, the slope increases with time, meaning the velocity increases with time; the car is accelerating. A wrong answer (e.g., saying Graph B is uniform because it's smooth) reveals inability to interpret graphical slope as velocity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims: "A satellite orbiting Earth at constant speed is in uniform motion because its speed doesn't change." Evaluate this statement and correct it if necessary.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The statement is incorrect. Uniform motion requires constant velocity (both magnitude and direction). A satellite in orbit continuously changes direction (towards Earth's center), so its velocity changes even though speed is constant. This is uniform circular motion, which is a type of non-uniform motion. A student who agrees reveals the misconception that constant speed alone defines uniform motion, ignoring the vector nature of velocity and the role of centripetal acceleration.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple experiment using a toy car, a measuring tape, and a stopwatch to determine whether the car moves with uniform or non-uniform motion on a tilted wooden plank. Describe the procedure, data to collect, and how you will conclude.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Procedure: Mark positions at 20 cm intervals along the plank. Release the car from rest at the top. Use the stopwatch to record the time when the car passes each mark. Calculate displacement per time interval (e.g., 0-20 cm, 20-40 cm). If displacements per equal time intervals are equal, motion is uniform; if they increase, motion is non-uniform (accelerated). A poor answer (e.g., only measuring total time) reveals inability to design an experiment that tests the definition of uniform motion through equal intervals.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think "uniform motion = constant speed" and classify uniform circular motion as uniform motion. They fail to recognize that velocity is a vector and any change in direction (even at constant speed) makes motion non-uniform. For example, they say a car turning a corner at constant 30 km/h is in uniform motion because the speedometer reading doesn't change.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A cyclist rides on a straight road. She passes a kilometer stone at 8:00 AM, the next at 8:05 AM, the next at 8:10 AM, and the next at 8:15 AM. Determine if her motion is uniform. If yes, calculate her velocity in m/s.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given quantities – positions at 1 km intervals (1000 m each), time intervals of 5 minutes (300 s each). Need to find: motion type and velocity.
- Step 2: Check condition for uniform motion – equal displacements in equal time intervals. Displacements: 1000 m, 1000 m, 1000 m. Time intervals: 300 s, 300 s, 300 s. All equal.
- Step 3: Since displacements and time intervals are equal, motion is uniform. Velocity = displacement / time interval = 1000 m / 300 s.
- Step 4: Compute: 1000/300 = 10/3 ≈ 3.33 m/s. Direction is along the road (positive direction).
- Step 5: Verify reasonableness: 3.33 m/s ≈ 12 km/h, a typical cycling speed. Answer with units.
REASONING: Uniform motion is defined by constant velocity, which means equal vector displacements in equal time intervals. The straight road ensures direction is constant. The calculation uses the definition of velocity as displacement per unit time.
ANSWER: Motion is uniform. Velocity = 3.33 m/s (or 10/3 m/s) along the road.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A car travels on a straight highway. Its odometer reads 5000 km at 9:00 AM, 5060 km at 9:30 AM, 5130 km at 10:00 AM, and 5210 km at 10:30 AM. Is the motion uniform? If not, during which interval is the speed highest?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Convert odometer readings to displacements in each 30-minute (1800 s) interval. Interval 1: 5060-5000 = 60 km = 60,000 m. Interval 2: 5130-5060 = 70 km = 70,000 m. Interval 3: 5210-5130 = 80 km = 80,000 m.
- Step 2: Check uniformity – displacements are 60 km, 70 km, 80 km in equal time intervals (30 min each). They are not equal.
- Step 3: Motion is non-uniform because displacements in equal time intervals are unequal (velocity changes).
- Step 4: Calculate speed in each interval to find highest: Speed = distance/time. Interval 1: 60 km / 0.5 h = 120 km/h. Interval 2: 70 km / 0.5 h = 140 km/h. Interval 3: 80 km / 0.5 h = 160 km/h.
- Step 5: Highest speed is in the third interval (10:00-10:30 AM) at 160 km/h. Verify: increasing displacements indicate acceleration.
REASONING: Non-uniform motion is detected by unequal displacements in equal time intervals. Speed in each interval is the magnitude of average velocity for that interval. The trend shows the car is accelerating.
ANSWER: Motion is non-uniform. Highest speed is 160 km/h during 10:00-10:30 AM.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A boy throws a ball vertically upward with an initial velocity of 20 m/s. The ball rises, stops momentarily, and falls back. Consider the upward journey only. Is the motion uniform or non-uniform? Explain using velocity changes. If g = 10 m/s², find the time to reach the highest point and the displacement in the first second and the second second. Compare displacements to confirm non-uniform motion.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify forces – gravity acts downward, so velocity decreases continuously. Motion is non-uniform because velocity changes (magnitude decreases, direction same until top).
- Step 2: Use v = u - gt for upward journey. At highest point, v = 0. 0 = 20 - 10t → t = 2 s to reach top.
- Step 3: Displacement in first second (0 to 1 s): s₁ = ut - ½gt² = 20(1) - 5(1)² = 15 m.
- Step 4: Displacement in second second (1 to 2 s): total displacement in 2 s = 20(2) - 5(4) = 20 m. Displacement in second second = 20 - 15 = 5 m.
- Step 5: Compare: 15 m in first second, 5 m in second second. Unequal displacements in equal time intervals → non-uniform motion confirmed.
- Step 6: Connect to prior concept: acceleration (c9_physics_ch2_5) is the rate of change of velocity; here a = -g = -10 m/s² causes non-uniform motion.
REASONING: This example combines uniform/non-uniform classification with kinematic equations (preview of c9_physics_ch2_8). The decreasing displacements per second directly demonstrate non-uniform motion due to constant acceleration.
ANSWER: Motion is non-uniform. Time to highest point = 2 s. Displacement in 1st second = 15 m, in 2nd second = 5 m. Unequal displacements confirm non-uniform motion.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A train moves on a straight track. It passes a pole in 10 seconds and a 200 m long platform in 30 seconds. (a) Is the train's motion uniform? Justify. (b) Calculate the length and speed of the train. (c) If the train then enters a curved section at the same speed, does its motion remain uniform? Explain.
COMPETENCY: Interpreting physical situations using formulas and vector concepts
STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand the situation – train passing pole (length of train) and platform (train length + platform length). Time intervals given.
- Step 2: Assume uniform motion on straight track (constant velocity). Let train length = L, speed = v. Passing pole: L = v × 10. Passing platform: L + 200 = v × 30.
- Step 3: Solve: v × 10 + 200 = v × 30 → 200 = 20v → v = 10 m/s. Then L = 10 × 10 = 100 m.
- Step 4: On curved section, speed is same (10 m/s) but direction changes → velocity changes → motion becomes non-uniform.
- Step 5: Conclude: (a) Yes, uniform on straight track (constant v). (b) Length = 100 m, speed = 10 m/s (36 km/h). (c) No, motion becomes non-uniform due to direction change.
ANSWER: (a) Yes, uniform on straight track because it covers equal lengths in equal times (implied by constant speed). (b) Train length = 100 m, speed = 10 m/s. (c) On curved section, motion is non-uniform because velocity direction changes even though speed is constant.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two athletes, A and B, run a 100 m race. Athlete A runs with uniform velocity of 8 m/s. Athlete B starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s². (a) Who wins the race? (b) At what time does Athlete B overtake Athlete A? (c) Sketch the displacement-time graphs for both on the same axes and label the overtaking point. (d) Explain why Athlete B's motion is non-uniform while Athlete A's is uniform.
COMPETENCY: Predicting outcomes from changing variables and graphical reasoning
STEPS:
- Step 1: Athlete A: uniform motion, s_A = 8t. Time to finish 100 m: t_A = 100/8 = 12.5 s.
- Step 2: Athlete B: non-uniform (accelerated), s_B = ½ × 2 × t² = t². Time to finish: t_B = √100 = 10 s.
- Step 3: Compare: t_B (10 s) < t_A (12.5 s) → Athlete B wins.
- Step 4: Overtaking: when s_A = s_B → 8t = t² → t = 8 s (excluding t=0). At t=8 s, displacement = 64 m.
- Step 5: Graph: Athlete A – straight line through origin slope 8. Athlete B – parabola s = t². They intersect at (8, 64). Athlete B's curve gets steeper (increasing slope = increasing velocity).
- Step 6: Explanation: Athlete A has constant velocity (uniform). Athlete B has velocity v = 2t, which increases with time (non-uniform).
ANSWER: (a) Athlete B wins (finishes in 10 s vs 12.5 s). (b) Overtakes at t = 8 s, at 64 m mark. (c) Graph: straight line for A, upward-curving parabola for B, intersecting at (8 s, 64 m). (d) Athlete A's velocity is constant (8 m/s); Athlete B's velocity increases linearly (v=2t), so displacement per second increases.
DIFFICULTY: hard
