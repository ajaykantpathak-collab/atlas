=== c10_math_cbse_02_009.md:WORKED_EXAMPLE_1 ENV
**PROBLEM:** A cubic polynomial \(p(x) = 2x^3 - 5x^2 - 4x + 3\) models the volume (in cubic metres) of a storage box where \(x\) is the side length of the square base. It is known that \(x = 3\) metres is one possible dimension. Find all possible dimensions (zeros) of the box.  
**STEPS:**  
- Step 1: Identify the known zero \(\alpha = 3\). Form the divisor \(g(x) = x - 3\). Write \(p(x)\) in standard form with all powers: \(2x^3 - 5x^2 - 4x + 3\).  
- Step 2: Perform polynomial long division of \(p(x)\) by \(x-3\).  
  \[
  \begin{array}{r|l}
  x-3 & 2x^3 - 5x^2 - 4x + 3 \\
  \hline
  & 2x^2 + x - 1 \\
  & 2x^3 - 6x^2 \\
  \hline
  & x^2 - 4x \\
  & x^2 - 3x \\
  \hline
  & -x + 3 \\
  & -x + 3 \\
  \hline
  & 0
  \end{array}
  \]  
  Quotient \(q(x) = 2x^2 + x - 1\).  
- Step 3: Factorise the quadratic quotient: \(2x^2 + x - 1 = 2x^2 + 2x - x - 1 = 2x(x+1) -1(x+1) = (2x-1)(x+1)\).  
- Step 4: Find zeros of \(q(x)\): \(2x-1=0 \Rightarrow x = \frac{1}{2}\); \(x+1=0 \Rightarrow x = -1\).  
- Step 5: Combine with the known zero: zeros are \(3, \frac{1}{2}, -1\). Since side length cannot be negative, the valid dimensions are \(x = 3\) m or \(x = \frac{1}{2}\) m.  
**REASONING:** The Division Algorithm reduces the cubic to a quadratic, which is factorised using the splitting-the-middle-term method (Class 9 skill). The physical context (side length) requires rejecting negative zeros.  
**ANSWER:** Valid dimensions: \(3\) m and \(0.5\) m (i.e., \(\frac{1}{2}\) m).

=== c10_math_cbse_02_009.md:WORKED_EXAMPLE_2 ENV
**PROBLEM:** Find all zeros of \(p(x) = x^4 - 5x^3 + 2x^2 + 20x - 24\) given that two of its zeros are \(2\) and \(-2\).  
**STEPS:**  
- Step 1: Known zeros: \(2\) and \(-2\). Their corresponding factors are \((x-2)\) and \((x+2)\). Product: \((x-2)(x+2) = x^2 - 4\). We can divide \(p(x)\) by \(x^2 - 4\) directly (since both zeros are known) or divide sequentially. Sequential is safer: first divide by \(x-2\).  
- Step 2: Divide \(p(x)\) by \(x-2\).  
  \[
  \begin{array}{r|l}
  x-2 & x^4 - 5x^3 + 2x^2 + 20x - 24 \\
  \hline
  & x^3 - 3x^2 - 4x + 12 \\
  & x^4 - 2x^3 \\
  \hline
  & -3x^3 + 2x^2 \\
  & -3x^3 + 6x^2 \\
  \hline
  & -4x^2 + 20x \\
  & -4x^2 + 8x \\
  \hline
  & 12x - 24 \\
  & 12x - 24 \\
  \hline
  & 0
  \end{array}
  \]  
  Quotient \(q_1(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 4x + 12\).  
- Step 3: Divide \(q_1(x)\) by the second factor \(x+2\) (since zero \(-2 \Rightarrow x - (-2) = x+2\)).  
  \[
  \begin{array}{r|l}
  x+2 & x^3 - 3x^2 - 4x + 12 \\
  \hline
  & x^2 - 5x + 6 \\
  & x^3 + 2x^2 \\
  \hline
  & -5x^2 - 4x \\
  & -5x^2 - 10x \\
  \hline
  & 6x + 12 \\
  & 6x + 12 \\
  \hline
  & 0
  \end{array}
  \]  
  Quotient \(q_2(x) = x^2 - 5x + 6\).  
- Step 4: Factorise \(q_2(x)\): \(x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x-2)(x-3)\).  
- Step 5: All zeros: from first division: \(2\); from second division: \(-2\); from quadratic: \(2, 3\). Combined set: \(2, -2, 2, 3\). So zeros are \(2\) (repeated), \(-2\), \(3\).  
**REASONING:** When multiple zeros are given, we can divide sequentially by each linear factor. The repeated zero \(2\) appears because \((x-2)\) is a factor twice (once given, once from the final quadratic). This shows how the algorithm handles multiplicity.  
**ANSWER:** Zeros: \(2, 2, -2, 3\).

=== c10_math_cbse_02_009.md:WORKED_EXAMPLE_3 ENV
**PROBLEM:** The polynomial \(p(x) = 3x^3 - 10x^2 + 9x - 2\) represents the profit (in thousands of rupees) from selling \(x\) hundred units of a product. The company knows that selling 100 units (\(x=1\)) gives zero profit. Find the other break-even points (zeros).  
**STEPS:**  
- Step 1: Known zero \(\alpha = 1\) → divisor \(x-1\). Write \(p(x) = 3x^3 - 10x^2 + 9x - 2\).  
- Step 2: Divide by \(x-1\).  
  \[
  \begin{array}{r|l}
  x-1 & 3x^3 - 10x^2 + 9x - 2 \\
  \hline
  & 3x^2 - 7x + 2 \\
  & 3x^3 - 3x^2 \\
  \hline
  & -7x^2 + 9x \\
  & -7x^2 + 7x \\
  \hline
  & 2x - 2 \\
  & 2x - 2 \\
  \hline
  & 0
  \end{array}
  \]  
  Quotient \(q(x) = 3x^2 - 7x + 2\).  
- Step 3: Factorise \(3x^2 - 7x + 2\). Split middle term: product \(3 \times 2 = 6\), sum \(-7\) → \(-6x - x\).  
  \(3x^2 - 6x - x + 2 = 3x(x-2) -1(x-2) = (3x-1)(x-2)\).  
- Step 4: Zeros of \(q(x)\): \(3x-1=0 \Rightarrow x = \frac{1}{3}\); \(x-2=0 \Rightarrow x = 2\).  
- Step 5: All zeros: \(1, \frac{1}{3}, 2\). Interpret: break-even at 100 units (\(x=1\)), 33.33 units (\(x=\frac{1}{3}\)), and 200 units (\(x=2\)). Since units sold must be positive, all are valid.  
- Step 6: Verify using sum of zeros: For cubic \(ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\), sum of zeros \(= -b/a = 10/3\). Our zeros: \(1 + \frac{1}{3} + 2 = \frac{10}{3}\). ✓  
**REASONING:** This example combines division, factorisation of a quadratic with leading coefficient ≠ 1, and verification using the zero-coefficient relationship (a board-exam expectation). The real-world context (profit) requires interpreting fractional zeros as valid quantities (hundreds of units).  
**ANSWER:** Break-even points at \(x = \frac{1}{3}, 1, 2\) hundred units (i.e., 33.33, 100, 200 units).
