## ELI5
Rahul buys 2 packets of chips and a cold drink; Priya buys 3 packets of biscuits and a juice. Who spent more? Write each snack run as its own expression, evaluate both fully, and only then let the winner sign - less than, greater than, or equal - stand between them. Comparing before simplifying is like judging a race at the starting line: brackets and multiplication still have distance to run, and they change final values. Finish both sums, then compare the totals; the sign goes to the bigger number.

## DEFINITION
Comparing arithmetic expressions involves evaluating two separate expressions using the order of operations and determining their relative size using the relational symbols less than (<), greater than (>), or equal to (=).
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can evaluate two distinct arithmetic expressions containing multiple operations and brackets, and correctly place <, >, or = between them with full justification.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers comparing arithmetic expressions using integers, fractions, and decimals with standard operations and parentheses. Does not cover algebraic inequalities with variables or solving for unknown values.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at a school canteen buying snacks. Rahul buys 2 packets of chips for ₹15 each and a cold drink for ₹20, while Priya buys 3 packets of biscuits for ₹10 each and a juice for ₹25. To find out who spent more money without just guessing, we need to write down both purchases as arithmetic expressions: Rahul's expression is $(2 \times 15) + 20$ and Priya's expression is $(3 \times 10) + 25$. We evaluate each expression completely using the correct order of operations, just like we learned in evaluating arithmetic expressions. Finally, we compare the two final numbers and place the correct sign (<, >, or =) between the original expressions. The key insight is that expressions can look completely different before calculation, but once simplified, their relationship becomes clear.

**Takeaway:** a comparison sign is earned: evaluate both expressions completely, and only then let the totals argue. **In the exam:** for compare questions, show both reductions line by line, then place the sign with a one-line reason. **Check yourself:** evaluate the two snack purchases and justify the chosen sign in one clear sentence. **Why students miss it:** they compare first digits or bracketless shapes, judging the race before it has been run.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What symbol (<, >, or =) is used to show that two expressions evaluate to the exact same numerical value?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The equals sign (=) is used when two expressions have the same value. A wrong answer like using (<) or (>) reveals a failure to recognize that these symbols indicate inequality, whereas an equals sign represents mathematical equivalence between two quantities.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why we must simplify expressions fully before attempting to place a comparison sign between them, rather than just comparing the first numbers in each expression.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
We must simplify fully because operations and grouping symbols change the final value of an entire expression, meaning the first numbers alone do not represent the total quantity. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks expression comparison is just comparing left-to-right numbers without respecting the order of operations or overall value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Compare the following two expressions by evaluating them and inserting the correct sign (<, >, or =): $15 + 4 \times 3$ and $(15 + 4) \times 3$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
First, evaluate the left expression: $15 + (4 \times 3) = 15 + 12 = 27$. Second, evaluate the right expression: $(15 + 4) \times 3 = 19 \times 3 = 57$. Third, compare the results: $27 < 57$. Therefore, $15 + 4 \times 3 < (15 + 4) \times 3$. A wrong answer often reveals that the student ignored the brackets on the right or failed to apply the multiplication precedence rule on the left.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the expressions $50 - (12 + 8)$ and $50 - 12 + 8$. Without calculating the full value, can you determine which one is larger, or are they equal? Explain your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The first expression $50 - (12 + 8)$ equals $50 - 20 = 30$. The second expression $50 - 12 + 8$ equals $38 + 8 = 46$. Therefore, $50 - (12 + 8) < 50 - 12 + 8$. A wrong answer shows confusion over how brackets distribute subtraction and alter the grouping of operations.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that $24 \div 4 \times 2$ is equal to $24 \div (4 \times 2)$ because the numbers and operations are identical. Evaluate both expressions to determine if the student is correct, and identify their exact error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The student is incorrect. Left expression: $24 \div 4 \times 2 = 6 \times 2 = 12$. Right expression: $24 \div (4 \times 2) = 24 \div 8 = 3$. Since $12 \neq 3$, they are not equal. The student's error was assuming brackets do not change the order of division and multiplication operations evaluated from left to right.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Construct two different arithmetic expressions using the numbers 5, 4, and 2, along with addition and multiplication, such that one expression is strictly greater than the other. Show their evaluation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An acceptable response provides two expressions like $5 \times 4 + 2$ and $5 + 4 \times 2$. Evaluation: $5 \times 4 + 2 = 20 + 2 = 22$, and $5 + 4 \times 2 = 5 + 8 = 13$, so $22 > 13$. A poor answer uses incorrect order of operations or fails to use the required numbers.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often evaluate expressions strictly from left to right across the entire string without respecting brackets or the order of operations, leading to incorrect comparisons. For example, when comparing $10 + 2 \times 5$ and $(10 + 2) \times 5$, a student might incorrectly calculate the left side as $(10 + 2) \times 5 = 60$ by rushing or adding first, missing that multiplication must occur before addition.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to always use parentheses or underline operations that must be done first according to BODMAS/PEMDAS. Have them write out the intermediate step for each expression on separate lines before looking at the comparison sign. Say: "Evaluate the left side completely into a single number, then evaluate the right side completely into a single number, and only then compare the two final numbers."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Amit has ₹100. He compares two possible book purchases to see which costs less: Expression A is $4 \times 15 + 3 \times 10$ (4 notebooks at ₹15 each and 3 pens at ₹10 each) and Expression B is $2 \times 25 + 4 \times 12$ (2 storybooks at ₹25 each and 4 sketch pens at ₹12 each). Determine which expression is smaller by inserting <, >, or =.
STEPS:
- step 1: Write down Expression A ($4 \times 15 + 3 \times 10$) and Expression B ($2 \times 25 + 4 \times 12$).
- step 2: Evaluate Expression A by performing multiplications first: $4 \times 15 = 60$ and $3 \times 10 = 30$.
- step 3: Complete Expression A by adding the products: $60 + 30 = 90$.
- step 4: Evaluate Expression B by performing multiplications first: $2 \times 25 = 50$ and $4 \times 12 = 48$.
- step 5: Complete Expression B by adding the products: $50 + 48 = 98$.
- step 6: Compare the final values: $90 < 98$, so Expression A < Expression B.
REASONING: We evaluate each independent arithmetic expression completely following the order of operations before applying the comparison operator. This ensures we are comparing the final total costs rather than intermediate values.
ANSWER: $4 \times 15 + 3 \times 10 < 2 \times 25 + 4 \times 12$ (90 < 98)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Compare the expressions $(50 - 15) \div 5$ and $50 - (15 \div 5)$ using <, >, or =.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the two expressions to compare: $(50 - 15) \div 5$ and $50 - (15 \div 5)$.
- step 2: For the left expression, evaluate inside the brackets first: $50 - 15 = 35$.
- step 3: Complete the left expression division: $35 \div 5 = 7$.
- step 4: For the right expression, evaluate inside the brackets first: $15 \div 5 = 3$.
- step 5: Complete the right expression subtraction: $50 - 3 = 47$.
- step 6: Compare the final results: $7 < 47$.
REASONING: The placement of brackets completely changes the order of operations and therefore the final value, proving that grouping symbols matter significantly when comparing expressions.
ANSWER: $(50 - 15) \div 5 < 50 - (15 \div 5)$ (7 < 47)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A school science club compares two experiment setups. Setup 1 score is given by $3 \times [12 + (8 - 3)]$, and Setup 2 score is given by $2 \times [20 - (5 + 2)]$. Which setup has a higher score?
STEPS:
- step 1: Write down Setup 1 expression: $3 \times [12 + (8 - 3)]$ and Setup 2 expression: $2 \times [20 - (5 + 2)]$.
- step 2: Simplify the innermost brackets for Setup 1: $(8 - 3) = 5$, giving $3 \times [12 + 5]$.
- step 3: Simplify the outer brackets for Setup 1: $[12 + 5] = 17$, then multiply: $3 \times 17 = 51$.
- step 4: Simplify the innermost brackets for Setup 2: $(5 + 2) = 7$, giving $2 \times [20 - 7]$.
- step 5: Simplify the outer brackets for Setup 2: $[20 - 7] = 13$, then multiply: $2 \times 13 = 26$.
- step 6: Compare the final values: $51 > 26$, so Setup 1 > Setup 2.
REASONING: Nested grouping symbols require working from the inside out. Once both complex expressions are fully reduced to single integers, the relational sign is easily determined.
ANOVA: Setup 1 > Setup 2 (51 > 26)
ANSWER: Setup 1 > Setup 2 (51 > 26)
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Neha states that without calculating the values of $56 \times 12$ and $56 \times 15$, she immediately knows that $56 \times 12 < 56 \times 15$. Explain the mathematical reasoning behind her claim and verify it by evaluating both expressions.
COMPETENCY: Relational reasoning and proportional comparison
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the structure of both expressions to identify common factors and varying multipliers.
- step 2: Recognize that both expressions share the same positive multiplier (56).
- step 3: Apply the property that multiplying a positive number by a larger number yields a greater product ($12 < 15$, so $56 \times 12 < 56 \times 15$).
- step 4: Verify by calculation: $56 \times 12 = 672$ and $56 \times 15 = 840$, confirming $672 < 840$.
ANSWER: Neha's reasoning is correct because when multiplying a positive number by two different values, the expression with the larger multiplier results in a greater product. Calculation confirms $672 < 840$.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students are comparing the expressions $100 - (20 + 30)$ and $100 - 20 + 30$. Student A says they are equal because the numbers and signs are the same. Student B says they are different. Determine who is correct, explain the exact effect of the brackets in the first expression, and find the difference between the two evaluated values.
COMPETENCY: Error identification, bracket analysis, and comparative evaluation
STEPS:
- step 1: Evaluate Student A's claim by checking the expressions.
- step 2: Calculate the first expression: $100 - (20 + 30) = 100 - 50 = 50$.
- step 3: Calculate the second expression: $100 - 20 + 30 = 80 + 30 = 110$.
- step 4: Compare the results ($50 \neq 110$) to prove Student B is correct.
- step 5: Calculate the difference between the values: $110 - 50 = 60$.
ANSWER: Student B is correct. The brackets in the first expression group $(20 + 30)$ together to be subtracted as a single sum of 50, whereas the second expression subtracts 20 and then adds 30 sequentially. The difference between their values is $110 - 50 = 60$.
DIFFICULTY: hard
