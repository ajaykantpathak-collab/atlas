## ELI5
Speed answers one question per journey: how much road per beat of clock. The race to the playground settles it without argument - same start, same road, and the earlier arrival simply divided better. It is a rate, not a trophy: constant speed still moves, and distance alone is not speed until the clock signs the receipt.

## DEFINITION
Speed is a measure of how quickly an object covers a specific distance over a period of time. It is calculated by dividing the total distance travelled by the time taken to complete that journey.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers calculating speed from measured distance and time and the concept of speed as a rate. It excludes radar physics, GPS navigation, and formal error analysis of measurement.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you and your friend decide to race from the school gate to the playground. If you both start at the same time, how do you decide who is faster? You would look at who reached the end first. Speed is just a way of putting a number to that "fastness." To measure it, you need two things: a measuring tape to find the distance of the path and a stopwatch to record exactly how many seconds it took. By dividing the distance by the time, you get a number that tells you how many metres you covered in every single second. This gives us the key insight: speed is essentially "distance per unit of time."

**Takeaway:** speed is road over clock - a rate that needs both numbers, and constant speed never means stopped. **In the exam:** in speed questions, demand both the road and the clock before judging, then divide in that order. **Check yourself:** judge the walkthrough's playground race - the same start and road leave the clock as the only witness. **Why students miss it:** students accept distance as speed - the unsigned receipt is a no to that; no clock, no rate. Rehearse the exam move once more - road, clock, divide - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that "speed" and "distance" are the same thing. They might see a car that has travelled a very long way and assume it must be travelling at a high speed, forgetting that it might have taken a very long time to get there.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A delivery boy rides his bicycle 100 metres from the shop to a house in 20 seconds. What is his speed?
STEPS:
- identify given values: distance = 100 m, time = 20 s.
- identify formula: Speed = Distance / Time.
- substitute: Speed = 100 / 20.
- compute: 100 divided by 20 equals 5.
- verify: The units are metres divided by seconds (m/s).
REASONING: By dividing the total distance by the total time, we find out exactly how many metres were covered in each individual second, which is the definition of speed.
ANSWER: 5 m/s
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An athlete runs a 400-metre track race in 80 seconds. What is the athlete's speed?
STEPS:
- identify given values: distance = 400 m, time = 80 s.
- identify formula: Speed = Distance / Time.
- substitute: Speed = 400 / 80.
- compute: 400 / 80 = 5.
- verify: The athlete covers 5 metres every second.
REASONING: The calculation breaks down the total effort into a uniform rate, allowing us to compare this athlete's performance to others regardless of the total track length.
ANSWER: 5 m/s
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A toy car travels 50 metres at a constant speed in 10 seconds. If the car continued at this same speed, how far would it travel in 25 seconds?
STEPS:
- calculate current speed: Speed = 50 m / 10 s = 5 m/s.
- use formula for new distance: Distance = Speed × Time.
- substitute: Distance = 5 m/s × 25 s.
- compute: 5 × 25 = 125.
- interpret: The car covers 125 metres.
REASONING: First, we find the rate (speed), then we apply that rate to a longer time period to predict future distance, showing the relationship between these three variables.
ANSWER: 125 m
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You and your friend are both running. You cover 50 metres in 10 seconds. Your friend covers 60 metres in 12 seconds. Who is faster, or are you both at the same speed? Explain your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- calculate your speed: 50/10 = 5 m/s.
- calculate friend's speed: 60/12 = 5 m/s.
- compare results: both are 5 m/s.
- justify: Since the speeds are equal, neither is faster.
ANSWER: You are both moving at the same speed of 5 m/s. Even though your friend covered more total distance, they also took more time, resulting in the same rate of motion.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A bus travels 100 km in 2 hours. A car travels 150 km in 3 hours. Using the speed formula, determine which vehicle is travelling faster and show your work.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- calculate bus speed: 100 km / 2 h = 50 km/h.
- calculate car speed: 150 km / 3 h = 50 km/h.
- compare: 50 km/h = 50 km/h.
- interpret: Both vehicles have the same speed.
ANSWER: Both vehicles are moving at the same speed of 50 km/h. By converting the total journey into a "per hour" rate, we see that the larger distance of the car is perfectly balanced by the extra time taken.
DIFFICULTY: hard
