## ELI5
Equivalent fractions are the same slice wearing different uniforms. Break one chocolate bar into halves, or into quarters and hand over two - your friend chews the identical amount either way. The renaming trick is mechanical: multiply or divide top and bottom by the same non-zero number, and the value never blinks. Adding the same number to both, though, reshapes the slice - that move forges a new fraction, not a synonym.

## DEFINITION
Equivalent fractions are different-looking fractions that represent the same physical amount or value. They occur when the same part of a whole is divided into more or fewer equal pieces.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you have a chocolate bar and break it into two halves, giving one to a friend. Now, imagine you break that same bar into four pieces and give your friend two of them—does your friend get the same amount of chocolate? To find equivalent fractions, we look for this "same-value" relationship by either multiplying or dividing both the top (numerator) and bottom (denominator) numbers by the same value. If you multiply 1/2 by 2/2, you get 2/4; if you divide 6/8 by 2/2, you get 3/4. The key insight is that as long as you change both the numerator and the denominator by the same scale, the total "share" remains exactly the same.

**Takeaway:** equivalent fractions rename the same amount by multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number. **In the exam:** in equivalence questions, show the single matching operation on both parts and confirm the physical amount is unchanged. **Check yourself:** re-break the walkthrough's chocolate bar into finer pieces and check the shared portion stays the same. **Why students miss it:** students add the same number top and bottom - the walkthrough's re-cut bar is a no to that; renaming scales the pieces, it does not sweeten both counts. Rehearse the exam move once more - scale both, same amount - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to find equivalent fractions by adding the same number to both the numerator and denominator (e.g., changing 1/2 to 2/3). This is wrong because fractions represent ratios, and addition does not preserve the proportion of the parts to the whole.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student adds instead of multiplies, show them a visual model. Draw a square divided into 2 parts (1/2 shaded). Then, draw the same square divided into 4 parts. Show that to shade the same area, you must shade 2 of the 4 parts. Point out that 1x2 = 2 and 2x2 = 4. Explicitly tell the student: "Adding just adds 'size' to the numbers, but multiplying keeps the 'share' of the whole the same."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Meena has 2/3 of a litre of milk. She wants to write this in terms of "sixths" so she can add it to another measurement. What is the equivalent fraction with 6 as the denominator?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify current fraction (2/3) and target denominator (6).
- step 2: Find the multiplier: 3 times what equals 6? (It is 2).
- step 3: Apply the rule: multiply both numerator and denominator by 2.
- step 4: Calculate: (2x2) / (3x2) = 4/6.
REASONING: Multiplying by 2/2 is the same as multiplying by 1, which keeps the total value of the milk volume identical while changing the "label" of the pieces.
ANSWER: 4/6 litre
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper divides 12 kg of rice into 16 equal bags. He says this is the same as 3 kg divided into 4 equal bags. Is he correct?
STEPS:
- step 1: Write the fractions as 12/16 and 3/4.
- step 2: Check if 12/16 can be reduced to 3/4.
- step 3: Identify a common divisor for 12 and 16, which is 4.
- step 4: Divide: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 16 ÷ 4 = 4.
- step 5: Result is 3/4.
REASONING: Since reducing 12/16 results in 3/4, the proportions are identical, meaning each bag in both scenarios represents the same weight.
ANSWER: Yes, he is correct.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Rani has 4/10 of a metre of ribbon. She wants to simplify this to the smallest possible numbers. What is the simplest form?
STEPS:
- step 1: List factors of 4 (1, 2, 4) and 10 (1, 2, 5, 10).
- step 2: Identify the greatest common factor (2).
- step 3: Divide numerator by 2: 4 ÷ 2 = 2.
- step 4: Divide denominator by 2: 10 ÷ 2 = 5.
- step 5: Compare 2 and 5 to ensure no other common factors exist.
REASONING: Dividing by the greatest common factor ensures the fraction is in its simplest form, where the parts are as small as possible while keeping the ratio accurate.
ANSWER: 2/5 metre
