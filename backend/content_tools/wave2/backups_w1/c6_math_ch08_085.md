## ELI5
Perimeter is the fence-length of a shape: walk its boundary once, heel to toe, and the distance covered is the answer. Lace around a notebook, fencing around a field, trim around a photo - all boundary journeys. Every side counts, including the ones at the back, and a square's equal sides simply let the walk become a multiplication.

## DEFINITION
Perimeter is the total length of the boundary surrounding a closed 2D shape. It represents the distance you would travel if you walked exactly once along the entire edge of a figure.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to calculate the perimeter of squares, rectangles, and triangles by summing side lengths and be able to identify missing sides when given the total perimeter.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the perimeter of rectangles, squares, triangles, and simple polygons. It excludes area, circle circumference, 3D surface area, and complex irregular curves.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are asked to put a decorative lace around your rectangular notebook cover. How would you figure out how much lace to buy? You would start at one corner and measure along every edge until you return to the start. By adding these four lengths together, you find the "total distance around," which is the perimeter. This works for any closed shape made of straight lines: simply add up the length of every side.

**Takeaway:** perimeter is the total boundary length of a closed shape - every side counted once around the walk. **In the exam:** in perimeter questions, list every side before adding; for equal-sided shapes, multiply instead of repeating. **Check yourself:** lace the walkthrough's notebook - the tape must travel the whole rim, not two edges. **Why students miss it:** students stop after two sides - the notebook's rim is a no to that; the walk covers every edge once. ## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often add only two sides of a rectangle (length + width) and stop, forgetting that a rectangle has four sides (two lengths and two widths). They treat the perimeter as the sum of just the dimensions given in the problem statement rather than the full boundary.
## ON_FAILURE

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A rectangular park is 150 m long and 100 m wide. If a man walks around it twice, what is the total distance he covers?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the length (150 m) and width (100 m).
- step 2: Calculate the perimeter of one round using P = 2(l + w).
- step 3: P = 2(150 + 100) = 2(250) = 500 m.
- step 4: Since he walks around twice, multiply the perimeter by 2.
- step 5: Total distance = 500 m × 2 = 1000 m.
REASONING: The perimeter represents the boundary of one round. Multiplying by 2 accounts for the two laps he takes around the park.
ANSWER: 1000 m
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A square photo frame has a side of 25 cm. If you want to put a decorative border around it, how many centimetres of border are needed?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the shape as a square and side length as 25 cm.
- step 2: Use the formula for the perimeter of a square, P = 4s.
- step 3: Substitute the value: P = 4 × 25 cm.
- step 4: Calculate the total: 100 cm.
- step 5: Verify by adding 25 + 25 + 25 + 25 = 100.
REASONING: A square has four identical sides. Summing them is equivalent to multiplying the side length by four.
ANSWER: 100 cm
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A triangular plot of land has a perimeter of 120 m. Two sides are 40 m and 35 m long. What is the length of the third side, and how much will it cost to fence the entire plot if one metre of fencing costs ₹50?
STEPS:
- step 1: Use the perimeter formula P = a + b + c.
- step 2: Find the third side (c) by subtracting the known sides from the total: 120 - (40 + 35).
- step 3: 120 - 75 = 45 m.
- step 4: Calculate the cost by multiplying the total perimeter by the rate: 120 m × ₹50/m.
- step 5: 120 × 50 = ₹6000.
REASONING: The third side must complete the total boundary length of 120m. The total cost is determined by the total length of fencing required.
ANSWER: 45 m, ₹6000
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have 40 cm of wire. You want to make a rectangle with it. If the length must be 12 cm, what will the width be? Show your working.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions.
STEPS:
- step 1: Set P = 40, l = 12.
- step 2: Use 40 = 2(12 + w).
- step 3: 20 = 12 + w (dividing both sides by 2).
- step 4: w = 20 - 12 = 8 cm.
ANSWER: The width must be 8 cm. This is found by realizing that half the perimeter (20 cm) must equal the sum of one length and one width.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students, A and B, have different shapes made of 12 equal-length sticks. Student A makes a square. Student B makes a rectangle that is not a square. Do they have the same perimeter? Explain.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- step 1: Recognize that perimeter is the sum of the boundary lengths.
- step 2: Note that both shapes use the same number of identical sticks (12).
- step 3: Conclude that the total length of the boundary for both is 12 × (length of one stick).
- step 4: Explain that because the number of segments is the same, the total perimeter is identical regardless of the shape.
ANSWER: Yes, both have the same perimeter because they both use exactly 12 segments of the same length to enclose their respective shapes.
DIFFICULTY: hard
