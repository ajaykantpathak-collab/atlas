## ELI5
Borrow 5 rupees a day for 4 days and the debt arithmetic is a positive times a negative: multiply the sizes, attach one minus, and the four-day hole is plainly visible. Unlike signs always breed a negative product, whichever side the minus rides on - the commutative habit survives sign changes. Repeated addition tells the story: a negative added to itself again and again marches the balance down the line. Debts, temperature drops and elevator descents are all this one rule in different uniforms.

## DEFINITION
The product of a positive integer and a negative integer is always a negative integer, found by multiplying their absolute values and attaching a negative sign. For any two positive whole numbers $a$ and $b$, $(-a) \times b = -(a \times b)$ and $a \times (-b) = -(a \times b)$.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you borrow 5 rupees from your friend every day for 4 days in a row, meaning your account change each day is $-5$ rupees. To find your total balance change after 4 days, you need to calculate $4 \times (-5)$, which means adding $-5$ four times: $(-5) + (-5) + (-5) + (-5) = -20$. To begin solving these without writing out long addition chains, we write down the numbers and multiply their absolute values first, just like whole numbers. Then, we apply the core rule: multiplying numbers with unlike signs always results in a negative product. Connecting this back to our number line and whole number multiplication from Class 6, we see that scaling in the negative direction flips the sign of the product. The key insight is that a positive multiplier tells us how many groups we have, while the negative integer tells us the size and direction of each group.

**Takeaway:** positive times negative is negative: multiply magnitudes, attach one minus, either order. **In the exam:** use repeated addition to explain the sign, then compute the magnitude and answer with units. **Check yourself:** total the four days of borrowing both by addition and by multiplication and match them. **Why students miss it:** they let the larger factor's sign decide, though unlike signs always land negative.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often apply the rule "two negatives make a positive" indiscriminately to any multiplication problem containing a negative sign, resulting in a positive product when multiplying one positive and one negative integer. For example, when solving $(-6) \times 5$, they incorrectly write $+30$ instead of $-30$ because they see a negative sign and assume the rule overrides mixed-sign multiplication.
## ON_FAILURE
Guide the student by separating the problem into two distinct steps: sign determination and magnitude calculation. Say: "First, ignore the signs and multiply the numbers normally ($6 \times 5 = 30$). Second, look at the original signs: one is negative and one is positive, which are unlike signs. Unlike signs always fight and make a negative product, so put a negative sign in front to get $-30$." Reinforce this by showing that multiplying by a positive number means keeping the negative direction.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A merchant incurs a loss of 15 rupees on each defective toy he sells. If he sells 8 defective toys in a day, what is his total financial change for those toys?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given values: loss per toy is $-15$ rupees, and the number of toys sold is $8$ (positive integer).
- Step 2: Set up the multiplication expression representing the total change: $8 \times (-15)$.
- Step 3: Multiply the absolute values of the numbers: $8 \times 15 = 120$.
- Step 4: Apply the sign rule for multiplying a positive integer by a negative integer: positive $\times$ negative = negative, so $8 \times (-15) = -120$.
- Step 5: Interpret the result with proper units: the total financial change is $-120$ rupees (representing a loss of 120 rupees).
REASONING: Since each toy represents a negative financial change ($-15$ rupees) and there are 8 such toys, we multiply a positive count by a negative value. The rule dictates that the product of a positive and a negative integer is negative, giving $-120$.
ANSWER: -120 rupees
