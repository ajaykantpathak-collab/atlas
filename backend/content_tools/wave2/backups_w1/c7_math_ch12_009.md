## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often convert fractions to decimals incorrectly by dividing the denominator by the numerator instead of the numerator by the denominator (e.g., converting $\frac{2}{5}$ by doing $5 \div 2 = 2.5$ instead of $2 \div 5 = 0.4$). Another common error is failing to align formats before performing operations, such as directly adding $0.3 + \frac{1}{3}$ as $0.3 + 0.3 = 0.6$ without realizing $\frac{1}{3}$ is a repeating decimal, leading to precision loss.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student of the fraction-as-division rule: the fraction bar always means "divided by," so $\frac{numerator}{denominator}$ means numerator $\div$ denominator. Show them how to set up long division by adding a decimal point and zeros (e.g., $2.0 \div 5 = 0.4$). Practice converting simple fractions like $\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{3}{4}$, and $\frac{1}{5}$ into decimals before returning to mixed operations.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Riya bought $1\frac{3}{4}$ metres of red ribbon and $2.5$ metres of blue ribbon for a school craft project. What is the total length of ribbon she bought, expressed as a decimal?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given lengths: red ribbon = $1\frac{3}{4}$ m, blue ribbon = $2.5$ m.
- step 2: Choose the target format: convert the mixed fraction $1\frac{3}{4}$ into a decimal to match the blue ribbon's format.
- step 3: Convert the fractional part $\frac{3}{4}$ by multiplying numerator and denominator by $25$, giving $\frac{75}{100} = 0.75$, so $1\frac{3}{4} = 1.75$.
- step 4: Set up the addition expression for total length: $1.75 + 2.50$.
- step 5: Compute the sum: $1.75 + 2.50 = 4.25$ metres with proper alignment of decimal points.
REASONING: Converting the fraction to a decimal allows us to use standard column addition with place value alignment, avoiding the need to find a common denominator.
ANSWER: 4.25 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A container has $3.6$ litres of juice. If $\frac{2}{3}$ of the remaining juice is poured out after using $1.2$ litres, wait, let us use a simpler exact fraction problem: Amit has $\frac{5}{8}$ of a ₹100 note, and he spends ₹37.50. How much money does he have left?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given amounts: total money = ₹100, fraction of money = $\frac{5}{8}$, amount spent = ₹37.50.
- step 2: Calculate Amit's initial money by multiplying $\frac{5}{8}$ by $100$.
- step 3: Convert $\frac{5}{8}$ to a decimal by dividing $5$ by $8$, which gives $0.625$.
- step 4: Multiply $0.625 \times 100 = 62.5$ to find he starts with ₹62.50.
- step 5: Subtract the amount spent: $62.50 - 37.50 = 25.00$ rupees.
REASONING: Converting the fraction $\frac{5}{8}$ to the decimal $0.625$ simplifies multiplication by powers of 10, making it straightforward to find the monetary value before subtraction.
ANSWER: ₹25.00
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Evaluate the expression $3.75 \div \left(1\frac{1}{2} \times 0.5\right)$ by converting all numbers into fractions in their simplest form.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify all terms in the expression: decimal $3.75$, mixed number $1\frac{1}{2}$, and decimal $0.5$.
- step 2: Convert $3.75$ to a fraction: $3\frac{75}{100} = 3\frac{3}{4} = \frac{15}{4}$.
- step 3: Convert $1\frac{1}{2}$ to an improper fraction: $\frac{3}{2}$, and convert $0.5$ to a fraction: $\frac{1}{2}$.
- step 4: Evaluate the product inside the parentheses: $\frac{3}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{4}$.
- step 5: Substitute back into the division expression: $\frac{15}{4} \div \frac{3}{4}$.
- step 6: Multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor: $\frac{15}{4} \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{60}{12} = 5$.
REASONING: Converting all terms into fractions simplifies division because dividing by a fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal, and common denominators or numerators can cancel out cleanly.
ANSWER: 5
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two students, Kabir and Meera, are solving the expression $0.4 \times \frac{5}{8}$. Kabir converts the fraction to a decimal first ($0.4 \times 0.625$), while Meera converts the decimal to a fraction first ($\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{5}{8}$). Show both methods step-by-step and explain which method is more efficient in this specific case.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying mathematical methods
STEPS:
- step 1: Execute Kabir's method (decimal conversion): convert $\frac{5}{8}$ to $0.625$, then compute $0.4 \times 0.625 = 0.250$.
- step 2: Execute Meera's method (fraction conversion): convert $0.4$ to $\frac{2}{5}$, then compute $\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{5}{8} = \frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25$.
- step 3: Compare computational complexity: Meera's fraction method allows cross-cancelling the $5$s and $2$ with $8$, resulting in immediate simplification without multi-digit decimal multiplication.
- step 4: Formulate justification: Meera's method is more efficient because it avoids decimal multiplication place-value errors through fraction cancellation.
ANSWER: Both methods yield $0.25$, but Meera's fraction method is more efficient due to direct cross-cancellation.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A rectangular park has a length of $12\frac{1}{2}$ metres and a breadth of $8.75$ metres. A running track of uniform width is to be laid around it, but first, the ground staff needs the area in square metres. Calculate the area of the park by converting both dimensions into decimals, and then verify your answer by converting both dimensions into fractions.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations and verifying through multiple representations
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify dimensions: length = $12\frac{1}{2}$ m, breadth = $8.75$ m.
- step 2: Method 1 (Decimals): Convert length to decimal $12.5$ m. Compute area = $12.5 \times 8.75$.
- step 3: Perform multiplication: $125 \times 875 = 109375$, adjust for 3 decimal places to get $109.375 \text{ m}^2$.
- step 4: Method 2 (Fractions): Convert breadth to fraction $8.75 = 8\frac{3}{4} = \frac{35}{4}$ m, and length = $\frac{25}{2}$ m.
- step 5: Compute area fraction: $\frac{25}{2} \times \frac{35}{4} = \frac{875}{8} = 109.375 \text{ m}^2$.
- step 6: Verify consistency: Both methods give exactly $109.375 \text{ m}^2$, confirming accuracy.
ANSWER: The area of the park is $109.375 \text{ square metres}$, verified through both decimal and fraction computations.
DIFFICULTY: hard
