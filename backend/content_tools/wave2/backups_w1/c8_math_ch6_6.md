## ELI5
Negative numbers change nothing about distribution — only about signs. The multiplier outside still visits every term inside; what the visit produces now follows the integer sign rules, so a negative times a negative term contributes a positive product. Expanding is therefore two skills in tandem: distribute faithfully, then settle each product's sign. Skip either skill and the expression loses money somewhere in the middle.

## DEFINITION
The distributive property of multiplication over addition (and subtraction) holds for all integers, including negative integers: for any integers \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\), \(a(b + c) = ab + ac\) and \(a(b - c) = ab - ac\). This means multiplying a negative integer by a sum (or difference) is equivalent to multiplying the negative integer by each term inside the bracket and then adding (or subtracting) the results, following the sign rules for integer multiplication.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you owe your friend ₹5 for each of the 3 samosas you ate, and you also owe ₹2 for a chutney. Your total debt can be written as \(-5 \times 3 + (-2)\) or as \(-5(3) + (-2)\). But what if you want to combine the costs first? You could think: "Each samosa with chutney costs ₹5 + ₹2 = ₹7, and I ate 3 sets, so my debt is \(-3 \times 7\)". This is the distributive property with negatives: \(-3(5 + 2) = -3 \times 5 + (-3) \times 2\). Start by identifying the integer outside the bracket and the terms inside (treating subtraction as adding a negative). Multiply the outside integer by each term inside separately, carefully applying the sign rules: negative × positive = negative, negative × negative = positive. Then combine the results. The key insight is that the distributive property is not just a rule for positive numbers; it is a fundamental structure of arithmetic that remains true when we extend numbers to include negatives.

Takeaway: the distributive property is sign-blind structure — it holds for all integers — but each product it creates must be signed by the integer rules before terms combine. Exam format: expect expansions with negative multipliers and negative bracket terms, then collection of like terms; sign slips are the marked errors. Verification beat: recheck every product's sign individually before adding anything. Why students miss it: they distribute correctly and then fumble one sign — treat distribution and sign-settling as two separate passes, and the algebra stays clean.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
State the distributive property of multiplication over addition for integers \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\). Then write the version for subtraction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The distributive property over addition: \(a(b + c) = ab + ac\). Over subtraction: \(a(b - c) = ab - ac\). This holds for all integers \(a, b, c\). A wrong answer (e.g., writing \(a(b + c) = ab + c\) or forgetting the subtraction version) reveals the student has memorized a pattern without understanding the structure, or does not recognize that subtraction is addition of a negative and the property applies identically.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why \(-4 \times (6 - 3)\) equals \((-4 \times 6) - (-4 \times 3)\) using the concept of multiplication as repeated addition or a number line. Why does the minus sign in front of the 3 become a plus sign in the expanded form?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
\(6 - 3\) means \(6 + (-3)\). By distributive property, \(-4 \times (6 + (-3)) = (-4 \times 6) + (-4 \times -3)\). Since \(-4 \times -3 = +12\), this becomes \(-24 + 12 = -12\). The minus sign in front of 3 becomes a plus in the expanded form because subtracting a number is adding its negative, and the distributive property distributes over addition. A wrong answer that says "the two minuses make a plus" without explaining the additive inverse shows a rote memorization of sign rules without conceptual understanding of why the property holds for negatives.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Expand and simplify: \(-7(2x - 5y) + 3(-x + 4y)\).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Distribute \(-7\): \(-7 \times 2x = -14x\); \(-7 \times (-5y) = +35y\). So first part: \(-14x + 35y\).
Step 2: Distribute \(3\): \(3 \times (-x) = -3x\); \(3 \times 4y = 12y\). So second part: \(-3x + 12y\).
Step 3: Combine: \(-14x + 35y - 3x + 12y = (-14x - 3x) + (35y + 12y) = -17x + 47y\).
A wrong answer like \(-14x - 35y\) (sign error on \(-7 \times -5y\)) or \(-17x + 23y\) (combining \(35y + 12y\) incorrectly) reveals specific sign-rule gaps or like-term combination errors.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the two expressions: \(A = -5(3 - 8)\) and \(B = (-5 \times 3) - (-5 \times 8)\). Without calculating the final numeric value, explain why they must be equal. Then, identify which expression would be easier to evaluate mentally and why.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
They are equal because the distributive property \(a(b - c) = ab - ac\) holds for all integers; here \(a = -5\), \(b = 3\), \(c = 8\). Expression \(A\) is easier mentally: \(3 - 8 = -5\), then \(-5 \times -5 = 25\). Expression \(B\) requires two multiplications and a subtraction of a negative: \(-15 - (-40) = -15 + 40 = 25\). A student who says \(B\) is easier or cannot articulate why the property guarantees equality without computation shows a lack of structural understanding and flexibility in choosing efficient strategies.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student simplified \(-2(4 - 9)\) as follows:
Step 1: \(-2 \times 4 = -8\)
Step 2: \(-2 \times -9 = -18\)
Step 3: \(-8 - 18 = -26\)
Is the student's final answer correct? If not, identify the exact step where the error occurred and explain why it is wrong using the sign rules for integer multiplication.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The student's answer is incorrect. The error is in Step 2: \(-2 \times -9\) should be \(+18\) (negative × negative = positive), not \(-18\). The correct simplification: \(-2(4 - 9) = -2 \times 4 - (-2 \times 9) = -8 - (-18) = -8 + 18 = 10\). Alternatively, \(-2 \times -5 = 10\). A wrong evaluation that says "the answer is correct" or misidentifies the error reveals the student does not have fluent sign-rule application and cannot self-monitor when distributing a negative over a subtraction.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often forget to multiply the outside negative integer by the second term when it is subtracted (i.e., a negative term inside the bracket). For example, in \(-3(4 - 5)\), they write \(-12 - 15\) instead of \(-12 + 15\). The specific wrong step is treating the subtraction sign as an operation to perform after distribution rather than recognizing the term inside is \(-5\). They compute \(-3 \times 4 = -12\) and then \(-3 \times 5 = -15\) and keep the minus sign from the original expression, yielding \(-12 - 15 = -27\), instead of \(-3 \times 4 + (-3) \times (-5) = -12 + 15 = 3\). This error stems from not rewriting subtraction as addition of a negative before distributing.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student makes this error, first have them rewrite the expression inside the bracket using only addition: \(4 - 5 = 4 + (-5)\). Then apply the distributive property to \(-3(4 + (-5))\). Explicitly multiply \(-3\) by each term: \((-3 \times 4) + (-3 \times -5)\). Use a two-color pen or highlighter: one color for the multiplier outside, another for each term inside. Drill the sign rule: "negative times negative gives positive" with a quick reference chart. Finally, have them verify by evaluating the bracket first: \(4 - 5 = -1\), then \(-3 \times -1 = 3\), confirming the distributed result must also be 3.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper sells notebooks at a profit of ₹8 each and pens at a loss of ₹3 each. On Monday, he sold 12 notebooks and 15 pens. On Tuesday, he sold 10 notebooks and 20 pens. Use the distributive property to find the total profit/loss over the two days combined, given that the total items sold each day are multiplied by the respective profit/loss per item.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the profit/loss per item: Notebook = +₹8 (profit), Pen = -₹3 (loss). Items sold: Mon (12, 15), Tue (10, 20).
- Step 2: Write expression for total profit/loss: \(8(12 + 10) + (-3)(15 + 20)\). This uses distributive property to combine days first.
- Step 3: Distribute the profit/loss rates: \(8 \times 22 + (-3) \times 35\).
- Step 4: Compute each product: \(8 \times 22 = 176\); \((-3) \times 35 = -105\).
- Step 5: Add the results: \(176 + (-105) = 176 - 105 = 71\).
REASONING: We grouped the same items across days using the distributive property in reverse (factoring), then distributed the per-item rates. The negative sign for the pen's loss is carried through the multiplication, and adding a negative gives the net profit.
ANSWER: ₹71 profit
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Simplify the algebraic expression: \(-2(3x - 4y) - 5(-x + 2y)\).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Rewrite subtractions as additions of negatives: \(-2(3x + (-4y)) - 5((-x) + 2y)\).
- Step 2: Distribute \(-2\) to each term in the first bracket: \((-2 \times 3x) + (-2 \times -4y) = -6x + 8y\).
- Step 3: Distribute \(-5\) to each term in the second bracket (note the minus sign before 5 is part of the multiplier): \(-5 \times (-x) = +5x\); \(-5 \times 2y = -10y\). So second part becomes \(+5x - 10y\).
- Step 4: Combine the two distributed results: \(-6x + 8y + 5x - 10y\).
- Step 5: Combine like terms: \((-6x + 5x) + (8y - 10y) = -x - 2y\).
REASONING: The key is treating the leading minus sign in front of the 5 as part of the multiplier (-5). Distributing a negative over a negative term yields a positive term. Careful bookkeeping of signs at each distribution step prevents the common error of dropping a sign.
ANSWER: \(-x - 2y\)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: The temperature in a cold storage room is initially -4°C. A cooling system decreases the temperature by 2°C every hour for 3 hours, then a heater increases it by 1°C every hour for the next 4 hours. Write an expression for the final temperature using the distributive property to combine the hourly rates first, then evaluate.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify initial temperature = -4°C. Cooling rate = -2°C/hr for 3 hrs. Heating rate = +1°C/hr for 4 hrs.
- Step 2: Write net change expression using distributive property on time: Net change = \((-2 \times 3) + (1 \times 4)\). But we can also factor the time if intervals were same; here intervals differ, so we distribute rates over their respective times.
- Step 3: Alternatively, think of average rate? No, distributive property applies per interval. So net change = \((-2)(3) + (1)(4) = -6 + 4 = -2°C\).
- Step 4: Wait, the problem asks to use distributive property to combine rates first. Let's reframe: Total hours = 7. But rates are different for different periods. So we cannot combine rates first unless we use weighted average. Instead, use distributive property on the expression for final temp: Final = Initial + Rate1×Time1 + Rate2×Time2.
- Step 5: To show distributive property with negatives: Let's group the negative contributions: \(-4 + [(-2) \times 3 + 1 \times 4]\). Or write as \(-4 + (-2)(3) + (1)(4)\).
- Step 6: Evaluate: \(-4 + (-6) + 4 = -6°C\).
REASONING: This problem combines integer addition/subtraction (initial temp, net change) with distributive property (rate × time). The negative rate for cooling is distributed over positive time, yielding a negative change. The final sum of integers gives the final temperature.
ANSWER: -6°C
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student claims that \(-3(5 - 2)\) is equal to \(-3 \times 5 - 3 \times 2 = -15 - 6 = -21\). Another student says it should be \(-3 \times 5 - (-3 \times 2) = -15 + 6 = -9\). Who is correct? Explain the reasoning using the distributive property and integer sign rules. Then, write a general rule for distributing a negative integer over a subtraction.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recognize the expression \(-3(5 - 2)\) involves a negative multiplier and a subtraction inside.
- Step 2: Apply distributive property correctly: \(a(b - c) = ab - ac\). Here \(a = -3\), \(b = 5\), \(c = 2\).
- Step 3: Compute \(ab = -3 \times 5 = -15\). Compute \(ac = -3 \times 2 = -6\). Then \(ab - ac = -15 - (-6) = -15 + 6 = -9\).
- Step 4: Identify the first student's error: they treated the multiplier as +3 for the second term, forgetting that the multiplier is -3 for both terms.
- Step 5: State the general rule: When distributing a negative integer over a subtraction, the negative multiplier multiplies both terms, and subtracting a negative product becomes addition.
ANSWER: The second student is correct. The first student incorrectly used +3 for the second multiplication instead of -3. The correct expansion is \(-3(5 - 2) = (-3 \times 5) - (-3 \times 2) = -15 - (-6) = -15 + 6 = -9\). General rule: For any integers \(a, b, c\), \(a(b - c) = ab - ac\). If \(a\) is negative, both products \(ab\) and \(ac\) carry the sign of \(a\), and the subtraction of \(ac\) is handled by adding the opposite of \(ac\).
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: In a game, a player's score changes by \(-4\) points for each penalty and \(+6\) points for each bonus. In Round 1, the player gets 3 penalties and 2 bonuses. In Round 2, the player gets 1 penalty and 4 bonuses. The total score change can be calculated in two ways:
Method A: Calculate each round separately using distributive property, then add.
Method B: Combine total penalties and total bonuses first, then use distributive property once.
Show both methods step-by-step and verify they give the same result. Which method is more efficient and why?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying methods
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define variables: penalty = -4, bonus = +6. Round 1: 3 penalties, 2 bonuses. Round 2: 1 penalty, 4 bonuses.
- Step 2: Method A (per round): Round 1 change = \(-4(3) + 6(2) = -12 + 12 = 0\). Round 2 change = \(-4(1) + 6(4) = -4 + 24 = 20\). Total = \(0 + 20 = 20\).
- Step 3: Method B (combine first): Total penalties = \(3 + 1 = 4\). Total bonuses = \(2 + 4 = 6\). Total change = \(-4(4) + 6(6) = -16 + 36 = 20\).
- Step 4: Verify equality: Both yield 20.
- Step 5: Compare efficiency: Method B is more efficient because it requires only two multiplications and one addition, whereas Method A requires four multiplications and three additions. Method B uses the distributive property in reverse (factoring) to simplify computation.
ANSWER: Both methods give a total score change of +20 points. Method B is more efficient as it reduces the number of operations by combining like terms (total penalties and total bonuses) before multiplying by the rates. This demonstrates the power of the distributive property to restructure calculations for simplicity.
