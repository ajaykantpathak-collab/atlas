## ELI5
Every word problem is a story wearing a mask; your job is to pull the mask off. 'Rahul has some marbles' — that 'some' is your variable. 'He wins twice as many' — that is a multiplication. 'Now he has thirty' — that is your equals sign. Sentence by sentence, the story converts into an equation, and once it is an equation, the balancing tools you already own finish the job. Reading carefully is not the boring part of algebra; it is the whole game.

## DEFINITION
Translating word problems into linear equations is the process of identifying unknown quantities, representing them with variables, and converting the verbal relationships and constraints described in the problem into one or more algebraic equations of the form \(ax + b = c\) (or \(ax + b = cx + d\)) where \(a, b, c, d\) are constants and \(x\) is the unknown variable.

apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can independently read a multi-step word problem, identify all known and unknown quantities, assign appropriate variables, translate each sentence into algebraic expressions, formulate a correct linear equation, solve it using balancing or transposition, and verify that the solution satisfies the original problem context.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers translation of word problems involving a single unknown leading to linear equations in one variable. It includes problems on ages, numbers, money, geometry (perimeter), and distance-speed-time. It does NOT cover systems of two linear equations in two variables (Class 9), quadratic equations, or problems requiring inequalities.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you and your friend are saving money for a cricket bat costing ₹1,200. You have ₹300 already and save ₹50 per week; your friend has ₹150 and saves ₹70 per week. After how many weeks will you both have the same amount? This real-life situation requires translating "same amount" into an equation. Start by asking: "What is the unknown?" (number of weeks). Write down what each person has as an expression: you = 300 + 50w, friend = 150 + 70w. The phrase "same amount" means these two expressions are equal. This connects to the balancing method (c8_math_ch13_8) where we keep both sides equal. The key insight: every sentence in the problem gives a mathematical relationship — your job is to spot the equality words like "is", "equals", "same as", "more than", "less than" and turn them into =, +, −.

Takeaway: translate sentence by sentence — unknowns become variables, relationships become operations, 'is / equals / same as' becomes the equals sign — then solve and verify the solution against the story. Exam format: expect multi-step problems; assign the variable, write the equation, solve, and finish with a sentence answer in the problem's own units. Verification beat: push your answer back through the original words — it must satisfy every condition stated. Why students miss it: they grab numbers and guess an operation; the equality words are the anchors that decide where the equals sign goes.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the first step you should always take when starting to translate a word problem into a linear equation?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The first step is to identify the unknown quantity and assign a variable (like x) to represent it. A wrong answer (e.g., "write the equation immediately" or "pick numbers") reveals the student does not understand that variable definition anchors the entire translation process; without it, expressions like "5 more than the number" become ambiguous.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
In the sentence "Rohan's age is 5 years more than twice Sita's age," a student writes the equation \(R = 2S + 5\). Another student writes \(R + 5 = 2S\). Which translation is correct and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The first translation \(R = 2S + 5\) is correct because "5 years more than twice Sita's age" means start with twice Sita's age (2S) and add 5. The second student incorrectly added 5 to Rohan's age, reversing the relationship. A wrong choice shows the student treats "more than" as attaching to the subject (Rohan) rather than the object of comparison (Sita's age).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
The sum of three consecutive even numbers is 108. Translate this into a linear equation and solve for the smallest number.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Let the smallest even number be \(x\). The next two consecutive even numbers are \(x+2\) and \(x+4\). Equation: \(x + (x+2) + (x+4) = 108\). Simplify: \(3x + 6 = 108\). Subtract 6: \(3x = 102\). Divide by 3: \(x = 34\). The smallest number is 34. A wrong answer (e.g., using \(x, x+1, x+2\)) reveals the student does not recall that consecutive even numbers differ by 2, not 1.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
A rectangle's length is 3 cm more than its breadth. Its perimeter is 42 cm. Two students set up equations differently:
Student A: \(2(x + x+3) = 42\) where \(x\) = breadth.
Student B: \(2(y + y-3) = 42\) where \(y\) = length.
Are both equations valid? Explain.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Yes, both are valid. Student A lets breadth = \(x\), so length = \(x+3\); perimeter = \(2(x + x+3) = 42\). Student B lets length = \(y\), so breadth = \(y-3\); perimeter = \(2(y + y-3) = 42\). Both yield the same dimensions (breadth 9 cm, length 12 cm). A student who says only one is correct fails to see that variable choice is arbitrary as long as relationships are preserved.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student solves this problem: "A number is multiplied by 4, then 12 is subtracted, giving 20." Their working: \(4x - 12 = 20 \Rightarrow 4x = 8 \Rightarrow x = 2\). Is the solution correct? If not, identify the error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The solution is incorrect. The error is in the transposition step: \(4x - 12 = 20\) should give \(4x = 20 + 12 = 32\), not \(4x = 8\). The student subtracted 12 from 20 instead of adding. The correct answer is \(x = 8\). A student who misses this error lacks fluency in inverse operations and sign rules when moving terms across the equals sign.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a word problem that translates to the equation \(5x + 30 = 2x + 75\). Use a real-life context (money, distance, or age). Solve your problem and explain what \(x\) represents.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: "Anjali has ₹30 and saves ₹5 per week. Bhavesh has ₹75 and saves ₹2 per week. After how many weeks will they have the same amount?" Let \(x\) = number of weeks. Anjali: \(5x + 30\), Bhavesh: \(2x + 75\). Equation: \(5x + 30 = 2x + 75\). Solving: \(3x = 45 \Rightarrow x = 15\) weeks. A poor answer uses mismatched contexts (e.g., mixing age and money) or creates a problem where the equation does not naturally arise, showing the student cannot reverse-engineer the translation process.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often translate "5 less than x" as \(5 - x\) instead of \(x - 5\). For example, in "Ravi has 5 less than twice the number of pens Shyam has," they write \(5 - 2x\) instead of \(2x - 5\). This happens because they read left-to-right and map words directly to symbols without recognizing that "less than" reverses the order: "A less than B" means \(B - A\).
## ON_FAILURE
Write the phrase "5 less than x" on the board. Ask: "If x = 10, what is 5 less than 10?" Student says 5. Show that \(x - 5 = 5\) but \(5 - x = -5\). Emphasize: "less than" means subtraction but the order is opposite to the English order. Practice 5 similar phrases: "3 less than y", "7 less than 2a", "10 less than the number". Have student say the arithmetic value for a sample number, then write the algebraic expression. Repeat until they consistently write \(y - 3\), \(2a - 7\), \(x - 10\).

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 9. If the digits are reversed, the new number is 27 more than the original number. Find the original number.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify unknowns. Let the tens digit be \(x\) and the units digit be \(y\). The original number is \(10x + y\). The reversed number is \(10y + x\).
- Step 2: Translate first condition: "sum of digits is 9" → \(x + y = 9\).
- Step 3: Translate second condition: "reversed number is 27 more than original" → \(10y + x = 10x + y + 27\).
- Step 4: Simplify the second equation: \(10y - y + x - 10x = 27\) → \(9y - 9x = 27\) → divide by 9: \(y - x = 3\).
- Step 5: Solve the system of two linear equations: \(x + y = 9\) and \(y - x = 3\). Add them: \(2y = 12 \Rightarrow y = 6\). Substitute: \(x + 6 = 9 \Rightarrow x = 3\). Original number = \(10(3) + 6 = 36\).
- Step 6: Verify: Digits sum = 3+6=9. Reversed = 63. 63 - 36 = 27. Correct.

REASONING: This problem requires two variables because there are two unknown digits, but it reduces to a single linear equation in one variable after substitution (or we solve a simple system). The key is recognizing place value: a two-digit number with tens digit \(x\) and units digit \(y\) is \(10x + y\), not \(xy\). This builds on Class 6 place value concepts.

ANSWER: 36
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A man is 3 times as old as his son. Ten years ago, he was 5 times as old as his son. Find their present ages.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Let the son's present age be \(x\) years. Then the man's present age is \(3x\) years.
- Step 2: Express ages 10 years ago: son = \(x - 10\), man = \(3x - 10\).
- Step 3: Translate the past condition: "man was 5 times as old as his son" → \(3x - 10 = 5(x - 10)\).
- Step 4: Solve the equation: \(3x - 10 = 5x - 50\). Bring variables to one side: \(-10 + 50 = 5x - 3x\) → \(40 = 2x\) → \(x = 20\).
- Step 5: Find man's age: \(3x = 60\). Verify: 10 years ago, son was 10, man was 50. 50 is 5 times 10. Correct.

REASONING: Age problems are classic linear equation applications. The critical step is correctly expressing past/future ages by adding/subtracting the same number of years to both persons. The equation balances the relationship at a different time point. This connects to the transposition method (c8_math_ch13_9) for moving terms.

ANSWER: Son is 20 years old, man is 60 years old.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: In a class test, 5 marks are awarded for every correct answer and 2 marks are deducted for every incorrect answer. A student attempts all 40 questions and scores 130 marks. How many questions did the student answer correctly?

STEPS:
- Step 1: Let \(x\) = number of correct answers. Then incorrect answers = \(40 - x\) (since all 40 attempted).
- Step 2: Marks from correct answers = \(5x\). Marks deducted for incorrect = \(2(40 - x)\).
- Step 3: Total score = marks gained - marks deducted = \(5x - 2(40 - x)\). This equals 130.
- Step 4: Form equation: \(5x - 2(40 - x) = 130\). Expand bracket: \(5x - 80 + 2x = 130\).
- Step 5: Combine like terms: \(7x - 80 = 130\). Add 80: \(7x = 210\). Divide by 7: \(x = 30\).
- Step 6: Verify: Correct = 30, incorrect = 10. Score = \(30 \times 5 - 10 \times 2 = 150 - 20 = 130\). Correct.

REASONING: This combines the concept of "total = part1 + part2" with negative marks (deduction). The student must recognize that "attempts all questions" links the two unknowns (correct and incorrect) so only one variable is needed. The deduction is handled by subtracting \(2(40-x)\), which uses the distributive property learned in c8_math_ch13_7.

ANSWER: 30 correct answers
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Priya and Ravi are comparing their savings. Priya says, "If you give me ₹20, I will have twice as much as you." Ravi replies, "If you give me ₹20, we will have the same amount." Translate both statements into equations. Let Priya's money be \(P\) and Ravi's money be \(R\). Solve the system to find how much each has. Explain why the two statements give different equations but the same solution.

COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and interpreting multiple constraints

STEPS:
- Step 1: Translate Priya's statement: "If Ravi gives Priya ₹20" → Priya has \(P+20\), Ravi has \(R-20\). "Priya has twice as much as Ravi" → \(P+20 = 2(R-20)\).
- Step 2: Translate Ravi's statement: "If Priya gives Ravi ₹20" → Priya has \(P-20\), Ravi has \(R+20\). "Same amount" → \(P-20 = R+20\).
- Step 3: Simplify both equations. Eq1: \(P+20 = 2R-40 \Rightarrow P = 2R - 60\). Eq2: \(P = R + 40\).
- Step 4: Equate the two expressions for \(P\): \(2R - 60 = R + 40 \Rightarrow R = 100\). Then \(P = 140\).
- Step 5: Verify both statements with P=140, R=100. Priya's: 140+20=160, 100-20=80, 160=2×80 ✓. Ravi's: 140-20=120, 100+20=120 ✓.

ANSWER: Priya has ₹140, Ravi has ₹100. The two statements describe different hypothetical transactions but both are true for the same actual amounts; they provide two independent linear equations that together determine the unique solution.

DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A train travels from Station A to Station B at 60 km/h and returns at 40 km/h. The total journey takes 5 hours. A student sets up the equation \(\frac{d}{60} + \frac{d}{40} = 5\) where \(d\) is the one-way distance. Another student argues the equation should be \(60t_1 + 40t_2 = 2d\) with \(t_1 + t_2 = 5\). Are both approaches valid? Which is more efficient for finding \(d\)? Solve using the more efficient method.

COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying solution methods for the same problem

STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze first approach: \(d/60 + d/40 = 5\). This directly uses time = distance/speed for each leg. One variable \(d\). Efficient.
- Step 2: Analyze second approach: Uses two variables \(t_1, t_2\) and two equations. More complex but also correct.
- Step 3: Choose first approach for efficiency. Solve: \(\frac{d}{60} + \frac{d}{40} = 5\). LCM of 60,40 is 120. Multiply: \(2d + 3d = 600 \Rightarrow 5d = 600 \Rightarrow d = 120\).
- Step 4: Verify: Time forward = 120/60 = 2h. Time return = 120/40 = 3h. Total = 5h. Correct.
- Step 5: Explain that the first method eliminates the need for a system by expressing total time directly in terms of the unknown distance.

ANSWER: Both approaches are mathematically valid. The first method (\(\frac{d}{60} + \frac{d}{40} = 5\)) is more efficient because it uses a single variable and directly translates the given total time. The distance is 120 km.

DIFFICULTY: hard
