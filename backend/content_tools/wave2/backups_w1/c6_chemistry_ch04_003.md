## ELI5
An iron gate left out in the monsoon turns brown and powdery — that is rusting at work. Leave one nail in a dry corner and another where the air is damp: days later, the damp one wears a reddish-brown flaky coat while the dry one still shines. The little experiment is the whole explanation: iron alone does not rust, air alone does not do it — iron reacts with oxygen and water together, cooking up a brand-new substance called iron oxide. That is why rusting is a chemical change, not the metal simply getting old. And it hands you the defence for free: paint the iron, keep air and water off, and the recipe never starts.

## DEFINITION
Rusting is a chemical change where iron reacts with oxygen and water to form a new substance called iron oxide, which appears as a reddish-brown, flaky coating on the metal surface.

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever noticed that an old iron gate left out in the monsoon rains starts turning brown and gets a powdery layer? To understand this, start by observing an iron nail left in a dry place versus one left in a damp, humid area. You will notice that the nail exposed to both air and water changes its appearance, while the dry one remains shiny. This tells us that rusting isn't just "getting old"—it is a reaction with the environment. Once you identify that iron, air, and water are the "ingredients" for rust, you can understand why we paint iron objects to keep them safe.

**Takeaway:** rusting is chemistry, not dirt: iron plus air plus moisture brews a new flaky substance, and the monsoon is its favourite season. **In the exam:** in rusting questions, demand both ingredients - air and water - before predicting any rust. **Check yourself:** watch the walkthrough's monsoon gate - the brown powder is a new substance, not washed-off grime. **Why students miss it:** students scrub rust as dirt - the new substance is a no to that; no washing returns the iron. Rehearse the exam move once more - iron, air, water, rust - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A farmer leaves a 5 kg iron plough in the field. After the monsoon, it gains 0.2 kg in weight due to rust. Explain why it gained weight.
STEPS:
- Identify the starting mass (5 kg).
- Recognize that rust is a combination of iron, oxygen, and water.
- Understand that the oxygen and water from the environment are now chemically part of the rust.
- Conclude the weight gain comes from the added oxygen and water molecules.
REASONING: Since iron reacts with atoms from the air and water, those atoms add their mass to the iron object. It didn't just "get dirty"; it chemically absorbed other elements.
ANSWER: The plough gained 0.2 kg because it combined with oxygen and water from the environment to form iron oxide.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Three nails are placed in different conditions: A (Dry air), B (Water only, no air), C (Air and Water). Predict which will rust.
STEPS:
- List the requirements for rust: Iron + Oxygen + Water.
- Check Nail A: Has iron and air, but no water. (Result: No rust)
- Check Nail B: Has iron and water, but no air. (Result: No rust)
- Check Nail C: Has iron, air, and water. (Result: Rust)
REASONING: Rusting is a chemical reaction that requires all three components simultaneously. Missing one component stops the reaction.
ANSWER: Only Nail C will rust because it has access to both oxygen and water.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: You have a bicycle chain. How does applying oil help it last longer?
STEPS:
- Identify the goal: Prevent the chain from rusting.
- Recall the factors of rusting: Exposure to air and water.
- Analyze the oil: It is a liquid that does not mix with water and sits on top of the metal.
- Conclude the function: It creates a physical shield.
REASONING: By coating the iron links, the oil acts as a hydrophobic (water-repelling) barrier that prevents air and water from reaching the iron.
ANSWER: The oil creates a protective layer that blocks air and water from contacting the iron, preventing the chemical reaction of rusting.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You find a rusted iron key. You scrape off the rust and find the metal underneath is thinner than the original key. Why?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Acknowledge that iron was converted into iron oxide (rust).
- Realize that the rust is a flaky layer that falls off.
- Connect the loss of metal to the conversion of iron into rust.
ANSWER: The metal is thinner because the iron on the surface was chemically changed into iron oxide, which then fell off, taking the material with it.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why are iron bridges painted regularly, even if they don't look "dirty"?
COMPETENCY: Evaluating and applying
STEPS:
- Understand that rusting is a slow process that happens constantly in the presence of humidity.
- Recognize that waiting until it "looks dirty" means the damage has already started.
- Justify the preventive maintenance.
ANSWER: They are painted to prevent the start of the reaction; if you wait until you see rust, the structural integrity of the bridge has already been weakened by the conversion of iron to iron oxide.
DIFFICULTY: hard
