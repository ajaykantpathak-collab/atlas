## ELI5
A parallelogram is a quadrilateral that learned to lean without breaking: both pairs of opposite sides stay parallel, like a bookshelf leaning but holding. The parallel sides drag consequences with them. Opposite sides come out equal; opposite angles match; neighbouring angles always pair to a straight-line total; and the diagonals slice each other exactly in half at their crossing. Slide the top of a rectangle sideways and nothing essential breaks — that slideability is the parallelogram's whole personality.

## DEFINITION
A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel. This implies opposite sides are equal in length, opposite angles are equal in measure, adjacent angles are supplementary (sum to 180°), and the diagonals bisect each other.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are helping your father tile a rectangular room, but the tiles delivered are slanted — each tile is a parallelogram with sides 30 cm and 20 cm, and one angle is 70°. To calculate how many tiles fit, you first need to find the other angles and confirm opposite sides match. Start by recalling Class 7 parallel lines and transversals (c7_math_ch05_007): when parallel lines are cut by a transversal, corresponding angles are equal and interior angles on the same side are supplementary. Apply this to the two pairs of parallel sides. The key insight: the parallel sides create transversals, forcing opposite angles equal and adjacent angles supplementary — this is the engine behind all parallelogram properties.

Takeaway: from parallel opposite sides everything follows — equal opposite sides, equal opposite angles, supplementary adjacent angles, bisecting diagonals; the parallel lines create transversals whose alternate angles are the engine. Exam format: expect 'is this a parallelogram? prove it' and unknown side/angle finds; cite which parallel pair generates which equal angle. Verification beat: check adjacent angles sum to a straight line as a quick audit of any claimed angles. Why students miss it: they memorise the four properties as disconnected facts; each one is forced by the parallel sides through transversal angle pairs, and showing that linkage is the reasoning being assessed.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think "opposite sides equal" and "opposite angles equal" are the definition of a parallelogram, rather than consequences of the parallel-sides definition. They then incorrectly assume any quadrilateral with equal opposite sides is a parallelogram (true) but also that any quadrilateral with equal opposite angles is a parallelogram (false — isosceles trapezium is a counterexample). In worked problems, they mistakenly set adjacent angles equal (e.g., if angle A = 70°, they write angle B = 70°) instead of supplementary.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student sets adjacent angles equal: 1) Draw a parallelogram on paper, extend one side to show the transversal. 2) Ask: "These two angles are interior on the same side of the transversal — what did we learn in Class 7 about them?" (c7_math_ch05_009: they sum to 180°). 3) Have them measure with a protractor to see 70° and 110°. 4) Practice: "In parallelogram, if one angle is x, the neighbour is 180-x. Write this as a rule." Repeat with 3 different angle values until automatic.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A parallelogram-shaped garden plot has one side 15 m long and the adjacent side 10 m long. The angle between them is 75°. Find the lengths of the other two sides and the measures of the other three angles.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given: side a = 15 m, side b = 10 m, included angle = 75°. In parallelogram, opposite sides are equal and parallel.
- Step 2: Apply opposite sides equal property: the side opposite 15 m is also 15 m; the side opposite 10 m is also 10 m.
- Step 3: Apply opposite angles equal property: the angle opposite 75° is also 75°.
- Step 4: Apply adjacent angles supplementary property: the angle adjacent to 75° is 180° - 75° = 105°. The fourth angle (opposite this) is also 105°.
- Step 5: Verify sum of all angles = 75° + 105° + 75° + 105° = 360° (quadrilateral angle sum).
REASONING: The definition (both pairs of opposite sides parallel) forces these properties via transversal angle rules. Opposite sides equal comes from triangle congruence (ASA) using alternate angles; opposite angles equal from corresponding angles; adjacent supplementary from interior angles on same side of transversal.
ANSWER: Other sides: 15 m and 10 m. Other angles: 105°, 75°, 105°.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In parallelogram ABCD, diagonal AC is drawn. Angle BAC = 30° and angle CAD = 40°. Find angle BCA and angle ACD.

- Step 3: Since AB || CD and AC is a transversal, ∠BAC and ∠ACD are alternate interior angles → ∠ACD = ∠BAC = 30°.
- Step 4: Check: ∠C = ∠ACB + ∠ACD = 40° + 30° = 70° = ∠A (opposite angles equal, consistent).
- Step 5: State answers clearly.
