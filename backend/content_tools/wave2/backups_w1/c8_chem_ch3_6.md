## ELI5
Boil salt water in a pan and the water escapes as steam, leaving salt behind — but you have lost the water forever. Distillation refuses to lose either. The mixture is heated so the lower-boiling liquid vaporises first; its vapour travels down a glass tube cooled by running water, turns back into droplets — like a cold plate held over a steaming kettle — and drips pure into a separate flask. The difference in boiling points does the sorting: vaporise, then condense, into a second container.

## DEFINITION
Distillation is an advanced separation technique used to separate the components of a liquid mixture by utilizing differences in their boiling points through consecutive vaporization and condensation. In this process, a liquid solution is heated to vaporize the more volatile component, and the resulting vapors are subsequently cooled and condensed back into a liquid in a separate container.

## WALKTHROUGH
When you boil salt water in a kitchen vessel to dry it up completely, the water escapes as steam into the air, leaving only the salt behind—this is simple evaporation where the liquid solvent is lost. But what if you want to recover *both* the pure liquid solvent and the dissolved solid, or separate two liquids that have mixed completely like alcohol and water? To begin investigating this, we observe how steam turns back into water droplets when it hits a cold metal plate held above a boiling kettle. We connect this to our prior knowledge of physical changes like evaporation and condensation, but take it a step further by trapping and cooling those vapors inside a closed apparatus. By carefully controlling the heating, we make the component with the lower boiling point boil off first, travel down a glass tube cooled by running water, and drip into a receiver flask as a pure liquid.

Takeaway: distillation is evaporation with a return ticket — vaporise the lower-boiling component, condense it in the water-cooled tube, collect it pure — and a fractionating column upgrades the same idea for mixtures boiling close together. Exam format: expect simple-versus-fractional contrast, the condenser's role, and boiling-point reasoning. Verification beat: state which liquid boils first and why — the more volatile one leaves first. Why students miss it: they describe apparatus without principle — the entire technique is one number: the boiling-point gap.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the primary physical property of components in a mixture that allows them to be separated by simple distillation?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The primary property is a significant difference in their boiling points. A wrong answer—such as answering "solubility" or "magnetic susceptibility"—reveals a confusion with filtration, evaporation, or magnetic separation techniques where boiling points are irrelevant.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
During the distillation of a salt-water solution, why does pure water collect in the receiver flask while solid salt remains behind in the distilling flask?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Water has a much lower boiling point than salt, so it vaporizes into steam when heated and then condenses back into liquid water in the condenser, whereas salt does not vaporize at that temperature. A student who thinks the salt also evaporates is confusing boiling points with melting points or sublimation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A laboratory mixture contains 100 mL of water (boiling point $100^\circ\text{C}$) and 100 mL of ethanol (boiling point $78.4^\circ\text{C}$). If you heat this mixture slowly, which substance will vaporize first, and what temperature will the thermometer first stabilize at?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Ethanol will vaporize first because it has a lower boiling point, and the thermometer will stabilize at approximately $78.4^\circ\text{C}$ during its collection. A student who answers $100^\circ\text{C}$ fails to understand that the substance with the lower boiling point changes phase first before the mixture temperature can rise further.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why is running cold water in the outer jacket of a Liebig condenser made to flow upwards (from bottom to top) rather than downwards (from top to bottom)?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Water flows upward to ensure the condenser jacket remains completely filled with cold water at all times, maximizing condensation efficiency. A wrong answer suggests the student views the condenser as a mere funnel rather than a heat-exchange jacket operating on counter-current cooling principles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the safety of using simple distillation to separate a mixture of acetone (boiling point $56^\circ\text{C}$) and water ($100^\circ\text{C}$) using an open flame directly under the glass flask containing acetone. Why is this considered dangerous?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Acetone is highly flammable; using an open flame directly beneath it creates a serious fire hazard if vapors escape or the flask cracks. A student who claims it is safe demonstrates a lack of awareness regarding the chemical properties (flammability) of organic solvents and the need for water baths or heating mantles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a modified distillation setup to obtain pure drinking water from muddy seawater. Which two separation techniques would you combine, and in what order?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
First, use filtration or sedimentation to remove insoluble mud particles, followed by simple distillation to remove dissolved salts from the filtrate. A poor answer fails to separate suspended solids before distillation, which would clog or contaminate the distillation flask.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that during distillation, liquid turns directly into gas and then instantly disappears into the atmosphere, completely missing the condensation step. Another common error is thinking that both components boil and vaporize at the exact same time if they are miscible liquids.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this, demonstrate a physical condensation experiment: boil water in a kettle with a cold plate held over the spout so droplets collect visibly, proving that vapor can be turned back into liquid. Then emphasize that miscible liquids have different boiling points, meaning the thermometer will pause at the boiling point of the first liquid until it has completely boiled away.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student has 250 mL of a saltwater solution containing copper sulfate ($CuSO_4$) dissolved in water. They want to recover both dry copper sulfate crystals and pure distilled water. Outline the 5-step process using simple distillation.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that water has a boiling point of $100^\circ\text{C}$ and copper sulfate crystals decompose at much higher temperatures, making simple distillation appropriate.
- Step 2: Assemble the distillation apparatus including a round-bottom flask, a thermometer, a Liebig condenser, and a receiver flask.
- Step 3: Pour the saltwater solution into the round-bottom flask and connect the cooling water supply to the Liebig condenser.
- Step 4: Heat the flask gently using a burner; record the temperature when the thermometer stabilizes at $100^\circ\text{C}$ as water turns to steam and condenses into the receiver flask.
- Step 5: Stop heating when all the liquid has evaporated; pure distilled water is collected in the receiver flask, and dry copper sulfate crystals remain in the distilling flask.
REASONING: The technique works because thermal energy breaks intermolecular forces between water molecules, vaporizing them, while non-volatile salt ions remain behind in the heated container. The condenser removes latent heat from the steam, converting it back to liquid water.
ANSWER: Pure distilled water in the receiver flask and dry copper sulfate crystals in the distilling flask.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An industrial laboratory receives a miscible liquid mixture containing acetone ($BP = 56^\circ\text{C}$) and water ($BP = 100^\circ\text{C}$). Describe the 5 steps to separate them effectively without decomposing either liquid.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the two volatile miscible liquids and note their distinct boiling points ($56^\circ\text{C}$ vs $100^\circ\text{C}$).
- Step 2: Set up a distillation flask containing the mixture, attaching a fractionating column or standard distillation head with a thermometer.
- Step 3: Heat the mixture gradually, keeping a close eye on the thermometer to monitor rising temperatures.
- Step 4: Collect the distillate when the thermometer reads $56^\circ\text{C}$ until the temperature begins to rise again, indicating acetone has fully boiled off.
- Step 5: Change the receiver flask to collect the remaining water fraction as the temperature reaches $100^\circ\text{C}$.
REASONING: Because acetone has a much lower boiling point than water, it vaporizes first while the temperature remains constant at $56^\circ\text{C}$, allowing complete separation of two miscible liquids.
ANSWER: Acetone fraction collected first at $56^\circ\text{C}$, followed by pure water collected at $100^\circ\text{C}$ in separate flasks.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A contaminated water sample contains dissolved sodium chloride ($NaCl$) and suspended clay particles. Design a 6-step purification sequence to obtain pure drinking water from this sample.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the contaminants: suspended insoluble clay particles and dissolved soluble salt ($NaCl$).
- Step 2: Pass the muddy saltwater mixture through filter paper in a funnel (filtration) to remove insoluble clay particles.
- Step 3: Collect the clear filtrate, which is now a homogeneous solution of salt and water.
- Step 4: Transfer the clear filtrate into a distillation flask and set up a simple distillation apparatus with a Liebig condenser.
- Step 5: Heat the solution until the water boils at $100^\circ\text{C}$, letting steam pass through the cooled condenser.
- Step 6: Collect the condensed pure water droplets in a clean receiver flask while solid salt remains behind in the boiling flask.
REASONING: Combining filtration and distillation is necessary because filtration only removes insoluble impurities, whereas dissolved salts require phase change separation via distillation to be removed.
ANSWER: Pure drinking water in the receiver flask, leaving solid salt in the boiling flask and clay on the filter paper.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Why is simple distillation ineffective for separating two miscible liquids like ethanol (boiling point $78.4^\circ\text{C}$) and methanol (boiling point $64.7^\circ\text{C}$) whose boiling points are very close to each other?
COMPETENCY: Evaluating limitations of separation techniques
STEPS:
- Step 1: Compare the boiling points of ethanol ($78.4^\circ\text{C}$) and methanol ($64.7^\circ\text{C}$).
- Step 2: Note that their boiling point difference is small (less than $15^\circ\text{C}$).
- Step 3: Conclude that vapors of both liquids will evaporate simultaneously during simple heating.
- Step 4: Explain that fractional distillation with a fractionating column is required instead of simple distillation.
ANSWER: Simple distillation fails because the vapors of both liquids will rise together when the temperature approaches their boiling points, resulting in poor separation. Fractional distillation is needed instead.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student attempts to perform simple distillation on a sugar-water solution. During the process, they forget to turn on the cold water flow in the Liebig condenser. Predict what will happen to the steam inside the condenser tube and explain the consequence on the purity of the recovered water.
COMPETENCY: Predicting experimental failure and troubleshooting
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the function of the Liebig condenser, which requires cold running water to cool and condense steam.
- Step 2: Determine that without cold water, the glass tube remains hot.
- Step 3: Predict that steam will pass straight through the condenser tube without condensing into liquid.
- Step 4: Conclude that no distillate will be collected in the receiver flask, and hot vapor will escape into the room.
ANSWER: Without cold water flowing through the condenser, the steam will not cool down enough to condense. It will escape out of the apparatus as hot vapor, and no pure water will be collected in the receiver flask.
DIFFICULTY: hard
