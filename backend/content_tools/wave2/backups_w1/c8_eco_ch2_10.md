## ELI5
Picture a family: four working adults supporting one child and one retired grandparent. Thirty years on, the child has moved away, three adults have retired, and a single worker supports three elderly people. Multiply that family by a whole country and you have population ageing: the share of elderly people grows while the working-age share shrinks. The old-age dependency ratio climbs, and public finances, labour markets and social infrastructure all feel the squeeze — an older society needs completely different economic planning than a young one.

## DEFINITION
Population ageing is an economic and demographic phenomenon characterized by an increasing median age in a population, where the proportion of elderly individuals (usually aged 60 or 65 and above) grows relative to the working-age population (aged 15 to 59). This structural shift increases the old-age dependency ratio, placing significant pressure on public finances, labor markets, and social infrastructure.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine a family where four working adults earn money to support themselves, one young child, and one retired grandparent. Now, imagine this family thirty years later: the child has grown up but moved away, three of the adults have retired, and only one adult is left working to support three elderly grandparents. This micro-level shift is exactly what happens to an entire country during population ageing. 

To analyze this, we first look at the age structure of a population, which we learned to classify in [c8_eco_ch2_4](c8_eco_ch2_4). We then calculate the Old-Age Dependency Ratio to measure the economic burden on the productive work force. Finally, we connect this demographic shift to concepts learned in Class 7, such as how banks manage savings and how governments fund public social infrastructure, to understand why an older society requires completely different economic planning.

Takeaway: population ageing is the median age climbing — longer lives and fewer births handing economies more elders to support and fewer hands per elder — reshaping pensions, healthcare and workforces. Exam format: expect the causes named and two economic consequences traced. Verification beat: follow the support ratio — earners per elder — as births fall. Why students miss it: they read ageing as a health story only — it is an economics story about who supports whom.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the "Old-Age Dependency Ratio" (OADR), and which two demographic age groups are compared to calculate it?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The Old-Age Dependency Ratio is the ratio of the number of elderly dependents (typically those aged 60 or 65 and above) to the working-age population (aged 15 to 59 or 64), expressed as a percentage. 

*What a wrong answer reveals:* If a student includes children (0–14 years) in the numerator, they are confusing the *Old-Age Dependency Ratio* with the *Total Dependency Ratio*. If they use the total population as the denominator, they fail to realize that dependency is measured specifically against the productive labor force that supports them.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why does a continuous decline in birth rates, combined with an increase in life expectancy, lead to population ageing? Explain the role of both factors.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
A decline in birth rates reduces the influx of newborn children, which shrinks the younger cohorts at the base of the population. Simultaneously, an increase in life expectancy means people are living longer, which expands the older cohorts at the top of the population. Together, these two forces cause the median age of the population to rise, increasing the proportion of elderly citizens.

*What a wrong answer reveals:* The student might believe that population ageing is only caused by people living longer. This reveals a failure to understand that a shrinking base of young people (due to low birth rates) is often the primary driver of rapid population ageing.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A town called Sundarnagar has a total population of 20,000. Among them, 4,000 are children (0–14 years), 12,000 are working-age adults (15–59 years), and 4,000 are senior citizens (60+ years). Calculate the Old-Age Dependency Ratio for Sundarnagar.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
**Step 1:** Identify the elderly population ($60+$ years) = $4,000$.  
**Step 2:** Identify the working-age population ($15-59$ years) = $12,000$.  
**Step 3:** Use the formula:  
$$\text{OADR} = \left(\frac{\text{Population Aged 60+}}{\text{Population Aged 15-59}}\right) \times 100$$  
**Step 4:** Substitute the values:  
$$\text{OADR} = \left(\frac{4,000}{12,000}\right) \times 100 = 33.33\%$$  

*What a wrong answer reveals:* If the student calculates $\left(\frac{4,000}{20,000}\right) \times 100 = 20\%$, they have mistakenly used the total population as the denominator, showing they do not understand that the ratio measures the burden specifically on the active working-age group.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If a country's population is ageing rapidly, how will this trend affect the government's tax revenues and its spending on social infrastructure?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
An ageing population shrinks the proportion of working-age adults, leading to a smaller active workforce. This reduces the government's collection of direct income taxes. Simultaneously, the growing number of elderly citizens forces the government to spend more on social infrastructure, specifically healthcare, geriatric hospitals, and pension schemes. Thus, tax revenues decrease while public expenditures increase.

*What a wrong answer reveals:* The student might think that older people pay more taxes because they are wealthier, or that government spending remains unchanged. This shows they do not understand the relationship between active employment, tax collection, and demographic-specific public spending.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A local council suggests converting three vacant primary school buildings into senior citizen community health centers. Evaluate this proposal using demographic and economic reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
This is an excellent, economically sound proposal. An ageing population is caused by falling birth rates, which means there are fewer children and less demand for primary school infrastructure. At the same time, the growing elderly population increases the demand for healthcare services. Reallocating physical infrastructure from education to healthcare avoids waste, saves construction costs, and directly addresses the shifting needs of the community.

*What a wrong answer reveals:* A weak answer might oppose the plan simply because "schools are important," failing to connect the decline in child population with the rise in senior population. This shows an inability to evaluate infrastructure re-allocation based on demographic shifts.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a basic 3-point economic action plan for an Indian state experiencing population ageing to ensure its state-run pension system remains financially sustainable.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A strong action plan should include:
1. **Raise the Retirement Age:** Increase the retirement age from 60 to 62 or 65, keeping older adults in the active workforce longer so they continue to pay taxes rather than drawing pensions immediately.
2. **Encourage Private Retirement Savings:** Introduce tax incentives for young workers to save in pension plans like the National Pension System (NPS), reducing their future reliance on state funds.
3. **Skill Development for Seniors:** Create light-duty, part-time, or digital work opportunities for healthy seniors to help them remain economically productive.

*What a poor answer reveals:* Proposing unrealistic or inflation-inducing solutions (such as "print more money to pay pensions" or "force companies to pay seniors without working") reveals a lack of understanding of fiscal realities and basic monetary economics.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often assume that population ageing is a positive economic sign because older people have accumulated lifetime savings in banks, which they believe will boost investment. 

In reality, retirees transition from being "savers" to "dis-savers." They stop earning a salary and begin withdrawing their accumulated savings to pay for daily living, medicines, and healthcare. When a large percentage of the population retires at the same time, the total savings in the banking system can actually drop. This reduces the funds available for banks to lend to young entrepreneurs (a concept learned in [c7_eco_ch04_004](c7_eco_ch04_004)), which can slow down national economic growth.
## ON_FAILURE
1. **Step 1 (Micro-demonstration):** Have the student draw a balance sheet for a 3-person household: Mother (earns ₹40,000), Grandmother (retired, zero income, needs ₹10,000 for medicine), and Child (zero income, school fees ₹5,000). The family surplus is ₹25,000.
2. **Step 2 (The Shift):** Now, change the scenario: The mother retires (income drops to zero, needs ₹10,000 for medicine). Only the child is now working, earning ₹40,000. But now, the child must support *two* retired elders. 
3. **Step 3 (Calculate the strain):** The child's income is ₹40,000, but medical expenses for the two elders are ₹20,000. The surplus drops to ₹20,000.
4. **Step 4 (Macro-connection):** Explain that when millions of families experience this shift, the whole country's "surplus" (national savings) shrinks, leaving less money in banks for development.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
### PROBLEM
The agricultural district of Punjabrao has a total population of 80,000. A recent demographic survey revealed the following data:
- Children (0–14 years): 16,000
- Working-age adults (15–59 years): 48,000
- Elderly citizens (60+ years): 16,000

Calculate the Old-Age Dependency Ratio for Punjabrao, and explain how a high ratio might impact the local government's ability to build new rural roads.

### STEPS
1. **Identify the economic variables and given data:**  
   - Elderly population ($60+$) = $16,000$  
   - Working-age population ($15-59$) = $48,000$  
   - Total population = $80,000$ (Note: This is extra information not needed for the OADR formula).

2. **Recall the economic formula:**  
   $$\text{OADR} = \left(\frac{\text{Population Aged 60+}}{\text{Population Aged 15-59}}\right) \times 100$$

3. **Substitute the values into the formula:**  
   $$\text{OADR} = \left(\frac{16,000}{48,000}\right) \times 100$$

4. **Perform the calculation:**  
   $$\text{OADR} = 0.3333 \times 100 = 33.33\%$$

5. **Interpret the economic results:**  
   An OADR of 33.33% means that for every 100 working-age adults in Punjabrao, there are approximately 33 elderly dependents. Economically, this high dependency ratio means the local government must allocate a larger portion of its tax revenue to elderly healthcare and pensions. This leaves fewer public funds available for physical infrastructure, such as building new rural roads.

### REASONING
The Old-Age Dependency Ratio measures the non-working elderly population against the productive working-age population. Because the working-age population pays the majority of local taxes, a high ratio indicates that a large share of tax revenue is diverted to elderly care, creating an opportunity cost that reduces spending on development projects like roads.

### ANSWER
33.33%

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
### PROBLEM
The state of Haripura is experiencing rapid demographic transition. Over a span of 15 years:
- The elderly population (60+) increased from 2 million to 5 million.
- The working-age population (15–59) decreased from 20 million to 15 million due to outward migration of youth.

Calculate the change in the Old-Age Dependency Ratio (in percentage points) and analyze the impact on Haripura's state pension fund solvency.

### STEPS
1. **Identify the initial data (Year 1) and final data (Year 15):**  
   - Year 1: Elderly = $2\text{ million}$, Working-age = $20\text{ million}$  
   - Year 15: Elderly = $5\text{ million}$, Working-age = $15\text{ million}$

2. **Calculate the OADR for Year 1:**  
   $$\text{OADR}_1 = \left(\frac{2\text{ million}}{20\text{ million}}\right) \times 100 = 10\%$$

3. **Calculate the OADR for Year 15:**  
   $$\text{OADR}_{15} = \left(\frac{5\text{ million}}{15\text{ million}}\right) \times 100 = 33.33\%$$

4. **Calculate the absolute change in the ratio:**  
   $$\text{Change} = \text{OADR}_{15} - \text{OADR}_1 = 33.33\% - 10\% = 23.33\text{ percentage points}$$

5. **Interpret the economic impact on pension solvency:**  
   In Year 1, there were 10 workers for every 1 elderly person ($100 / 10 = 10$). By Year 15, there are only 3 workers for every 1 elderly person ($100 / 33.33 = 3$). This massive increase in the dependency burden means the pension fund will face severe insolvency unless the government dramatically increases tax rates on the remaining workers or reduces pension payouts.

### REASONING
By comparing the OADR at two different points in time, we can measure the speed and severity of population ageing. A rising ratio indicates a shrinking support base (fewer contributors) and a growing beneficiary base (more claimants), which destabilizes public pension funds.

### ANSWER
The Old-Age Dependency Ratio increased by 23.33 percentage points (from 10% to 33.33%), severely threatening the solvency of the state pension fund as the support ratio fell from 10 workers per senior to just 3 workers per senior.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
### PROBLEM
A regional cooperative bank, Gramin Bank, operates in an ageing district. Over the last decade, the percentage of the district's population aged 60+ rose from 8% to 25%. Consequently, the bank observed two trends:
1. Retirees withdrew a total of ₹50 lakhs from their fixed deposits to cover medical expenses.
2. The demand for agricultural loans from young farmers remained high at ₹40 lakhs.

Using banking concepts from Class 7 (savings, borrowing, and interest), explain how this demographic shift creates a liquidity crisis for Gramin Bank and how it affects interest rates.

### STEPS
1. **Identify the demographic shift and its economic effects:**  
   - An increase in the elderly population leads to massive cash withdrawals (₹50 lakhs) as retirees draw down their savings for healthcare.
   - The demand for credit (loans) from young borrowers remains high at ₹40 lakhs.

2. **Recall the banking mechanism:**  
   - Banks use household savings (deposits) to fund loans for borrowers. 
   - A healthy bank needs a continuous inflow of deposits to sustain its lending activities.

3. **Analyze the mismatch (Liquidity Crisis):**  
   - The bank lost ₹50 lakhs in deposits (outflow) while facing ₹40 lakhs
ANSWER: The demographic shift creates a liquidity crisis through a two-sided squeeze: ₹50 lakhs of deposits flow OUT (retirees drawing down savings faster than new households deposit), while ₹40 lakhs of loan demand from young farmers must still be FUNDED from deposits. Gramin Bank's lendable base shrinks exactly when demand peaks, so it faces the classic mismatch: short-run cash obligations exceed available liquid funds. Effects on interest rates: (1) to attract fresh deposits the bank raises deposit rates; (2) because lendable funds are scarce, it must raise lending rates or ration credit; (3) the deposit-lending spread narrows, hurting the bank's viability. This links to the Class 7 concepts — banks intermediate savings into loans, so any deposit drain starves credit and raises its price. A wrong answer says 'the bank can always print money' — Gramin institutions cannot create money; they can only relend the savings they mobilise.
