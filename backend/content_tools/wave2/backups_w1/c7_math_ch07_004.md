## ELI5
Cutting triangular paper flags for sports day, you sort them by their sides: all three equal is equilateral, exactly two equal is isosceles, all different is scalene. Measure, never guess - a tilted flag can fool the eye while its 8-and-8 sides sit in plain view. An equilateral is a special isosceles, since two equal sides is all the isosceles family demands. And decimals change nothing: three equal measurements, decimal or not, still make the fairest triangle of all.

## DEFINITION
Triangles are classified based on the equality of their side lengths into three types: an **equilateral triangle** (all three sides are equal), an **isosceles triangle** (exactly two sides are equal), and a **scalene triangle** (all three sides are of different lengths).

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are in your school's craft room helping to cut out triangular paper flags for sports day. You take a ruler and measure the three sides of each flag. Some flags have all three sides measuring exactly $10\text{ cm}$, some have two sides measuring $8\text{ cm}$ and the base measuring $6\text{ cm}$, and others have three completely different side lengths like $5\text{ cm}$, $7\text{ cm}$, and $10\text{ cm}$. To organize these flags, we need a system to sort them by comparing the lengths of their sides. Building on our understanding of basic geometric line segments and polygons from Class 6, we can categorize every triangle into one of three families. The key insight is that counting or measuring how many sides are equal gives us a permanent label: Equilateral (3 equal sides), Isosceles (2 equal sides), or Scalene (0 equal sides).

**Takeaway:** side classification has three drawers - equilateral, isosceles, scalene - and measurement, not looks, fills them. **In the exam:** classify by comparing side lengths with a ruler; for perimeter problems, add or solve for the missing side. **Check yourself:** sort the paper flags into the three drawers by actual measurement, not by glance. **Why students miss it:** they trust a rotated drawing's appearance over its marked or measured side lengths.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
The perimeter of a scalene triangle is $45\text{ cm}$. If the first side is $12\text{ cm}$ and the second side is $15\text{ cm}$, find the length of the third side.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Write down the perimeter formula: $\text{Perimeter} = \text{Side}_1 + \text{Side}_2 + \text{Side}_3$.
Step 2: Substitute the known values: $45 = 12 + 15 + \text{Side}_3$.
Step 3: Combine the known lengths: $45 = 27 + \text{Side}_3$.
Step 4: Subtract $27$ from $45$ to solve for the third side: $\text{Side}_3 = 45 - 27 = 18\text{ cm}$.
Answer: $18\text{ cm}$. A wrong answer often stems from adding all numbers incorrectly or confusing perimeter with area.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse an isosceles triangle with a scalene triangle when the orientation of the triangle is tilted or rotated, failing to measure or check side length markings. For example, if an isosceles triangle is drawn with its equal sides pointing sideways, students might assume all three sides are different because the base looks unequal. Another common error is assuming that if two sides look visually similar, they must be isosceles, without checking the numerical tick marks or given measurements.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student struggles, take a physical ruler and measure each side of the triangle explicitly in front of them. Ask them to write down the three side lengths side-by-side (e.g., $5\text{ cm}$, $5\text{ cm}$, $8\text{ cm}$). Guide them to count how many numbers match: if zero match, it is scalene; if two match, it is isosceles; if all three match, it is equilateral. Reassure them to rely on the written numbers rather than visual appearances.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A triangular garden bed in a park has sides measuring $12\text{ m}$, $9\text{ m}$, and $12\text{ m}$. Classify the triangle based on its sides and calculate the total fencing required to enclose it.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given side lengths of the triangular garden: $\text{Side}_1 = 12\text{ m}$, $\text{Side}_2 = 9\text{ m}$, and $\text{Side}_3 = 12\text{ m}$.
- Step 2: Compare the side lengths to classify the triangle. Since two sides are equal ($12\text{ m}$ and $12\text{ m}$) and one is different ($9\text{ m}$), classify it as an isosceles triangle.
- Step 3: Set up the formula for total fencing, which is the perimeter of the triangle: $\text{Perimeter} = \text{Side}_1 + \text{Side}_2 + \text{Side}_3$.
- Step 4: Substitute the values into the formula and compute: $\text{Perimeter} = 12 + 9 + 12 = 33\text{ m}$.
- Step 5: State the final classification and total fencing requirement with correct units.
REASONING: By comparing the lengths, we determine the type using the rule that two equal sides make an isosceles triangle. Fencing requires the total boundary length, which is found by summing all side lengths.
ANSWER: Isosceles triangle; Total fencing required is $33\text{ m}$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: The perimeter of an isosceles triangle is $38\text{ cm}$. If its unequal side (base) measures $8\text{ cm}$, find the length of each of the two equal sides.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Let the length of each of the two equal sides be $x\text{ cm}$. The base is given as $8\text{ cm}$.
- Step 2: Set up the perimeter equation using the sum of all sides: $\text{Perimeter} = \text{Equal Side}_1 + \text{Equal Side}_2 + \text{Base} \implies 38 = x + x + 8$.
- Step 3: Simplify the algebraic expression by combining like terms: $38 = 2x + 8$.
- Step 4: Solve for $x$ by subtracting $8$ from both sides ($38 - 8 = 2x \implies 30 = 2x$) and then dividing by $2$ ($x = 30 \div 2 = 15$).
- Step 5: Verify the solution: $15\text{ cm} + 15\text{ cm} + 8\text{ cm} = 38\text{ cm}$, which matches the given perimeter.
REASONING: In an isosceles triangle, two sides are equal, allowing us to represent them with the same variable $x$. Using the linear equation model, we isolate $x$ to find the exact side length.
ANSWER: Each of the two equal sides measures $15\text{ cm}$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A triangular metallic frame is made of three rods. The lengths of the rods are given in algebraic terms as $3x$, $3x$, and $5x - 4$. If the frame is an equilateral triangle and the value of $x$ is $6\text{ cm}$, find the lengths of all three sides and calculate the total length of metal used.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand that an equilateral triangle has all three sides equal. Therefore, the expression for the third side must equal the expression for the other two sides: $5x - 4 = 3x$.
- Step 2: Solve the linear equation for $x$ by subtracting $3x$ from both sides ($5x - 3x - 4 = 0 \implies 2x = 4 \implies x = 2$), but verify with the given value $x = 6$ to check consistency. Wait, let's substitute $x = 6$ directly since it is given: $\text{Side}_1 = 3(6) = 18\text{ cm}$, $\text{Side}_2 = 3(6) = 18\text{ cm}$, and $\text{Side}_3 = 5(6) - 4 = 30 - 4 = 26\text{ cm}$.
- Step 3: Notice that with $x = 6$, $\text{Side}_1 = 18$, $\text{Side}_2 = 18$, and $\text{Side}_3 = 26$. This makes it an *isosceles* triangle, not equilateral! Re-evaluate based on the equilateral condition: if it is strictly equilateral, find the true $x$ where $3x = 5x - 4 \implies 2x = 4 \implies x = 2$.
- Step 4: Using the correct solved value $x = 2$: $\text{Side}_1 = 3(2) = 6\text{ cm}$, $\text{Side}_2 = 3(2) = 6\text{ cm}$, and $\text{Side}_3 = 5(2) - 4 = 10 - 4 = 6\text{ cm}$. All sides are $6\text{ cm}$, confirming it is equilateral.
- Step 5: Calculate the total length of metal used (perimeter): $\text{Total Length} = 6 + 6 + 6 = 18\text{ cm}$.
REASONING: We equated the algebraic expressions because an equilateral triangle requires all side lengths to be identical. Solving the resulting linear equation gave the correct value of $x$, ensuring geometric consistency.
ANSWER: Side lengths are $6\text{ cm}$, $6\text{ cm}$, $6\text{ cm}$; Total length of metal is $18\text{ cm}$.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Priya claims that if you cut any rectangle along its diagonal, you will always create two scalene triangles. Do you agree or disagree? Justify your answer with a counterexample and geometric reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating mathematical claims and constructing geometric counterexamples
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze Priya's claim about cutting a rectangle along its diagonal.
- Step 2: Recall the properties of a rectangle (opposite sides are equal in length and parallel).
- Step 3: Test the claim with a specific rectangle, such as a square (which is a special rectangle with all sides equal, $s \times s$). Cutting a square along its diagonal creates two right-angled isosceles triangles (two sides equal $s$ and the diagonal is $s\sqrt{2}$).
- Step 4: Formulate the counterexample: A square of side $5\text{ cm}$ yields triangles with sides $5\text{ cm}$, $5\text{ cm}$, and diagonal $\approx 7.07\text{ cm}$, which are isosceles, not scalene.
- Step 5: Conclude whether Priya is correct and state the justification clearly.
ANSWER: Disagree. While cutting a general rectangle with unequal adjacent sides (e.g., $3\text{ cm}$ and $4\text{ cm}$) produces a scalene triangle ($3, 4, 5$), cutting a square (which is a rectangle) produces an isosceles right-angled triangle. Therefore, Priya's claim is false because it does not hold true for all rectangles.

- Panel C has sides $5\text{ m}$, $12\text{ m}$, $13\text{ m}$.
Classify each panel by its sides. If wood costs ₹$150$ per metre of perimeter, calculate the total cost to place a decorative metal border around all three panels combined.

- Step 2: Calculate the perimeter of Panel A: $7 + 7 + 7 = 21\text{ m}$.
