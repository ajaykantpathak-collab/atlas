## ELI5
The vendor adds 500 grams of potatoes to one pan - the beam tips - so a matching 500-gram weight joins the other pan. That is the entire method: an equation is a balance, and every operation must happen to both sides at once. Undo what was done to the unknown - subtract an added number, divide a multiplied one - and the variable stands alone on its pan with the beam still level.

## DEFINITION
Finding the unknown by balancing both sides is the process of maintaining equality in an equation by performing identical operations on both the left-hand side (LHS) and right-hand side (RHS) until the unknown variable is isolated. This relies on the balance beam model, where any change made to one side must be mirrored on the other to preserve the equality statement.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can solve linear equations in one variable using the balance model by performing inverse operations on both sides simultaneously, verify the solution by substitution, and apply this method to practical word problems.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers single-variable linear equations requiring addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division on both sides. Does not cover quadratic equations, systems of simultaneous linear equations, or inequalities with directional symbols.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at a local vegetable market in India, and a vegetable vendor uses a traditional beam balance to weigh 2 kilograms of potatoes. If the vendor adds 500 grams of potatoes to one pan, the balance tilts, and they must add a matching 500-gram weight to the other pan to keep it level and equal. In algebra, an equation is exactly like this balance scale: the Left-Hand Side (LHS) must always equal the Right-Hand Side (RHS). If you subtract a number or divide by a number on one side to isolate your unknown variable, you must do the exact same operation to the other side. Connecting back to our prior study of algebraic expressions, an equation sets two expressions equal to each other. The key insight of the balance model is that equality is sacred: whatever you do to one pan of the scale, you must do to the other to keep the equation true.

**Takeaway:** whatever you do to one side, do to the other - balance is the only law. **In the exam:** undo operations in reverse: addition bows to subtraction, multiplication bows to division, always on both sides at once. **Check yourself:** weigh the vendor's move: 500 grams added to one pan is answered by 500 grams on the other, and the beam never learns the difference. **Why students miss it:** students 'fix' one side alone and the beam silently tips - Rahul multiplying only the LHS by 3 is the walkthrough's cautionary tale. The unknown is isolated not by force but by mirrored moves, and substitution afterwards is the receipt proving the beam still sits level.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often perform an operation on only one side of the equation (e.g., adding 5 to the LHS to cancel out $-5$, but forgetting to add 5 to the RHS), or they apply the wrong inverse operation (e.g., subtracting a number when they should divide because they see a coefficient attached to a variable). For example, when solving $3x = 12$, a struggling student might write $3x - 3 = 12$, completely ignoring the multiplicative relationship between 3 and $x$.

Walk them through rewriting the equation line by line, explicitly writing the operation on both sides in parentheses or distinctive colors.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Priya went to the market with some money. After spending ₹45 on books, she has ₹120 left. Set up an equation and use the balance method to find her initial amount of money $m$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify what is given and define the unknown. Let $m$ be the initial money in rupees. Amount spent = 45, remaining money = 120. Formulate the equation: $m - 45 = 120$.
- step 2: Identify the operation needed to isolate $m$. Since 45 is subtracted from $m$, the inverse operation is adding 45.
- step 3: Apply the balance principle by adding 45 to both the LHS and RHS: $m - 45 + 45 = 120 + 45$.
- step 4: Compute the arithmetic on both sides: $m = 165$.
- step 5: Verify the result by substituting back: $165 - 45 = 120$, which matches the given remaining amount. The initial money was ₹165.
REASONING: By adding 45 to both sides, we cancel out the $-45$ on the left side while keeping the scale balanced, successfully isolating $m$.
ANSWER: ₹165
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A farmer packs mangoes into boxes. Each box contains 6 kg of mangoes, and the total weight of the mangoes is 72 kg. Set up an equation and use the balance method to find the number of boxes $b$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values and form the equation. Weight per box = 6 kg, total weight = 72 kg, number of boxes = $b$. The equation is $6b = 72$.
- step 2: Identify the operation connecting the coefficient 6 to the variable $b$. Here, 6 is multiplied by $b$, so the inverse operation is division by 6.
- step 3: Apply the balance principle by dividing both sides by 6: $\frac{6b}{6} = \frac{72}{6}$.
- step 4: Simplify both sides: $b = 12$.
- step 5: Verify the result: $6 \times 12 = 72$, confirming the total weight matches. There are 12 boxes.
REASONING: Division is the inverse operation of multiplication; dividing both sides by 6 preserves the equality while isolating the variable $b$.
ANSWER: 12 boxes
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Amit has 3 times the number of stamps that Rohan has, plus 5 extra stamps. If Amit has 26 stamps in total, set up an equation and use the balance method to find how many stamps Rohan has ($s$).
STEPS:
- step 1: Translate the word problem into an algebraic equation. Amit's stamps = $3s + 5$, and we are given this equals 26. Equation: $3s + 5 = 26$.
- step 2: Address the addition first by applying the inverse operation (subtracting 5) to both sides: $3s + 5 - 5 = 26 - 5$.
- step 3: Simplify both sides to get the intermediate equation: $3s = 21$.
- step 4: Address the multiplication by applying the inverse operation (dividing by 3) to both sides: $\frac{3s}{3} = \frac{21}{3}$.
- step 5: Compute the final value: $s = 7$.
- step 6: Verify by substitution: $3(7) + 5 = 21 + 5 = 26$, which matches Amit's total stamps. Rohan has 7 stamps.
REASONING: When solving multi-step equations, we undo addition or subtraction first to isolate the term containing the variable, and then undo multiplication or division last.
ANSWER: 7 stamps
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A teacher asks two students to solve the equation $\frac{x}{4} = 5$. 
Student A multiplies both sides by 4 to get $x = 20$. 
Student B divides both sides by 5 to get $\frac{x}{20} = 1$. 
Who solved it correctly, and what error did the other student make in applying the balance method?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction in applying the balance method
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze Student A's method: multiplied both sides by 4, resulting in $\frac{x}{4} \times 4 = 5 \times 4$, which gives $x = 20$.
- step 2: Analyze Student B's method: divided both sides by 5, resulting in $\frac{x}{4 \times 5} = \frac{5}{5}$, which distorts the equation and does not isolate $x$.
- step 3: Evaluate correctness: Student A correctly used the inverse operation of division (multiplication by 4) on both sides.
- step 4: Explain Student B's error: Student B tried to operate on the constant on the RHS instead of using the inverse operation to eliminate the denominator 4 from the LHS.
ANSWER: Student A is correct. Student B made the error of dividing by the value on the RHS rather than multiplying both sides by 4 to undo the division by 4 on the LHS.
DIFFICULTY: medium
