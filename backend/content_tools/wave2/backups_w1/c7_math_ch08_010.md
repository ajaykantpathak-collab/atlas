## ELI5
A fraction sitting inside another fraction looks like a badge on a badge - until you remember each bar is a division sign in disguise. Simplify layer by layer: tidy the top, tidy the bottom, then flip the bottom and multiply. The outermost bar is the boss; the inner bars are luggage to be unpacked first. Fraction bars behave like brackets, never like loose terms to cancel - respect the storeys and the tower collapses into one honest fraction.

## DEFINITION
Simplifying complex fraction expressions involves reducing multi-layered or compound fraction expressions—where numerators and denominators themselves contain fractions, sums, or products—into a single, simplest proper or improper fraction by systematically applying the order of operations and fractional division rules.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers numerical compound fractions and simple algebraic fraction expressions with arithmetic operations, up to three levels of nesting, excluding advanced rational function calculus or polynomial partial fractions.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are helping at a school science fair in Delhi, and you need to divide a recipe of $\frac{3}{4}$ litres of chemical solution equally among test tubes, but the measuring beaker itself has a fractional scale of $\frac{2}{3}$ of another amount. When faced with a fraction divided by another fraction sitting inside a larger fraction bar, where do you even begin writing? We begin by identifying the main fraction bar that separates the overall numerator from the overall denominator, treating the top and bottom expressions as separate mini-problems. This builds directly upon our earlier skills of dividing fractions using reciprocals and finding common denominators for addition. The key insight is to resolve the top and bottom completely into single fractions first, and then multiply the numerator by the reciprocal of the denominator.

**Takeaway:** a complex fraction is layered division: resolve top and bottom fully, then flip the denominator and multiply. **In the exam:** treat each fraction bar as brackets; simplify numerator and denominator separately before the main division. **Check yourself:** simplify one compound fraction on paper, marking the primary bar with a highlighter first. **Why students miss it:** they cancel across storeys, treating separate fraction bars as if all terms mixed freely.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the primary rule for simplifying a fraction expression where a fraction is divided by another fraction, such as $\frac{\frac{a}{b}}{\frac{c}{d}}$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is to multiply the numerator fraction ($\frac{a}{b}$) by the reciprocal of the denominator fraction ($\frac{d}{c}$), yielding $\frac{a \times d}{b \times c}$. A wrong answer often shows the student adding or subtracting the denominators, or cross-multiplying the inner and outer numbers incorrectly, revealing a confusion between fraction division and fraction addition or cross-multiplication of proportions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
When simplifying a complex fraction expression like $\frac{1 + \frac{1}{2}}{1 - \frac{1}{3}}$, why is it incorrect to simply cancel the fractions or add the numerators and denominators straight across to get $\frac{2}{2}$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct answer is that fraction bars act like grouping symbols (brackets), meaning you must evaluate the entire numerator expression and the entire denominator expression separately before performing the final division. A wrong answer claiming that you can cancel numbers across addition signs reveals a fundamental misunderstanding of fraction bars as grouping symbols rather than division signs.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Simplify the complex fraction expression: $\frac{\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{2}}{\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{3}}$. Show your result as a reduced fraction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $\frac{15}{8}$. Step 1: Find a common denominator for the numerator ($\frac{3}{4} + \frac{2}{4} = \frac{5}{4}$). Step 2: Find a common denominator for the denominator ($\frac{5}{6} - \frac{2}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$). Step 3: Divide the numerator fraction by the denominator fraction: $\frac{5}{4} \div \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{4} \times \frac{2}{1} = \frac{10}{4} = \frac{5}{2}$. Wait, let's re-compute carefully: $\frac{5}{4} \times \frac{6}{3} = \frac{30}{12} = \frac{15}{6} = \frac{5}{2}$. Let's check denominator: $\frac{5}{6} - \frac{2}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$. Then $\frac{5}{4} \div \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{4} \times 2 = \frac{10}{4} = \frac{5}{2}$. A wrong answer like $\frac{8}{3}$ usually happens because the student inverted terms prematurely or made an error in finding the LCD for subtraction.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often cancel terms across addition and subtraction signs in complex fractions—such as cancelling the 3 in $\frac{2 + 3}{3 + 4}$ to get $\frac{2}{4}$—or they invert individual terms of a numerator sum separately before combining them, violating the order of operations by treating the main fraction bar as optional rather than a grouping symbol.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A confectionery shop in Jaipur is packing dry fruit mixtures. A worker has a complex order ratio represented by $\frac{\frac{3}{5} + \frac{1}{2}}{\frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{3}}$ kg of almonds per box. Simplify this complex fraction expression to find the exact weight in kilograms.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the numerator expression ($\frac{3}{5} + \frac{1}{2}$) and denominator expression ($\frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{3}$).
- Step 2: Simplify the numerator by finding a common denominator (LCD for 5 and 2 is 10): $\frac{3 \times 2}{5 \times 2} + \frac{1 \times 5}{2 \times 5} = \frac{6}{10} + \frac{5}{10} = \frac{11}{10}$.
- Step 3: Simplify the denominator by finding a common denominator (LCD for 4 and 3 is 12): $\frac{3 \times 3}{4 \times 3} - \frac{1 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \frac{9}{12} - \frac{4}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$.
- Step 4: Rewrite the complex fraction as a division problem and apply the reciprocal rule: $\frac{\frac{11}{10}}{\frac{5}{12}} = \frac{11}{10} \div \frac{5}{12} = \frac{11}{10} \times \frac{12}{5}$.
- Step 5: Multiply numerators and denominators, simplifying by common factors if possible: $\frac{11 \times 12}{10 \times 5} = \frac{132}{50} = \frac{66}{25}$ kg.
REASONING: We treat the main fraction bar as a division sign. By reducing both the top and bottom sums into single fractions first, we eliminate ambiguity and convert a daunting multi-layer fraction into a standard multiplication of fractions using reciprocals.
ANSWER: \frac{66}{25} \text{ kg (or } 2\frac{16}{25} \text{ kg)}
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In a science experiment at a school in Mumbai, students analyze a liquid solution flow rate given by the expression $2 - \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{3}}$. Simplify this nested fraction expression into a single fraction.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the deepest nested fraction part in the denominator, which is $1 + \frac{1}{3}$.
- Step 2: Simplify this inner sum by writing the whole number 1 as a fraction: $\frac{3}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{3}$.
- Step 3: Substitute this back into the expression, making the denominator $1 \div \frac{4}{3}$ (or $\frac{1}{\frac{4}{3}}$).
- Step 4: Evaluate the fraction $\frac{1}{\frac{4}{3}}$ by multiplying 1 by the reciprocal of $\frac{4}{3}$, which gives $1 \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$.
- Step 5: Perform the final subtraction from the whole number 2: $2 - \frac{3}{4} = \frac{8}{4} - \frac{3}{4} = \frac{5}{4}$.
REASONING: Nested fractions must always be simplified from the inside out. We evaluate the innermost denominator sum first, convert that tier into a reciprocal division, and work our way outward to the final subtraction.
ANSWER: \frac{5}{4} (or 1\frac{1}{4})
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A carpenter in Chennai cuts wooden planks according to a blueprint formula given by $\frac{\frac{5}{6} \times \frac{3}{10}}{\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{6}}$. Simplify this complex expression to find the exact fraction of a metre needed.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine the numerator product ($\frac{5}{6} \times \frac{3}{10}$) and denominator sum ($\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{6}$).
- Step 2: Multiply the numerator fractions directly (cancelling common factors 5 and 10, 3 and 6): $\frac{5 \times 3}{6 \times 10} = \frac{15}{60} = \frac{1}{4}$.
- Step 3: Add the denominator fractions by finding the LCD (which is 6): $\frac{2 \times 2}{3 \times 2} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{4}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$.
- Step 4: Set up the resulting division of the simplified numerator and denominator: $\frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\frac{5}{6}}$.
- Step 5: Apply the reciprocal rule to complete the division: $\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{6}{5} = \frac{6}{20} = \frac{3}{10}$ metres.
REASONING: When numerator and denominator contain different operations (multiplication on top, addition on the bottom), we perform the respective fraction operations on each level independently before applying the final division rule.
ANSWER: \frac{3}{10} \text{ metres}
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student claims that the complex fraction expression $\frac{\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b}}{\frac{1}{ab}}$ simplifies directly to $a + b$ for any non-zero integers $a$ and $b$. Prove whether this statement is correct by breaking down the simplification into steps and testing it with $a = 2$ and $b = 3$.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and proving algebraic equivalence
STEPS:
- Step 1: Substitute $a = 2$ and $b = 3$ into the numerator: $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$.
- Step 2: Substitute $a = 2$ and $b = 3$ into the denominator: $\frac{1}{2 \times 3} = \frac{1}{6}$.
- Step 3: Divide the simplified numerator by the simplified denominator: $\frac{\frac{5}{6}}{\frac{1}{6}} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{6}{1} = 5$.
- Step 4: Compare the result (5) with $a + b$ ($2 + 3 = 5$) and show algebraically that $\frac{\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b}}{\frac{1}{ab}} = \frac{\frac{b+a}{ab}}{\frac{1}{ab}} = \frac{b+a}{ab} \times ab = a + b$.
ANSWER: The statement is correct. Through algebraic manipulation, the common denominator of the numerator is $ab$, giving $\frac{b+a}{ab}$, and dividing by $\frac{1}{ab}$ multiplies by its reciprocal $ab$, leaving $a + b$.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: In an electrical circuit calculation project at a school in Bengaluru, total resistance involves the complex fraction expression $\frac{1}{\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{12}}$ ohms. A technician suggests that this is equal to $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{12}$. Evaluate the technician's claim, calculate the correct total resistance, and explain why inverting individual terms is invalid.
COMPETENCY: Error identification, mathematical justification, and advanced fraction evaluation
STEPS:
- Step 1: Find the common denominator for all terms in the denominator sum ($\frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{6}, \frac{1}{12}$), which is 12.
- Step 2: Convert and add the fractions in the denominator: $\frac{3}{12} + \frac{2}{12} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}$.
- Step 3: Substitute back into the main expression: $\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}}$.
- Step 4: Perform the division ($1 \div \frac{1}{2} = 1 \times 2 = 2$ ohms) and compare with the technician's incorrect sum ($\frac{3+2+1}{12} = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}$).
- Step 5: Conclude that the technician's claim is false because the reciprocal of a sum is not equal to the sum of the reciprocals.
ANSWER: The technician's claim is false. The correct total resistance is 2 ohms, whereas the technician's incorrect method yields $\frac{1}{2}$ ohm. Inverting individual terms across a sum violates fundamental fraction division laws.
DIFFICULTY: hard
