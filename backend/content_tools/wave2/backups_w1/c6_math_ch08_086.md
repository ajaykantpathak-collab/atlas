## ELI5
Area counts the floor space a shape covers - how many unit squares tile its inside. Covering a desk with paper sheets asks for area; edging the desk with tape asks for perimeter. The answer wears square units, because the counting is done in little squares laid shoulder to shoulder, not steps along an edge.

## DEFINITION
Area is the total measure of the flat surface or region enclosed inside the boundary of a closed geometric figure. It represents the "amount of space" inside a shape, measured in square units.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can calculate the area of rectangles and squares using dimensions and can distinguish between the concepts of "boundary length" (perimeter) and "inner space" (area).
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the definition of area and calculation for rectangles and squares using lengths and widths. It excludes complex polygon areas, surface area, volume, or calculus-based derivations.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are asked to cover your study desk with paper sheets. How do you know how many sheets you need? You wouldn't measure the edge of the desk (that's perimeter); instead, you would measure the flat top surface from side to side and top to bottom. By multiplying the length by the width, you find how many small squares of paper can fit on that surface. This "inner space" is what we call Area. Always remember: while perimeter is the "fencing" around a shape, area is the "carpet" covering the floor inside.

**Takeaway:** area measures the enclosed surface in square units - how many unit squares tile the inside of the shape. **In the exam:** in area questions, multiply the covering dimensions and state the answer in square units, never plain ones. **Check yourself:** cover the walkthrough's desk with paper sheets - the count of sheets is the area. **Why students miss it:** students reuse the perimeter walk - the desk's paper cover is a no to that; area tiles the inside, it never patrols the rim. ## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the formula for perimeter (2 × (l + w)) with the formula for area (l × w). They attempt to add the dimensions for area or multiply them for perimeter because they treat both formulas as "just some math rule" rather than understanding that perimeter measures lines (edges) and area measures squares (surface).
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to draw a rectangle on a 1cm grid paper that is 3cm by 2cm. Ask them to physically count the little squares inside the box. Then, show them that 3 rows of 2 squares equals 6, which matches 3 × 2. Reinforce that "Area = multiplication" because we are counting rows of squares.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A rectangular vegetable patch in a school garden is 10 metres long and 5 metres wide. The gardener wants to cover the path with fertilizer. If 1 bag of fertilizer covers 2 square metres, how many bags are needed?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify dimensions: l = 10 m, w = 5 m.
- step 2: Use the formula for Area: A = l × w.
- step 3: Calculate total area: 10 m × 5 m = 50 m².
- step 4: Determine bags needed: 50 m² / 2 m² per bag = 25 bags.
- step 5: Conclusion: 25 bags are required to cover the patch.
REASONING: We find the total surface area first, then divide by the coverage per unit to find the total quantity needed.
ANSWER: 25 bags
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A square courtyard has a side of 8 metres. A local decorator wants to place marble tiles, each 1 square metre in size. How many tiles are needed to cover the entire courtyard?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify side length: s = 8 m.
- step 2: Use the formula for Area of a square: A = s × s.
- step 3: Calculate the area: 8 m × 8 m = 64 m².
- step 4: Since each tile is 1 m², the number of tiles equals the area.
- step 5: Conclusion: 64 tiles are needed.
REASONING: Multiplying the side by itself gives the total capacity of the surface in terms of 1x1 squares.
ANSWER: 64 tiles
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A hall is 12m long and 8m wide. A carpet of 6m x 6m is placed in the center. What is the area of the floor not covered by the carpet?
STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate total floor area: 12m × 8m = 96 m².
- step 2: Calculate carpet area: 6m × 6m = 36 m².
- step 3: Subtract carpet area from total floor area: 96 m² - 36 m².
- step 4: Perform the subtraction: 60 m².
- step 5: Interpretation: 60 m² of the floor remains uncovered.
REASONING: By finding the area of two distinct rectangles and finding the difference, we isolate the specific region required.
ANSWER: 60 m²
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have 20 meters of fencing wire. Can you form a rectangular garden with an area of 30 square meters? Justify your answer.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- step 1: Recall Perimeter = 2(l+w) = 20, so l+w = 10.
- step 2: Test pairs of l and w that add to 10 (e.g., 5+5, 6+4, 7+3).
- step 3: Calculate area (l×w) for each: 5×5=25, 6×4=24, 7×3=21.
- step 4: Compare results to 30.
ANSWER: No, it is not possible. The maximum area for a perimeter of 20m is 25m² (for a 5x5 square). All other combinations result in smaller areas.
DIFFICULTY: hard
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A farmer has two fields. Field A is 10m x 4m. Field B is 8m x 5m. Which field has a larger area? Which has a larger perimeter?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate Area A: 10×4 = 40. Area B: 8×5 = 40.
- step 2: Calculate Perimeter A: 2(10+4) = 28. Perimeter B: 2(8+5) = 26.
- step 3: Compare values.
ANSWER: Both fields have the same area (40 m²). Field A has a larger perimeter (28m) compared to Field B (26m). This shows that shapes with equal areas can have different perimeters.
DIFFICULTY: medium
