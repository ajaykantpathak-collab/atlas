## ELI5
Capitals move for reasons, and the timeline shows Magadha's two bets. First Rajgir: a city clasped by five steep hills, a natural fortress for a young state that feared its neighbours. Then Pataliputra: planted where great rivers meet, a water-fort that traded as hard as it defended. Hill-ring to river-junction is the whole arc - from hiding behind stones to moving goods on water - and each capital was the right tool for its century's job.

## DEFINITION
This topic explores the strategic shifting of administrative capitals among the ancient Mahajanapadas of the Gangetic plain during the period of second urbanisation (c. 600 BCE – 300 BCE), focusing specifically on the evolution of Rajgir (Rajagriha) and Pataliputra as the powerhouse capitals of Magadha. It examines why rulers relocated and fortified these urban centers based on geography, military security, and economic dominance.

Covers the strategic, economic, and defensive factors behind the selection and shifting of capitals between Rajgir and Pataliputra within the Mahajanapada era, NOT the later imperial administrative structures under the Guptas or Mauryas beyond their capital functions.
