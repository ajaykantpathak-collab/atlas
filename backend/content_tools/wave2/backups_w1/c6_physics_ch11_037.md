## ELI5
Materials grade their doors to light: glass throws them wide open, frosted panes and butter paper leave them ajar with a blur, and wood keeps them bolted. The test is what you see through - the street, or a smudge of glow, or nothing at all - and privacy is just choosing the bolt.

## DEFINITION
Materials are classified by how much light they allow to pass through them: transparent materials let most light pass so objects are clearly visible, translucent materials let some light pass but scatter it, and opaque materials block light entirely.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are looking through your bedroom window; why can you see the street clearly, but when you look through a piece of buttered paper or a frosted glass, everything looks blurry? Start by picking up three objects: a clear glass tumbler, a piece of tracing paper, and a heavy wooden book. Hold them up to a light source one by one. Notice how the light passes through the glass, gets distorted by the paper, and is stopped by the book. This simple observation is the key to understanding how we group materials based on their light-transmitting properties.

**Takeaway:** materials pass light in three grades - transparent all, translucent some with scatter, opaque none - and the view tells you the grade. **In the exam:** in material questions, judge by the image: clear means transparent, blurry means translucent, nothing means opaque. **Check yourself:** compare the walkthrough's window pair - the clear street and the buttered blur are the grades side by side. **Why students miss it:** students promote any glow to clear vision - the smudge test is a no to that; scattered light blurs the image. ## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that if they can see *any* light through an object, it must be transparent. They fail to realize that transparency requires the image to be *clear*, whereas translucent materials only allow light to pass while scattering it, preventing a clear image.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A science teacher asks a student to arrange three items in order of increasing light transmission: A wooden ruler, a sheet of tracing paper, and a clear plastic bag.
STEPS:
- 1: Classify the items: Ruler (opaque, 0%), Tracing paper (translucent, <100%), Bag (transparent, ~100%).
- 2: Determine order: Opaque < Translucent < Transparent.
- 3: Substitute objects: Wooden ruler < Tracing paper < Clear plastic bag.
- 4: Verify against definitions.
REASONING: Light transmission is the fraction of light allowed through; opaques block it, translucent scatter it, and transparent permit it.
ANSWER: Wooden ruler -> Tracing paper -> Clear plastic bag.
