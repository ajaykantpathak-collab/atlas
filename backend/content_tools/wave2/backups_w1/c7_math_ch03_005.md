## ELI5
The sabziwala's machine prints 1.450 kg for your potatoes and nobody blinks - because decimals are how metric units speak. One kilogram holds a thousand grams, so the 450 grams ride after the point as thousandths. The same trick folds centimetres into metres, millilitres into litres, paise into rupees: convert the small unit to its decimal seat and mixed units collapse into one clean number. Align the points, and these decimals add, subtract and compare like ordinary numbers.

## DEFINITION
Units of measurement and decimal notation involve expressing fractional parts of standard metric units (such as metres, kilograms, and litres) using decimal fractions and place value notation. This allows precise quantification of physical quantities that fall between whole numbers by extending the place value system to tenths, hundredths, and thousandths.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at a vegetable market in India buying 1 kg and 450 g of potatoes, and the shopkeeper's digital weighing machine shows $1.450\text{ kg}$. How does the machine turn $450\text{ grams}$ into a part of a kilogram? To figure this out, we recall from Class 6 fractions that $1\text{ kilogram} = 1000\text{ grams}$, meaning $1\text{ gram} = \frac{1}{1000}\text{ of a kilogram}$ or $0.001\text{ kg}$. By understanding that sub-units are fractional parts of whole base units, we can write any smaller unit as a decimal fraction of the larger base unit. The key insight is that the decimal point acts as a separator where digits to the right represent tenths ($\frac{1}{10}$), hundredths ($\frac{1}{100}$), and thousandths ($\frac{1}{1000}$) of the base unit.

**Takeaway:** metric units are decimal by design: milli into base means thousandths, centi into base means hundredths. **In the exam:** convert the small unit first, write one decimal, then operate with points aligned, carrying the unit into the answer. **Check yourself:** convert the potato weight into grams and back into a decimal of a kilogram, and check the round trip. **Why students miss it:** they divide by 100 where 1000 is due, drifting a decimal place - dangerous in doses and prices alike.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2

Since $1\text{ rupee} = 100\text{ paise}$, $5\text{ paise}$ is $\frac{5}{100}$ of a rupee, which occupies the hundredths place, making it $₹4.05$. ## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-world scenario involving capacity (litres and millilitres) where three liquid containers contain $2\text{ L } 45\text{ mL}$, $1.5\text{ L}$, and $750\text{ mL}$. Write their total volume in litres using decimal notation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Container 1 = $2.045\text{ L}$, Container 2 = $1.500\text{ L}$, Container 3 = $0.750\text{ L}$. Total = $2.045 + 1.500 + 0.750 = 4.295\text{ L}$. A poor answer reveals an inability to convert millilitres to litres ($1\text{ mL} = 0.001\text{ L}$) or misaligning place values during decimal addition.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently misalign place values when converting mixed sub-units directly into a single decimal number, writing $5\text{ kg } 8\text{ g}$ as $5.8\text{ kg}$ instead of $5.008\text{ kg}$. They treat the smaller unit number ($8$) as a tenths or hundredths value without accounting for the power of ten required by the conversion factor ($1000\text{ g} = 1\text{ kg}$), ignoring intermediate zero place holders.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to always write out the full fractional form first before converting to a decimal. For example, show that $5\text{ kg } 8\text{ g} = 5\text{ kg} + \frac{8}{1000}\text{ kg}$. Emphasize that since there are three zeros in $1000$, there must be three decimal places after the point, filling vacant place values with zeros to get $5.008\text{ kg}$.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Priya walks $2\text{ km } 45\text{ m}$ to school every day. Express this distance entirely in kilometres using decimal notation, and calculate the total distance she walks in a 5-day school week.

STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: Distance per day = $2\text{ km } 45\text{ m}$. Conversion factor: $1\text{ km} = 1000\text{ m}$, so $1\text{ m} = \frac{1}{1000}\text{ km} = 0.001\text{ km}$.
- step 2: Convert the metres part into kilometres by dividing by $1000$: $45\text{ m} = \frac{45}{1000}\text{ km} = 0.045\text{ km}$.
- step 3: Combine the whole kilometres and decimal kilometres: $2\text{ km} + 0.045\text{ km} = 2.045\text{ km}$ per day.
- step 4: Multiply the daily distance by $5$ for the weekly total: $2.045 \times 5 = 10.225\text{ km}$.
- step 5: Verify unit and calculation: $2000 \times 5 = 10000\text{ m}$ ($10\text{ km}$) plus $45 \times 5 = 225\text{ m}$ ($0.225\text{ km}$), yielding $10.225\text{ km}$.

REASONING: Since metres are a thousandth part of a kilometre, converting them requires placing the digit $45$ so that its last digit sits in the thousandths place. Adding this to the whole kilometres gives the correct single decimal number, which can then be multiplied normally.

ANSWER: $10.225\text{ km}$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper mixes $3\text{ kg } 50\text{ g}$ of cashew nuts with $1\text{ kg } 450\text{ g}$ of raisins. If he sells $2\text{ kg } 200\text{ g}$ of the mixture, what is the mass of the remaining mixture in kilograms?

STEPS:
- step 1: Convert all quantities to kilograms using decimals ($1\text{ g} = 0.001\text{ kg}$). Cashews = $3.050\text{ kg}$, Raisins = $1.450\text{ kg}$, Sold amount = $2.200\text{ kg}$.
- step 2: Calculate the total mass of the mixture by adding cashews and raisins: $3.050 + 1.450 = 4.500\text{ kg}$.
- step 3: Set up subtraction for the remaining mixture: $4.500 - 2.200$.
- step 4: Perform subtraction column by column: $4.500 - 2.200 = 2.300\text{ kg}$.
- step 5: Verify the result: Total mass in grams is $4500\text{ g}$, sold is $2200\text{ g}$, remaining is $4500 - 2200 = 2300\text{ g}$, which is $2.3\text{ kg}$.

REASONING: Converting all mixed units into consistent decimal fractions before performing arithmetic operations prevents place value errors and simplifies addition and subtraction.

ANSWER: $2.3\text{ kg}$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Rahul buys $3\text{ notebooks}$ at $₹45.50$ each, $2$ pens at $₹12.75$ each, and gives a $₹500$ note to the shopkeeper. How much change does he get back, and how can this total cost be expressed if currency sub-units are treated as fractional parts of a rupee?

STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate the cost of notebooks: $3 \times 45.50 = ₹136.50$.
- step 2: Calculate the cost of pens: $2 \times 12.75 = ₹25.50$.
- step 3: Add both costs to find the total bill: $136.50 + 25.50 = ₹162.00$.
- step 4: Subtract the total bill from the amount given ($₹500.00$) to find the change: $500.00 - 162.00 = ₹338.00$.
- step 5: Interpret paise as decimal fractions of a rupee: $₹162.00$ represents $162\text{ whole rupees}$ and $\frac{0}{100}$ of a rupee, confirming decimal place value alignment for currency.

REASONING: Money naturally uses decimal notation where the whole number represents rupees and the digits after the decimal point represent paise ($\frac{1}{100}$ of a rupee), requiring standard decimal multiplication and subtraction.

ANSWER: $₹338.00$

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A runner completes a race track where three consecutive legs measure $1\text{ km } 250\text{ m}$, $850\text{ m}$, and $2\text{ km } 5\text{ m}$. Convert all leg lengths to decimal kilometres, find the total distance of the race, and explain why keeping all measurements in the same decimal format prevents addition errors.
COMPETENCY: Translating and computing mixed metric units in multi-step word problems
STEPS:
- step 1: Convert Leg 1 to decimal km: $1\text{ km} + \frac{250}{1000}\text{ km} = 1.250\text{ km}$.
- step 2: Convert Leg 2 to decimal km: $\frac{850}{1000}\text{ km} = 0.850\text{ km}$.
- step 3: Convert Leg 3 to decimal km: $2\text{ km} + \frac{5}{1000}\text{ km} = 2.005\text{ km}$.
- step 4: Add all decimal lengths: $1.250 + 0.850 + 2.005 = 4.105\text{ km}$.
- step 5: Justify format consistency: Mixing raw metres ($850$) with decimal kilometres ($1.250$) without conversion leads to catastrophic place-value misalignment during addition.
ANSWER: Total distance is $4.105\text{ km}$. Converting all units to decimal kilometres aligns the decimal points, ensuring that tenths, hundredths, and thousandths of a kilometre are added to their exact matching place values.
