## ELI5
A Jaipur builder holds two roof beams and must prove them twins before cutting expensive wood: name the criterion - three sides, two sides with the caught angle, two angles with the bridging side, or the right-triangle special. Once congruence is certified, CPCTC opens the cupboard - corresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal - so any unknown side or angle in one twin is simply read off the other. In overlapping figures, the shared side counts for both, stated plainly like a borrowed rupee returned.

## DEFINITION
Applying congruence involves using known triangle congruence criteria (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS) and corresponding parts of congruent triangles (CPCTC) to prove geometric properties, find unknown side lengths, or determine missing angle measures in complex figures.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can decompose a complex geometric figure into individual triangles, identify valid congruence criteria (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS), write formal corresponding vertex matches, and apply CPCTC to solve for unknown lengths or angles in multi-step problems.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the application of triangle congruence criteria and CPCTC to multi-step geometric problems, proofs of line bisectors, angle bisectors, and isosceles properties. Does NOT cover similarity of triangles or circle theorems.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are a builder at a construction site in Jaipur and you need to verify if two triangular support beams for a roof are exact twins so that one can replace the other. You know their side lengths, but you need to prove whether they match completely before cutting expensive wood. To do this, you start by writing down the given information like side lengths and angles marked on your blueprint diagram. You connect this to what you learned about SSS, SAS, ASA, and RHS criteria for triangle congruence. The key insight is that once two triangles are proven congruent, every single corresponding side and angle must be equal—a principle known as CPCTC (Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent)—allowing you to solve for any unknown measurement.

**Takeaway:** congruence criteria certify twins; CPCTC then transfers any corresponding side or angle. **In the exam:** pick the valid criterion, write the vertex-matched statement, and quote CPCTC for each unknown. **Check yourself:** prove the two beams congruent by SSS, then read off a missing angle by correspondence. **Why students miss it:** they quote CPCTC before establishing congruence, or misread which parts correspond. Write the vertex-matched statement before quoting a single transfer, and the proof reads like a ledger.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often mix up the corresponding order of vertices when applying CPCTC in complex figures. For example, if $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle FED$, a student will incorrectly equate side $BC$ to side $ED$ instead of side $ED$ matching $BC$ only if the letters align properly, or they will match $AB$ with $FD$ instead of $FE$.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to trace the triangles on their paper and write the congruence statement letter-by-letter, matching index positions: 1st with 1st, 2nd with 2nd, 3rd with 3rd. Say: "Put your finger on vertex A, then on vertex F. If your congruence statement says $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle FED$, then AB must pair with FE, not FD." Have them redraw the triangles side-by-side in the exact same orientation before writing CPCTC equations.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: In a local park in Delhi, a triangular flower bed $ABC$ has side $AB = 5\text{ m}$ and side $AC = 5\text{ m}$. A path runs from vertex $A$ to the midpoint $D$ on side $BC$. Prove that $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle ACD$ and find the length of angle $\angle ADB$ if $AD$ is perpendicular to $BC$.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given information in $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle ACD$: $AB = AC = 5\text{ m}$ (given), $AD$ is a common side for both triangles ($AD = AD$, Reflexive Property), and $AD \perp BC$ gives $\angle ADB = \angle ADC = 90^\circ$.
- Step 2: Choose the appropriate congruence criterion. Since we have a right angle, a hypotenuse ($AB = AC$), and a side ($AD = AD$), we use the RHS (Right angle-Hypotenuse-Side) congruence criterion.
- Step 3: Write the formal congruence statement matching corresponding vertices: $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle ACD$ by RHS.
- Step 4: Apply CPCTC to determine unknown angle measures. Since $\angle ADB$ and $\angle ADC$ form a linear pair along line $BC$ and are equal by CPCTC ($\angle ADB = \angle ADC$), we have $\angle ADB + \angle ADC = 180^\circ$, so $2 \cdot \angle ADB = 180^\circ$.
- Step 5: Compute the final angle: $\angle ADB = \frac{180^\circ}{2} = 90^\circ$.
REASONING: We establish congruence using the RHS criterion because the triangles are right-angled with equal hypotenuses and a shared leg. Then, CPCTC ensures that corresponding angles are equal, confirming the perpendicularity condition.
ANSWER: $\angle ADB = 90^\circ$ and $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle ACD$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Two triangular metal plates used in a bridge structure in Mumbai have $\angle B = \angle E = 90^\circ$, hypotenuse $AC = DF = 13\text{ cm}$, and side $AB = DE = 5\text{ cm}$. If side $BC$ is given algebraically as $(3x - 1)\text{ cm}$ and side $EF = 8\text{ cm}$, find the value of $x$.

STEPS:
- Step 1: List the given values for $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DEF$: $\angle B = \angle E = 90^\circ$ (Right angles), hypotenuse $AC = DF = 13\text{ cm}$, and side $AB = DE = 5\text{ cm}$.
- Step 2: Check the congruence condition. We have a right angle, the hypotenuse, and one side equal, so $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$ by the RHS congruence criterion.
- Step 3: Use CPCTC to equate corresponding sides: side $BC$ corresponds to side $EF$, therefore $BC = EF$.
- Step 4: Substitute the algebraic expression and numerical value into the equation: $3x - 1 = 8$.
- Step 5: Solve the linear equation: add $1$ to both sides giving $3x = 8 + 1 \Rightarrow 3x = 9$, then divide by $3$ to get $x = \frac{9}{3} = 3$.
REASONING: After proving triangle congruence via RHS, CPCTC dictates that corresponding legs like $BC$ and $EF$ must be equal. Setting them equal creates a linear equation that we solve using standard algebraic transposition.
ANSWER: $x = 3$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A triangular plot of land in Chennai has a fence dividing it from vertex $P$ to a point $S$ on side $QR$. It is given that $PQ = PR$ and $PS$ bisects angle $\angle QPR$. If $QS = (2y + 3)\text{ m}$ and $SR = (3y - 1)\text{ m}$, find the total length of the base $QR$.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given information for $\triangle PQS$ and $\triangle PRS$: $PQ = PR$ (given), angle $\angle QPS = \angle RPS$ (since $PS$ bisects $\angle QPR$), and $PS = PS$ (common side, Reflexive Property).
- Step 2: Select the congruence criterion. We have two sides and the included angle equal ($PQ = PR$, $\angle QPS = \angle RPS$, and $PS = PS$), so $\triangle PQS \cong \triangle PRS$ by the SAS congruence criterion.
- Step 3: Apply CPCTC to the corresponding base segments: $QS = SR$.
- Step 4: Substitute the algebraic expressions: $2y + 3 = 3y - 1$. Solve for $y$ by subtracting $2y$ from both sides: $3 = y - 1$, then adding $1$ gives $y = 4$.
- Step 5: Substitute $y = 4$ back into the segment expressions: $QS = 2(4) + 3 = 8 + 3 = 11\text{ m}$, and $SR = 3(4) - 1 = 12 - 1 = 11\text{ m}$. Calculate total base length $QR = QS + SR = 11 + 11 = 22\text{ m}$.
REASONING: SAS congruence proves $\triangle PQS \cong \triangle PRS$, allowing us to use CPCTC to equate parts $QS$ and $SR$. Solving the resulting linear equation gives $y$, which we use to compute individual lengths and sum them for the total base.
ANSWER: Total length $QR = 22\text{ m}$
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A carpenter builds two triangular wooden roof supports, $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle PQR$, where $AB = PQ$, $BC = QR$, and median $AM$ is equal to median $PN$. The carpenter claims that because three parts are equal, the triangles must be congruent by SSS. Analyze the carpenter's claim, identify any error in reasoning, and explain the correct approach using congruence.
COMPETENCY: Error identification, reasoning justification, and application of triangle congruence.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand the given parts: two sides and a median are equal ($AB = PQ$, $BC = QR$, $AM = PN$).
- Step 2: Analyze the carpenter's claim of SSS: SSS requires all *three full sides* of the triangle to be equal ($AB = PQ$, $BC = QR$, and $AC = PR$). A median is not a full side.
- Step 3: Correct the error: Explain that equality of a median does not directly equal the third side $AC$ and $PR$ without further proof.
- Step 4: Formulate the correct conclusion: To prove congruence, one must first establish congruence of smaller sub-triangles formed by the median (e.g., $\triangle ABM \cong \triangle PQN$) before applying CPCTC to obtain full side equality.
ANSWER: The carpenter's claim is incorrect because SSS requires all three sides of the triangle to be equal ($AC = PR$), not a median. The median only helps prove sub-triangle congruence first.
DIFFICULTY: hard
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: In a town layout design, two triangular parks $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DEF$ are laid out such that altitude $AP \perp BC$ is equal to altitude $DQ \perp EF$, and base $BC = EF$. If it is also given that side $AB = DE$, prove that $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$ and explain how CPCTC helps city planners verify that the two parks have identical area footprints.
COMPETENCY: Multi-step geometric proof and real-world application of congruence and area correspondence.
STEPS:
- Step 1: List given properties: $AP = DQ$ (equal altitudes), $BC = EF$ (equal bases), $AB = DE$ (equal sides), and right angles at $P$ and $Q$.
- Step 2: Consider right-angled sub-triangles $\triangle APB$ and $\triangle DQE$: we have hypotenuse $AB = DE$ and leg $AP = DQ$, with $\angle APB = \angle DQE = 90^\circ$.
- Step 3: Apply RHS congruence criterion to sub-triangles: $\triangle APB \cong \triangle DQE$, which by CPCTC gives $\angle B = \angle E$ and segment $BP = EQ$.
- Step 4: Use the full base relation $BC = EF$ with subtraction/addition to show remaining segments are equal, or apply SAS on the full triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DEF$ using $AB = DE$, $\angle B = \angle E$, and $BC = EF$.
- Step 5: Conclude congruence ($\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$) and interpret via CPCTC: congruent triangles have identical corresponding dimensions, meaning identical area footprints for the city planners.
ANSWER: By proving sub-triangle congruence using RHS and then applying SAS to the main triangles, we establish $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$. CPCTC ensures all corresponding dimensions match, proving identical area footprints.
