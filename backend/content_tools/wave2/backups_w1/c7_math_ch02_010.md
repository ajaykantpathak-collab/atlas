## ELI5
Toss three items into your tiffin - the order never matters: a notebook at 15 rupees plus a pen at 10 plus an eraser at 5 lands on the same bill whichever end you start from. That is commutativity: swapping order changes nothing in addition and multiplication. Associativity is its cousin: regrouping with brackets - add the first pair then the third, or the last pair first - also leaves the total untouched. But try either trick on subtraction or division and the answer breaks.

## DEFINITION
Commutativity states that changing the order of numbers in addition or multiplication does not change the result (e.g., $a + b = b + a$), while associativity states that changing the grouping of numbers does not affect the result (e.g., $(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$).

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are buying notebooks for ₹15, pens for ₹10, and erasers for ₹5 at the school stationery shop. Does it matter whether you add the cost of the notebook first or the eraser first when calculating the total bill? Just like addition of whole numbers and integers learned in earlier grades, operations have hidden shortcuts called properties. When you add or multiply numbers, rearranging the order or regrouping them with brackets can make mental math much faster. The key insight of this node is learning how to recognize when you can swap or regroup numbers without changing the final value.

**Takeaway:** order and grouping are completely free for addition and multiplication; the two properties are a standing licence to rearrange numbers into friendly, easy pairs. **In the exam:** in 'simplify by rearranging' questions, name the property used at each move; in true-or-false items, test subtraction and division first as the failing cases. **Check yourself:** regroup the shopping bill into pairs that make round numbers, then confirm the total is genuinely unchanged by adding in the original order too. **Why students miss it:** they extend the licence to subtraction and division too, where regrouping silently flips the result and costs the whole mark.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which of the following mathematical statements illustrates the associative property of multiplication?
A) $4 \times (5 \times 2) = (4 \times 5) \times 2$
B) $4 \times (5 + 2) = (4 \times 5) + (4 \times 2)$
C) $4 \times 5 = 5 \times 4$
D) $4 \times 1 = 4$
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is A. Statement A shows that changing the grouping of factors using parentheses does not change the product. A student who chooses B confuses associativity with the distributive property, while a student who chooses C selects commutativity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Does the associative property hold true for subtraction? Test with the numbers 10, 4, and 2.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, subtraction is not associative. Testing this gives $(10 - 4) - 2 = 6 - 2 = 4$, but $10 - (4 - 2) = 10 - 2 = 8$, and since $4 \neq 8$, the grouping changes the result. A student who says yes likely forgot to compute both sides independently or incorrectly subtracted left-to-right.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate the value of the expression $37 + 48 + 13$ by smartly rearranging the terms using the commutative and associative properties. Show your steps.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps: 1. Rewrite using commutativity to place numbers with compatible units together: $37 + 13 + 48$. 2. Apply associativity to group them: $(37 + 13) + 48$. 3. Compute inside the brackets: $50 + 48$. 4. Final calculation: $98$. A student who computes sequentially without rearranging misses the opportunity to use properties and often makes simple addition errors.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze why the expression $(24 \div 4) \div 2$ does not yield the same result as $24 \div (4 \div 2)$. What property is failing here?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Evaluating the first expression gives $6 \div 2 = 3$, while the second gives $24 \div 2 = 12$. Since $3 \neq 12$, division is not associative. A student who thinks they are equal fails to understand that division depends strictly on the order and grouping of operations.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the validity of the student's claim: "Since multiplication is commutative, $(a - b) \times c$ is equal to $(b - a) \times c$." Is this correct or incorrect, and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The claim is incorrect. While multiplication *itself* is commutative, the subtraction term $(a - b)$ is not commutative because $a - b \neq b - a$ (they differ by a negative sign). A student who agrees with the claim incorrectly assumes that properties of outer operations override the inner components of an expression.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create an arithmetic expression involving four different integers where using the associative and commutative properties allows you to compute the answer entirely in your head without paper. Write the steps of your mental regrouping.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An example expression is $16 + 29 + 14 + 21$. Steps: 1. Group complementary numbers: $(16 + 14) + (29 + 21)$. 2. Simplify inside brackets: $30 + 50$. 3. Final answer: $80$. A poor answer would list random numbers that do not form easy pairs or fail to show how properties simplify the work.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often apply commutative or associative properties blindly to subtraction and division expressions, writing things like $15 - 8 = 8 - 15$ or treating $(12 \div 4) \div 2$ as equal to $12 \div (4 \div 2)$. Another common error is mixing up the distributive property with the associative property when parentheses are involved.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that commutativity and associativity only work for addition and multiplication. Write out subtraction and division examples side by side, such as $5 - 3$ versus $3 - 5$, and ask them to check if the answers match. Show them that changing the order or grouping breaks the operation for minus and division signs.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Simplify the expression $25 \times 17 \times 4$ by rearranging the factors using properties of operations to compute it mentally.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given expression: $25 \times 17 \times 4$.
- Step 2: Apply the commutative property of multiplication to swap the middle and last factors: $25 \times 4 \times 17$.
- Step 3: Apply the associative property of multiplication to group the first two numbers: $(25 \times 4) \times 17$.
- Step 4: Compute inside the parentheses: $100 \times 17$.
- Step 5: Perform the final multiplication to get the result with proper units: $1700$.
REASONING: By swapping 17 and 4, we bring 25 and 4 together because their product is a clean multiple of 100, which makes mental calculation trivial compared to multiplying $25 \times 17$ first.
ANSWER: 1700
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Calculate the total sum of money collected if four students contributed ₹18, ₹25, ₹12, and ₹75 respectively, using properties to group compatible amounts.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Formulate the arithmetic expression for the total sum: $18 + 25 + 12 + 75$.
- Step 2: Use the commutative property of addition to rearrange terms: $18 + 12 + 25 + 75$.
- Step 3: Use the associative property of addition to group numbers that sum to multiples of 10: $(18 + 12) + (25 + 75)$.
- Step 4: Evaluate each group separately: $30 + 100$.
- Step 5: Add the grouped totals to find the final amount in rupees: $130$ rupees.
REASONING: Grouping numbers that end in complementary digits ($8+2=10$ and $5+5=10$) eliminates carry-over errors and simplifies mental arithmetic.
ANSWER: ₹130
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Evaluate the integer expression $-14 + 39 + 14 + (-9)$ efficiently by applying properties.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write down the expression: $-14 + 39 + 14 + (-9)$.
- Step 2: Use the commutative property of addition to bring opposite or matching numbers together: $(-14 + 14) + (39 + (-9))$.
- Step 3: Apply the associative property to formalize the groupings: $[(-14 + 14)] + [(39 + (-9))]$.
- Step 4: Evaluate the additive inverse pair in the first bracket: $0 + (39 + (-9))$.
- Step 5: Evaluate the remaining addition: $0 + 30$.
- Step 6: State the final calculated value: $30$.
REASONING: Bringing $-14$ and its additive inverse $14$ together eliminates them to zero first, turning a messy four-term integer problem into a simple addition with zero.
ANSWER: 30
