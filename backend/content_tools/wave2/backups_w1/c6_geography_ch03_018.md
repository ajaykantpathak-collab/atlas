## ELI5
Your rice comes from the wet, warm south and east; your roti's wheat from the cooler north — the menu was written by climate. Every crop is a picky guest with favourite clothes: rice loves a raincoat of heavy rain and a warm sweater of heat; wheat prefers a light jacket of cool, seasonal weather. Sow a crop where its wardrobe does not match and it sulks, then fails; match it and it thrives. Nature acts as a filter across India: a crop can only prosper where the climate menu offers exactly what it needs — which is why the food map and the climate map overlap.

## DEFINITION
Climate and agriculture describe the relationship where specific weather conditions—such as temperature, rainfall, and sunlight—directly determine which crops can successfully grow in a particular region. Different plants have unique environmental requirements to sprout, grow, and reach harvest.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think crops can grow anywhere if farmers just "work hard enough." They fail to realize that climate is a biological limit, not just a preference. For example, they might think rice can grow in the Thar Desert simply because someone could carry water there, ignoring that the heat and humidity levels are entirely different from the natural habitat required for the plant.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A farmer in a high-temperature, low-rainfall area (30 cm/year) wants to grow crops. Should he choose rice or wheat?
STEPS:
- Step 1: List the rainfall requirements for rice (>100 cm) and wheat (50-75 cm).
- Step 2: Compare the farmer's rainfall (30 cm) with these needs.
- Step 3: Determine if either crop's minimum threshold is met.
- Step 4: Conclude that both crops will fail without massive external water support.
REASONING: Agriculture is constrained by the "limiting factor" principle—if one requirement (rain) isn't met, the plant cannot grow.
ANSWER: Neither is suitable, as 30 cm is below the threshold for both.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In the Gangetic Plain, it is warm and receives 80 cm of rain. Is this better for rice or wheat?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Check rice needs (warmth + >100 cm rain).
- Step 2: Check wheat needs (cool + 50-75 cm rain).
- Step 3: Compare region traits (warm + 80 cm rain) to crop traits.
- Step 4: Identify that the rainfall is too low for rice but acceptable for wheat.
REASONING: Agriculture is a process of matching regional climate data to crop requirements.
ANSWER: Wheat is the better choice because the rainfall level fits its 50-75 cm requirement range.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Why do we see cotton growing in warm climates, and how does this differ from the wheat-growing cycle?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the temperature requirement for cotton (warm/hot).
- Step 2: Identify the temperature requirement for wheat (cool/temperate).
- Step 3: Map these to Indian seasons (Wheat = Rabi/Winter, Cotton = Warm/Summer-Autumn).
- Step 4: Analyze the growth cycle duration.
- Step 5: Conclude that crops are "seasonal indicators."
REASONING: By comparing the two, we see that Indian agriculture is a "relay race" where different crops take over based on what the weather allows.
ANSWER: Cotton requires warmth, which is why it grows during the summer months, whereas wheat requires the cool winter, creating two distinct farming seasons.
