## ELI5
Three notebooks at 45 rupees, two pens at 15 rupees, and a 10-rupee discount on the whole bill: the story becomes 3 x 45 + 2 x 15 - 10. Turning words into expressions is translation - each phrase is an operation, and phrases like 'the sum of' or 'the difference between' demand brackets to guard their order. Skip the brackets and the discount lands on the wrong item. Read the sentence, name each operation, bracket the groupings, and the mathematics writes itself.

## DEFINITION
Writing expressions from word problems involves translating verbal descriptions of real-life situations into mathematical expressions using numbers, operation symbols (+, -, ×, ÷), and grouping symbols (parentheses, brackets) in the correct operational order. It bridges the gap between everyday language and the language of arithmetic and algebra.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you go to a stationery shop in your town and buy 3 notebooks for ₹45 each and 2 pens for ₹15 each, but the shopkeeper gives you a ₹10 discount on the total bill. How do you write this entire story as a single mathematical expression instead of doing it in separate messy steps? To begin, you break down the sentence into smaller numerical chunks: the cost of notebooks is $3 \times 45$, the cost of pens is $2 \times 15$, and you need to add these two costs before subtracting the ₹10 discount. Building on our work with order of operations and brackets from earlier nodes, we know that grouping parts of the sentence using parentheses ensures the operations are performed in the correct order. The key insight is identifying cue words like "sum", "product", "increased by", "decreased by", "times", and "shared equally" to translate words directly into mathematical symbols.

**Takeaway:** translating words to expressions is operation-mapping: 'sum' adds, 'difference' subtracts, 'product' multiplies, and group-phrases demand brackets. **In the exam:** read the sentence twice, underline the operation phrases, then bracket each grouped quantity before writing the line. **Check yourself:** convert the discount story into an expression, then back-translate it into words to test yourself. **Why students miss it:** they write operations in story order, ignoring that 'from the sum subtract the product' flips the sequence.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which arithmetic expression correctly represents the statement: "Subtract the product of 7 and 4 from the sum of 15 and 9"?
A) $(15 + 9) - (7 \times 4)$
B) $15 + 9 - 7 \times 4$
C) $(7 \times 4) - (15 + 9)$
D) $15 + (9 - 7) \times 4$
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is A: $(15 + 9) - (7 \times 4)$. 
A student who chooses B might understand the operations but fail to use brackets to establish the correct priority, which violates the phrase "subtract *from* the sum". A student who chooses C has reversed the order of subtraction, misunderstanding that "subtract A from B" means $B - A$, not $A - B$.

A) Yes, it is completely correct.
B) No, because division takes precedence over addition, so it will divide 8 by 4 first instead of dividing the total sum.
C) No, because the numbers should be multiplied instead.
D) No, because 12 should be divided first.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct answer is B.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A shopkeeper had 250 kilograms of rice. He sold 3 bags of rice weighing 25 kg each on Monday, and 4 bags weighing 20 kg each on Tuesday. Write an expression for the remaining rice in kilograms, and compute its value.
A) $250 - (3 \times 25 + 4 \times 20) = 115$ kg
B) $(250 - 3) \times 25 + 4 \times 20 = 6230$ kg
C) $250 - 3 \times 25 - 4 \times 20 = 115$ kg (without brackets)
D) $250 + (3 \times 25) - (4 \times 20) = 245$ kg
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is A. 
Working steps:
1. Identify initial stock: $250$ kg.
2. Calculate total sold on Monday: $3 \times 25$ kg.
3. Calculate total sold on Tuesday: $4 \times 20$ kg.
4. Total sold = $(3 \times 25) + (4 \times 20) = 75 + 80 = 155$ kg.
5. Remaining rice = $250 - 155 = 115$ kg.
Expressed as a single expression with proper grouping: $250 - ((3 \times 25) + (4 \times 20))$. 
A student who chooses B has completely misinterpreted the word problem by grouping the initial stock with the number of bags. A student who chooses D added Monday's sales instead of subtracting them from the total stock.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the two expressions written by two students for the problem: "Divide the difference between 50 and 10 by the sum of 2 and 3."
Student X writes: $(50 - 10) \div (2 + 3)$
Student Y writes: $50 - 10 \div 2 + 3$
Which student's expression correctly matches the word problem, and what is the mathematical flaw in the other?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Student X's expression is correct. 
Explanation: The phrase "the difference between 50 and 10" must be computed first, requiring parentheses: $(50 - 10)$. Similarly, "the sum of 2 and 3" must be computed as a single divisor, requiring parentheses: $(2 + 3)$. Student Y's expression lacks brackets entirely, meaning division ($10 \div 2$) would be performed first according to order of operations, completely altering the meaning of the word problem. A student who thinks Student Y is correct does not understand how grouping phrases in language translate to parentheses in arithmetic.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate if the following expression correctly translates the word problem: "Meera had ₹500. She bought 4 notebooks at ₹45 each and 3 pens at ₹12 each. How much money is left?"
Expression: $500 - 4 \times 45 + 3 \times 12$
Is this expression valid? If not, identify the exact error and write the corrected expression.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The expression is INCORRECT. 
Explanation: Writing $500 - 4 \times 45 + 3 \times 12$ means Meera subtracts the cost of notebooks and then *adds* the cost of pens back to her remaining money, which is wrong because she spent money on both items. Both expenses must be grouped together and subtracted from ₹500.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently omit necessary brackets when translating phrases containing "the sum of" or "the difference of" followed by multiplication or division. For example, when translating "Multiply 4 by the sum of 5 and 3", students often write $4 \times 5 + 3$ instead of $4 \times (5 + 3)$. This computational error happens because they translate words from left to right mechanically without considering that the addition must happen *before* the multiplication according to the sentence structure.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student makes this bracket error, point directly to the order of operations: ask them, "If you write $4 \times 5 + 3$, what does standard arithmetic order make you calculate first?" They will reply, "Multiplication ($4 \times 5$)." Then ask, "Does your sentence want you to multiply 4 by 5 first, or find the sum of 5 and 3 first?" Guide them to see that to force addition to happen first, they must wrap the addition in parentheses: $4 \times (5 + 3)$. Have them evaluate both versions to see the different results.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Priya went to a book fair with ₹1000. She bought 3 comic books for ₹120 each and 2 science magazines for ₹85 each. She also received a cashback of ₹50 on her total purchase. Write a single arithmetic expression to represent the total money left with Priya, and calculate the final amount.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify all given financial quantities and actions: Initial money = ₹1000, 3 comic books at ₹120 each, 2 science magazines at ₹85 each, cashback of ₹50 (which adds money back).
- Step 2: Translate the cost of comic books into a product expression: $3 \times 120$. Translate the cost of magazines into a product expression: $2 \times 85$.
- Step 3: Combine total money spent using addition inside a bracket: $(3 \times 120) + (2 \times 85)$. Subtract this from the initial amount, then add the cashback: $1000 - ((3 \times 120) + (2 \times 85)) + 50$.
- Step 4: Compute step by step following order of operations: 
  $3 \times 120 = 360$
  $2 \times 85 = 170$
  Total spent = $360 + 170 = 530$
  Remaining money before cashback = $1000 - 530 = 470$
  Final amount with cashback = $470 + 50 = 520$.
- Step 5: Verify the result: Priya started with ₹1000, spent ₹530 (leaving ₹470), and got ₹50 back, resulting in ₹520.
REASONING: Breaking down the word problem into individual financial components allows us to construct sub-expressions. Grouping the total spending with brackets ensures we calculate the sum of all purchases before subtracting it from the starting amount.
ANSWER: ₹520
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A water tank contains 500 litres of water. On Monday, 12 families withdrew 15 litres each. On Tuesday, a pipe leaked out 80 litres, but a motor pumped in 250 litres. Write an expression for the final volume of water in the tank, and calculate the result.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify initial volume = 500 litres. Identify withdrawals and additions: 12 families took 15 litres each (subtraction), a leak lost 80 litres (subtraction), and a motor pumped in 250 litres (addition).
- Step 2: Write the total withdrawal by families as a product: $12 \times 15$. 
- Step 3: Combine all changes into a single expression starting from the initial volume: $500 - (12 \times 15) - 80 + 250$.
- Step 4: Evaluate the expression step by step using order of operations:
  First, calculate the product: $12 \times 15 = 180$.
  Substitute back: $500 - 180 - 80 + 250$.
  Perform subtraction from left to right: $500 - 180 = 320$.
  Next subtraction: $320 - 80 = 240$.
  Final addition: $240 + 250 = 490$.
- Step 5: Verify units and result: The final volume of water in the tank is 490 litres.
REASONING: Each withdrawal or loss is represented by subtraction, and each addition of water is represented by addition. Evaluating strictly from left to right after multiplication handles sequential daily events correctly.
ANSWER: 490 litres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A school ordered sports equipment. They bought 5 footballs at ₹450 each and 4 cricket bats at ₹600 each. The total bill had a 10% tax-like service charge of ₹200 added, but because they were a school, the shopkeeper gave a flat ₹300 discount on the final combined amount. If 5 teachers share the final cost equally, write an expression for what each teacher pays, and calculate the amount.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the components: Cost of footballs = $5 \times 450$, cost of bats = $4 \times 600$, service charge = $+200$, discount = $-300$, divided among 5 teachers = $\div 5$.
- Step 2: Formulate the total cost inside a large set of brackets before division: $[(5 \times 450) + (4 \times 600) + 200 - 300] \div 5$.
- Step 3: Compute the products inside the inner brackets:
  $5 \times 450 = 2250$
  $4 \times 600 = 2400$
- Step 4: Substitute products into the expression and evaluate inside the square brackets:
  $[2250 + 2400 + 200 - 300]$
  $= [4650 + 200 - 300]$
  $= [4850 - 300]$
  $= 4550$
- Step 5: Perform the final division by the number of teachers:
  $4550 \div 5 = 910$.
- Step 6: Verify the final amount: Each teacher pays ₹910.
REASONING: Multi-step financial word problems require nested brackets or clear order precedence. The entire net cost must be calculated as a single numerator before division distributes the burden equally among the teachers.
ANSWER: ₹910 per teacher
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Rahul and Sneha were asked to write an expression for: "Multiply the sum of 14 and 6 by 3, and then subtract 10." 
Rahul wrote: $14 + 6 \times 3 - 10$
Sneha wrote: $(14 + 6) \times 3 - 10$
Who is correct? Explain the mathematical error in the incorrect expression by showing what value it evaluates to, and compare it with the correct value.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and error analysis
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the verbal phrase "sum of 14 and 6" and realize it requires parentheses: $(14 + 6)$.
- Step 2: Analyze the instruction "multiply the sum by 3": $(14 + 6) \times 3$.
- Step 3: Analyze the final instruction "then subtract 10": $(14 + 6) \times 3 - 10$.
- Step 4: Evaluate Sneha's correct expression: $(20) \times 3 - 10 = 60 - 10 = 50$.
- Step 5: Evaluate Rahul's incorrect expression: $14 + (6 \times 3) - 10 = 14 + 18 - 10 = 22$.
ANSWER: Sneha is correct. Rahul's expression fails because he did not put brackets around the sum of 14 and 6, which caused multiplication ($6 \times 3$) to happen before addition, yielding 22 instead of the required 50.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A cooperative dairy collects milk from two villages. Village A provides 45 litres of milk twice a day, and Village B provides 60 litres of milk twice a day. Out of the total daily collection, 15 litres are kept for the local village school, and the remaining milk is packed into packets of 2 litres each. Write a single arithmetic expression that calculates the total number of packets produced in one day, and compute the final number of packets.
COMPETENCY: Multi-step translation and proportional packing in real-world contexts
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate daily collection for Village A: $2 \times 45$ litres.
- Step 2: Calculate daily collection for Village B: $2 \times 60$ litres.
- Step 3: Combine total collection and subtract the milk kept for the school, enclosing this entire numerator in brackets: $(2 \times 45 + 2 \times 60) - 15$.
- Step 4: Divide the remaining milk by the packet size (2 litres) by enclosing the entire preceding expression in outer brackets: $((2 \times 45 + 2 \times 60) - 15) \div 2$.
- Step 5: Compute the value step by step:
  $2 \times 45 = 90$
  $2 \times 60 = 120$
  Total collection = $90 + 120 = 210$ litres
  Remaining after school share = $210 - 15 = 195$ litres
  Number of packets = $195 \div 2 = 97.5$ packets.
ANSWER: Expression: $((2 \times 45 + 2 \times 60) - 15) \div 2$. Final calculation yields 97.5 packets (or 97 full packets with 1 litre remaining).
DIFFICULTY: hard
