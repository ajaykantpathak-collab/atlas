## ELI5
Light is a disciplined traveller: through one uniform transparent medium it walks perfectly straight - rectilinear propagation. See it in the sunlight beam streaming through a dusty window, the projector's shaft in a dark hall, the shadow's knife-edge. A ray is just the line drawn along that path. Strightness is why shadows are sharp, why pinhole cameras invert, and why you cannot see around corners without help.

## DEFINITION
Light travels in straight lines in a homogeneous, transparent medium, a property known as rectilinear propagation of light. This path can be represented by a ray of light, which has no SI unit as it is a geometric representation of the direction of light energy flow.

Look through the final hole and you will see the flame only when all three holes are aligned in a straight line. The key principle governing this behavior is rectilinear propagation, meaning light rays always move along straight-line paths in any uniform medium.

For example, they believe you can hear someone shouting around a corner, so you should also be able to see them around a corner with a mirrorless tube.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Three perforated cardboard cards A, B, and C are placed at distances of 0.2 m, 0.5 m, and 0.9 m from a table lamp. When their holes are adjusted to lie along a straight line, a bright spot is seen on a screen placed at 1.2 m. If card B is displaced sideways by 0.05 m, what happens to the light spot on the screen?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the initial condition where all holes are collinear, allowing light rays to pass unimpeded due to rectilinear propagation.
- Step 2: Note the displacement of card B by 0.05 m, which breaks the collinearity of the pinholes.
- Step 3: Apply the physical principle that light travels strictly in straight lines and cannot make a jog or turn to pass through a displaced hole.
- Step 4: Conclude that the light beam is completely blocked at card B.
- Step 5: Verify that no light spot appears on the screen (the spot disappears).
REASONING: Because light rays cannot bend around obstacles or make sharp sideways turns inside a uniform medium, displacing card B blocks the linear path completely.
ANSWER: The light spot on the screen disappears completely because the straight-line path is obstructed.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A straight light ray travels through air and strikes a flat glass slab at normal incidence (perpendicularly). If the thickness of the glass slab is 0.015 m, calculate the total straight-line distance the light ray travels inside the glass slab.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given values: slab thickness $d = 0.015\text{ m}$, angle of incidence is normal ($0^\circ$).
- Step 2: Recall the law of rectilinear propagation: light continues along a straight line when entering perpendicularly without deviation.
- Step 3: Set up the geometric path length equal to the thickness of the medium traversed.
- Step 4: Compute the distance: $s = d = 0.015\text{ m}$.
- Step 5: Verify that the unit is in meters and the physical behavior matches normal incidence transmission.
REASONING: Normal incidence means the light ray enters the medium without bending or changing direction, continuing along the same straight line across the thickness of the slab.
ANSWER: 0.015 m straight-line distance inside the slab.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A vertical pole of height 3.0 m casts a shadow of length 4.0 m on level ground when sunlight strikes it at a certain angle. Using the straight-line propagation of light rays, determine the length of the hypotenuse path taken by the light ray grazing the top of the pole to the tip of its shadow on the ground.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given perpendicular height (pole $h = 3.0\text{ m}$) and base (shadow length $b = 4.0\text{ m}$).
- Step 2: Recognize that light rays form a right-angled triangle with the pole and the ground due to rectilinear propagation.
- Step 3: Apply Pythagoras theorem for the ray path: $\text{Hypotenuse } c = \sqrt{h^2 + b^2}$.
- Step 4: Substitute values with units: $c = \sqrt{(3.0\text{ m})^2 + (4.0\text{ m})^2} = \sqrt{9.0\text{ m}^2 + 16.0\text{ m}^2} = \sqrt{25.0\text{ m}^2}$.
- Step 5: Compute the final value: $c = 5.0\text{ m}$ with unit analysis confirming meters.
REASONING: Sunrays travel in straight lines, creating a geometric right-angled triangle where the light ray itself forms the hypotenuse connecting the top of the pole to the tip of the shadow.
ANSWER: 5.0 m

QUESTION: A student looks through a hollow pipe at a glowing bulb. When the pipe is straight, the bulb is visible. When the pipe is bent midway into an angle of 45 degrees, the bulb is no longer visible. If an internal plane mirror is placed precisely at the 45-degree bend facing the correct angle, the bulb becomes visible again. Does this experiment disprove the rectilinear propagation of light? Justify your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Analyzing complex optical setups combining rectilinear propagation and reflection.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the first two scenarios (straight pipe vs bent pipe) where light travels in straight lines and is blocked by the bend.
- Step 2: Analyze the third scenario where a mirror is introduced at the bend.
- Step 3: Identify that reflection involves light bouncing off a surface while still obeying straight-line travel before and after the bounce.
- Step 4: Conclude whether rectilinear propagation is violated or maintained.
ANSWER: No, this does not disprove rectilinear propagation. Light still travels in straight lines; however, the mirror changes the direction of the straight-line rays by reflection. The light travels in a straight line from the bulb to the mirror, and then in another straight line from the mirror to the eye.
