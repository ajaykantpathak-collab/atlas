## ELI5
Meet your mirror double: same size, standing as far behind the glass as you stand before it, left-right flipped, and - here is the trick - not really there. The plane mirror's image is virtual: rays only appear to come from behind, so no screen can catch your twin. Comb your hair rightwards and the image combs leftwards. Every bathroom glass runs this same quiet illusion, five properties deep.

## DEFINITION
The image formed by a plane mirror is virtual, erect, of the exact same size as the object, located as far behind the mirror as the object is in front of it, and laterally inverted. In physics, these properties describe the characteristics of light reflection off a smooth, flat reflective surface measured in units of distance (meters, m) and angles (degrees, °).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the properties of images formed by single flat (plane) mirrors in air, including lateral inversion and object-image distance relationships. Does NOT cover curved spherical mirrors (concave/convex) or multiple reflections between inclined mirrors.
## WALKTHROUGH
Stand in front of the large mirror in your dressing room or look into your bathroom glass while brushing your teeth in the morning. Notice how your reflection looks upright, matches your exact height, and appears to be standing just as far behind the glass wall as you are standing in front of it. When you raise your right hand, the reflection raises what looks like its left hand, which is our first clue about lateral inversion. To understand this mathematically, we measure the perpendicular distance from the object to the mirror surface and compare it to the distance from the image to the mirror. Building on our prior knowledge of how light travels in straight lines and reflects off smooth surfaces, we find that these light rays appear to diverge from a point behind the mirror where no actual light exists, creating a virtual image.

**Takeaway:** the plane mirror's image is virtual, erect, same-sized, equidistant behind the glass, and laterally inverted - five properties, one illusion. **In the exam:** answer property-questions with the list in full; examiners deduct for the missing left-right flip. **Check yourself:** stand at the walkthrough's dressing mirror and test each property - especially the part no screen can catch. **Why students miss it:** students try to project their mirror twin onto paper - the flagged virtuality miss. The rays only pretend to converge behind the glass. Say 'virtual' with the reason - rays appear to diverge from behind - and the five properties hang on one hook.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If you stand 1.5 meters in front of a large plane mirror in your school hallway, what is the distance between you and your image?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The distance between you and your image is 3.0 meters. The image is formed 1.5 meters behind the mirror, and you are 1.5 meters in front of the mirror, so the total distance is 1.5 m + 1.5 m = 3.0 m. A wrong answer like 1.5 meters reveals that the student confused the object-to-mirror distance with the total object-to-image distance.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is the image formed by a plane mirror called a "virtual image" instead of a "real image"?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
A plane mirror forms a virtual image because the reflected light rays do not actually meet behind the mirror; they only *appear* to diverge from a point behind it when extended backward, meaning the image cannot be caught on a screen. A wrong answer stating that images are real because "we can clearly see them with our eyes" reveals a failure to distinguish between light rays actually intersecting (real) versus light rays being extrapolated backward by our brain (virtual).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A textbook is placed 40 cm in front of a vertical plane mirror. If the mirror is moved 10 cm closer to the textbook, what is the new distance between the textbook and its new image?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps: 1. Identify initial distance: object distance ($d_o$) = 40 cm. 2. Account for mirror movement: mirror moves 10 cm closer, so new object distance ($d_o'$) = 40 cm - 10 cm = 30 cm. 3. Apply mirror property: image distance behind the mirror ($d_i$) equals object distance in front of it ($d_i' = d_o' = 30\text{ cm}$). 4. Calculate total distance between object and image: $d_o' + d_i' = 30\text{ cm} + 30\text{ cm} = 60\text{ cm}$. Answer: 60 cm. A wrong answer like 80 cm reveals that the student did not recalculate the new object distance after the mirror moved.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The word "AMBULANCE" is painted laterally inverted (backwards) on the vehicle so that drivers looking into their rearview plane mirrors see the reflection flipped laterally once more, rendering the word normal and easily readable. A wrong answer suggesting it is written normally reveals a lack of understanding of lateral inversion where left and right are reversed during reflection.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that the image formed in a plane mirror changes its size as the observer moves closer to or farther away from the mirror. For example, they think that walking backwards makes their reflection smaller. This happens because our eyes perceive objects as smaller when they are farther away in the real world, causing students to incorrectly apply perspective rules to mirror reflections where the image size always maintains a strict 1:1 magnification ratio relative to the object.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this misconception, have the student stand at a fixed distance from a wall mirror holding a ruler vertically against the glass. Have them measure the height of their own reflection directly on the mirror surface using a dry-erase marker (or a transparent plastic sheet taped to the mirror). Then, have them take two giant steps backward and measure the reflection height again on the same sheet. They will observe that the measured height of the reflection remains identical, proving that image size depends only on object size, not viewer distance.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A science student places a glowing LED bulb 25 cm in front of a flat plane mirror in a physics lab in Delhi. Calculate the distance of the image from the mirror, and find the total distance between the LED bulb and its image.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: object distance ($d_o$) = 25 cm. Identify what to find: image distance ($d_i$) and total distance ($D_{total}$).
- step 2: Recall the plane mirror property formula relating object distance and image distance: $d_i = d_o$. Check units (centimeters, cm).
- step 3: Substitute the given object distance into the property equation: $d_i = 25\text{ cm}$.
- step 4: Compute the total distance between object and image by adding object distance and image distance: $D_{total} = d_o + d_i = 25\text{ cm} + 25\text{ cm}$. Track unit consistency ($cm + cm = cm$).
- step 5: Final calculation: $D_{total} = 50\text{ cm}$. Verify that 50 cm is physically reasonable for a table-top lab setup.
REASONING: Plane mirrors form images at a perpendicular distance behind the mirror that is exactly equal to the object's perpendicular distance in front of the mirror. Adding these two equal distances gives the total separation gap.
ANSWER: Image distance = 25 cm; Total distance between object and image = 50 cm.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An artist is painting a portrait while looking into a vertical plane mirror. The tip of her paintbrush is held 60 cm away from the mirror glass surface. If she steps backward by 15 cm away from the mirror, what is the new distance between her paintbrush and its image?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify initial object distance ($d_{o1}$) = 60 cm. Identify displacement of the artist/paintbrush = 15 cm away from the mirror.
- step 2: Calculate the new object distance ($d_{o2}$): $d_{o2} = d_{o1} + \text{displacement} = 60\text{ cm} + 15\text{ cm} = 75\text{ cm}$.
- step 3: Apply plane mirror image distance property: the new image distance ($d_{i2}$) is equal to the new object distance: $d_{i2} = 75\text{ cm}$.
- step 4: Compute total separation distance between the paintbrush and its image: $D_{total} = d_{o2} + d_{i2} = 75\text{ cm} + 75\text{ cm}$.
- step 5: Final computation: $D_{total} = 150\text{ cm}$ (or 1.5 m). Verify unit and physical logic.
REASONING: When the object moves relative to the stationary mirror, the object distance changes. The image distance updates instantaneously to match this new object distance, maintaining the 1:1 ratio.
ANSWER: 150 cm (or 1.5 m)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A toy car is moving at a uniform speed of 4 cm/s directly toward a large flat plane mirror fixed on a classroom wall in Mumbai. If the toy car starts at a distance of 100 cm from the mirror, calculate the speed of the image relative to the mirror, and the speed of the image *relative to the toy car* after 5 seconds of motion.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given parameters: speed of object ($v_o$) = 4 cm/s, initial object distance ($d_{o0}$) = 100 cm, time ($t$) = 5 s.
- step 2: Calculate object distance after 5 seconds ($d_{o}$) using uniform motion: distance traveled = speed $\times$ time = $4\text{ cm/s} \times 5\text{ s} = 20\text{ cm}$. New object distance $d_o = 100\text{ cm} - 20\text{ cm} = 80\text{ cm}$.
- step 3: Determine the image distance at $t = 5\text{ s}$: since image distance equals object distance, $d_i = 80\text{ cm}$.
- step 4: Calculate the speed of the image relative to the mirror: since the image moves closer to the mirror at the exact same rate as the object, image speed ($v_i$) = 4 cm/s.
- step 5: Calculate the speed of the image relative to the toy car: the car approaches the mirror at 4 cm/s, and the image approaches the mirror at 4 cm/s from behind, so the rate at which the distance between the car and its image decreases is $v_o + v_i = 4\text{ cm/s} + 4\text{ cm/s} = 8\text{ cm/s}$.
- step 6: Verify units and physical reasonableness: relative approach speed is double the object speed.
REASONING: While the image moves toward the mirror at the same speed as the object relative to the stationary mirror frame, an observer sitting on the moving toy car sees the image approaching at twice the object speed because both are closing the gap simultaneously.
ANSWER: Speed of image relative to mirror = 4 cm/s; Speed of image relative to toy car = 8 cm/s.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two parallel plane mirrors are placed facing each other, and a small candle is lit exactly midway between them at a distance of 15 cm from each mirror. Determine the distance of the first three images formed behind each mirror.
COMPETENCY: Spatial reasoning and multi-step distance calculation
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the candle's position relative to Mirror A ($d_{oA} = 15\text{ cm}$) and Mirror B ($d_{oB} = 15\text{ cm}$).
- step 2: For Mirror A, calculate the first image position behind Mirror A ($d_{iA1} = 15\text{ cm}$).
- step 3: For Mirror B, calculate the first image position behind Mirror B ($d_{iB1} = 15\text{ cm}$).
- step 4: Account for multiple reflections where the first image in one mirror acts as an object for the opposite mirror. For Mirror A's second image, the distance from Mirror B includes Mirror B to Mirror A gap (30 cm) plus Mirror A's first image distance (15 cm), giving $30 + 15 = 45\text{ cm}$ behind Mirror B.
- step 5: Conclude the positions for the first set of reflections behind respective mirrors: 15 cm, 45 cm, and 75 cm.
ANSWER: The first three image distances behind each mirror are 15 cm, 45 cm, and 75 cm respectively, due to successive reflections bouncing between the parallel mirrors.
DIFFICULTY: hard
