## ELI5
A line graph of your city's week unfolds in the school diary: title first, then axes and units, then the story in the slopes. Reading a graph is interrogation, not staring - find the exact point, subtract to measure the change between two days, call a steep climb a rapid rise and a flat stretch no change at all. But respect the fine print: the straight segments only assume steady movement between recorded points; between them, the weather was free to do anything.

## DEFINITION
Interpreting graphs involves reading visual data representations (such as line graphs and bar graphs) to extract specific information, identify trends, make comparisons, and draw logical conclusions about real-world situations.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can read values from horizontal and vertical axes, calculate changes between data points, identify increasing or decreasing trends, and answer analytical questions based on a given graph.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers reading, interpreting, and drawing conclusions from single and multiple line/bar graphs presented in Cartesian grids (first quadrant only). Does NOT cover constructing complex equations of lines or advanced statistical regression analysis.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you open your school diary and see a line graph showing the daily maximum temperature of your city over a week. How do you find out which day was the hottest without reading every single number? You start by looking at the title, axes labels, and units—like degrees Celsius on the vertical axis and days of the week on the horizontal axis. Connecting back to what you learned about bar graphs and number lines, each point on a grid represents an ordered pair of data. By tracing across from the highest point on the line to the vertical axis, you instantly find the peak temperature. The key insight is that the slope or direction of the line tells you the story: rising lines mean an increase, falling lines mean a decrease, and flat lines mean no change.

**Takeaway:** reading a graph is interrogation: title, axes, units, then points, slopes and gaps on demand. **In the exam:** locate exact values by axis, subtract to measure change, and describe trends with slope language. **Check yourself:** pull the temperature peak out of the diary graph and compute the day-to-day jump by subtraction. **Why students miss it:** students trust the drawn segments as data - but the line only assumes steady travel between recorded points. Between the dots, the real story was free to wander; the dots carry the evidence, the lines merely connect it.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Look at a line graph tracking plant growth over 4 weeks. Between Week 1 and Week 2, the line goes steeply upward. Between Week 3 and Week 4, the line is completely horizontal. What is the difference in plant growth behavior between these two intervals?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
A steeply upward line means the plant grew rapidly during that week, whereas a horizontal line means the plant's height remained constant (no growth) during that time. A wrong answer—such as thinking a horizontal line means the plant died or shrank—shows a misunderstanding of what a zero slope on a value-vs-time graph represents.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A line graph shows a car's distance from home over 4 hours: Hour 0 (0 km), Hour 1 (50 km), Hour 2 (100 km), Hour 3 (100 km), and Hour 4 (150 km). How many total kilometres did the car travel between Hour 1 and Hour 3?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Identify the distance at Hour 1, which is 50 km. 
Step 2: Identify the distance at Hour 3, which is 100 km. 
Step 3: Subtract the distance at Hour 1 from the distance at Hour 3 to find the progress during that interval ($100\text{ km} - 50\text{ km} = 50\text{ km}$). 
Answer: 50 km. 
A wrong answer like 100 km or 150 km indicates the student added the coordinate values together or misread the specific interval endpoints instead of finding the difference.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often read a line graph as if it were a physical picture or a literal map of a route (e.g., thinking a rising line means the road is going uphill physically, or treating the slope of the line as the actual path of a moving object). For instance, if a graph shows distance against time and goes straight up, students might think the object is climbing a mountain instead of moving further away horizontally over time.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this, guide the student back to the axis labels step by step. Ask: "What does the horizontal axis measure? What does the vertical axis measure?" Emphasize that a line going up simply means the *value* of the vertical quantity (like money, distance, or temperature) is increasing as time passes, not that the object is moving vertically in space.

## THIS_FORMULA
Rate of Change = $\frac{\text{Final Value} - \text{Initial Value}}{\text{Final Time} - \text{Initial Time}}$
## FORMULA_VARIABLES
Final Value = value on vertical axis at later point, Initial Value = value on vertical axis at earlier point, Final Time = value on horizontal axis at later point, Initial Time = value on horizontal axis at earlier point.
## FORMULA_UNITS
Units of vertical axis divided by units of horizontal axis (e.g., $\text{km/h}$, $\text{rupees/month}$, ${^\circ\text{C/hour}}$).

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A line graph records the height of a sapling in a nursery measured at the end of every month from January (Month 1) to June (Month 6). The heights recorded are: Jan: 10 cm, Feb: 15 cm, Mar: 25 cm, Apr: 25 cm, May: 35 cm, Jun: 50 cm. Find the total growth from January to June, and identify the month-to-month interval with the greatest growth.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Extract the initial value in January ($10\text{ cm}$) and the final value in June ($50\text{ cm}$).
- Step 2: Compute total growth by subtracting initial height from final height: $\text{Total Growth} = 50 - 10 = 40\text{ cm}$.
- Step 3: Calculate growth for each monthly interval: Jan-Feb ($15 - 10 = 5$), Feb-Mar ($25 - 15 = 10$), Mar-Apr ($25 - 25 = 0$), Apr-May ($35 - 25 = 10$), May-Jun ($50 - 35 = 15$).
- Step 4: Compare all interval growth values ($5, 10, 0, 10, 15$) to find the maximum. The maximum value is $15\text{ cm}$, which occurs between May and June.
- Step 5: Verify results and state answers with proper units ($40\text{ cm}$ total growth, greatest growth between May and June).
REASONING: Total growth represents the net vertical distance covered from start to finish, while interval growth is found by comparing consecutive data points, where the steepest upward slope corresponds to the greatest numerical increase.
ANSWER: Total growth is 40 cm; greatest growth occurred between May and June (15 cm).
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A line graph shows Rahul's savings account balance in rupees over 5 months: Month 1: ₹1200, Month 2: ₹1500, Month 3: ₹1000, Month 4: ₹1800, Month 5: ₹2200. Calculate the average monthly change in savings from Month 1 to Month 5.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the starting balance at Month 1 ($₹1200$) and the ending balance at Month 5 ($₹2200$).
- Step 2: Calculate the net change in savings over the entire period: Net Change = $\text{Final Balance} - \text{Initial Balance} = 2200 - 1200 = ₹1000$.
- Step 3: Determine the total number of month intervals elapsed: $5 - 1 = 4$ intervals.
- Step 4: Compute the average monthly change by dividing the net change by the number of intervals: $\text{Average Change} = \frac{1000}{4} = ₹250\text{ per month}$.
- Step 5: Interpret the sign and units: positive ₹250/month indicates an overall average saving trend of ₹250 added each month despite fluctuations.
REASONING: Average change over a duration in a graph is found by taking the total difference in the vertical variable divided by the total difference in the horizontal variable, smoothing out intermediate dips and rises.
ANSWER: ₹250 per month increase.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A delivery van's journey is tracked on a distance-time graph. From 9:00 AM (0 hours) to 11:00 AM (2 hours), it travels from 0 km to 90 km. From 11:00 AM to 1:00 PM (4 hours from start), it stays at a rest stop 90 km away. From 1:00 PM to 3:00 PM (6 hours from start), it travels from 90 km to 180 km. Calculate the speed of the van during its first traveling phase and its overall average speed including the rest stop.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Find distance and time for Phase 1: Distance traveled = $90 - 0 = 90\text{ km}$, Time taken = $2 - 0 = 2\text{ hours}$.
- Step 2: Calculate Phase 1 speed using $\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$: $\text{Speed}_1 = \frac{90\text{ km}}{2\text{ h}} = 45\text{ km/h}$.
- Step 3: Find total distance and total time for the entire journey: Total Distance = $180\text{ km}$, Total Time = $6\text{ hours}$.
- Step 4: Calculate overall average speed: $\text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} = \frac{180\text{ km}}{6\text{ h}} = 30\text{ km/h}$.
- Step 5: Verify calculations and compare speeds: The moving speed was 45 km/h, but including the standstill reduced the average speed to 30 km/h.
REASONING: Speed during a specific interval uses only that interval's rise and run, whereas overall average speed requires the total net distance divided by total elapsed time regardless of rests.
ANSWER: Phase 1 speed is 45 km/h; overall average speed is 30 km/h.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two students, Amit and Priya, look at a line graph representing the temperature of a town over 12 hours. Amit says, "The temperature dropped fastest between 2 PM and 3 PM because the line is the steepest downward." Priya says, "No, the temperature dropped most between 6 PM and 9 PM because the total drop in degrees was greater." Who is correct and why?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying graph interpretations (slope vs. absolute change)
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze Amit's claim: he is looking at the steepness (slope) of a single hour interval, representing the rate of change per unit time.
- Step 2: Analyze Priya's claim: she is looking at the total vertical distance between 6 PM and 9 PM, representing total accumulated decrease over a longer duration.
- Step 3: Differentiate between "rate of change" (steepness per hour) and "total change" (net difference over multiple hours).
- Step 4: Conclude that both are right in their respective metrics, but Amit correctly identifies the *fastest rate* (steepest gradient) while Priya identifies the *greatest total drop*.
ANSWER: Both students are describing different aspects of the graph. Amit is correct about the *rate* of drop (speed of change) because a steeper slope over a shorter time means a faster rate. Priya is correct about the *magnitude* of total drop over a longer window.
DIFFICULTY: hard
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A line graph shows the water level in a reservoir over a week. The line shows a steady decrease from Monday to Thursday, a sudden vertical spike upward on Friday due to heavy rain, and then a gradual decline over Saturday and Sunday. Explain what story this graph tells about the weather and water management during this week, and identify on which day water was likely extracted or consumed fastest relative to inflow.
COMPETENCY: Translating visual graph features into comprehensive real-world narratives
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine the Monday-Thursday downward trend to infer dry weather and continuous water consumption or release.
- Step 2: Examine the sharp upward spike on Friday to conclude a heavy rainfall event instantly replenished the reservoir.
- Step 3: Examine the Saturday-Sunday gradual decline to understand post-rain stabilization.
- Step 4: Identify the interval of fastest water depletion by looking for the steepest downward slope (Monday-Thursday segment with the maximum negative gradient).
ANSWER: The graph tells a story of a dry spell causing water depletion early in the week, followed by a sudden storm on Friday that refilled the reservoir, and subsequent normalization over the weekend. Water was depleted fastest during the steepest downward segment between Monday and Tuesday.
DIFFICULTY: medium
