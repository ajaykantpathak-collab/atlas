## ELI5
Two quantities can walk together or trade places. Direct proportion: both rise or both fall together, their ratio locked — more guests, more food. Inverse proportion: as one rises the other sinks, their product locked — the same job shared among more workers takes fewer days. The test is a question you ask the data: is the ratio staying constant or the product staying constant? Same direction, direct; opposite directions, inverse. Classify first, then the right equation — x1 over y1 equals x2 over y2, or x1 times y1 equals x2 times y2 — does the rest.

## DEFINITION
Two quantities are in **direct proportion** if they change in the same direction (both increase or both decrease) such that their ratio remains constant (\(x/y = k\)). They are in **inverse proportion** if they change in opposite directions (one increases while the other decreases) such that their product remains constant (\(x \times y = k\)). Distinguishing between them requires analyzing whether the relationship between the quantities is "more-more/less-less" (direct) or "more-less/less-more" (inverse).
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
analyze
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can examine a word problem, table, or real-life scenario and correctly classify the relationship as direct or inverse proportion, then set up and solve the appropriate equation (\(x_1/y_1 = x_2/y_2\) for direct, \(x_1y_1 = x_2y_2\) for inverse) to find unknown values.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers distinguishing between direct and inverse proportion in two-variable situations using tables, algebraic statements, and word problems. It does NOT cover compound proportion (three or more variables), non-linear proportional relationships, or calculus-based rate problems.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are planning a class party. You need to buy samosas: if more students come, you need more samosas (direct proportion). But you also have a fixed budget of ₹500 for return gifts: if more students come, each gift must cost less (inverse proportion). Start by asking: "When one quantity goes up, does the other go up or down?" Write down the two quantities (e.g., number of students vs. samosas needed; number of students vs. cost per gift). Check if their ratio is constant (direct) or product is constant (inverse). This connects to c8_math_ch10_6 (direct proportion tables) and c8_math_ch10_7 (inverse proportion identification). The key insight: **same direction = direct, opposite direction = inverse**.

Takeaway: classify before you calculate — ask of any table whether the ratio or the product stays constant, and let the answer choose the equation. Exam format: expect classify-and-solve word problems; misclassification loses everything downstream, however clean the algebra. Verification beat: test your classification against a second data pair — if the constant fails to hold, you chose the wrong proportion. Why students miss it: they see two numbers changing and reach for multiplication by reflex; the words more-more versus more-less in the problem statement are the classification signal.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
State the defining condition for two quantities to be in (a) direct proportion and (b) inverse proportion. Use the terms "ratio" and "product" in your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
(a) Direct proportion: The ratio of the two quantities is constant (\(x/y = k\)). (b) Inverse proportion: The product of the two quantities is constant (\(x \times y = k\)). A wrong answer (e.g., saying "sum is constant" or confusing ratio/product) reveals the student has memorized keywords without understanding the mathematical invariant that defines each relationship.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
A car travels a fixed distance of 240 km. Explain why the speed of the car and the time taken are in inverse proportion, using the concept of constant product. Why would it be incorrect to say they are in direct proportion because "higher speed means less time, so both change"?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
For a fixed distance, speed \(\times\) time = distance (constant). As speed increases, time decreases such that the product remains 240. It is incorrect to call it direct proportion because direct proportion requires the *ratio* (speed/time) to be constant, which would mean speed and time change in the *same* direction. A student who says "both change" confuses "any relationship" with "proportional relationship" and misses the directional requirement (same vs. opposite).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
The table below shows the number of workers and days to complete a fixed job.

| Workers | 4 | 6 | ? | 12 |
| Days    | 15 | 10 | 6 | ? |

(a) Identify the type of proportion. (b) Find the two missing values.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
(a) Inverse proportion (more workers → fewer days). Constant product \(k = 4 \times 15 = 60\).
(b) For 6 days: workers = \(60/6 = 10\). For 12 workers: days = \(60/12 = 5\).
Missing values: 10 workers, 5 days.
A wrong answer (e.g., using direct proportion \(4/15 = 6/x \rightarrow x=22.5\)) reveals the student defaults to direct proportion without checking the direction of change.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare these two situations:
Situation A: The cost of apples (₹) and weight purchased (kg) at a fixed rate of ₹80/kg.
Situation B: The number of pipes filling a tank and the time taken to fill it.
Analyze the relationship in each. Which is direct and which is inverse? Justify by describing what happens to the second quantity when the first is doubled.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Situation A: Direct. Doubling weight doubles cost (ratio cost/weight = 80 constant). Situation B: Inverse. Doubling pipes halves the time (product pipes \(\times\) time = constant tank volume). A wrong classification (e.g., saying both are direct because "more pipes, more water") shows the student focuses on the intuitive "more input → more output" without recognizing the fixed total work (tank volume) that creates the inverse relationship.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student solves this problem: *"6 pumps empty a tank in 40 minutes. How long for 10 pumps?"*
Their working: \(\frac{6}{40} = \frac{10}{x} \Rightarrow x = \frac{400}{6} = 66.7\) minutes.
Evaluate the student's method. Is the answer reasonable? Identify the exact error and give the correct answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The student incorrectly used direct proportion. More pumps should take *less* time, not more. The answer 66.7 min > 40 min is unreasonable. Correct method: Inverse proportion. \(6 \times 40 = 10 \times x \Rightarrow x = 24\) minutes. The error reveals the student matches numbers to a fraction template without reasoning about the physical situation (fixed tank volume).

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: *"A school bus uses 15 litres of diesel to travel 180 km. (a) How much diesel for 300 km at the same speed? (b) If the bus travels 180 km at 60 km/h, it takes 3 hours. How long at 45 km/h?"*
(a) Diesel & distance: Direct (more distance → more diesel). \(15/180 = x/300 \Rightarrow x = 25\) litres.
(b) Speed & time: Inverse (fixed distance). \(60 \times 3 = 45 \times t \Rightarrow t = 4\) hours.
A poor answer gives two problems of the same type or misclassifies, showing inability to generate contrasting scenarios.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students mechanically apply the direct proportion formula \(x_1/y_1 = x_2/y_2\) to *every* problem with two quantities, even when the relationship is inverse (e.g., workers-days, speed-time). They see two numbers and a missing value and default to "cross-multiply." The specific wrong step: **writing a fraction equality without verifying if the quantities change in the same direction.** For instance, in "8 workers take 12 days; how many days for 6 workers?", they write \(8/12 = 6/x\) and get \(x = 9\) days (more workers → more days), which is illogical.
## ON_FAILURE
1. Have the student draw an **arrow diagram**: Write the two quantities with an arrow showing change (e.g., Workers: \(8 \uparrow 6\)? No, \(8 \downarrow 6\); Days: \(12 \rightarrow ?\)). 2. Ask: "Do the arrows point the same way or opposite?" Same → Direct (use ratio). Opposite → Inverse (use product). 3. Practice with 3-4 simple tables where they *only* classify (no calculation) until direction recognition is automatic. 4. Then return to calculation, insisting they write "Direct: \(x_1y_2 = x_2y_1\)" or "Inverse: \(x_1y_1 = x_2y_2\)" before substituting numbers.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A contractor estimates that 5 workers can paint a school building in 18 days. Due to rain, only 3 workers are available. How many days will it take them to complete the same painting job?

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the two quantities: Number of workers (W) and Number of days (D). The total work (painting the building) is fixed.
- Step 2: Determine the proportion type. Fewer workers (3 < 5) will take MORE days. Quantities change in opposite directions → **Inverse Proportion**.
- Step 3: Write the inverse proportion equation: \(W_1 \times D_1 = W_2 \times D_2\). Substitute known values: \(5 \times 18 = 3 \times D_2\).
- Step 4: Solve for \(D_2\): \(90 = 3 \times D_2 \Rightarrow D_2 = 90 / 3 = 30\).
- Step 5: Verify: 3 workers take 30 days. Product = \(3 \times 30 = 90\) worker-days, matches original \(5 \times 18 = 90\). Answer makes sense (fewer workers → more days).

REASONING: The total amount of work (worker-days) is constant. This is the invariant "product" that defines inverse proportion. We use \(W_1D_1 = W_2D_2\) because the job size doesn't change.

ANSWER: 30 days
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A car travels 240 km on 15 litres of petrol. How many litres of petrol will be needed to travel 400 km at the same speed? If petrol costs ₹95 per litre, what is the cost for the 400 km trip?

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the two quantities for the first part: Distance travelled (d) and Petrol used (p). Speed is constant.
- Step 2: Determine proportion type. More distance requires more petrol. Both increase together → **Direct Proportion**.
- Step 3: Write direct proportion equation: \(\frac{d_1}{p_1} = \frac{d_2}{p_2}\). Substitute: \(\frac{240}{15} = \frac{400}{p_2}\).
- Step 4: Solve for \(p_2\): Cross-multiply: \(240 \times p_2 = 15 \times 400 \Rightarrow p_2 = \frac{6000}{240} = 25\) litres.
- Step 5: Second part: Cost (C) and Petrol (p) at fixed price. More petrol → more cost. Direct proportion. \(C = 25 \times 95 = ₹2375\).
- Step 6: Verify: Mileage = \(240/15 = 16\) km/litre. For 400 km, petrol = \(400/16 = 25\) litres. Cost = \(25 \times 95 = 2375\). Consistent.

REASONING: At constant speed, fuel consumption is directly proportional to distance (constant km/litre). At constant price, cost is directly proportional to quantity. Both are "same direction" relationships.

ANSWER: 25 litres; ₹2375
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A water tank can be filled by Pipe A in 6 hours. Pipe B can fill the same tank in 4 hours. If both pipes are opened together, how long will it take to fill the tank? (Hint: Work rate is additive. Use inverse proportion between rate and time.)

STEPS:
- Step 1: Recognize this combines inverse proportion (rate vs time) with addition of rates. Let tank volume = 1 unit (or LCM of 6 and 4 = 12 units for easier arithmetic).
- Step 2: Find individual rates (volume per hour). Pipe A: \(1/6\) tank/hour. Pipe B: \(1/4\) tank/hour. (Using 12 units: A = 2 units/hr, B = 3 units/hr).
- Step 3: Combined rate = \(1/6 + 1/4 = 2/12 + 3/12 = 5/12\) tank/hour.
- Step 4: Time and Rate are inversely proportional for a fixed job (Rate \(\times\) Time = 1 tank). So \( \text{Time} = \frac{1}{\text{Combined Rate}} = \frac{1}{5/12} = \frac{12}{5} = 2.4\) hours.
- Step 5: Convert 0.4 hours to minutes: \(0.4 \times 60 = 24\) minutes. Total = 2 hours 24 minutes.
- Step 6: Verify: In 2.4 hrs, Pipe A fills \(2.4/6 = 0.4\) tank. Pipe B fills \(2.4/4 = 0.6\) tank. Sum = 1.0 tank. Correct.

REASONING: This problem requires distinguishing that *rate* and *time* are inverse for a fixed task, but *rates* from different pipes add directly. The core inverse proportion concept (\(R \times T = \text{constant}\)) is used to find time from the combined rate.

ANSWER: 2 hours 24 minutes (or 2.4 hours)
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Riya says: "In any situation where two quantities are related, if you double one, the other doubles. So everything is direct proportion." Give two real-life examples (Indian context) that prove Riya wrong: one where doubling the first quantity *halves* the second, and one where doubling the first leaves the second *unchanged*. Explain the relationship in each.

COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying

STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand the claim (all relationships are direct) and what is needed to disprove it (counter-examples).
- Step 2: Identify an inverse proportion example (e.g., speed-time for fixed distance, workers-days for fixed work).
- Step 3: Identify a non-proportional example (e.g., age of mother and child, fixed fee + variable cost).
- Step 4: Write the explanation showing the "double first → halve second" and "double first → no change" patterns.
- Step 5: Conclude why Riya's statement is false.

ANSWER: Example 1 (Inverse): A car travels 120 km. At 60 km/h, time = 2 hours. Double speed to 120 km/h → time halves to 1 hour. Relationship: Speed × Time = constant (120). Example 2 (Non-proportional): Auto-rickshaw fare: ₹25 fixed + ₹10/km. For 2 km, fare = ₹45. Double distance to 4 km → fare = ₹65 (not doubled). Relationship: Fare = 25 + 10×distance (linear, not proportional).

DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A farmer has enough cattle feed to last 30 cows for 12 days. He buys 10 more cows. (a) How many days will the feed last now? (b) After 4 days, he sells 5 cows. How many *additional* days will the remaining feed last? Show all reasoning.

COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions

STEPS:
- Step 1: (a) Identify quantities: Cows (C) and Days (D). Total feed = constant. More cows → fewer days → Inverse Proportion.
- Step 2: (a) Calculate total feed units: \(30 \times 12 = 360\) cow-days. New cows = 40. Days = \(360 / 40 = 9\) days.
- Step 3: (b) Feed consumed in first 4 days by 40 cows = \(40 \times 4 = 160\) cow-days. Remaining feed = \(360 - 160 = 200\) cow-days.
- Step 4: (b) Cows now = 40 - 5 = 35 cows. Additional days = Remaining feed / Current cows = \(200 / 35 = 40/7 \approx 5.71\) days.
- Step 5: Verify: Total days = 4 + 40/7 = 68/7 ≈ 9.71? Wait, check: 40 cows for 4 days + 35 cows for 40/7 days = 160 + 200 = 360. Correct.

ANSWER: (a) 9 days. (b) \(40/7\) days (or 5 days and about 17 hours).

DIFFICULTY: hard
