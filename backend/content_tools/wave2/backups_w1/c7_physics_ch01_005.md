## ELI5
Every switch you flip has a shadow: the torch's electricity comes from a power plant somewhere, and the heater's warmth from fuel burned somewhere else. Science and society carry a responsibility clause - discoveries must serve people without wrecking the ecology they depend on. Sustainability is the design rule: meet today's need without billing tomorrow. Choose the efficient bulb, question the wasteful process - citizenship is part of the syllabus.

## DEFINITION
Science and Society: Responsibility and Sustainability refers to the ethical duty of scientists, engineers, and citizens to ensure that scientific discoveries and technological applications preserve ecological balance, conserve natural resources, and protect human well-being for future generations, measured qualitatively through environmental impact assessments and sustainable development indices (dimensionless ratios).

## WALKTHROUGH
Observe how a simple household appliance like an electric torch or a room heater consumes electrical energy drawn from thermal power plants that burn coal, which releases carbon dioxide into the air. When we switch on lights unnecessarily or discard old batteries improperly, we deplete natural resources and pollute our local environment. To understand our responsibility, we first measure or estimate our resource consumption—such as counting the hours appliances run or tracking household waste. This connects directly to our understanding of energy flow, circuits, and human impact on ecosystems from earlier studies. The guiding principle of sustainability is meeting current human needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A small village uses diesel generators that consume 5 liters of diesel per hour to pump groundwater for irrigation. If burning 1 liter of diesel produces approximately 2.6 kg of carbon dioxide ($CO_2$), calculate the total mass of $CO_2$ released into the atmosphere if the generator runs for 4 hours a day over a 30-day month.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps: 
1. Identify given values: Fuel consumption rate = 5 liters/hour; operating time = 4 hours/day; duration = 30 days; $CO_2$ emission rate = 2.6 kg/liter.
2. Calculate total operating hours in the month: $4\text{ hours/day} \times 30\text{ days} = 120\text{ hours}$.
3. Calculate total fuel consumed: $120\text{ hours} \times 5\text{ liters/hour} = 600\text{ liters}$.
4. Compute total $CO_2$ emission by multiplying fuel volume by emission rate: $600\text{ liters} \times 2.6\text{ kg/liter} = 1560\text{ kg}$.
5. Verify physical reasonableness: 1560 kg of $CO_2$ per month from a single village generator shows a heavy carbon footprint, justifying a shift to solar-powered pumps. 
Answer: 1560 kg of $CO_2$. A wrong answer usually stems from forgetting to multiply all three factors (hours, days, and rate) together, indicating poor multi-step unit tracking.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that "technology always solves environmental problems," assuming that whatever negative ecological impact a scientific invention creates can be effortlessly cleaned up by a newer invention, without realizing that resource extraction and waste generation often cause permanent or long-term damage that prevention is the only way to manage.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this misconception, have the student conduct a mini-audit of household waste for one day, collecting all plastic wrappers, dead cells, and paper. Ask them to trace where each item came from (raw materials) and where it goes after being thrown in the dustbin, emphasizing that trash does not simply disappear. Discuss how designing products to last longer or using renewable materials prevents waste before it is even created.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A school in Delhi has 20 classrooms, and each classroom has four 40-watt fluorescent tube lights left burning unnecessarily for 3 hours every day after school hours. If the school operates for 200 days a year, calculate the total electrical energy wasted in kilowatt-hours (kWh).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given values and what to find. Number of classrooms = 20; tubes per room = 4; power of each tube = 40 W; daily waste time = 3 hours; annual operating days = 200 days. Find total energy wasted in kWh.
- Step 2: Calculate total power consumed by all wasted lights simultaneously. Total Power ($P$) = Number of classrooms $\times$ Tubes per room $\times$ Power per tube. $P = 20 \times 4 \times 40\text{ W} = 3200\text{ W}$.
- Step 3: Convert power from Watts (W) to kilowatts (kW) by dividing by 1000. $P(\text{kW}) = 3200 / 1000 = 3.2\text{ kW}$.
- Step 4: Calculate total daily energy wasted and then multiply by annual days. Daily Energy = $3.2\text{ kW} \times 3\text{ hours} = 9.6\text{ kWh/day}$. Annual Energy Wasted = $9.6\text{ kWh/day} \times 200\text{ days} = 1920\text{ kWh}$.
- Step 5: Verify that the answer is physically reasonable. Consuming 1920 kWh annually for empty rooms highlights a massive, preventable carbon footprint, emphasizing the need for student responsibility in turning off switches.
REASONING: Multiplying the number of units by their individual power ratings gives the total rate of energy consumption. Converting Watts to kilowatts aligns the unit with standard electrical billing units (kWh), and multiplying by time gives total energy consumed.
ANSWER: 1920 kWh
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An electric car travels a standard distance, consuming 15 kWh of electrical energy stored in its battery pack per 100 km. If the electricity used to charge the car is generated by a coal power plant that emits 0.9 kg of $CO_2$ per kWh of electricity produced, calculate the total mass of $CO_2$ emitted to drive the car for a distance of 1200 km in a month.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given values. Energy consumption rate = 15 kWh per 100 km; total monthly distance = 1200 km; $CO_2$ emission factor = 0.9 kg/kWh. Find total $CO_2$ emitted.
- Step 2: Determine how many 100-km units are in 1200 km. Number of units = $1200\text{ km} / 100\text{ km} = 12$.
- Step 3: Calculate total electrical energy consumed for 1200 km. Total Energy = $12 \times 15\text{ kWh} = 180\text{ kWh}$.
- Step 4: Multiply total energy by the emission factor to find total $CO_2$ mass. Total $CO_2$ = $180\text{ kWh} \times 0.9\text{ kg/kWh} = 162\text{ kg}$.
- Step 5: Verify the physical reasonableness. Driving 1200 km results in 162 kg of $CO_2$, demonstrating that electric vehicles are only as clean as the power source used to charge them.
REASONING: We scale the energy consumption per unit distance up to the total distance traveled, then apply the emission factor per kilowatt-hour to track how energy generation impacts atmospheric pollution.
ANSWER: 162 kg of $CO_2$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A rural household replaces four 60-watt traditional incandescent bulbs (which last 1,000 hours each and cost ₹20 each) with four 10-watt LED bulbs (which last 10,000 hours each and cost ₹100 each). If each bulb is used for 5 hours a day, calculate the total financial and energy savings over 10,000 hours of operation, assuming electricity costs ₹5 per kWh.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify parameters for both bulb types over 10,000 hours of operation. Incandescent power = 60 W; LED power = 10 W; total operating hours = 10,000 hours; electricity cost = ₹5/kWh.
- Step 2: Calculate energy consumed by incandescent bulbs over 10,000 hours. Energy = $(60\text{ W} / 1000) \times 10,000\text{ hours} = 600\text{ kWh}$ per bulb. For 4 bulbs: $4 \times 600\text{ kWh} = 2400\text{ kWh}$.
- Step 3: Calculate energy consumed by LED bulbs over 10,000 hours. Energy = $(10\text{ W} / 1000) \times 10,000\text{ hours} = 100\text{ kWh}$ per bulb. For 4 bulbs: $4 \times 100\text{ kWh} = 400\text{ kWh}$.
- Step 4: Calculate total electricity costs for both options. Incandescent cost = $2400\text{ kWh} \times ₹5/\text{kWh} = ₹12,000$. LED cost = $400\text{ kWh} \times ₹5/\text{kWh} = ₹2,000$. Electricity savings = $₹12,000 - ₹2,000 = ₹10,000$.
- Step 5: Account for bulb replacement costs over 10,000 hours. Incandescent bulbs last 1,000 hours, so each needs 10 replacements ($4\text{ bulbs} \times 10 = 40\text{ bulbs}$). Cost = $40 \times ₹20 = ₹800$. LED bulbs last 10,000 hours, so 0 replacements needed (initial purchase: $4 \times ₹100 = ₹400$).
- Step 6: Compute net total savings (electricity savings + bulb replacement difference - initial LED cost difference). Total Net Savings = $₹10,000 + (₹800 - ₹400) - (₹400\text{ initial LED cost}) = ₹10,000$.
REASONING: Sustainable technology often requires a slightly higher initial investment (buying LEDs) but yields massive resource and financial savings over its lifetime by drastically reducing energy waste and replacement frequency.
ANSWER: ₹10,000 net savings and 2000 kWh of energy saved.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A city is considering building either a thermal power plant or a solar farm to meet rising electricity demands. A local citizens' group argues against the thermal power plant, citing sustainability principles. Evaluate their argument by comparing the two energy sources in terms of resource depletion and long-term environmental impact.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating technological choices based on scientific principles of sustainability and resource management.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the resource requirements of a thermal power plant (consumes finite coal reserves) versus a solar farm (uses abundant sunlight).
- Step 2: Examine the waste products of both options (thermal power plants release large amounts of $CO_2$ and sulfur dioxide causing global warming and acid rain; solar panels produce zero emissions during operation).
- Step 3: Assess long-term impacts on future generations (depleting coal leaves none for the future, whereas solar energy remains continuously available).
- Step 4: Formulate a conclusion showing how scientific responsibility supports the citizens' stance.
ANSWER: The citizens' argument is scientifically sound. Thermal power plants rely on finite fossil fuels that deplete Earth's reserves and release greenhouse gases that alter global climate, violating the core principle of sustainability. In contrast, solar farms harness renewable solar radiation without depleting natural resources or polluting the atmosphere, preserving environmental health for future generations.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two farmers grow the same crop. Farmer A uses flood irrigation, pumping 50,000 liters of groundwater per day using a diesel pump. Farmer B installs a drip irrigation system powered by a small solar pump, reducing water usage to 20,000 liters per day. Using scientific principles of conservation, analyze how Farmer B's method demonstrates both resource sustainability and reduced carbon emissions.
COMPETENCY: Applying conservation laws and environmental ethics to real-world resource management scenarios.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Compare the water consumption rates between flood irrigation (Farmer A) and drip irrigation (Farmer B) to determine water conservation.
- Step 2: Calculate the water saved per day and explain how drip irrigation minimizes evaporation and runoff.
- Step 3: Analyze the energy source difference (diesel generator emitting pollutants vs. clean solar power).
- Step 4: Synthesize how these combined factors reduce both environmental strain on aquifers and atmospheric pollution.
ANSWER: Farmer B's method conserves 30,000 liters of water daily by delivering water directly to plant roots, preventing loss from evaporation and runoff. Furthermore, by replacing a diesel generator with a solar pump, Farmer B eliminates local fuel emissions. This dual approach protects underground water tables from depletion and reduces greenhouse gas emissions, embodying true environmental sustainability.
