## DEFINITION
Electricity and its uses in daily life encompass the generation, distribution, and conversion of electrical energy into other forms of energy (such as light, heat, and mechanical work) to power household appliances and devices. Electrical energy is measured in Joules (J), and electrical power is measured in Watts (W).

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that electricity is "consumed" or used up permanently and disappears completely after passing through an appliance, rather than realizing it is converted from electrical energy into other forms of energy like heat, light, or sound in accordance with the law of conservation of energy.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this, guide the student to touch an electric bulb or motor a few minutes after it has been switched on. Ask them to feel the heat generated and listen to the sound or see the light. Explain that the electrical energy flowing from the cell or socket did not vanish; it transformed into heat, light, and motion.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A kitchen mixer grinder operates with a power rating of $500\text{ W}$. If a homemaker runs it continuously for $120\text{ seconds}$ to grind spices, calculate the total electrical energy consumed by the mixer grinder.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given values with units: Power ($P$) = $500\text{ W}$, Time ($t$) = $120\text{ s}$.
- Step 2: Identify the relevant formula linking energy, power, and time: Energy ($E$) = $P \times t$.
- Step 3: Substitute the numerical values and units into the formula: $E = 500\text{ W} \times 120\text{ s}$.
- Step 4: Perform the arithmetic calculation and track unit cancellation ($1\text{ Watt} = 1\text{ Joule/second}$, so $\text{W} \times \text{s} = (\text{J/s}) \times \text{s} = \text{Joules}$): $E = 500 \times 120 = 60,000\text{ J}$.
- Step 5: Verify that the answer is physically reasonable for a high-power kitchen appliance running for two minutes, yielding $60,000\text{ J}$ or $60\text{ kJ}$.
REASONING: Power represents the rate at which electrical energy is used. Multiplying power by the duration of operation gives the total electrical energy transformed into mechanical and thermal energy by the appliance.
ANSWER: $60,000\text{ J}$ (or $60\text{ kJ}$)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An LED study lamp consumes $10\text{ W}$ of power. It is kept switched on for $3,600\text{ seconds}$ while a student studies in the evening. Calculate the total electrical energy consumed by the study lamp.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given values with units: Power ($P$) = $10\text{ W}$, Time ($t$) = $3,600\text{ s}$.
- Step 2: State the formula for electrical energy: Energy ($E$) = $P \times t$.
- Step 3: Substitute the given values: $E = 10\text{ W} \times 3,600\text{ s}$.
- Step 4: Calculate the product and analyze the units: $E = 36,000\text{ J}$ ($1\text{ W} \times 1\text{ s} = 1\text{ J}$).
- Step 5: Verify the physical reasonableness: A low-power LED lamp consumes much less energy than a heavy motor over the same duration.
REASONING: The total energy converted to light and heat depends directly on how long the lamp stays on and its power rating.
ANSWER: $36,000\text{ J}$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A ceiling fan runs at a power of $75\text{ W}$ for $2\text{ hours}$ ($7,200\text{ seconds}$). Part of this electrical energy is converted into mechanical work (spinning the blades) and part into thermal energy (heating the motor coil). Calculate the total electrical energy consumed in Joules.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Extract the given quantities with units: Power ($P$) = $75\text{ W}$, Time ($t$) = $7,200\text{ s}$ (converted from $2\text{ hours}$).
- Step 2: Choose the appropriate energy formula: $E = P \times t$.
- Step 3: Check unit consistency: Power is in Watts and time is in seconds.
- Step 4: Substitute values into the equation: $E = 75\text{ W} \times 7,200\text{ s}$.
- Step 5: Compute the numerical value: $75 \times 7,200 = 540,000\text{ J}$.
- Step 6: Verify physical reasonability: Large household fans consume hundreds of kilojoules over hours of operation.
REASONING: Even though the energy transforms into multiple output forms (motion and heat), the total electrical energy consumed is calculated solely by multiplying the electrical power drawn by the total time of operation.
ANSWER: $540,000\text{ J}$ (or $540\text{ kJ}$)

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student connects a small toy DC motor to two electric cells in a circuit. When the switch is closed, the motor spins a paper fan blade. The student notices that after running for 5 minutes, both the motor casing and the cells become slightly warm. Trace the flow of energy transformations from the chemical energy inside the cells to the final outputs, and explain why the motor gets warm.
COMPETENCY: Predicting outcomes from changing variables
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the initial energy source: chemical energy stored in the electric cells.
- Step 2: Trace the energy as it moves through the wires as electrical energy into the motor.
- Step 3: Identify the primary useful output: mechanical energy (spinning fan blade).
- Step 4: Identify secondary outputs caused by internal resistance: thermal energy (heat) warming the motor casing and cells.
- Step 5: Conclude that energy is conserved through these transformations.
ANSWER: Chemical energy in the cells transforms into electrical energy, which then converts into mechanical energy (rotation) and thermal energy (heat due to electrical resistance in the motor windings). The motor gets warm because not all electrical energy is converted into motion; some is inevitably lost as heat.
DIFFICULTY: hard
