## ELI5
Inverse proportion is one idea in three forms: a table of pairs, the equation x × y = k, and the rule y = k/x. Take speed and time for a fixed journey: every row of the table multiplies to the same k. Write the equation once and k is locked in; then y = k/x works like a machine — feed it any x and the matching y drops out, because y is simply k shared among x. Fill a missing table cell by dividing the constant by the known value, whether the columns are speed and time, workers and days, or pressure and volume.

## DEFINITION
In inverse proportion, two quantities vary such that their product remains constant: when one quantity increases, the other decreases proportionally so that \(x \times y = k\) (where \(k\) is a non-zero constant). The algebraic representation is \(y = \frac{k}{x}\) or equivalently \(xy = k\), and tabular representation shows pairs \((x, y)\) satisfying this constant product.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can construct a table of values for an inverse proportion given one pair and the constant of proportionality, write the algebraic equation \(xy = k\) or \(y = k/x\), and use either representation to find missing values in real-world problems (e.g., speed-time, workers-days, pressure-volume).
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers representing inverse proportion relationships in tables and algebraic equations (using \(xy = k\) or \(y = k/x\)) and solving for unknowns. It does NOT cover graphing inverse proportion (hyperbolas) or joint variation with more than two variables, which are beyond Class 8 scope.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine your school is organizing a picnic: 12 students can be seated in each bus, and you need 5 buses. If only 8 students sit per bus, how many buses are needed? This is inverse proportion – as students per bus decrease, buses needed increase. Start by identifying the two varying quantities (students per bus, number of buses) and check if their product is constant (12 × 5 = 60). Write the constant product \(k = 60\). Then set up the table with known and unknown pairs, and write the equation \(x \times y = 60\). The key insight: in inverse proportion, the product of the two quantities stays the same, so we can always find a missing value by dividing the constant by the known value.

Takeaway: one known pair fixes k forever — compute it, write xy = k or y = k/x, and generate the whole table by division. Exam format: expect 'complete the table and write the equation' — present the equation explicitly; filled cells alone earn only part of the marks. Verification beat: multiply each completed pair back; every product must return k exactly. Why students miss it: they fill cells by 'same difference' reasoning, which belongs to linear patterns, not to inverse proportion — here the product is constant, never the gap between values.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If \(x\) and \(y\) are in inverse proportion and \(x = 4\) when \(y = 15\), what is the constant of proportionality \(k\)?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The constant \(k = x \times y = 4 \times 15 = 60\). A wrong answer (e.g., \(k = 19\) or \(k = 3.75\)) reveals the student is adding or dividing instead of multiplying, confusing inverse proportion with direct proportion or not grasping that the product is constant.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why the equation \(y = \frac{k}{x}\) represents inverse proportion, and describe what happens to \(y\) when \(x\) is doubled.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The equation \(y = k/x\) shows that \(y\) is the constant \(k\) divided by \(x\); since \(xy = k\), the product is fixed. If \(x\) doubles, \(y\) becomes half because \(y_{\text{new}} = k/(2x) = \frac{1}{2}(k/x) = y/2\). A wrong explanation (e.g., "\(y\) also doubles") shows the student thinks it is direct proportion or does not understand the reciprocal relationship.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A table shows the number of workers \(w\) and days \(d\) to complete a job: \(w = 6, d = 10\); \(w = 12, d = ?\); \(w = ?, d = 4\). Fill in the missing values assuming inverse proportion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Constant \(k = 6 \times 10 = 60\). For \(w = 12\): \(d = 60/12 = 5\). For \(d = 4\): \(w = 60/4 = 15\). Completed table: (6,10), (12,5), (15,4). A wrong answer (e.g., \(d = 20\) for 12 workers) indicates the student used direct proportion (\(w\) doubled so \(d\) doubled) instead of inverse.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the tabular representations of direct proportion (\(y = 3x\)) and inverse proportion (\(xy = 24\)) for \(x = 2, 4, 6\). What pattern do you notice in the products and ratios?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Direct: \(x=2,y=6\); \(x=4,y=12\); \(x=6,y=18\). Ratio \(y/x = 3\) constant, product \(xy\) changes (12,48,108). Inverse: \(x=2,y=12\); \(x=4,y=6\); \(x=6,y=4\). Product \(xy = 24\) constant, ratio \(y/x\) changes (6, 1.5, 0.67). A student who says both have constant ratio or both have constant product confuses the two types.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Rohan solved an inverse proportion problem: "If 8 machines take 6 hours, how many hours for 12 machines?" He wrote: \(8 \times 6 = 48\), then \(12 \times h = 48\), so \(h = 48/12 = 4\). But he also wrote \(8/6 = 12/h\) and got \(h = 9\). Which method is correct? Identify the error in the incorrect method.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The first method (constant product) is correct: \(h = 4\) hours. The second method sets up a direct proportion (\(8/6 = 12/h\)), which is wrong because machines and hours are inversely proportional. The error is treating inverse proportion as direct proportion by equating ratios instead of products. A student who picks the second method does not recognize the inverse relationship.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-life problem (Indian context) that involves inverse proportion, write the algebraic equation, and make a table with at least four pairs of values.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: "A water tank is filled by 5 pipes in 8 hours. How long with 8 pipes?" Equation: \(p \times t = 40\) (where \(p\) = pipes, \(t\) = hours). Table: (1,40), (2,20), (4,10), (5,8), (8,5), (10,4). A poor answer uses direct proportion (e.g., more pipes, more time) or fails to keep the product constant, showing the student cannot generate a valid inverse proportion scenario.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often write the equation as \(y = kx\) (direct proportion) instead of \(y = k/x\) or \(xy = k\). For example, given "10 workers finish in 15 days", they set up \(10/15 = w/d\) and solve for \(w\) when \(d=5\) getting \(w = 10/3\), not realizing the product \(10 \times 15 = 150\) must be constant, so \(w = 150/5 = 30\). They confuse the constant ratio of direct proportion with the constant product of inverse proportion.
## ON_FAILURE
Remediation: 1) Have the student physically model with counters: "6 workers × 10 days = 60 worker-days". 2) Ask: "If we double workers to 12, what happens to days? (half, 5 days)". 3) Show the product stays 60. 4) Contrast with direct proportion: "If 6 workers earn ₹600 in 10 days, what do 12 workers earn in 10 days?" (double, ₹1200) – here ratio is constant. 5) Practice: Given a table, compute both ratios and products; identify which is constant. 6) Finally, write the correct equation \(xy = k\) and solve.

## THIS_FORMULA

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A school has 20 classrooms each with 40 desks. If they rearrange to have 25 desks per classroom, how many classrooms will be needed? Assume the total number of desks remains the same.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the two quantities: number of classrooms (\(c\)) and desks per classroom (\(d\)). They are inversely proportional because total desks = constant.
- Step 2: Find the constant product \(k = c \times d = 20 \times 40 = 800\) desks.
- Step 3: Write the inverse proportion equation: \(c \times d = 800\) or \(c = \frac{800}{d}\).
- Step 4: Substitute the new desks per classroom \(d = 25\): \(c = \frac{800}{25} = 32\).
- Step 5: Interpret: 32 classrooms are needed. Verify: \(32 \times 25 = 800\) desks, matches total.
REASONING: The total number of desks is fixed, so as desks per classroom increase, the number of classrooms must decrease such that their product (total desks) stays 800. The algebraic equation \(c \times d = 800\) captures this constraint directly.
ANSWER: 32 classrooms
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A car travels a fixed distance at 60 km/h taking 4 hours. If the speed is increased to 80 km/h, how long will the journey take? Show the inverse proportion table for speeds 40, 60, 80, 120 km/h.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recognize speed (\(s\)) and time (\(t\)) for a fixed distance are inversely proportional: \(s \times t = \text{distance} = k\).
- Step 2: Calculate constant \(k = 60 \times 4 = 240\) km.
- Step 3: Write equation: \(s \times t = 240\) or \(t = \frac{240}{s}\).
- Step 4: For new speed \(s = 80\): \(t = \frac{240}{80} = 3\) hours.
- Step 5: Build table: for \(s = 40\), \(t = 240/40 = 6\); \(s = 60\), \(t = 4\); \(s = 80\), \(t = 3\); \(s = 120\), \(t = 2\). Check each product equals 240.
REASONING: Distance is constant, so speed and time vary inversely. The table shows the reciprocal relationship: as speed doubles from 60 to 120, time halves from 4 to 2. The algebraic form \(t = 240/s\) allows quick calculation for any speed.
ANSWER: 3 hours; Table: (40,6), (60,4), (80,3), (120,2)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: 15 workers can build a wall in 12 days. After 4 days, 5 workers leave. How many more days will the remaining workers take to finish the wall? (Assume uniform work rate.)
STEPS:
- Step 1: Total work = workers × days = \(15 \times 12 = 180\) worker-days (constant).
- Step 2: Work done in first 4 days = \(15 \times 4 = 60\) worker-days.
- Step 3: Remaining work = \(180 - 60 = 120\) worker-days.
- Step 4: Workers now = \(15 - 5 = 10\). Let \(d\) be additional days needed. Inverse proportion: \(10 \times d = 120\).
- Step 5: Solve: \(d = 120 / 10 = 12\) days.
- Step 6: Total days = \(4 + 12 = 16\) days. Verify: First 4 days (15 workers) + next 12 days (10 workers) = \(60 + 120 = 180\) worker-days.
REASONING: This combines inverse proportion with a multi-step work problem. The total work (constant product) is split into two phases with different worker counts. The algebraic representation \(w \times d = k\) is used for each phase separately, then combined.
ANSWER: 12 more days (total 16 days)
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A farmer has enough feed to last 30 cows for 10 days. He buys 10 more cows. 
(a) Write the inverse proportion equation relating cows (\(c\)) and days (\(d\)). 
(b) How many days will the feed last now? 
(c) If he wants the feed to last 12 days, how many cows can he have? 
Explain your reasoning for each part.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and solving for different unknowns using the inverse proportion model.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that total feed = cows × days = constant.
- Step 2: Calculate constant: \(30 \times 10 = 300\) cow-days.
- Step 3: For (a), equation: \(c \times d = 300\).
- Step 4: For (b), new cows = 40, so \(d = 300/40 = 7.5\) days.
- Step 5: For (c), desired days = 12, so \(c = 300/12 = 25\) cows.
ANSWER: (a) \(c \times d = 300\) or \(d = 300/c\). (b) 7.5 days. (c) 25 cows. The feed quantity is fixed, so cows and days are inversely proportional.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: In a factory, 6 machines produce 300 units in 5 hours. The manager wants to produce the same 300 units in 3 hours. 
(a) Are machines and hours directly or inversely proportional for a fixed output? Justify.
(b) How many machines are needed? 
(c) If only 8 machines are available, how long will it take to produce 300 units? 
(d) A student says: "Since 6 machines take 5 hours, 1 machine takes 30 hours, so 10 machines take 3 hours." Evaluate this reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying methods, identifying correct proportional reasoning, and evaluating a peer's solution.
STEPS:
- Step 1: For fixed output, more machines → less time, so inverse proportion.
- Step 2: Constant \(k = 6 \times 5 = 30\) machine-hours.
- Step 3: For 3 hours: machines needed = \(30/3 = 10\) machines.
- Step 4: For 8 machines: time = \(30/8 = 3.75\) hours = 3 hours 45 minutes.
- Step 5: Evaluate student's reasoning: "1 machine takes 30 hours" is correct (unitary method for inverse proportion), and "10 machines take 3 hours" is correct (\(30/10 = 3\)). The reasoning is valid and matches the constant product method.
ANSWER: (a) Inversely proportional because product (machine-hours) is constant for fixed output. (b) 10 machines. (c) 3.75 hours (3 hours 45 minutes). (d) The student's reasoning is correct; they used the unitary method for inverse proportion, which is equivalent to the constant product method.
DIFFICULTY: hard
