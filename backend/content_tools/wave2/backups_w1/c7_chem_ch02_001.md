## ELI5
Morning in an Indian kitchen: water boiling on the stove, dough rolled into rounds, milk souring in a pot, a mango pickling in spice. Every scene is a change - but only some create new substances. Identifying changes in everyday life means observing, recording and sorting such transformations into physical or chemical categories based on whether the substance's identity survives. The kitchen issues a quiz before breakfast.

## DEFINITION
Identifying changes in everyday life is the systematic process of observing, recording, and categorizing natural and man-made transformations—such as the melting of ice, the burning of wood, or the souring of milk—into physical or chemical categories based on whether new substances are formed.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which of the following processes represents a change where no new substance is formed: dissolving sugar in water, burning paper, rusting of an iron nail, or souring of milk?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Dissolving sugar in water is a change where no new substance is formed because the sugar molecules merely disperse among water molecules and can be recovered by evaporating the water. A student who incorrectly chooses "rusting of an iron nail" confuses a temporary physical mixing with a permanent chemical reaction that creates iron oxide.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why does melting a candle wax block differ fundamentally from burning a candle wick during a demonstration in the science lab?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Melting only changes the solid wax into a liquid state without altering its chemical composition, whereas burning the wick involves a chemical reaction with oxygen in the air to produce carbon dioxide gas, water vapor, and soot. A student who thinks both are identical physical changes fails to recognize that combustion creates entirely new chemical substances with different properties.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A student takes a magnesium ribbon, cleans it with sandpaper, and burns it in the air with a dazzling white light, leaving behind a white powdery ash (magnesium oxide). Calculate or identify the nature of this change and explain why it cannot be classified as a simple physical change.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Identify the starting material (magnesium ribbon, Mg) and the reactant in air (oxygen gas, $O_2$). Step 2: Recognize the product formed as a white powder, which is magnesium oxide ($MgO$). Step 3: Compare the properties of magnesium ($Mg$) and magnesium oxide ($MgO$); they have entirely different chemical properties, confirming a new substance is born. Step 4: Conclude that because the original metal is consumed and cannot be easily recovered by cooling or physical separation, it is a chemical change. A student who calls this a physical change because "it just changed shape into powder" misses the fundamental creation of a new chemical compound ($2Mg + O_2 \rightarrow 2MgO$).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the following two changes observed in a household: cutting a fresh lemon into slices and squeezing the juice, versus leaving iron nails out in the monsoon humidity where they turn reddish-brown. Compare how these two changes differ in terms of substance identity and reversibility.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Cutting a lemon alters only its size and shape while the juice retains its chemical makeup (citric acid), making it a physical change that is mechanically irreversible in form but chemically identical. Conversely, rusting of iron creates hydrated iron(III) oxide ($Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O$), a brand new chemical species that destroys the original metal property, rendering it an irreversible chemical change. A student who believes cutting a lemon is chemical because "you can never put the slices back together" confuses physical reversibility of shape with chemical composition permanence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the statement: "All changes that involve a release of heat are chemical changes, and all changes that absorb heat are physical changes." Is this statement scientifically valid? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
This statement is invalid because heat changes can occur in both physical and chemical processes. For example, dissolving certain salts like sodium hydroxide in water releases heat (an exothermic physical process), while melting ice absorbs heat (an endothermic physical change). A student who judges the statement as true relies on an oversimplified rule of thumb instead of checking whether chemical bonds are broken and new substances are formed.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple home observation log with four everyday household activities and explain the exact observable criteria you would use to prove whether each activity causes a physical or chemical change.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good response lists activities such as boiling water, ripening a banana, dissolving salt, and cooking an egg, assigning specific criteria like "no new gas or color change = physical" versus "permanent color change, odor change, or new solid formed = chemical." A poor response merely guesses labels without giving concrete, observable sensory evidence or chemical reasoning.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often assume that any change involving a drastic change in appearance, size, or state (such as tearing paper into tiny shreds, boiling water into steam, or dissolving salt in water) is a chemical change because "the original object is gone and cannot be easily put back together." They confuse physical alterations in dimensions or phase with chemical reactions that rearrange atoms into entirely different molecules.
## ON_FAILURE
To correct this, take a sample of common salt and dissolve it in water until it disappears completely. Ask the student if the salt is gone forever; then, heat the saltwater solution in a flat dish until all the water evaporates, revealing solid salt crystals left behind. Emphasize that because the original substance can be recovered through simple physical methods, no new chemical substance was created, proving it is a physical change.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: During a kitchen cooking session in a typical Indian home, a piece of fresh paneer is accidentally left out on the counter overnight and develops a sour smell, a slimy texture, and altered taste by morning. Identify the type of change and outline the observation steps to prove your classification.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the initial substance (fresh paneer with mild taste and solid texture) and the final altered material (spoiled paneer with sour odor and changed texture).
- Step 2: Check whether any physical separation method can easily revert the spoiled paneer back into fresh, sweet paneer.
- Step 3: Observe if new chemical compounds (such as organic acids produced by microbial action) have been generated, altering the internal chemical composition permanently.
- Step 4: Conclude that because new substances with distinct odors and tastes are formed and the change is completely irreversible by physical means, it is a chemical change.
REASONING: Spoilage in food involves biochemical breakdown where microorganisms convert sugars and proteins into new chemical products, making it a classic permanent chemical change rather than a simple physical state alteration.
ANSWER: Chemical change, because new substances with a sour odor and altered chemical composition are permanently formed.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A block of solid camphor ($C_{10}H_{16}O$) is kept in a wooden wardrobe to keep insects away over several weeks, and it slowly disappears completely without leaving any liquid behind. Classify this change and explain the step-by-step molecular behavior.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the starting state of camphor as a solid block inside the wardrobe.
- Step 2: Observe that the solid transitions directly into gas (sublimation) without melting into a liquid phase.
- Step 3: Analyze whether the gas molecules escaping into the air are chemically different from the solid camphor molecules; determine they remain identical ($C_{10}H_{16}O$) molecules merely dispersed in air.
- Step 4: Conclude that since no new chemical species are created and the process involves only a state transition, it is classified as a physical change.
REASONING: Sublimation is a phase change where thermal energy allows molecules to escape intermolecular forces directly into the vapor phase without breaking intramolecular chemical bonds.
ANSWER: Physical change (sublimation), because camphor molecules change only their physical state without forming a new chemical substance.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student mixes iron filings and sulfur powder in an evaporating dish at room temperature, then takes half the mixture and heats it strongly over a Bunsen burner until a glowing, dark-grey solid compound (iron(II) sulfide, $FeS$) is formed. Compare the unheated mixture versus the heated product to classify the changes.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine the unheated mixture of iron ($Fe$) and sulfur ($S$); note that iron can be physically separated using a bar magnet because individual properties remain unchanged.
- Step 2: Identify the action of heating the mixture as supplying activation energy for a chemical reaction.
- Step 3: Write the chemical equation for the heated reaction: $Fe + S \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} FeS$.
- Step 4: Test the final dark-grey solid ($FeS$) with a bar magnet and observe that it is no longer attracted to the magnet.
- Step 5: Conclude that the unheated mixture represents a physical change (no new substance, easy physical separation), while the heated product represents a chemical change (new compound with entirely different properties formed).
REASONING: Combining elements physically retains their individual properties, whereas heating them initiates a chemical bond formation resulting in a distinct new chemical substance with unique properties.
ANSWER: Unheated mixture = physical change (physical blending, easy separation); Heated product = chemical change ($FeS$ formed, properties altered).
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A shiny copper vessel ($Cu$) left exposed to moist air in a courtyard gradually develops a dull green coating (basic copper carbonate) over several months. A student argues this is just a physical change of color caused by dust settling on the metal. Explain why the student's reasoning is chemically flawed using observable evidence.
COMPETENCY: Interpreting chemical equations and observable evidence in everyday corrosion
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine the actual composition of the green coating formed on copper: it is a mixture of copper carbonate ($CuCO_3 \cdot Cu(OH)_2$) resulting from reaction with oxygen, moisture, and carbon dioxide in the air.
- Step 2: Note that unlike loose surface dust, this green layer is chemically bonded to the metal surface and cannot be wiped off with a simple dry cloth without scraping away underlying metal.
- Step 3: Contrast physical color changes (like painting a wall) with chemical corrosion where the original metal is consumed to form a new compound.
- Step 4: Conclude that the formation of the green layer is a chemical change because a new chemical substance is born on the copper surface.
ANSWER: The student is incorrect because the green coating is not mere dust; it is a new chemical compound (basic copper carbonate) formed by the reaction of copper with atmospheric gases and moisture, making it a permanent chemical change.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: During a science exhibition, a student demonstrates three activities: (i) crushing ice cubes into fine shaved ice, (ii) burning a sugar cube until it turns into a black carbon mass, and (iii) dissolving washing soda in water. The student classifies all three as physical changes because "heat or mechanical force was applied in each case." Evaluate this claim, correct any misclassifications, and provide the chemical justification for each.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating and classifying complex multi-activity everyday transformations
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze activity (i)—crushing ice into shaved ice. The chemical formula ($H_2O$) remains identical; only particle size changes. This is a physical change.
- Step 2: Analyze activity (ii)—burning a sugar cube into carbon and water vapor. Organic molecules undergo combustion ($C_{12}H_{22}O_{11} + O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + H_2O + C$), producing new substances and energy. This is a chemical change.
- Step 3: Analyze activity (iii)—dissolving washing soda in water. Sodium carbonate dissolves into ions without forming a new chemical substance, and evaporation recovers the solid salt. This is a physical change.
- Step 4: Evaluate the student's claim: the student mistakenly assumed that applying heat or force automatically dictates chemical change, ignoring molecular identity preservation.
ANSWER: The student's claim is false. Crushing ice and dissolving washing soda are physical changes (no new substances formed), while burning sugar is a chemical change (new substances like carbon dioxide and water are created via combustion).
DIFFICULTY: hard
