## ELI5
Count marigold petals in the school garden - 3, 5, 8, 13, 21 keep turning up, and sunflower seed spirals hum the same tune. This is the Virahanka-Fibonacci rhythm: choose a starting pair and build each newcomer by adding the two terms immediately before it. The step is recursive, not fixed - the gap between terms keeps changing, because each fresh sum becomes raw material for the next. Generations of Sanskrit poets counted matra metres with it, and the garden keeps printing it: nature's favourite addition chain.

## DEFINITION
The Virahanka-Fibonacci sequence is an ordered list of numbers where each term after the first two is formed by taking the sum of the two immediately preceding terms, starting with $1, 1$ (or $0, 1$). This recursive pattern frequently appears in biological structures and growth patterns in nature.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are watching sunflower seeds spiral outwards or counting the petals on different flowers like marigolds and jasmine in your school garden. You might notice that flower petals often come in specific numbers: 3, 5, 8, 13, or 21! To understand how these numbers grow, we begin by writing down our first two terms: $1$ and $1$. Just like adding integers from Class 6, we look at patterns by combining numbers step-by-step. The key insight of this sequence is its recursive rule: to find any new term, you simply add the current term to the one right before it.

**Takeaway:** each Virahanka-Fibonacci term is the sum of the two terms immediately preceding it; the chosen starting pair seeds the whole sequence. **In the exam:** write the first two terms, then add neighbouring pairs forward; missing terms fall out by working around each gap both ways. **Check yourself:** continue the petal count two more terms and check every new entry against the pair immediately before it. **Why students miss it:** they add a fixed amount each time, ignoring that the step itself grows because each new sum feeds the next.The garden keeps printing the chain, and every new term arrives from the marriage of its two elders.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the first five terms of the Virahanka-Fibonacci sequence if we start with $1$ and $1$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct sequence is $1, 1, 2, 3, 5$. A student who answers $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ is confusing this sequence with natural counting numbers because they are using arithmetic addition of $1$ rather than adding the two previous terms together ($1+1=2$, $1+2=3$, $2+3=5$).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is the 6th term of the Virahanka-Fibonacci sequence equal to $8$? Explain the rule used to get this number.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The 6th term is $8$ because the sequence starts as $1, 1, 2, 3, 5$, and the 6th term is found by adding the 4th and 5th terms ($3 + 5 = 8$). A student who says the answer is $6$ because it is the 6th position is confusing the term's *position* in the sequence with its *value*.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If a rabbit breeding puzzle gives a sequence starting with $1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13$, what is the sum of the first 7 terms of this sequence?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps:
1. Identify the first 7 terms: $1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13$.
2. Write out the addition expression: Sum = $1 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 5 + 8 + 13$.
3. Compute step-by-step using integer addition: $1 + 1 = 2$, $2 + 2 = 4$, $4 + 3 = 7$, $7 + 5 = 12$, $12 + 8 = 20$, $20 + 13 = 33$.
4. Verify with the special property that the sum of the first $n$ Fibonacci numbers equals the $(n+2)\text{th}$ term minus $1$. Here, the 9th term is $34$, so $34 - 1 = 33$.
Answer: 33. A student who gets 20 likely stopped adding at the 6th term or made an arithmetic addition error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the differences between the Virahanka-Fibonacci sequence ($1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8\dots$) and a standard arithmetic sequence where each term increases by $2$ ($1, 3, 5, 7, 9\dots$). How does the rule for generating the next term differ between them?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
In an arithmetic sequence, you add a constant value (common difference, e.g., $+2$) to the previous term. In the Virahanka-Fibonacci sequence, the amount you add is not constant; instead, you add the two *variable* preceding terms together, which causes the numbers to grow much faster. A student who thinks both are generated by adding a fixed number fails to grasp the recursive nature of nonlinear growth patterns.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that in any Virahanka-Fibonacci sequence, if you take any three consecutive terms like $3, 5, 8$, the product of the outer two terms ($3 \times 8 = 24$) is always close to the square of the middle term ($5^2 = 25$). Evaluate this claim and state whether it is true for the terms $2, 3, 5$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The claim is evaluated by testing the given terms: the outer terms are $2$ and $5$, giving a product of $2 \times 5 = 10$. The middle term is $3$, and its square is $3^2 = 9$. The values $10$ and $9$ differ by $1$, which follows Cassini's identity pattern for Fibonacci numbers ($Fn-1 \times Fn+1 = Fn^2 \pm 1$). A student who calculates $2+5$ instead of multiplication or incorrectly squares the middle term does not understand how to evaluate mathematical relationships between sequence terms.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a new number sequence by changing the starting two terms to $2$ and $3$, while keeping the Virahanka-Fibonacci rule (each term is the sum of the previous two). Write down the first six terms of your new sequence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The expected sequence is: Term 1 = $2$, Term 2 = $3$, Term 3 = $2 + 3 = 5$, Term 4 = $3 + 5 = 8$, Term 5 = $5 + 8 = 13$, Term 6 = $8 + 13 = 21$. The sequence is $2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21$ (this is known as the Lucas sequence). A poor answer shows incorrect addition (e.g., adding Term 1 and Term 3 instead of the two immediate predecessors).
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the Virahanka-Fibonacci rule by adding the term number (index) to the value, or by adding the *first* and *current* term instead of the *two immediate preceding* terms. For example, given the sequence $1, 1, 2, 3, 5$, a student might try to find the next term by adding $1 + 5 = 6$ or $2 + 5 = 7$ incorrectly, rather than adding the last two terms ($3 + 5 = 8$).
## ON_FAILURE
Guide the student by physically pointing to the last two numbers written down on the page. Say: "Put your left finger on the very last number, and your right finger on the number just before it. Now, use addition to combine *only* these two finger values to write down the next number." Practice this explicitly with the sequence $1, 1, 2$ to find $3$.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A nursery in Delhi plants saplings in a special layout where the number of saplings in row 1 is $1$, row 2 is $1$, and each subsequent row follows the Virahanka-Fibonacci rule. Calculate the total number of saplings across the first 6 rows.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the starting terms given by the problem: Row 1 = $1$, Row 2 = $1$.
- Step 2: Apply the recursive rule to find rows 3 through 6. Row 3 = $1 + 1 = 2$, Row 4 = $1 + 2 = 3$, Row 5 = $2 + 3 = 5$, Row 6 = $3 + 5 = 8$.
- Step 3: List all 6 terms representing the saplings per row: $1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8$.
- Step 4: Add the values of all terms together to find the total: Total = $1 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 5 + 8 = 20$.
- Step 5: Verify the addition steps: $1+1=2$, $2+2=4$, $4+3=7$, $7+5=12$, $12+8=20$ saplings.
REASONING: By strictly following the rule that each row is the sum of the previous two rows, we build the sequence accurately. Summing these individual row values gives the cumulative total required by the real-world garden scenario.
ANSWER: 20 saplings
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In a math club activity, a student writes down a Virahanka-Fibonacci sequence starting with $2$ and $3$. Find the 8th term of this modified sequence.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write down the given first two terms: Term 1 = $2$, Term 2 = $3$.
- Step 2: Compute Term 3 by adding Term 1 and Term 2: $2 + 3 = 5$.
- Step 3: Compute subsequent terms using the rule: Term 4 = $3 + 5 = 8$, Term 5 = $5 + 8 = 13$, Term 6 = $8 + 13 = 21$, Term 7 = $13 + 21 = 34$.
- Step 4: Compute the final requested 8th term by adding Term 6 and Term 7: $21 + 34 = 55$.
- Step 5: Double-check the full sequence list: $2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 55$.
REASONING: Even when the starting numbers change from the standard $1, 1$, the recursive rule remains identical: every new term is generated by summing the two preceding terms.
ANSWER: 55
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A spiral staircase in a heritage building in Jaipur has steps whose widths follow the standard Virahanka-Fibonacci sequence ($1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13\dots$). If the width of step 4 is 3 cm and step 7 is 13 cm, find the absolute difference between the sum of the first 5 steps' widths and the width of step 7.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the first 5 terms of the sequence: $1, 1, 2, 3, 5$.
- Step 2: Calculate the sum of the first 5 steps: Sum = $1 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 5 = 12$ cm.
- Step 3: Identify the width of step 7 from the problem statement: Width of step 7 = $13$ cm.
- Step 4: Set up the subtraction for the absolute difference between step 7's width and the sum of the first 5 steps: $|13 - 12| = 1$ cm.
- Step 5: Interpret the result with proper units: The absolute difference is 1 cm.
REASONING: Combining operations on sequences requires first generating or recalling the sequence values accurately, computing sums using whole number addition, and finally calculating absolute differences as learned in integer and number operations.
ANSWER: 1 cm

Which student's sequence reaches a larger value, and by how much does Student B's 5th term exceed Student A's 5th term?
