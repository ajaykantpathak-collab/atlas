## ELI5
Cut roof beams to meet at 45, 90 or 120 degrees and the largest corner names the triangle's family: all angles under a right angle is acute-angled, exactly one right angle is right-angled, one angle above a right angle is obtuse-angled. The inside total never changes with the family, which is why two obtuse corners can never share a triangle - they would overspend the budget before the third corner arrived. Judge by the largest angle; it crowns the type every time.

## DEFINITION
Triangles based on angles are classified into three types: an acute-angled triangle (where all three interior angles are less than $90^\circ$), a right-angled triangle (where exactly one interior angle is equal to $90^\circ$), and an obtuse-angled triangle (where exactly one interior angle is greater than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are building a wooden roof truss for a classroom shed, and you need to cut timber beams so they meet at specific corner angles like $45^\circ$, $90^\circ$, or $120^\circ$. How do you know what kind of structural triangle you have created just by looking at its corner measurements? To begin, recall from geometry that angles are measured in degrees using a protractor, where a square corner represents exactly $90^\circ$. By examining all three interior angles of any triangle and comparing them to $90^\circ$, we can sort every triangle into one of three distinct families. The key insight is that the largest angle in the triangle determines its classification: if the largest angle is less than $90^\circ$, it's acute; if it equals $90^\circ$, it's right; and if it is greater than $90^\circ$, it's obtuse.

**Takeaway:** angle classification keys off the largest angle: under, equal to, or beyond a right angle. **In the exam:** sum the given angles, compare the largest to a right angle, and name the family with reason. **Check yourself:** sort the truss corners 45, 90, 120 into their families and justify each. **Why students miss it:** they read one small angle and ignore the obtuse giant waiting at another corner.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Can a triangle have two obtuse angles? Explain why or why not using mathematical reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, a triangle cannot have two obtuse angles because an obtuse angle is greater than $90^\circ$. If two angles were each greater than $90^\circ$, their sum would exceed $180^\circ$, which violates the fundamental angle sum property of triangles. A student who says "yes" does not understand the limitation on the total sum of angles in a triangle.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
In $\triangle PQR$, angle $\angle P = 50^\circ$, $\angle Q = 60^\circ$, and $\angle R = 70^\circ$. Classify this triangle based on its angles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps:
1. Examine the given interior angles: $\angle P = 50^\circ$, $\angle Q = 60^\circ$, and $\angle R = 70^\circ$.
2. Compare each angle to $90^\circ$: $50^\circ < 90^\circ$, $60^\circ < 90^\circ$, and $70^\circ < 90^\circ$.
3. Identify the largest angle, which is $70^\circ$.
4. Since all angles are less than $90^\circ$, classify the triangle.
Answer: Acute-angled triangle. A wrong answer suggests the student added the angles together instead of checking individual angle sizes.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How many right angles can a single triangle contain, and why can it not contain more than that number?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
A triangle can contain at most one right angle ($90^\circ$). Since the sum of all three angles in a triangle is $180^\circ$, having two right angles would mean $90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ$, leaving $0^\circ$ for the third angle, which is impossible. A student who thinks a triangle can have two right angles forgets that a triangle requires three distinct vertices and sides with non-zero angle openings.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Rahul is incorrect. To classify a triangle by its angles, we must look at the *largest* angle present. Since $95^\circ$ is greater than $90^\circ$, the triangle is an obtuse-angled triangle, regardless of the other two acute angles. A wrong evaluation shows the student misinterprets classification rules by averaging or focusing on the majority rather than the maximum angle.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often look at only one angle or confuse classification by sides (scalene, isosceles, equilateral) with classification by angles (acute, right, obtuse). For example, given a triangle with angles $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$, a student might incorrectly call it an "acute triangle" because they see two acute angles, ignoring the vital $90^\circ$ right angle that makes it a right-angled triangle.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student makes this error, guide them through a step-by-step checklist: 
1. Ask the student to list all three angle measurements of the triangle on their slate. 
2. Have them use a pencil to point to the largest angle among the three. 
3. Ask: "Is this largest angle less than, equal to, or greater than $90^\circ$?" 
4. Remind them: "The classification depends entirely on this single largest angle—if it's $90^\circ$, it's right; if it's over $90^\circ$, it's obtuse; if all are under, it's acute."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A carpenter cuts a triangular wooden brace for a shelf in his workshop in Delhi. The interior angles are measured as $42^\circ$, $48^\circ$, and $90^\circ$. Classify this triangle based on its angles and explain your reasoning with full steps.

STEPS:
- Step 1: List all the given interior angle measurements of the triangular brace: $\angle 1 = 42^\circ$, $\angle 2 = 48^\circ$, and $\angle 3 = 90^\circ$.
- Step 2: Compare each individual angle against the benchmark angle of $90^\circ$: $42^\circ < 90^\circ$, $48^\circ < 90^\circ$, and $90^\circ = 90^\circ$.
- Step 3: Identify the maximum or largest angle among the three values, which is $90^\circ$.
- Step 4: Apply the classification rule: since the largest angle is exactly equal to $90^\circ$, the triangle is classified as a right-angled triangle.
- Step 5: Verify the angle sum property: $42^\circ + 48^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ$, confirming the triangle is geometrically valid.

REASONING: By checking all interior angles and identifying that exactly one angle measures $90^\circ$, we satisfy the precise condition for a right-angled triangle, disregarding the other two acute angles.
ANSWER: Right-angled triangle
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An architect is designing a decorative garden pavilion in Jaipur. One of the structural steel triangular panels has interior angles measuring $110^\circ$, $35^\circ$, and $35^\circ$. Determine the classification of this triangular panel based on its angles.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Write down the given angle measurements of the triangular panel: $110^\circ$, $35^\circ$, and $35^\circ$.
- Step 2: Check the sum of these angles to ensure a valid triangle: $110^\circ + 35^\circ + 35^\circ = 180^\circ$, which satisfies the angle sum property.
- Step 3: Locate the largest angle among the three values by comparing them: $110^\circ$ is greater than both $35^\circ$ and $35^\circ$.
- Step 4: Compare the largest angle ($110^\circ$) to $90^\circ$. Since $110^\circ > 90^\circ$, this angle is an obtuse angle.
- Step 5: Conclude the triangle classification based on the presence of an obtuse angle: because one interior angle exceeds $90^\circ$, the triangle is an obtuse-angled triangle.

REASONING: An obtuse-angled triangle requires only one interior angle to be greater than $90^\circ$. The remaining two angles being acute ($35^\circ$ each) does not change this classification.
ANSWER: Obtuse-angled triangle
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A surveyor measures a triangular plot of land in Tamil Nadu. Two of the interior angles are $55^\circ$ and $35^\circ$. Find the third interior angle, and then classify the triangle based on its angles. Show all algebraic steps.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Let the unknown third angle be represented by the variable $x$. Identify the two given angles: $55^\circ$ and $35^\circ$.
- Step 2: Recall that the sum of all interior angles in any triangle is always $180^\circ$. Set up the algebraic equation: $55^\circ + 35^\circ + x = 180^\circ$.
- Step 3: Simplify the known terms on the left side of the equation: $90^\circ + x = 180^\circ$.
- Step 4: Solve for $x$ by subtracting $90^\circ$ from both sides: $x = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$.
- Step 5: Examine all three angles now known ($55^\circ, 35^\circ, 90^\circ$). Since the largest angle is $90^\circ$, classify the triangle as a right-angled triangle.

REASONING: By first finding the missing angle using the angle sum property ($180^\circ$), we uncover that the third angle is a right angle, which immediately dictates the triangle's classification.
ANSWER: The third angle is $90^\circ$, making it a right-angled triangle.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Priya says, "If a triangle has angles $50^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $70^\circ$, it is an acute triangle. Therefore, if I multiply all its angle measurements by 2, the new triangle with angles $100^\circ$, $120^\circ$, and $140^\circ$ will still be an acute triangle because all angles are still growing larger together." Evaluate Priya's statement mathematically and explain why she is correct or incorrect.
COMPETENCY: Error identification, justification, and conceptual reasoning regarding angle scaling and triangle properties.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze Priya's premise about the first triangle: angles $50^\circ, 60^\circ, 70^\circ$ are all less than $90^\circ$, so it is indeed an acute triangle, and their sum is $50^\circ + 60^\circ + 70^\circ = 180^\circ$.
- Step 2: Analyze Priya's proposed second triangle with angles $100^\circ, 120^\circ$, and $140^\circ$.
- Step 3: Test the angle sum property for Priya's second set of angles: $100^\circ + 120^\circ + 140^\circ = 360^\circ$.
- Step 4: Compare this sum to the geometric rule that the interior angles of a triangle must always sum to exactly $180^\circ$. Since $360^\circ \neq 180^\circ$, Priya's second shape cannot even exist as a triangle.
- Step 5: Furthermore, evaluate the angle values themselves: $100^\circ, 120^\circ, 140^\circ$ are all greater than $90^\circ$, and a triangle cannot have an angle sum exceeding $180^\circ$.
ANSWER: Priya is completely incorrect. First, multiplying all angles by 2 gives a sum of $360^\circ$, which violates the fundamental triangle angle sum of $180^\circ$ (her shape is impossible). Second, even if we considered individual angle types, angles over $90^\circ$ are obtuse, not acute.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: An architectural firm is designing roof trusses. Truss A has angles $x^\circ$, $x^\circ$, and $x^\circ$ (an equilateral triangle). Truss B has angles $40^\circ$, $50^\circ$, and $90^\circ$. Truss C has angles $120^\circ$, $30^\circ$, and $30^\circ$. 
(a) Find the exact value of $x$ for Truss A and classify all three trusses by their angles.
(b) If the firm needs a truss where one structural beam meets another at a square corner, which truss should they select, and what is its angle classification?
COMPETENCY: Multi-step algebraic angle calculation combined with practical design selection and classification justification.
STEPS:
- Step 1: For Truss A, use the angle sum property: $x + x + x = 180^\circ \implies 3x = 180^\circ \implies x = 60^\circ$. Since all angles are $60^\circ$ (less than $90^\circ$), Truss A is an acute-angled triangle.
- Step 2: For Truss B, examine the angles $40^\circ, 50^\circ, 90^\circ$. The largest angle is $90^\circ$, so Truss B is a right-angled triangle.
- Step 3: For Truss C, examine the angles $120^\circ, 30^\circ, 30^\circ$. The largest angle is $120^\circ$ (greater than $90^\circ$), so Truss C is an obtuse-angled triangle.
- Step 4: Address part (b): A "square corner" refers to a $90^\circ$ right angle. Review the classifications from Step 1-3.
- Step 5: Select Truss B because it contains a $90^\circ$ angle, making it a right-angled triangle suitable for square corner joints.
ANSWER: (a) $x = 60^\circ$. Truss A is acute-angled, Truss B is right-angled, and Truss C is obtuse-angled. (b) The firm should select Truss B, which is a right-angled triangle, because it contains the required $90^\circ$ square corner.
