## ELI5
Comparing decimals is a march that starts at the point: whole numbers first, then tenths give the verdict, then hundredths if the race is still tied. A longer tail of digits is a costume, not muscle - the first differing place outranks everything after it. Line the points up like runners at a start line and read the race place by place.

## DEFINITION
Comparing and ordering decimals is the process of determining which decimal number is greater, smaller, or if they are equal by comparing their place values from left to right, starting with the whole number part.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to correctly order a set of three or more decimal numbers and justify which is larger by comparing corresponding place values (tenths, hundredths, etc.).
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers comparing decimals using place value and ordering them from least to greatest or greatest to least. It excludes negative decimals, rounding, and scientific notation.
## WALKTHROUGH
If you went to the market to buy snacks, would you rather have 0.5 kilograms of mangoes or 0.45 kilograms of mangoes? To figure this out, imagine the decimal point is a anchor; first, compare the whole numbers (the left side), then move to the tenths place (the first digit after the point), then the hundredths. It is like a race: the number with a bigger digit in the earliest place value wins! The key insight is to treat empty spaces as zeros (so 0.5 becomes 0.50), which makes it much easier to see that 50 hundredths is more than 45 hundredths.

**Takeaway:** compare decimals place by place from the whole part through tenths to hundredths - the first differing place decides. **In the exam:** in ordering questions, align the points, pad tails with zeros freely, and compare column by column until a winner shows. **Check yourself:** re-run the walkthrough's market weighing - compare the tenths first and let the finer places only break ties. **Why students miss it:** students crown the longer decimal - the walkthrough's mango piles are a no to that; digit count never outranks the first differing place. Rehearse the exam move once more - align, compare, decide - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You have ₹12.40, ₹12.05, and ₹12.50. Order them from the smallest amount to the largest.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the whole number parts: All are 12.
- Step 2: Look at the tenths place: 4, 0, and 5.
- Step 3: Compare the tenths: 0 is smallest, 4 is next, 5 is largest.
- Step 4: Arrange: 12.05 < 12.40 < 12.50.
- Step 5: Verify: 05 paise < 40 paise < 50 paise.
REASONING: Since the whole numbers are the same, the tenths digit decides the order. This is like comparing paise in a rupee context.
ANSWER: ₹12.05, ₹12.40, ₹12.50
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A runner finished a race in 15.6 seconds, another in 15.62 seconds, and a third in 15.09 seconds. Who was the fastest?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write numbers as 15.60, 15.62, 15.09.
- Step 2: Compare whole parts: all 15.
- Step 3: Compare tenths: 6, 6, and 0. 0 is the smallest.
- Step 4: Compare the two with 6 in the tenths place: 60 vs 62. 60 is smaller.
- Step 5: Fastest time is the smallest number.
REASONING: In races, smaller time is better. Comparing place value determines the winner.
ANSWER: 15.09 seconds
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Arrange 0.7, 0.77, and 0.07 in descending order.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Align by decimal points: 0.70, 0.77, 0.07.
- Step 2: Compare tenths: 7, 7, 0. 0 is smallest.
- Step 3: Compare remaining: 0.70 vs 0.77. 7 in tenths is same, compare hundredths.
- Step 4: 7 > 0. So 0.77 > 0.70.
- Step 5: Order descending: 0.77, 0.7, 0.07.
REASONING: Descending means largest to smallest. Breaking into hundredths ensures clarity.
ANSWER: 0.77, 0.7, 0.07
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you have two water bottles, one with 0.9 litres and one with 0.89 litres, which has more water? Explain your reasoning using place values.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Step 1: Convert to same number of digits: 0.90 and 0.89.
- Step 2: Compare tenths: 9 vs 8.
- Step 3: Since 9 > 8, the number starting with 0.9 is larger.
- Step 4: Conclude 0.9 > 0.89.
ANSWER: 0.9 litres has more water because it has 9 tenths, while 0.89 only has 8 tenths. Even though 89 looks like a bigger number, the value is determined by the tenths place.
