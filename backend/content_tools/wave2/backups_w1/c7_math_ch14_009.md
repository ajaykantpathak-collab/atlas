## ELI5
A carpenter in Jaipur must raise a vertical support from a horizontal beam - dead square, no protractor in the toolbox. The compass does the job by refusing to measure: equal arcs swung from two marked points cross exactly where the perpendicular must rise. Whether the point sits on the line or hovers above it, the recipe is the same - equal distances, two crossings, one straight join - and the meeting angle is 90 degrees by construction, not by eye.

## DEFINITION
Constructing a perpendicular from a point to a line using a compass and a ruler involves drawing a line segment that meets the given line at a 90-degree angle, passing through a specified point either lying on the line or outside of it.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are a carpenter in your school workshop in Jaipur, and you need to hammer a vertical wooden support perfectly straight (perpendicular) up from a marked point on a horizontal wooden beam. To do this with mathematical precision without relying on a bulky protractor, you can use a basic geometric tool: your compass and ruler. We previously learned how to construct perpendicular bisectors of line segments and measure angles. Now, we will combine those ideas to anchor a 90-degree line directly through any specific target point. The key insight is that an arc drawn from our target point will intersect the baseline at two equidistant points, allowing us to find the exact midpoint and drop a plumb-like perpendicular.

**Takeaway:** a perpendicular is built from equal arcs, not measured - symmetry guarantees the right angle. **In the exam:** keep the compass width fixed between arcs; name the point, sweep equal arcs, join the crossings. **Check yourself:** check the finished support the way the walkthrough's carpenter would: the corner should read square against the beam. **Why students miss it:** students let the compass slip or change radius between arcs, and the crossing drifts a degree at a time - the carpenter gets a leaning support, not a wall. Constant radius keeps the crossings equidistant from the reference points, and that equidistance is exactly what forces the right angle.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Given a line $L$ and a point $P$ not on $L$, if you set your compass to a radius greater than the shortest distance from $P$ to $L$ and place the pointer at $P$, how many times will your arc intersect line $L$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is exactly 2 times. Working steps: 1) Place compass pointer at point $P$ outside line $L$. 2) Adjust the compass width so it easily reaches across line $L$. 3) Sweep an arc across line $L$; because the arc curves downwards and crosses the straight path of line $L$ at two distinct locations, it intersects it twice (let's call them points $A$ and $B$). A wrong answer indicates the student imagines a circle or arc touching a line tangentially instead of cutting across it.

A wrong answer shows confusion regarding where the center of the first compass sweep must be placed.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often change the compass radius halfway through a construction sequence—for instance, changing the radius when moving from marking arcs on the baseline to drawing the intersecting arcs below or above the line. For example, if a student opens the compass to 4 cm to find points $A$ and $B$ on the line, but then carelessly adjusts it to 5 cm to draw the final intersecting arcs, the resulting line will tilt and fail to form a true $90^\circ$ perpendicular.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that the compass is a tool for maintaining identical distances. Tell them: "Check your screw! If your compass legs wiggle or you change the gap between the pencil tip and needle, your entire symmetry breaks. Lock your compass width, write down or check the setting, and never alter the radius once an arc sequence has begun." Have them redraw the construction while consciously locking the hinge with their thumb.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Rahul is designing a rectangular garden bed in his school in Delhi. He draws a baseline path represented by line $MN$. A sprinkler must be installed at a flower bed located at point $P$, which is outside path $MN$. Help Rahul construct a perpendicular line from point $P$ to path $MN$ using only a ruler and compass.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given elements: Line $MN$ and an external point $P$. We need to construct a line segment passing through $P$ that meets $MN$ at $90^\circ$.
- Step 2: Place the compass pointer at point $P$. Adjust the compass width so that it is wide enough to cross line $MN$ at two distinct points.
- Step 3: Draw an arc that intersects line $MN$ at two points. Label these intersection points as $A$ and $B$.
- Step 4: With point $A$ as the center and a radius greater than half of the distance $AB$, draw an arc below line $MN$. Keeping the exact same compass width, place the compass pointer at point $B$ and draw another arc to intersect the first arc. Label this intersection point as $Q$.
- Step 5: Align your straightedge (ruler) with point $P$ and point $Q$. Draw a straight line passing through $P$ and $Q$ that meets line $MN$ at point $R$. Verify that $\angle PRM = 90^\circ$.

REASONING: By making arcs of equal radius from points $A$ and $B$, we create a symmetric kite or rhombus structure ($PAQB$). The line connecting $P$ and $Q$ is guaranteed to be the perpendicular bisector of segment $AB$, meaning it strikes line $MN$ precisely at a $90^\circ$ right angle.
ANSWER: Line segment $PR$ is perpendicular to line $MN$ ($PR \perp MN$).
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Priya needs to attach a vertical banner string right angle-wise from a specific anchor point $X$ that lies directly *on* a horizontal wire $CD$ stretched across the school stage in Mumbai. Construct a perpendicular line to wire $CD$ passing through point $X$.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given elements: Line $CD$ and a point $X$ lying directly on $CD$. We need to erect a perpendicular at point $X$.
- Step 2: Place the compass pointer at point $X$. Choose a convenient radius and draw two arcs on line $CD$, one to the left and one to the right of $X$. Label these intersection points on the line as $E$ and $F$ such that $EX = FX$.
- Step 3: Open the compass to a radius slightly larger than distance $EX$. Place the compass pointer at point $E$ and draw an arc above line $CD$.
- Step 4: Keeping the exact same compass width, place the pointer at point $F$ and draw a second arc above line $CD$ to intersect the first arc. Label this upper intersection point as $Y$.
- Step 5: Align your ruler with point $X$ and point $Y$. Draw the straight line $XY$. The line $XY$ is perpendicular to line $CD$ at point $X$.

REASONING: By creating two equal segments $EX$ and $FX$ on either side of $X$, we establish a balanced baseline. Constructing intersecting arcs of equal radius from $E$ and $F$ ensures that any point on that vertical path is equidistant from $E$ and $F$, creating a true $90^\circ$ normal line at $X$.
ANSWER: Line $XY \perp CD$ at point $X$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: An architect in Chennai is planning a triangular roof truss where the peak $T$ sits 6 cm above a beam $AB$ of length 10 cm. To ensure structural stability, she needs to drop a perpendicular from peak $T$ down to beam $AB$, meeting it at point $H$, and then calculate the length of the altitude $TH$ given that triangles $ATH$ and $BTH$ are congruent right triangles.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw baseline segment $AB = 10$ cm. Mark peak point $T$ at a perpendicular height of 6 cm above the midpoint of $AB$.
- Step 2: Place the compass at $T$ and sweep an arc intersecting $AB$ at two points, $S_1$ and $S_2$.
- Step 3: With centers at $S_1$ and $S_2$ and a fixed radius greater than half of $S_1S_2$, draw intersecting arcs on the opposite side of $AB$ to confirm the vertical alignment, verifying that the midpoint of $S_1S_2$ coincides with the midpoint of $AB$.
- Step 4: Draw the altitude line segment $TH$ from $T$ perpendicular to $AB$, meeting at $H$.
- Step 5: Since triangle $ATH$ is a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse $AT$ and base $AH = 5$ cm (half of $AB$), and height $TH = 6$ cm, apply the geometric property or verify lengths. Note that the construction guarantees $TH \perp AB$.
- Step 6: Conclude the exact length of altitude $TH$ as given by the initial problem parameters: 6 cm.
REASONING: Constructing the perpendicular from an external point establishes the height (altitude) of the triangle. The symmetry of the construction guarantees that the foot of the perpendicular $H$ bisects the base $AB$, allowing us to confirm the dimensions of the resulting right-angled triangles.
ANSWER: Altitude $TH = 6$ cm, and $TH \perp AB$.
