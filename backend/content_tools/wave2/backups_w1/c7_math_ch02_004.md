## ELI5
Rahul pays for 3 notebooks at 25 rupees and 2 at 15 rupees; Priya buys 5 notebooks at a blended rate - different slips, identical total. That is equivalence: same value, different costume. The costume tricks are the properties of operations - commute the order, regroup with brackets, or distribute a factor across a sum, the way 3 x (25 + 15) unfolds into 3 x 25 + 3 x 15. To test any claimed pair, evaluate both sides; matching totals mean equivalent. Form may change, value must not.

## DEFINITION
Equivalent expressions are arithmetic or algebraic expressions that yield the exact same numerical value for any given replacement of their components, reflecting the same underlying mathematical relationship in different forms.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can identify, generate, and verify equivalent arithmetic expressions by applying properties of operations (such as commutativity, associativity, and distributivity) without changing the final evaluated value.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers equivalent arithmetic expressions using integers and fractions with basic operations and grouping symbols (brackets), NOT solving linear algebraic equations with unknown variables on both sides.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are buying notebooks at the school stationery shop: Rahul buys 3 notebooks for ₹25 each and 2 notebooks for ₹15 each, while Priya buys 5 notebooks at a mixed bulk rate, but both end up paying the same total amount because their purchases can be written in different equivalent forms. To begin exploring this, we write down separate expressions for different grouping methods of the same total quantity. We connect this to our prior knowledge of operations and order of operations from Class 6, remembering how brackets can restructure a calculation. The key insight is that rearranging or grouping terms using arithmetic properties lets us write the same value in multiple distinct ways.

**Takeaway:** equivalence means an identical value for every single evaluation; commutativity, associativity and distributivity are the only legal reshufflings. **In the exam:** to prove equivalence, evaluate both sides once, or name the exact property used to reshape one side into the other. **Check yourself:** factor the common number out of the two-product sum and verify that the total still matches the original. **Why students miss it:** they distribute to the first term only, or reorder carelessly across a minus sign, and the value quietly changes.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often distribute a multiplier outside a bracket to only the first term inside the parentheses while ignoring subsequent terms, such as writing $3 \times (4 + 5)$ as $3 \times 4 + 5$ instead of $(3 \times 4) + (3 \times 5)$. Another common error is removing brackets preceded by a minus sign without changing the signs of the terms inside, such as writing $50 - (10 + 5)$ as $50 - 10 + 5$ instead of $50 - 10 - 5$.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that a multiplier outside a bracket acts on every single term living inside that bracket like a distributor sharing sweets with all children in a room. Have them draw arrows from the outside number to every term inside the parentheses before performing any calculation. For minus signs before brackets, explicitly coach them: "The negative sign outside is like a flashlight changing the sign of everything it shines on inside the bracket."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Ritu buys 4 packets of crayons for ₹12 each and 4 sketch pens for ₹8 each. Write two different equivalent arithmetic expressions for the total cost in rupees and evaluate them to verify they match.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given quantities and write Expression 1 representing separate item purchases: $(4 \times 12) + (4 \times 8)$.
- Step 2: Apply the distributive property in reverse to factor out the common number of items ($4$) to write Expression 2: $4 \times (12 + 8)$.
- Step 3: Evaluate Expression 1 step by step: $4 \times 12 = 48$, $4 \times 8 = 32$, then $48 + 32 = 80$.
- Step 4: Evaluate Expression 2 step by step: inside bracket $12 + 8 = 20$, then $4 \times 20 = 80$.
- Step 5: Verify both expressions yield ₹80, confirming they are equivalent forms of the total cost.
REASONING: Factoring out a common multiplier demonstrates how the distributive property allows us to write the same total calculation in two different forms while preserving the exact final value.
ANSWER: ₹80
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Simplify and find an equivalent expanded expression for $5 \times (10 - 3) + 2 \times (10 - 3)$ and evaluate its final value in kilograms of rice.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Observe that the binomial $(10 - 3)$ is common in both terms of the expression.
- Step 2: Factor out the common binomial $(10 - 3)$ to rewrite the expression as: $(5 + 2) \times (10 - 3)$.
- Step 3: Compute the sum in the first bracket: $5 + 2 = 7$.
- Step 4: Compute the difference in the second bracket: $10 - 3 = 7$.
- Step 5: Multiply the two results together: $7 \times 7 = 49$ kg.
REASONING: Treating identical bracketed quantities as common terms allows us to combine coefficients, transforming a sum of products into a single factored product.
ANSWER: 49 kg
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A school auditorium has rows of seats. Amit writes the total number of seats as $6 \times 15 + 6 \times 25 - 6 \times 10$. Rewrite this using a single bracket and calculate the total number of seats.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the common factor across all three terms in the expression, which is $6$.
- Step 2: Factor out $6$ using the generalized distributive property: $6 \times (15 + 25 - 10)$.
- Step 3: Perform addition inside the bracket first: $15 + 25 = 40$.
- Step 4: Perform subtraction inside the bracket next: $40 - 10 = 30$.
- Step 5: Multiply the outside factor by the simplified bracket result: $6 \times 30 = 180$ seats.
REASONING: The extended distributive property applies to any number of terms sharing a common factor, allowing us to factor it out once and simplify the internal arithmetic efficiently.
ANSWER: 180 seats

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2

Write two different expressions for the total revenue earned, prove they are equivalent, and calculate the total amount in rupees.

STEPS:

ANSWER: Expression 1: $(5 \times 18 + 5 \times 12) + (3 \times 18 + 3 \times 12)$ and Expression 2: $8 \times (18 + 12)$ are equivalent and both equal ₹240.
