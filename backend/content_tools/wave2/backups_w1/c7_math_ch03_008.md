## ELI5
Pay for a 45.50-rupee notebook and a 12.75-rupee pen and the shopkeeper adds rupees with rupees, paise with paise - decimal addition in one habit. Stack the numbers so the points sit in one straight column; only then do digits meet their own place value. Trailing zeros are welcome guests: annexing one to a shorter decimal changes nothing but fills every column for the carry. Add right to left as always, and the point in the answer drops straight down from where the columns held it.

## DEFINITION
The addition of decimal numbers is the mathematical process of combining two or more quantities involving fractional parts (tenths, hundredths, thousandths) by aligning their decimal points and adding digits within corresponding place-value columns.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can correctly set up, align, and add multi-digit decimal numbers of varying lengths and place values in real-world contexts (such as money and measurement) without making column-alignment or place-value carry errors.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers finite decimal numbers and their addition up to thousandths place, including carrying over across the decimal point, but excludes infinite repeating decimals or operations with complex algebraic decimal expressions beyond standard arithmetic and linear application.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you go to a local stationery shop in India and buy a notebook for ₹45.50 and a pen for ₹12.75. To find out the total amount you need to pay the shopkeeper, you cannot just add 4550 and 1275; you must combine whole rupees with whole rupees and paise with paise. To begin setting this up on paper, write the first number down and place the second number directly underneath it, ensuring that the decimal points are locked in a straight vertical column. This builds on your previous understanding of whole number addition and decimal place value. The key insight is that the decimal point acts like an anchor: aligning it guarantees that tenths are added to tenths, hundredths to hundredths, and ones to ones.

**Takeaway:** decimal addition is column addition wearing points: align the points, pad zeros, add right to left. **In the exam:** in money and measurement sums, keep the unit in the answer and drop the decimal point straight down. **Check yourself:** add the notebook-and-pen bill yourself and check the total against a quick mental estimate. **Why students miss it:** they align right edges instead of decimal points, letting tenths add to hundredths and quietly corrupt the total.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the primary rule for setting up the addition of two decimal numbers with different numbers of decimal places, such as $3.4$ and $2.15$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct approach is to align the numbers vertically by their decimal points so that digits of the same place value are in the same column. If a student says "line up the numbers on the right side like whole numbers" (writing $3.4$ + $2.15$ as $3.4 + 21.5$), it reveals that they are treating the decimal fraction part as a standard whole number, forgetting that place value positions dictate alignment.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do we sometimes add trailing zeros to a decimal number when performing addition, such as changing $7.2$ to $7.20$ before adding it to $4.35$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Adding trailing zeros does not change the value of the decimal number, but it helps visually balance the columns so that every place value has a digit to operate with, preventing place value shift errors. If a student believes that adding a zero changes the actual size or value of the number, they misunderstand the fundamental equivalence of decimals like $7.2$ and $7.20$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate the exact sum of $14.8$, $3.25$, and $0.076$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct sum is $18.126$. 
Steps:
1. Write vertically with aligned decimal points:
   $14.800$
   $+ 3.250$
   $+ 0.076$
2. Add the thousandths column: $0 + 0 + 6 = 6$
3. Add the hundredths column: $0 + 5 + 7 = 12$ (write 2, carry over 1 to tenths)
4. Add the tenths column including carry: $8 + 2 + 0 + 1 = 11$ (write 1, carry over 1 to ones)
5. Add ones, tens, and place the decimal point: $4 + 3 + 0 + 1 = 8$, and bring down $1$ in the tens place, resulting in $18.126$.
If a student gets $181.26$ or $4.736$, it reveals a failure to align the decimal points correctly or improper handling of carry-over values across place-value boundaries.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the following addition: $12.3 + 4.56 = 16.86$. Compare this to $12.3 + 0.456 = 12.756$. Why does shifting the decimal place of the second number drastically change the final sum's decimal component while leaving the whole number nearly identical?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The position of the digit relative to the decimal point determines its place value; in $4.56$, the $4$ is in the ones place, whereas in $0.456$, the $4$ is in the tenths place. When you add them to $12.3$, the whole number part changes based on whether you are adding $4$ ones or $0$ ones. If a student thinks the digits just shift positions without affecting the place value total, they fail to grasp that place value decreases tenfold with every step to the right of the decimal point.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently misalign decimal numbers by right-justifying them as if they were whole numbers (treating $12.4 + 3.15$ as $12.4 + 31.5$ by lining up the 4 and the 5), ignoring the position of the decimal point. Another common error is failing to carry over correctly across the decimal point when the sum of tenths exceeds 10.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student makes a right-alignment error, have them draw vertical grid lines for each place value column: Thousands, Hundreds, Tens, Ones, [DECIMAL POINT], Tenths, Hundredths, Thousandths. Physically force them to write the decimal points in the exact same vertical grid column before writing any digits. Remind them: "The decimal point is the anchor; never move the anchor, fill empty spaces with placeholder zeros instead!"

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Priya went to the market and bought $2.5\text{ kg}$ of potatoes, $1.75\text{ kg}$ of tomatoes, and $0.325\text{ kg}$ of green chillies. What is the total weight of vegetables she bought in kilograms?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given quantities and their units: Potatoes = $2.5\text{ kg}$, Tomatoes = $1.75\text{ kg}$, Chillies = $0.325\text{ kg}$. We need to find the total sum: $2.5 + 1.75 + 0.325$.
- Step 2: Append placeholder zeros so all numbers have the same number of decimal places (three places, matching thousandths): $2.500\text{ kg}$, $1.750\text{ kg}$, and $0.325\text{ kg}$.
- Step 3: Set up the vertical addition column aligning the decimal points:
  $2.500$
  $+ 1.750$
  $+ 0.325$
- Step 4: Add column by column from right to left:
  Thousandths: $0 + 0 + 5 = 5$
  Hundredths: $0 + 5 + 2 = 7$
  Tenths: $5 + 7 + 3 = 15$ (write 5, carry over 1 to the ones place)
  Ones: $2 + 1 + 0 + 1\text{ (carry)} = 4$
- Step 5: Place the decimal point directly aligned with the column, yielding $4.575\text{ kg}$.
REASONING: Aligning decimal points ensures that identical units (tenths with tenths, hundredths with hundredths) are combined. Adding placeholder zeros prevents column shifting errors when lengths of numbers differ.
ANSWER: $4.575\text{ kg}$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Rahul has a savings piggy bank. On Monday, he deposited ₹125.50, on Wednesday he added ₹48.75, and on Friday he added ₹12.00. How much total money is in his piggy bank now?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given monetary values: ₹$125.50$, ₹$48.75$, and ₹$12.00$. Total sum = $125.50 + 48.75 + 12.00$.
- Step 2: Verify that all decimal points are aligned vertically to separate rupees (whole numbers) from paise (hundredths).
- Step 3: Write out the vertical column addition:
  $125.50$
  $+ 048.75$
  $+ 012.00$
- Step 4: Compute the sum column by column:
  Hundredths (paise): $0 + 5 + 0 = 5$
  Tenths (paise): $5 + 7 + 0 = 12$ (write 2, carry 1 to ones)
  Ones: $5 + 8 + 2 + 1\text{ (carry)} = 16$ (write 6, carry 1 to tens)
  Tens: $2 + 4 + 1 + 1\text{ (carry)} = 8$
  Hundreds: $1 + 0 + 0 = 1$
- Step 5: Place the decimal point between ones and tenths to get ₹$186.25$.
REASONING: Money values are naturally expressed as decimals where two decimal places represent paise ($\frac{1}{100}$ of a rupee). Proper column alignment guarantees that rupees add to rupees and paise to paise.
ANSWER: ₹$186.25$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A runner is tracking her training laps around a park track. Her first lap was $1.08\text{ km}$, her second lap was $1.2\text{ km}$, and her third lap was $0.945\text{ km}$. If her goal is to run a total of $4.000\text{ km}$, how far has she run, and how much more does she need to run to reach her goal?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Add the lengths of the three completed laps: $1.08 + 1.2 + 0.945$.
- Step 2: Pad numbers with placeholder zeros to match three decimal places: $1.080$, $1.200$, and $0.945$.
- Step 3: Set up vertical addition:
  $1.080$
  $+ 1.200$
  $+ 0.945$
- Step 4: Compute total distance:
  Thousandths: $0 + 0 + 5 = 5$
  Hundredths: $8 + 0 + 4 = 12$ (write 2, carry 1)
  Tenths: $0 + 2 + 9 + 1\text{ (carry)} = 12$ (write 2, carry 1)
  Ones: $1 + 1 + 0 + 1\text{ (carry)} = 3$. Total run = $3.225\text{ km}$.
- Step 5: Subtract total run from the goal ($4.000 - 3.225$) to find the remaining distance:
  $4.000 - 3.225 = 0.775\text{ km}$.
REASONING: Combining multi-step arithmetic requires first finding the total sum of decimals through careful alignment and carrying, followed by a difference calculation to find the remaining shortfall.
ANSWER: She has run $3.225\text{ km}$ and needs $0.775\text{ km}$ more.
