## ELI5
Turn off a straight highway into a petrol pump and your two turns sit together on one straight edge: the exit angle and the leftover angle lie along the divider like two slices of one roti. Together they always make a straight angle - a full half-turn - because their outer arms form the straight road itself. The pair is therefore supplementary: measure one angle and the other surrenders by subtraction. Adjacent, sharing a common arm, summing to the straight line - that is the entire definition.

## DEFINITION
A linear pair of angles is a pair of adjacent angles formed when two lines intersect, whose non-common arms form a straight line and whose angle measures always sum up to exactly 180 degrees.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can identify linear pairs from geometric diagrams, set up linear equations using the 180-degree sum property, and accurately solve for unknown angle variables in multi-step algebraic problems.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers adjacent supplementary angles forming a straight line on a single plane, including algebraic equations involving linear pairs, but does not cover complex multi-line transversal angle networks or three-dimensional angle planes.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine walking along a straight divider on an Indian highway, then turning sharply onto a side road to enter a petrol pump; the two paths you took form angles along the straight edge of the main road. If you measure both turns together, they span a complete straight flat path, which is always a flat 180 degrees. To begin solving these problems mathematically, we first identify if two angles share a common vertex and a common arm while resting on a straight line. We connect this to our prior knowledge of straight angles from geometry and solving linear equations with variables on one side. The key insight is that whenever two angles form a linear pair, their sum is permanently locked at 180 degrees, allowing us to write an equation like $x + y = 180^\circ$ and solve for any missing value.

**Takeaway:** a linear pair is two adjacent angles whose outer arms form a straight line, so their sum is exactly a straight angle. **In the exam:** in algebra items, add the expressions and equate the sum to the straight-angle measure, never multiply. **Check yourself:** measure both turns at a side-road exit and check they total a half-turn. **Why students miss it:** they multiply the two expressions, or confuse the pair with complementary angles adding to a quarter-turn.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why the sum of the angles in a linear pair is always $180^\circ$ rather than $360^\circ$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
A linear pair rests on a straight line, which represents a straight angle measuring $180^\circ$ (half of a full circle's $360^\circ$). Students who believe it is $360^\circ$ confuse a straight line with a complete point rotation or full circle.

**A wrong answer (doubling the straight angle) reveals the student confused the line with the full turn** - a straight angle is the half-turn a folded fan shows, not the whole circle.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Two adjacent angles on a straight line are given as $(3x)^\circ$ and $(2x + 30)^\circ$. Find the exact numerical value of $x$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps: 
1. Recognize that the angles form a linear pair, so their sum is $180^\circ$.
2. Set up the equation: $3x + 2x + 30 = 180$.
3. Combine like terms: $5x + 30 = 180$.
4. Subtract 30 from both sides: $5x = 150$.
5. Divide by 5: $x = 30$. 
The final answer is $x = 30$. Students who subtract $30$ from $180$ before combining terms or make arithmetic errors during division will arrive at incorrect values like $36$ or $50$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare an angle of $95^\circ$ and its linear pair partner with two angles measuring $80^\circ$ and $100^\circ$. Can both sets be classified as linear pairs? Explain why or why not.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4

While $80^\circ$ and $100^\circ$ sum to $180^\circ$, they are not necessarily adjacent or sharing a common arm on a straight line unless explicitly stated. Students who say yes to both focus only on the numerical sum of $180^\circ$ and ignore the geometric requirement of adjacency on a straight line.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently confuse the linear pair sum property ($180^\circ$) with complementary angles ($90^\circ$) or full-turn angles ($360^\circ$). When solving algebraic equations like $4x + 20 = 180$, they often subtract $20$ from $180$ correctly to get $160$, but then divide by $4$ incorrectly or mistakenly add the coefficient to $180$, writing $4x = 200$ instead of $4x = 160$.
## ON_FAILURE

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A local park path splits into two diverging trails forming a linear pair. If one trail angle measures $48^\circ$, calculate the measure of the other trail angle, let's call it $y^\circ$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: Angle 1 = $48^\circ$, Angle 2 = $y^\circ$, and note they form a linear pair.
- step 2: Write down the linear pair equation: $48^\circ + y^\circ = 180^\circ$.
- step 3: Isolate the variable $y$ by subtracting $48$ from both sides: $y = 180 - 48$.
- step 4: Compute the subtraction: $180 - 48 = 132$.
- step 5: Verify the result: $48^\circ + 132^\circ = 180^\circ$, confirming the straight line sum.
REASONING: Because the two trails lie on a straight road divider, their combined span is a straight angle, which must equal $180^\circ$. Subtracting the known angle from $180^\circ$ gives the exact remaining span.
ANSWER: $132^\circ$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Two adjacent angles on a window frame form a linear pair where one angle is $5x$ and the other is $4x$. Find the value of $x$ and the measure of both angles.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify that the two angles form a linear pair, meaning their sum equals $180^\circ$.
- step 2: Set up the algebraic equation: $5x + 4x = 180$.
- step 3: Combine like terms on the left side: $9x = 180$.
- step 4: Divide both sides by the coefficient $9$: $x = \frac{180}{9} = 20$.
- step 5: Calculate individual angles by substitution: First angle = $5(20) = 100^\circ$, Second angle = $4(20) = 80^\circ$, and verify $100^\circ + 80^\circ = 180^\circ$.
REASONING: The linear pair property allows us to combine algebraic terms representing proportional parts of a $180^\circ$ straight angle into a single solvable equation.
ANSWER: $x = 20$, angles are $100^\circ$ and $80^\circ$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A surveyor measures two adjacent boundary angles forming a straight road divider. One angle is given as $(2x + 15)^\circ$ and the second angle is $(3x - 5)^\circ$. Find the numerical value of $x$ and the exact measure of each angle.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify that the adjacent boundary angles form a linear pair on a straight line, totaling $180^\circ$.
- step 2: Set up the equation incorporating both expressions: $(2x + 15) + (3x - 5) = 180$.
- step 3: Group like terms together (variables with variables, constants with constants): $(2x + 3x) + (15 - 5) = 180$, which simplifies to $5x + 10 = 180$.
- step 4: Isolate the variable term by subtracting 10 from both sides: $5x = 180 - 10 \Rightarrow 5x = 170$.
- step 5: Divide by 5 to solve for $x$: $x = \frac{170}{5} = 34$.
- step 6: Substitute $x = 34$ back into the expressions: First angle = $2(34) + 15 = 68 + 15 = 83^\circ$; Second angle = $3(34) - 5 = 102 - 5 = 97^\circ$. Verify sum: $83^\circ + 97^\circ = 180^\circ$.
REASONING: Combining algebraic expressions using the linear pair sum rule requires careful grouping of like terms and systematic inverse operations to find the unknown variable.
ANSWER: $x = 34$, angles are $83^\circ$ and $97^\circ$

- step 2: Set up the calculation using the given interior angle: $70^\circ + \text{Exterior} = 180^\circ$.
- step 3: Solve for the correct exterior angle: $\text{Exterior} = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ$.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two intersecting roads create four angles at a junction. Two adjacent angles along a straight path are expressed as $(4x - 10)^\circ$ and $(2x + 40)^\circ$. Set up an algebraic model to find $x$, determine if these specific expressions form a valid linear pair, and calculate all four angles at the junction.
COMPETENCY: Translating complex real-world situations to algebraic models and multi-step geometric deduction
STEPS:
- step 1: Model the linear pair relationship: $(4x - 10) + (2x + 40) = 180$.
- step 2: Simplify and combine like terms: $6x + 30 = 180$.
- step 3: Solve for $x$: $6x = 150 \Rightarrow x = 25$.
- step 4: Substitute $x = 25$ to find the two angles: First angle = $4(25) - 10 = 90^\circ$; Second angle = $2(25) + 40 = 90^\circ$.
- step 5: Use linear pair properties or vertical angle properties to find the remaining two angles at the junction (both are also $90^\circ$).
ANSWER: $x = 25$. The two angles are $90^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, meaning the intersecting roads are perpendicular, forming four right angles.
DIFFICULTY: hard
