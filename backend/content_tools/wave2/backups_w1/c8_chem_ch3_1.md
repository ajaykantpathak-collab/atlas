## ELI5
Stir sugar, lemon juice, salt and water into shikanji: you can still taste each one's sweetness or sourness, because nothing has turned into a new substance. That is a mixture — two or more substances physically combined in any proportion, each keeping its own chemical identity. A bowl of mixed dry fruits or a handful of soil is the same idea: distinct components, no chemical bonding between them. And whatever was mixed can be unmixed — physical methods can always take a mixture apart.

## DEFINITION
A mixture is a physical combination of two or more substances (elements or compounds) in any proportion, where each component retains its individual chemical properties and can be separated by physical methods. Unlike chemical compounds, the components of a mixture do not undergo a chemical reaction to form a new substance.

## WALKTHROUGH
When you stir a spoonful of sugar into a glass of lemon shikanji, you are making a mixture of water, sugar, lemon juice, and salt. You can still taste the sweetness of sugar and the sourness of lemon, which tells you that the components have not changed into something completely new. Building on your earlier understanding of physical changes where no new substances are formed, a mixture is created purely through physical mixing. To begin studying mixtures, we must observe how different materials are put together without any chemical bonding taking place between them. By examining everyday items like a bowl of mixed dry fruits or a handful of soil, we can identify their distinct components and recognize that no new chemical formula is created when they combine.

Takeaway: a mixture is a handshake, not a marriage — substances combined in any proportion, each keeping its own properties, separable by physical means because nothing chemically bonded. Exam format: expect mixture identification, component listing, and property-retention reasoning. Verification beat: name each component's surviving property — the iron in soil still rusts, the nuts in the mix still crunch. Why students miss it: they expect mixing to change things — the definition's whole point is that a mixture changes nothing chemical.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which of the following best defines a mixture in chemistry?
A) A substance formed by the chemical combination of two or more elements in a fixed ratio.
B) A substance consisting of two or more different materials physically combined in no fixed proportion.
C) A pure substance that cannot be broken down into simpler substances.
D) A liquid that dissolves any solid completely.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is B. A mixture consists of two or more different substances physically combined where each keeps its own properties and proportions can vary. Option A defines a compound, and Option C defines an element. A wrong answer shows the student is confusing the definitions of mixtures and chemical compounds, forgetting that mixtures have variable proportions and no chemical bonding.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do the individual components of a mixture retain their original properties?
A) Because they react chemically to form a stronger substance.
B) Because they are held together by powerful chemical bonds that change their structure.
C) Because no chemical reaction takes place between the components when they are mixed.
D) Because heat is always released when a mixture is formed.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct answer is C. When components form a mixture, they are only mixed physically, meaning their atoms and molecules do not bond chemically, allowing them to keep their original properties. A wrong answer indicates the student thinks mixing substances automatically triggers a chemical change, failing to distinguish between physical mixtures and chemical reactions.

The correct answer is B. Iron filings and sulfur powder mixed together form a physical mixture; the iron can be pulled out using a magnet because no chemical reaction has occurred between them yet. ## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The expected criteria: The student should propose testing a physical property where salt and sugar differ, or using a physical separation technique. For example, dissolving the mixture in a solvent where only one component dissolves, or heating carefully, or testing electrical conductivity of their solutions. A poor answer suggests performing a complex chemical titration or burning the powder, missing the key principle that physical mixtures can be separated by physical properties without altering chemical identities.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that when a solid dissolves in a liquid (like sugar in water), a chemical reaction has occurred to form a new substance because the solid "disappears." They fail to realize that dissolving is a physical mixing process where the solute particles merely disperse among the solvent molecules, and the original substance can be recovered intact by evaporation.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this, guide the student to perform a simple kitchen experiment: dissolve sugar in water, taste the solution to confirm it is sweet (proving sugar is still sugar), and then gently heat a sample in a pan until all water evaporates to show that solid sugar crystals remain behind unchanged. Emphasize that because the sugar did not change its chemical identity and can be physically recovered, it was always a mixture.

PROBLEM: A roadside snack vendor mixes puffed murmura (100g), roasted peanuts (30g), and sev (50g) to make bhel. State whether this is a mixture or a compound, identify its components, and explain why the components do not lose their individual taste.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify all individual ingredients added to the bowl (murmura, peanuts, sev).
- Step 2: Check if any new chemical substance is formed during the mixing process at room temperature.
- Step 3: Determine if the ingredients can be physically separated from each other by hand sorting.
- Step 4: Apply the definition of a mixture (physical combination, no fixed ratio, components retain properties).
- Step 5: Conclude that bhel is a physical mixture because no chemical reaction occurred and each item tastes like itself.
REASONING: Mixing solid food items involves no chemical bonding or heat exchange. Because the atoms of the murmura, peanuts, and sev do not bond chemically, their original flavors and textures remain completely unaltered.
ANSWER: Bhel is a physical mixture containing murmura, peanuts, and sev as its components. The components retain their individual tastes because no chemical reaction occurred during mixing.

- Step 2: Examine whether the soil particles have chemically reacted with the water molecules.

- Step 4: Check if the water molecule retains its chemical formula ($H_2O$) and properties while mixed with soil.
- Step 5: Conclude that muddy water is a mixture composed of solid soil and liquid solution.
REASONING: Muddy water is a classic heterogeneous mixture where insoluble solid particles are suspended in a liquid. Since water molecules and soil particles do not undergo a chemical reaction, they maintain their independent chemical identities.
ANSWER: Muddy water is a mixture. Its components are soil particles and water (containing dissolved minerals), and they retain their original properties because no chemical bond formed between them.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A laboratory technician has two unlabeled glass bottles: Bottle A contains a gray powder that is a mixture of iron filings and sulfur powder. Bottle B contains iron sulfide ($FeS$), which is a chemical compound formed by heating iron and sulfur. Describe two physical or chemical tests the technician can perform to definitively prove which bottle contains the mixture and which contains the compound.
COMPETENCY: Distinguishing mixtures from compounds based on property retention and separation methods
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall the key difference: in a mixture, components keep their individual properties; in a compound, properties change completely.
- Step 2: Apply a magnetic test—bring a magnet close to a sample from each bottle.
- Step 3: Apply an acid test—add dilute hydrochloric acid to a sample from each bottle and observe gas production.
- Step 4: Interpret the results chemically: iron in the mixture responds to a magnet, whereas the iron sulfide compound does not.
ANSWER: Test 1 (Magnetism): When a magnet is passed near Bottle A (mixture), the iron filings are attracted and separate from the sulfur. When passed near Bottle B (compound), the iron sulfide shows no magnetic attraction because iron is chemically bonded. Test 2 (Acid reaction): Adding dilute HCl to Bottle A releases hydrogen gas from iron while sulfur remains unaffected, whereas Bottle B releases hydrogen sulfide gas with a foul smell. This proves Bottle A is a mixture and Bottle B is a compound.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A manufacturing plant receives a shipment of industrial solvent described as "pure liquid acetone." However, testing reveals that the liquid has a variable boiling point range instead of a single sharp boiling point, and a small amount of solid residue is left behind when evaporated. Explain what this data reveals about the nature of the shipment and justify whether it classifies as a pure substance or a mixture.
COMPETENCY: Analyzing experimental evidence of physical properties to classify matter as pure or mixture
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the given data: variable boiling point range and solid residue left after evaporation.
- Step 2: Recall the properties of pure substances (fixed melting and boiling points, leave no residue upon complete vaporization).
- Step 3: Recall the properties of mixtures (boil over a temperature range, may leave residue if non-volatile components are present).
- Step 4: Conclude that the shipment is a mixture of acetone and non-volatile impurities.
- Step 5: Formulate the final chemical justification based on component independence and physical separation evidence.
ANSWER: The data proves that the shipment is NOT a pure substance; it is a liquid mixture. Pure substances melt and boil at exact, fixed temperatures and vaporize completely without leaving a residue. A variable boiling point range occurs because different components in a mixture boil at different temperatures. Furthermore, the solid residue left behind indicates the presence of non-volatile impurities physically dissolved or suspended in the liquid, confirming it is a mixture.
DIFFICULTY: hard
