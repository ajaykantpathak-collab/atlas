## ELI5
Pull up a mustard seedling and you are holding a two-part machine. Below the soil, the root system anchors the plant against wind and drinks up water and minerals — either one thick taproot like a carrot, or a bunch of thin, equal fibrous roots like wheat. Above ground, the shoot system runs the show: the stem is pillar and pipeline in one, holding the plant up while carrying water upward and food to every part; each leaf is a flat green kitchen trapping sunlight to cook food by photosynthesis; and each flower is the seed-maker, ripening into a fruit packed with seeds for the next generation.

## DEFINITION
A typical flowering plant has two systems: the shoot system above the ground — stem, leaves, flowers, and fruits — and the root system below it. The root fixes the plant and absorbs water and minerals; the stem supports the plant and transports water and food; the leaf performs photosynthesis; the flower is the reproductive part that develops into fruit and seeds.

## WALKTHROUGH
Pull up a small plant carefully — a mustard seedling or a tuft of grass — and hold the whole thing in your hand. The part that was under the soil is the root system; everything above is the shoot system. Look at the roots first: they grip the soil like an anchor so wind cannot topple the plant, and they drink — drawing water and minerals up from the soil. Some plants push one thick main root straight down, like the carrot or mustard — that is a taproot; others spread a bunch of thin, equal roots, like grass or wheat — that is a fibrous root. Now the stem: it is the plant's pillar and its pipeline — it holds the plant up toward the sun and carries water from the roots to the leaves and food from the leaves to every other part. The leaves are the plant's kitchen — flat green sheets that trap sunlight and mix it with water and air to cook food by photosynthesis; that is why a plant kept in darkness turns pale and dies. Finally the flowers: they are the seed-makers — after pollination a flower develops into a fruit carrying seeds, and each seed can grow into a new plant. Four parts, four jobs — anchor and drink, hold and carry, cook, and make seeds.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The root system (roots, below ground) and the shoot system (stem, leaves, flowers, and fruits, above ground). A wrong answer lumps everything above ground as "the plant" and forgets the roots' equal share.

**A wrong answer (splitting the systems wrongly) reveals the student housed the flower underground - roots and shoot divide the plant, and the shoot keeps stem, leaf, flower and fruit.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
What are the two main jobs of roots?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Roots anchor the plant firmly in the soil and absorb water and minerals from it. A wrong answer mentions only anchoring — forgetting that roots are also the plant's mouth, taking in everything the shoot needs.

**A wrong answer (assigning roots a single job) reveals the student hired the root for one post - it anchors the plant and drinks for it, two jobs in one.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
How are a carrot's roots different from a wheat plant's roots?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
A carrot has a taproot — one thick main root with side branches; wheat has fibrous roots — a cluster of many thin, equal roots with no main one. A wrong answer confuses the two types or names the plant part wrong (carrot's root is eaten, wheat's is not).

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why is the leaf called the kitchen of the plant?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The leaf is called the kitchen because it prepares food for the whole plant by photosynthesis — using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to make sugar. A wrong answer says "because leaves are eaten" — the kitchen is where food is made, not where it is eaten.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A plant's stem is cut. Which two functions stop working?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Support — the plant cannot stand upright — and transport — water from roots to leaves and food from leaves to other parts can no longer move. A wrong answer mentions only the standing-up role and misses the pipeline role.

**A wrong answer (losing transport in the count) reveals the student mourned only the droop - a cut stem also stops the water lift from roots to leaves.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
What does a flower develop into, and why does it matter?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A flower develops into a fruit containing seeds — it matters because the seeds grow into new plants; flowers are how the plant reproduces. A wrong answer treats the flower as decoration only, missing its reproductive job.

**A wrong answer (ending the flower at the petal) reveals the student closed the story early - the flower's job is the fruit and the seeds that travel forward in time.**

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think roots are only there to hold the plant. Roots are also the plant's mouth — they absorb all the water and minerals the shoot needs; a plant with damaged roots wilts even in full sun. Another error: thinking the stem merely "stands" — it is a live transport pipe, not just a pole.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A gardener pulls out a weed and finds a bunch of thin equal roots with no main one. Is it a taproot or fibrous-root plant, and how do you know?
STEPS:
- 1: Observe the root shape — many thin roots of equal size, no single main root.
- 2: Recall the two types — taproot has one main root; fibrous has many equal roots.
- 3: Match the observation to fibrous.
REASONING: The deciding feature is whether one main root exists or all roots are equal.
ANSWER: It is a fibrous-root plant — like grass or wheat — because no single main root stands out.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A potted plant is kept in a dark room for a week. Predict what happens to it and explain using the leaf's job.
STEPS:
- 1: Recall the leaf's job — it makes food using sunlight.
- 2: Remove sunlight — the kitchen shuts; no food is made.
- 3: Predict — the plant turns pale and weak because it cannot cook.
REASONING: The leaf needs light to run the food factory; darkness starves the plant.
ANSWER: The plant turns pale and may die — without light the leaf cannot photosynthesise and no food is produced.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A tree is uprooted in a storm. Explain which two functions failed the tree that day.
STEPS:
- 1: Identify what the roots were doing — anchoring the tree and absorbing water.
- 2: The uprooting means anchoring failed — the tree fell.
- 3: After falling, absorption stops too — no water reaches the leaves, so the tree wilts.
REASONING: The roots' double job means losing them breaks both support and supply.
ANSWER: The roots failed at anchoring (the tree fell) and absorption (no water can reach the leaves now).

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student says, "The flower is the most important part of the plant because it is the prettiest." Evaluate this claim using the jobs of all four parts.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating a claim by weighing functions, not appearance.
STEPS:
- 1: List the four parts and their jobs — roots anchor/drink, stem holds/carries, leaf cooks, flower makes seeds.
- 2: Ask whether the plant survives without each — no leaf means no food, no root means no water, no stem means no transport.
- 3: Compare with the flower — it matters for reproduction but a plant can live a season without blooming.
- 4: Conclude that "most important" depends on the function you measure, not on looks.
ANSWER: The claim confuses beauty with function — every part is essential to a different job; the leaf feeds the plant today while the flower only makes the next generation.
DIFFICULTY: medium

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A plant is wilted even though it is watered daily and gets sunlight. Which part would you check first and why?
COMPETENCY: Diagnosing a plant problem by tracing the part's job.
STEPS:
- 1: Wilting means water is not reaching the leaves.
- 2: Trace the supply line — water enters through roots and moves up through the stem.
- 3: Check the likely failure point — damaged roots cannot absorb water no matter how much is poured.
- 4: Conclude the roots (or a broken stem) are the first suspects.
ANSWER: Check the roots first — watering cannot help if the roots are damaged, because they are the plant's mouth and a broken supply line starves the leaves.
DIFFICULTY: hard
