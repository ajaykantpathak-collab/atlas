## ELI5
One multiplier standing outside a bracket is a host who must greet every guest inside. Whatever the bracket's terms — numbers, fractions, strangers — the host multiplies each in turn and pools the products. Left-to-right or right-to-left, the politeness is identical: the multiplier is distributed across the whole sum, and no term may leave un-greeted. That single social rule unpacks every bracket of this shape.

## DEFINITION
The distributive property of multiplication over addition states that multiplying a sum by a number gives the same result as multiplying each addend by the number and then adding the products. For any three numbers, variables, or algebraic expressions \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\), the expansion is \((a + b) \times c = a \times c + b \times c\). This rule allows us to remove brackets by distributing the multiplier \(c\) to each term inside the parentheses.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can correctly expand expressions of the form \((a + b)c\) where \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) are integers, fractions, decimals, or algebraic terms (including negative values), and can apply this expansion to simplify multi-step algebraic expressions and solve real-world problems involving grouped quantities multiplied by a common factor.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers expanding a binomial \((a + b)\) multiplied by a single term \(c\) (monomial) using the distributive property. It does NOT cover expanding the product of two binomials (e.g., \((a+b)(c+d)\)), factoring (the reverse process), or distributing over subtraction as a separate rule (since subtraction is handled by adding the additive inverse, covered in node c8_math_ch6_6).
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are buying stationery for your class: each student needs 1 pen (costing ₹\(a\)) and 1 notebook (costing ₹\(b\)). If there are \(c\) students, the total cost is \(c \times (a + b)\). You can calculate this by finding the cost per student \((a+b)\) and multiplying by \(c\), OR by finding the total cost of all pens \((c \times a)\) and all notebooks \((c \times b)\) separately and adding them. This is exactly the distributive property you used in Class 7 arithmetic (c7_math_ch02_006) with numbers like \(5 \times (3+2)\). Now, we apply it to algebraic terms. Write the expression \((a+b)c\). Identify the multiplier \(c\) outside the bracket. Multiply \(c\) by the first term \(a\) to get \(ac\). Multiply \(c\) by the second term \(b\) to get \(bc\). Add the two products: \(ac + bc\). The key insight is that the multiplier outside must reach *every* term inside the bracket, whether there are two terms or more.

Takeaway: the multiplier outside distributes to each term inside — two products, then the addition — with signs carried along by the integer rules. Exam format: expect expansions where the bracket sits on either side, negative multipliers included, followed by simplification of the result. Verification beat: spot-check one numeric case by computing both ways. Why students miss it: they greet the first term and wave at the rest — every term is owed its product, no exceptions.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
State the algebraic rule for expanding \((x + y) \times z\). Write the expanded form without using brackets.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The expanded form is \(xz + yz\) (or \(zx + zy\)). A wrong answer like \(xz + y\) or \(x + yz\) reveals the student does not understand that the multiplier \(z\) must be applied to *both* terms inside the bracket, a classic "incomplete distribution" error. If they write \((x+y)z = xyz\), they are confusing multiplication of terms with distribution.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why \(4 \times (5 + 3)\) and \(4 \times 5 + 4 \times 3\) give the same result. Use the meaning of multiplication as repeated addition in your explanation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
\(4 \times (5+3)\) means adding \((5+3)\) four times: \((5+3)+(5+3)+(5+3)+(5+3)\). Regrouping the additions gives four 5s and four 3s, which is \(4 \times 5 + 4 \times 3\). A student who only states "it's the distributive property" without explaining the grouping shows rote recall without conceptual understanding. If they say the results are different, they have a fundamental misconception about the property.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Expand and simplify: \((2p + 7q) \times (-3r)\). Show each step clearly.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Identify multiplier \(c = -3r\), terms \(a = 2p\), \(b = 7q\).
Step 2: Apply distributive property: \((2p) \times (-3r) + (7q) \times (-3r)\).
Step 3: Multiply coefficients and variables: \(-6pr + (-21qr)\).
Step 4: Simplify signs: \(-6pr - 21qr\).
A wrong answer like \(6pr - 21qr\) reveals a sign error in the first product (forgetting negative × positive = negative). An answer like \(-6p - 21q\) shows the student dropped the variable \(r\) during multiplication, treating it as if \(c\) were just \(-3\).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the two expansions: \((a + b) \times c\) and \(a + b \times c\). Are they equivalent? Justify your answer with a numerical example where \(a=2, b=3, c=4\).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
They are NOT equivalent. \((a+b) \times c = (2+3) \times 4 = 5 \times 4 = 20\). \(a + b \times c = 2 + 3 \times 4 = 2 + 12 = 14\) (order of operations: multiplication before addition). The brackets in the first expression force the addition to happen first. A student who says they are equal ignores the role of brackets and order of operations, a critical structural misunderstanding.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student expanded \((5x - 2y) \times 3z\) as \(15xz - 2y\). Identify the error, explain why it is wrong, and give the correct expansion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The error is incomplete distribution: the student multiplied \(3z\) by \(5x\) but forgot to multiply \(3z\) by \(-2y\). The correct expansion is \(15xz - 6yz\). The term \(-2y\) must become \(-2y \times 3z = -6yz\). If the student argues "the 3z only applies to the first term", they fundamentally misunderstand that a multiplier outside a bracket applies to the *entire* sum/difference inside.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-world word problem (using Indian context: rupees, metres, kg) that requires expanding \((a + b) \times c\) to solve. Define your variables clearly and show the solution.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: "A fruit seller packs gift boxes. Each box contains \(a\) apples (₹15 each) and \(b\) bananas (₹5 each). He prepares \(c = 20\) such boxes. Write an expression for total cost and expand it."
Variables: \(a\) = number of apples per box, \(b\) = number of bananas per box.
Cost per box = \(15a + 5b\). Total cost = \(20 \times (15a + 5b) = 300a + 100b\).
A poor answer uses only numbers (no variables) or creates a problem requiring \((a+b)(c+d)\), showing inability to distinguish the structure \((sum) \times (single\ term)\).
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
**Incomplete Distribution with Negative Terms:** Students correctly distribute a positive multiplier (e.g., \(3(x+2)=3x+6\)) but fail when the multiplier or the terms inside are negative. Specific error: \((-2)(x - 5) = -2x - 5\) (forgetting to multiply \(-2\) by \(-5\)) or \(4(-3a + 2b) = -12a + 2b\) (forgetting to multiply \(4\) by \(2b\)). They treat the negative sign as attached only to the first term or "forget" the second multiplication entirely when signs are involved. This stems from not viewing subtraction as adding a negative (i.e., \(a - b = a + (-b)\)) and not applying integer sign rules (c7_math_ch10_004, c7_math_ch10_005) during distribution.
## ON_FAILURE
1. Revert to arithmetic: Ask the student to compute \(-2 \times (3 - 5)\) numerically. First do bracket: \(3-5=-2\), then \(-2 \times -2 = 4\). Now distribute: \(-2 \times 3 + (-2) \times (-5) = -6 + 10 = 4\). Show both match.
2. Use the "area model" (rectangle): Draw a rectangle with sides \(-2\) and \((x-5)\). Split the \((x-5)\) side into \(x\) and \(-5\). Area = \(-2 \times x + (-2) \times (-5)\). Emphasize that the negative side length creates a "negative area" region that must be accounted for.
3. Drill integer sign rules separately: \((-) \times (+) = (-)\), \((-) \times (-) = (+)\). Then practice distribution with only integers: \((-3)(4-2)\), \(5(-6+1)\).
4. Finally, return to algebra: \((-2)(x-5) = (-2)(x) + (-2)(-5) = -2x + 10\). Insist on writing the intermediate step \((-2)(-5)\) explicitly.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A school orders uniform cloth for Class 8. Each student needs \(2.5\) metres of shirt cloth (cost ₹\(x\) per metre) and \(1.2\) metres of trouser cloth (cost ₹\(y\) per metre). If there are \(40\) students, write an algebraic expression for the total cost and expand it.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the cost per student: Shirt cloth cost = \(2.5 \times x = 2.5x\), Trouser cloth cost = \(1.2 \times y = 1.2y\). Total cost per student = \((2.5x + 1.2y)\) rupees.
- Step 2: Identify the multiplier: Number of students \(c = 40\). Total cost expression = \(40 \times (2.5x + 1.2y)\).
- Step 3: Apply distributive property: Multiply \(40\) by each term inside the bracket: \(40 \times 2.5x + 40 \times 1.2y\).
- Step 4: Compute the coefficients: \(40 \times 2.5 = 100\), so first term is \(100x\). \(40 \times 1.2 = 48\), so second term is \(48y\).
- Step 5: Write final expanded expression: \(100x + 48y\) rupees. Verify: If \(x=100, y=150\), per student = \(250+180=430\), total = \(430 \times 40 = 17200\). Expanded: \(100(100)+48(150)=10000+7200=17200\). Matches.

REASONING: The problem models a real-world scaling scenario where a combined per-unit cost (sum of two items) is multiplied by a quantity. The distributive property allows us to compute the total cost by scaling each component separately, which is often more practical for budgeting (knowing total spent on shirts vs trousers). The decimal multiplication builds on Class 7 decimal skills (c7_math_ch12_002, c7_math_ch12_003).

ANSWER: \(100x + 48y\) rupees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Simplify the expression: \(3a(2b - 5c) + 4a(3b + 2c)\). (Hint: Treat subtraction as adding a negative).

STEPS:
- Step 1: Recognize two separate distributive expansions added together. First part: multiplier \(3a\), binomial \((2b - 5c)\). Second part: multiplier \(4a\), binomial \((3b + 2c)\).
- Step 2: Expand first part: \(3a \times 2b + 3a \times (-5c) = 6ab - 15ac\). (Used integer rule: \(+ \times - = -\)).
- Step 3: Expand second part: \(4a \times 3b + 4a \times 2c = 12ab + 8ac\).
- Step 4: Combine the results: \((6ab - 15ac) + (12ab + 8ac)\).
- Step 5: Group like terms: \(6ab + 12ab = 18ab\); \(-15ac + 8ac = -7ac\).
- Step 6: Write final simplified expression: \(18ab - 7ac\).

REASONING: This example combines the current node (expanding (a+b)c) with the prior skill of simplifying algebraic expressions by collecting like terms (c7_math_ch04_006, c6_math_ch05_055). It also requires handling the negative term \(-5c\) correctly by treating the binomial as \(2b + (-5c)\), ensuring the distributive property applies uniformly. The variable \(a\) appears in both multipliers, creating like terms \(ab\) and \(ac\) after expansion.

ANSWER: \(18ab - 7ac\)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A rectangular garden has length \((3m + 2n)\) metres and breadth \(5p\) metres. Find the area in expanded form. If \(m=4, n=3, p=2\), calculate the numerical area and verify both forms give the same answer.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Area of rectangle = Length × Breadth = \((3m + 2n) \times 5p\).
- Step 2: Expand using distributive property: \(3m \times 5p + 2n \times 5p\).
- Step 3: Multiply coefficients and variables: \(15mp + 10np\). This is the expanded algebraic form.
- Step 4: Substitute values \(m=4, n=3, p=2\) into expanded form: \(15(4)(2) + 10(3)(2) = 15 \times 8 + 10 \times 6 = 120 + 60 = 180\) sq m.
- Step 5: Verify using unexpanded form: Length = \(3(4)+2(3)=12+6=18\) m. Breadth = \(5(2)=10\) m. Area = \(18 \times 10 = 180\) sq m.
- Step 6: Both methods yield 180 sq m, confirming the expansion is algebraically equivalent.

REASONING: This connects algebraic expansion to geometric interpretation (area model), reinforcing the concept visually and numerically. It uses substitution skills (c7_math_ch04_009) and integer/decimal arithmetic. The verification step is crucial for struggling students to build trust that the algebraic manipulation preserves value. It also previews the area model visualization in c8_math_ch6_10.

ANSWER: Expanded form: \(15mp + 10np\) sq m; Numerical area: \(180\) sq m
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Riya says: "To expand \((x + 5) \times 2\), I can just multiply the \(x\) by 2 and keep the 5 as it is, so the answer is \(2x + 5\)." Is Riya correct? Explain why or why not using the meaning of multiplication. Give the correct expansion.

COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction; conceptual understanding of distribution.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand Riya's method: she distributed 2 only to \(x\), not to 5.
- Step 2: Recall definition: multiplier must multiply *every* term in the sum.
- Step 3: Test with numbers: Let \(x=10\). LHS = \((10+5) \times 2 = 15 \times 2 = 30\). Riya's answer: \(2(10)+5=25\). Not equal.
- Step 4: Correct expansion: \(2 \times x + 2 \times 5 = 2x + 10\).
- Step 5: Verify: \(2(10)+10=30\). Matches.

ANSWER: Riya is incorrect. The multiplier 2 must be distributed to both terms inside the bracket: \((x+5) \times 2 = 2x + 10\). Her error is "incomplete distribution" – she treated the 5 as a constant not affected by the multiplier, but the bracket groups \(x\) and 5 together as a single quantity to be doubled.

DIFFICULTY: easy
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: The perimeter of an isosceles triangle is given by \((2a + b)\) cm, where \(a\) is the length of each equal side and \(b\) is the base. A larger similar triangle has sides scaled by factor \(k\). Write an expression for the perimeter of the larger triangle in expanded form. If the original triangle has \(a=6, b=4\) and the scale factor \(k=1.5\), find the new perimeter using both the unexpanded and expanded expressions to verify.

COMPETENCY: Translating real-world geometric scaling to algebraic expressions; applying distributive property with decimals; verification.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Original perimeter \(P = 2a + b\). Scaling by factor \(k\) means new perimeter \(P' = k \times (2a + b)\).
- Step 2: Expand using distributive property: \(P' = k \times 2a + k \times b = 2ka + kb\).
- Step 3: Substitute values into unexpanded form: \(P' = 1.5 \times (2 \times 6 + 4) = 1.5 \times (12 + 4) = 1.5 \times 16 = 24\) cm.
- Step 4: Substitute into expanded form: \(P' = 2(1.5)(6) + (1.5)(4) = 18 + 6 = 24\) cm.
- Step 5: Both give 24 cm. The expanded form \(2ka + kb\) shows the scaled contribution of each side type separately.

ANSWER: Expanded expression: \(2ka + kb\) cm. New perimeter: \(24\) cm.

DIFFICULTY: medium
