## ELI5
Two identical set-squares overlap corner for corner - and geometry writes that equality with a letter order: triangle PQR congruent to XYZ means P sits on X, Q on Y, R on Z. The order is law: side QR corresponds to YZ and angle P to angle X, purely from position in the name. Write the letters in a shuffled order and every matched part reports to the wrong address. Equal angles alone never guarantee congruence anyway - size must ride along with shape.

## DEFINITION
Congruence of triangles means that two triangles have the exact same shape and size, such that when one is placed over the other, they coincide completely. The corresponding sides and corresponding angles of congruent triangles are equal in measurement, denoted symbolically by the symbol $\cong$.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you and your friend buy two identical plastic triangular set-squares from the school stationery shop in Delhi. If you place your set-square directly on top of your friend's set-square, every edge and every corner fits over the other without any overhang. In geometry, we call such overlapping figures congruent geometric twins. To study them mathematically, we must match the corners (vertices) in the correct order so that side matches side and angle matches angle. We build upon our Class 6 knowledge of basic triangle classification and shapes to understand that rigid movement—sliding, turning, or flipping—does not change a triangle's size or shape. The key insight is that the order of letters in a triangle name matters: if $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$, then vertex $A$ matches $D$, $B$ matches $E$, and $C$ matches $F$.

**Takeaway:** a congruence statement is an address system: letter position fixes which vertex, side and angle correspond. **In the exam:** write the statement with vertices in matching order before quoting any equal side or angle. **Check yourself:** place one set-square over the other and name each corner's partner aloud. **Why students miss it:** they match parts by looks instead of by letter position, sending sides to the wrong twins.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If $\triangle PQR \cong \triangle XYZ$, which side corresponds to side $QR$?
A) $XY$
B) $YZ$
C) $XZ$
D) $YX$
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is B) $YZ$. In a congruence statement like $\triangle PQR \cong \triangle XYZ$, the order of the letters shows the exact correspondence of vertices: $P \leftrightarrow X$, $Q \leftrightarrow Y$, and $R \leftrightarrow Z$. Therefore, side $QR$ (formed by the second and third letters) corresponds to side $YZ$ (also formed by the second and third letters). A student who chooses A ($XY$) has matched the first two letters incorrectly or assumed the order doesn't matter, failing to realize that vertex position dictates correspondence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Rahul says that if two triangles have all three pairs of corresponding angles equal, they are always congruent, so $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$ just because their angles match. Is Rahul correct, and why?
A) Yes, equal angles mean identical size and shape.
B) No, triangles with equal angles can be different sizes (similar), just like a small photo and its enlarged copy.
C) Yes, because angle sum is always 180 degrees.
D) No, triangles can only be congruent if their perimeters are equal.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct answer is B) No, triangles with equal angles can be different sizes (similar), just like a small photo and its enlarged copy. Congruence requires both identical shape AND identical size. A student who chooses A believes that shape alone determines congruence, confusing congruence with similarity and ignoring that side lengths can be scaled up or down while keeping angles identical.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
In two congruent triangles, $\triangle DEF \cong \triangle LMN$. If side $DE = 5\text{ cm}$, angle $\angle E = 60^\circ$, and side $EF = 7\text{ cm}$, what is the length of side $MN$ and the measure of angle $\angle N$ if $LN = 5\text{ cm}$ and $EM$ is not given? Find the length of $LM$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $LM = 5\text{ cm}$ and angle $\angle M = 60^\circ$ (or dependent on the specific vertex matching). 
Steps:
1. Identify the given congruence: $\triangle DEF \cong \triangle LMN$.
2. Match the corresponding sides using vertex order: $DE \leftrightarrow LM$, $EF \leftrightarrow MN$, and $DF \leftrightarrow LN$.
3. Since $DE = 5\text{ cm}$ and $DE$ corresponds to $LM$, length $LM = 5\text{ cm}$.
4. Match corresponding angles: $\angle D \leftrightarrow \angle L$, $\angle E \leftrightarrow \angle M$, and $\angle F \leftrightarrow \angle N$.
5. Since $\angle E = 60^\circ$ and $\angle E$ corresponds to $\angle M$, angle $\angle M = 60^\circ$.
A student who computes incorrect values usually scrambles the vertex order, matching $DE$ with $MN$ instead of $LM$, demonstrating a failure to map corresponding positions using letter sequence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze why writing $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle FED$ is incorrect when vertex $A$ corresponds to $D$, vertex $B$ corresponds to $E$, and vertex $C$ corresponds to $F$. What does the incorrect notation imply about the triangles?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The correct answer is that vertex order must reflect exact point-to-point matching. Writing $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle FED$ implies that $A$ matches $F$, $B$ matches $E$, and $C$ matches $D$, which contradicts the actual given pairing where $A$ matches $D$ and $C$ matches $F$. A student who thinks the letter order is arbitrary misses that geometric congruence statements are precise mappings where position defines relationship.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that if $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$, then triangle $ACB$ is also congruent to triangle $PRQ$ under the same vertex correspondence. Evaluate this claim.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The statement is false regarding the specific vertex-to-vertex correspondence notation. While the geometric shape remains the same, changing the letter order from $\triangle ABC$ to $\triangle ACB$ changes the sequence of vertices, meaning side $AC$ is now paired with $PR$ instead of $AB$ with $PQ$. A student who agrees with the claim does not understand that a congruence statement is a strict ordered sequence where swapping letters alters the specified vertex matching.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often assume that the letters in a triangle's name can be written in any random order when stating congruence (e.g., writing $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle QPR$ when the actual correspondence is $A \leftrightarrow P$, $B \leftrightarrow Q$, $C \leftrightarrow R$). This error breaks the fundamental rule of vertex-to-vertex correspondence, leading to incorrect matching of sides and angles in multi-step geometry problems.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this error, have the student physically label the vertices of two cut-out paper triangles with letters $A, B, C$ and $P, Q, R$. Instruct them to place vertex $A$ directly on top of $P$, $B$ on top of $Q$, and $C$ on top of $R$, noting how they line up. Show them that writing $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$ matches the exact physical contact points, whereas mixing the letters creates a scrambled overlap where sides do not touch.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Two triangular garden beds in a school in Jaipur are congruent, such that $\triangle PQR \cong \triangle STU$. If side $PQ = 6\text{ m}$, side $QR = 8\text{ m}$, side $PR = 10\text{ m}$, and angle $\angle P = 37^\circ$, find the length of side $ST$ and the measure of angle $\angle S$.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given congruence statement: $\triangle PQR \cong \triangle STU$. This tells us the exact vertex correspondence: $P \leftrightarrow S$, $Q \leftrightarrow T$, and $R \leftrightarrow U$.
- Step 2: Match the required side $ST$ with its corresponding side in $\triangle PQR$. Since $S$ is the first vertex and $T$ is the second, $ST$ corresponds to side $PQ$.
- Step 3: Substitute the known value of $PQ$ into the relationship: $ST = PQ = 6\text{ m}$.
- Step 4: Match the required angle $\angle S$ with its corresponding angle in $\triangle PQR$. Since $S$ corresponds to $P$, angle $\angle S = \angle P$.
- Step 5: Substitute the known value of $\angle P$: $\angle S = 37^\circ$. Verify that all units are correctly stated as metres and degrees.

REASONING: Because congruent triangles have identical shape and size, every part of the first triangle matches its designated counterpart in the second triangle in exact order. Matching the letter positions guarantees we pick the correct corresponding side and angle without confusion.
ANSWER: Length $ST = 6\text{ m}$ and angle $\angle S = 37^\circ$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A carpenter cuts two triangular wooden tiles that are congruent, written as $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$. The perimeter of $\triangle ABC$ is $36\text{ cm}$. If $AB = 10\text{ cm}$ and $BC = 12\text{ cm}$, find the length of side $XZ$ of the second triangle.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall that the perimeter of a triangle is the sum of all its three sides: $\text{Perimeter} = AB + BC + CA$.
- Step 2: Substitute the known side lengths into the perimeter equation for $\triangle ABC$: $36 = 10 + 12 + CA$.
- Step 3: Solve for the unknown side $CA$ using algebraic manipulation: $36 = 22 + CA \implies CA = 36 - 22 = 14\text{ cm}$.
- Step 4: Use the congruence $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$ to find corresponding sides. Side $CA$ (connecting vertex $C$ to $A$) corresponds to side $ZX$ (connecting vertex $Z$ to $X$).
- Step 5: State the final length: $XZ = CA = 14\text{ cm}$, incorporating the correct unit.

REASONING: We first find the missing side of the first triangle using the total perimeter. Then, by applying the definition of triangle congruence, we transfer that side length to the corresponding side of the second triangle.
ANSWER: Length $XZ = 14\text{ cm}$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Two triangular metallic plates are congruent such that $\triangle LMN \cong \triangle RST$. We are given that $\angle L = 50^\circ$, $\angle M = 70^\circ$, and side $LM = 9\text{ cm}$. In $\triangle RST$, side $RS = 9\text{ cm}$, angle $\angle R = 50^\circ$, and angle $\angle S = (3x + 10)^\circ$. Find the value of $x$.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the congruence relationship $\triangle LMN \cong \triangle RST$ to identify corresponding angles. Vertex $M$ corresponds to vertex $S$, so angle $\angle M = \angle S$.
- Step 2: Substitute the known numerical value of $\angle M$ and the algebraic expression for $\angle S$: $70^\circ = (3x + 10)^\circ$.
- Step 3: Perform algebraic transposition to isolate the term with $x$: subtract $10$ from both sides: $70 - 10 = 3x \implies 60 = 3x$.
- Step 4: Solve for $x$ by dividing both sides by $3$: $x = \frac{60}{3} = 20$.
- Step 5: Verify the result by substituting $x = 20$ back into $\angle S$: $\angle S = 3(20) + 10 = 70^\circ$, which perfectly matches $\angle M = 70^\circ$.

REASONING: Congruent triangles have equal corresponding angles. By equating the measure of angle $M$ to its corresponding angle $S$, we form a linear equation in one variable and solve it using basic algebra.
ANSWER: $x = 20$.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two triangular fields in a village in Punjab are congruent, represented by $\triangle DEF \cong \triangle UVW$. The farmer measures side $EF = 15\text{ m}$ and perimeter of $\triangle UVW = 45\text{ m}$. If side $UV = 12\text{ m}$ and side $VW = 15\text{ m}$, calculate the length of side $DE$ and determine if $\triangle DEF$ is an equilateral, isosceles, or scalene triangle. Show all steps.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world measurements and multi-step geometric reasoning
STEPS:
- Step 1: Use the given congruence $\triangle DEF \cong \triangle UVW$ to match side $EF$ with its corresponding side in $\triangle UVW$, which is side $VW$. Thus, $EF = VW = 15\text{ m}$, and $DE$ corresponds to $UV = 12\text{ m}$.
- Step 2: Calculate the third side of $\triangle UVW$ using its perimeter: $\text{Perimeter} = UV + VW + WU \implies 45 = 12 + 15 + WU \implies 45 = 27 + WU \implies WU = 45 - 27 = 18\text{ m}$.
- Step 3: Find the corresponding side $DF$ in $\triangle DEF$: since $WU$ corresponds to $DF$, length $DF = 18\text{ m}$.
- Step 4: List all three side lengths of $\triangle DEF$: $DE = 12\text{ m}$, $EF = 15\text{ m}$, and $DF = 18\text{ m}$.
- Step 5: Classify the triangle based on its side lengths: since all three sides ($12\text{ m}$, $15\text{ m}$, $18\text{ m}$) are of different lengths, $\triangle DEF$ is a scalene triangle.
ANSWER: The length of side $DE$ is $12\text{ m}$, and $\triangle DEF$ is a scalene triangle because all three of its sides have different lengths ($12\text{ m}$, $15\text{ m}$, and $18\text{ m}$).
DIFFICULTY: hard
