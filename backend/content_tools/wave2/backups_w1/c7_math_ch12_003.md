## ELI5
Two and a half kilograms of handmade paper at fifteen and a half rupees a kilo: multiply the digits as if no points existed, then count the decimal places in BOTH factors and give the product that many. The point placement is bookkeeping - tenths times tenths land in hundredths, and the more decimal places on the inputs, the deeper the product digs. Ignore the points while multiplying, honour them while placing, and the shop's bill always balances.

## DEFINITION
Multiplying two decimal numbers involves multiplying their whole number values as if no decimal points exist, and then placing the decimal point in the product so that the total number of decimal places equals the sum of the decimal places in both multipliers.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can accurately multiply any two terminating decimal numbers by counting total decimal places and placing the decimal point correctly in the product, and can apply this skill to calculate areas, costs, and measurements in real-world contexts.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the multiplication of finite terminating decimal numbers up to three decimal places. Does not cover non-terminating repeating decimals, infinite decimals, or algebraic multiplication of variables with decimal coefficients.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you go to a local stationery shop in India and buy $2.5\text{ kg}$ of handmade chart paper, and the cost of the paper is ₹15.50 per kilogram. To find out the total amount you need to pay, you must multiply $15.50 \text{ by } 2.5$. Just like you learned how to multiply fractions and whole numbers, you can start by ignoring the decimal points temporarily and treating the numbers as whole integers ($1550 \text{ and } 25$). After finding their whole number product, you count how many total digits are to the right of the decimal points in both original numbers (two digits in $15.50$ and one digit in $2.5$, making a total of three decimal places). The key insight is that the product's decimal point must be moved three places from right to left, turning the whole number product into the correct decimal currency value.

**Takeaway:** decimal times decimal: whole-style product, then total decimal places from both factors. **In the exam:** count places before multiplying, place the point after, and simplify trailing zeros at the end. **Check yourself:** multiply the paper bill both ways - points ignored then placed - and verify by estimating. **Why students miss it:** they miscount one factor's places and slide the entire product by a power of ten.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the rule for determining the position of the decimal point when multiplying two decimal numbers like $0.4 \times 0.05$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct rule is to count the total number of decimal places in both multipliers and place the decimal point in the product so that it has the same total number of decimal places ($1 + 2 = 3$ places, giving $0.020$). A wrong answer such as aligning the decimal points vertically (like in addition) reveals a confusion between addition/subtraction rules and multiplication rules of decimals.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why multiplying $1.2 \times 3.4$ yields a product that is smaller than either $1.2$ or $3.4$ if we multiply numbers less than 1, but larger when multiplying numbers greater than 1. For $1.2 \times 3.4$, why is the product $4.08$ larger than both?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The product $1.2 \times 3.4$ is larger than both numbers because both multipliers are greater than $1$, meaning we are scaling $3.4$ up by more than one whole group ($1$ group of $3.4$ plus another $0.2$ groups). A wrong answer stating that decimal multiplication always makes numbers smaller shows a misconception carried over from multiplying proper fractions less than $1$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate the exact product of $3.25 \text{ and } 4.1$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $13.325$. Working steps: 
1. Multiply $325$ by $41$ as whole numbers: $325 \times 1 = 325$, and $325 \times 40 = 13000$. 
2. Add the partial products: $13000 + 325 = 13325$. 
3. Count decimal places: $3.25$ has two decimal places, and $4.1$ has one decimal place, totaling $3$ decimal places. 
4. Place the decimal point three places from the right: $13.325$. 
A wrong answer like $1.3325$ or $133.25$ indicates an error in counting or placing the total decimal places.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the results of $0.07 \times 0.08$ and $0.7 \times 0.8$. Without fully calculating both standard algorithms, explain how the position of the decimal point changes based on the number of decimal digits.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
$0.07 \times 0.08 = 0.0056$ (4 decimal places) while $0.7 \times 0.8 = 0.56$ (2 decimal places). The product with fewer decimal places has digits shifted two places to the right because the total number of decimal places in the factors decreases from $4$ to $2$. A wrong answer shows a failure to recognize that each decimal place in the factors shifts the product's decimal point by one power of $10$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student calculates $2.4 \times 1.5$ and writes the answer as $3.60$. Another student writes $3.6$. Are both answers mathematically correct, and which one is standard in decimal notation?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Both $3.60$ and $3.6$ represent the same numerical value, but $3.6$ is the simplified standard form because trailing zeros at the end of the decimal part can be omitted unless measuring precision (like money or significant figures) requires keeping them. A wrong answer claiming one is mathematically wrong shows a misunderstanding of equivalent decimals.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a multiplication problem involving two decimal numbers, each having two decimal places, such that their product is a whole number. Provide the problem and its solution.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An example is $2.50 \times 0.40$. Working: $250 \times 40 = 10000$. There are $2 + 2 = 4$ total decimal places, so placing the decimal point four places from the right gives $1.0000$, which simplifies to the whole number $1$. A poor answer provides numbers that do not result in a terminating whole number or fails to count decimal places correctly.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often line up the decimal points vertically as they do in addition and subtraction ($1.5 \times 0.23$ aligned at the dot), and then bring the decimal point straight down without counting the total decimal places of both factors. For example, they might compute $1.5 \times 0.2$ as $0.30$ correctly by coincidence, but for $2.5 \times 1.25$, they write the decimal point directly underneath, failing to account for the combined number of decimal digits ($1 + 2 = 3$ places needed).
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that decimal multiplication is like multiplying fractions where denominators ($10, 100, 1000$) multiply together to create a larger denominator. Say: "Stop! We never line up decimal points for multiplication. First, multiply the numbers ignoring the dots completely. Second, count every single digit that sits behind the decimal point in both numbers combined. Third, start at the right end of your answer and jump left that exact total number of steps to drop your anchor point."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Riya buys $3.5 \text{ metres}$ of ribbon for a craft project. If each metre of ribbon costs ₹12.50, how much total money does Riya pay to the shopkeeper?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given values: length = $3.5 \text{ m}$ ($1$ decimal place), cost per metre = ₹$12.50$ ($2$ decimal places).
- Step 2: Set up the multiplication of the numbers without decimal points: $35 \times 125$.
- Step 3: Perform whole number multiplication: $125 \times 5 = 625$, and $125 \times 30 = 3750$. Add them: $3750 + 625 = 4375$.
- Step 4: Count the total number of decimal places in the multipliers: $3.5$ has $1$ and $12.50$ has $2$, giving a total of $1 + 2 = 3$ decimal places.
- Step 5: Place the decimal point $3$ places from the right in $4375$, yielding $4.375$, which represents ₹$43.75$ when rounded to standard currency format.
REASONING: Multiplying decimals is equivalent to multiplying fractions with denominators of $10$ and $100$. The product of denominators ($10 \times 100 = 1000$) dictates that we divide the whole number product by $1000$, shifting the decimal point three places to the left.
ANSWER: ₹43.75
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A rectangular vegetable garden has a length of $4.2 \text{ metres}$ and a breadth of $2.15 \text{ metres}$. What is the total area of the garden?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the formula for the area of a rectangle: $\text{Area} = \text{length} \times \text{breadth}$. Substitute the given values: $4.2 \times 2.15$.
- Step 2: Treat the numbers as whole integers by ignoring decimal points: calculate $42 \times 215$.
- Step 3: Compute the product: $215 \times 2 = 430$, and $215 \times 40 = 8600$. Add them: $8600 + 430 = 9030$.
- Step 4: Count total decimal places: $4.2$ has $1$ decimal place and $2.15$ has $2$ decimal places, making $1 + 2 = 3$ decimal places in total.
- Step 5: Insert the decimal point $3$ places from the right in $9030$, resulting in $9.030 \text{ m}^2$, or simply $9.03 \text{ m}^2$.
REASONING: The area of a rectangle is found by multiplying its dimensions. When dimensions are decimals, the resulting area unit ($\text{metres} \times \text{metres} = \text{square metres}$) retains the combined decimal place count of the factors.
ANSWER: 9.03 square metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A petrol tank in a scooter leaks $0.015 \text{ litres}$ of fuel every minute. How many litres of fuel are leaked in $2.4 \text{ hours}$? (Hint: First convert hours into minutes).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Convert hours into minutes because the leak rate is given per minute. Multiply $2.4 \text{ hours}$ by $60 \text{ minutes/hour}$.
- Step 2: Compute time in minutes: $2.4 \times 60 = 24 \times 6 = 144 \text{ minutes}$.
- Step 3: Set up the total leakage calculation: multiply the rate ($0.015 \text{ litres/min}$, $3$ decimal places) by total time ($144 \text{ minutes}$, whole number).
- Step 4: Perform whole number multiplication: $15 \times 144$. Calculate: $15 \times 100 = 1500$, $15 \times 40 = 600$, $15 \times 4 = 60$. Sum them: $1500 + 600 + 60 = 2160$.
- Step 5: Count decimal places from the rate ($3$ places) and apply to $2160$: moving $3$ places left gives $2.160 \text{ litres}$.
REASONING: Multi-step problems require unit consistency first (converting hours to minutes). Once time is in minutes, multiplying rate by time yields total quantity, and tracking decimal places ensures correct scaling of the final volume.
ANSWER: 2.16 litres
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Amit says that $0.3 \times 0.3$ is equal to $0.9$ because $3 \times 3 = 9$ and there is one decimal. Priya says the answer is $0.09$. Who is correct, and what specific error did the other person make in counting decimal places?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and justification
STEPS:
- Step 1: Evaluate Amit's reasoning: he multiplied the whole numbers ($3 \times 3 = 9$) but placed only one decimal place.
- Step 2: Evaluate Priya's reasoning: she multiplied $3 \times 3 = 9$ and counted two total decimal places ($0.3$ has one, $0.3$ has another, totaling $1 + 1 = 2$ places).
- Step 3: Conclude that Priya is correct because $0.3 \times 0.3 = \frac{3}{10} \times \frac{3}{10} = \frac{9}{100} = 0.09$.
- Step 4: Identify Amit's error: he treated decimal multiplication like addition or forgot to sum the decimal places of both factors.
ANSWER: Priya is correct. Amit made the error of counting only a single decimal place instead of adding the decimal places of both factors ($1 + 1 = 2$).
DIFFICULTY: medium

QUESTION: A farmer has a rectangular plot of land measuring $12.5 \text{ metres}$ by $8.2 \text{ metres}$. He wishes to fence the plot and also pave it. If paving costs ₹$150.50$ per square metre, calculate the total cost of paving the entire plot.
COMPETENCY: Multi-step real-world application and financial calculation
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate the area of the rectangular plot by multiplying length ($12.5 \text{ m}$) by breadth ($8.2 \text{ m}$).
- Step 2: Perform multiplication: $125 \times 82 = (125 \times 80) + (125 \times 2) = 10000 + 250 = 10250$. Adjust for $1 + 1 = 2$ decimal places to get $102.50 \text{ m}^2$.
- Step 3: Calculate the total paving cost by multiplying the area ($102.5 \text{ m}^2$) by the cost per square metre (₹$150.50$).
- Step 4: Multiply whole numbers $10250 \times 15050$ or use decimal multiplication: $102.5 \times 150.5$. Compute $1025 \times 1505 = 1542625$.
