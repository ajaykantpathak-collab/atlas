## ELI5
Buy 3 notebooks and 2 pens, then 2 more notebooks and 4 more pens: 5 notebooks, 6 pens - you would never merge notebooks with pens. Combining like terms is that checkout habit in algebra: terms with identical variable parts add or subtract their coefficients, and unlike parts stay apart. Watch the signs ride along with their terms - a minus travels with its term into the family group. Do it right and any sprawling expression lands fully simplified, one term per family.

## DEFINITION
Combining like terms is the algebraic process of simplifying expressions by adding or subtracting terms that contain the exact same variables raised to the exact same powers, using their numerical coefficients.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can identify, group, and combine like terms in algebraic expressions containing multiple variables and integer coefficients, resulting in a fully simplified expression.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers addition and subtraction of like algebraic terms with integer coefficients and exponents up to 2 (e.g., $x, x^2, xy$). It does NOT cover multiplication or division of algebraic terms (which are separate operations) or polynomial factoring.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at an stationery shop buying notebooks and pens. If you buy 3 notebooks and 2 pens, then later buy 2 more notebooks and 4 more pens, how do you keep track of your items? You group your notebooks together ($3 + 2 = 5$ notebooks) and your pens together ($2 + 4 = 6$ pens) because you cannot add notebooks directly to pens! In algebra, terms are like these items—we can only combine "like terms" that share the exact same variable parts. We begin by scanning the expression, identifying terms with identical variable factors, and rearranging them using the commutative property. Then, we add or subtract their numerical coefficients while keeping the variable part completely unchanged. This builds directly on our previous understanding of variables and coefficients, allowing us to shrink long, messy expressions into short, clean ones.

**Takeaway:** combining like terms is checkout arithmetic: same-variable terms pool their coefficients, unlike terms stay separate. **In the exam:** group before combining, keep each sign with its term, and stop only when one term per family remains. **Check yourself:** merge the two shopping trips into one simplified order of notebooks and pens. **Why students miss it:** they subtract the smaller coefficient from the larger and keep whichever sign looks friendlier.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which of the following pairs of terms are considered "like terms"?
A) $5x$ and $5y$
B) $3x^2$ and $4x$
C) $-7xy$ and $2yx$
D) $8a$ and $8ab$
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is C) $-7xy$ and $2yx$. Like terms must have the exact same variables with the exact same exponents; because multiplication is commutative, $xy$ and $yx$ represent the exact same variable combination. A student who chooses A believes any terms with the same coefficient are alike, while a student who chooses B or D fails to notice that different exponents or missing variables prevent terms from being combined.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
When simplifying the expression $4x + 3 - 2x$, a student writes the answer as $5x$. Explain why this answer is incorrect and what error was made in combining the terms.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The answer $5x$ is incorrect because the student incorrectly added the constant term $3$ to the variable term $2x$ instead of only combining the like terms $4x$ and $-2x$. The correct simplification is $(4x - 2x) + 3$, which equals $2x + 3$. A student who makes this error is treating all parts of an algebraic expression as combinable, ignoring the rule that constants and variable terms cannot be merged.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Simplify the following algebraic expression completely: $6a - 3b + 2a + 5b - 4$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct simplified expression is $8a + 2b - 4$. 
Steps:
1. Identify like terms: The $a$ terms are $6a$ and $2a$; the $b$ terms are $-3b$ and $5b$; the constant is $-4$.
2. Group like terms together: $(6a + 2a) + (-3b + 5b) - 4$.
3. Combine the coefficients for $a$: $6 + 2 = 8$, giving $8a$.
4. Combine the coefficients for $b$: $-3 + 5 = 2$, giving $2b$.
5. Write the final combined expression: $8a + 2b - 4$.
A student who gets $10ab - 4$ has incorrectly multiplied or combined unlike terms ($a$ and $b$) into a single product.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the expression $5x^2 + 3x - 2x^2 + 7 - x + 4$. Why can $5x^2$ be combined with $-2x^2$, but not with $3x$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
$5x^2$ and $-2x^2$ can be combined because they share the exact same variable raised to the exact same power ($x^2$), meaning they represent quantities of the same "type." However, $3x$ has a variable raised to the power of 1 ($x$), making it a different algebraic entity, just as square metres cannot be directly added to linear metres. A student who thinks all terms containing $x$ are alike misses the crucial role of the exponent in defining like terms.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently add or multiply the variables themselves along with the coefficients, or they combine terms with different exponents (e.g., treating $x$ and $x^2$ as like terms, writing $3x + 2x^2 = 5x^3$). Another frequent error is ignoring the operational sign directly in front of a term, such as treating $-3x$ as a positive $3x$ when rearranging expressions.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student struggles, guide them to circle or underline like terms using different shapes or colors (e.g., box all $x$ terms in squares, circle all constants). Remind them that variables represent labels or units that never change during addition or subtraction—only the number in front (the coefficient) changes. Have them practice integer operations separately before applying them to coefficients.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Riya went to the market and bought $5x$ notebooks and $3y$ pens. Later, she bought another $2x$ notebooks and lost $y$ pens. Write an algebraic expression for her total items and simplify it.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Translate the word problem into an initial algebraic expression: $5x + 3y + 2x - y$.
- Step 2: Identify and group the like terms together: $(5x + 2x) + (3y - y)$.
- Step 3: Combine the coefficients of the $x$ terms: $5 + 2 = 7$, giving $7x$.
- Step 4: Combine the coefficients of the $y$ terms (remembering that $-y$ has an invisible coefficient of $-1$): $3 - 1 = 2$, giving $2y$.
- Step 5: Write the final combined expression with all simplified parts: $7x + 2y$.
REASONING: Grouping terms by their identical variable parts ensures we only add quantities that represent the same item. Using integer addition rules on the coefficients gives the correct net quantity for each variable.
ANSWER: $7x + 2y$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Simplify the following algebraic expression completely: $12m^2 - 5mn + 3m^2 + 8mn - 4m$.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Scan the expression to identify all distinct types of terms ($m^2$ terms, $mn$ terms, and $m$ terms).
- Step 2: Group the like terms together using the commutative property: $(12m^2 + 3m^2) + (-5mn + 8mn) - 4m$.
- Step 3: Combine the $m^2$ terms by adding their coefficients: $12 + 3 = 15$, resulting in $15m^2$.
- Step 4: Combine the $mn$ terms by adding their coefficients: $-5 + 8 = 3$, resulting in $3mn$.
- Step 5: Bring down the remaining unlike term ($-4m$) and write the final expression: $15m^2 + 3mn - 4m$.
REASONING: Terms with different variable combinations (such as $m^2$, $mn$, and $m$) are completely separate algebraic quantities and cannot be merged, so they remain as distinct parts of the final expression.
ANSWER: $15m^2 + 3mn - 4m$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Find the perimeter of a triangle whose three sides are given by the expressions $3x + 2$, $5x - 1$, and $2x + 4$. Simplify the resulting expression.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall that the perimeter of a polygon is the sum of all its side lengths, so write out the full addition expression: $(3x + 2) + (5x - 1) + (2x + 4)$.
- Step 2: Remove the parentheses and list all terms together: $3x + 2 + 5x - 1 + 2x + 4$.
- Step 3: Group all the variable terms together and all the constant terms together: $(3x + 5x + 2x) + (2 - 1 + 4)$.
- Step 4: Combine the coefficients of the $x$ terms: $3 + 5 + 2 = 10$, giving $10x$.
- Step 5: Combine the constant numbers: $2 - 1 + 4 = 5$, giving $+5$.
- Step 6: Write the final simplified perimeter expression with units: $10x + 5$ units.
REASONING: Finding perimeter requires adding all side lengths into one large expression. Combining like variable terms and constant numbers separately simplifies the geometric measurement into a clean algebraic formula.
ANSWER: $10x + 5$ units
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A school garden has a rectangular flower bed. The length is represented by $4x + 3$ metres and the breadth is represented by $2x - 1$ metres. 
Part A: Write an expression for the total perimeter of the flower bed.
Part B: Simplify the expression completely. Explain why the term containing $x$ and the constant number cannot be combined in your final answer.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to algebraic expressions and justifying simplification rules
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall the perimeter formula for a rectangle: $\text{Perimeter} = 2 \times (\text{Length} + \text{Breadth})$.
- Step 2: Substitute the given expressions into the formula: $2 \times ((4x + 3) + (2x - 1))$.
- Step 3: Simplify inside the inner parentheses first by combining like terms: $(4x + 2x) + (3 - 1) = 6x + 2$.
- Step 4: Multiply by 2: $2(6x + 2) = 12x + 4$.
- Step 5: Explain why $12x$ and $4$ cannot be added: $12x$ represents a variable quantity dependent on $x$, while $4$ is a fixed constant, so they represent different types of values.
ANSWER: Part A: $2(4x + 3 + 2x - 1)$. Part B: $12x + 4$ metres. They cannot be combined because one is a variable term and the other is a constant.
