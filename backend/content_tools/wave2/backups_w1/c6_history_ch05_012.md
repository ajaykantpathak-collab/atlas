## ELI5
When the Mauryan clock ran down around 185 BCE, the map did not go blank - it shattered into pieces: the Shunga dynasty under Pushyamitra Shunga held the Ganga plain while northern gates opened to newcomers, and every region began writing its own chapter. The empire shrank; history thickened.

## DEFINITION
The Post-Mauryan period refers to the era following the decline of the Mauryan Empire (around 185 BCE), characterized by the rise of smaller, regional kingdoms like the Shungas and the influence of Indo-Greek rulers in the northwest.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the transition from the Mauryan Empire to regional powers and the emergence of Indo-Greek influence in the northwest. It excludes detailed dynastic lists, Roman trade patterns, and specific south Indian state histories.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine your school team, which was once the largest and strongest in the city, suddenly loses its head coach and begins to break into smaller, independent clubs. How do you explain the change? Start by looking at the timeline: the Mauryan Empire didn't just disappear; it shrank because leaders after Ashoka couldn't manage such a vast area. Then, think about the new neighbors, like the Indo-Greeks, who moved into areas like the northwest because the central power was no longer there to guard the borders. The key insight is that the history of India is not just one long line of one big empire, but a puzzle of shifting, smaller pieces.

**Takeaway:** after 185 BCE the empire broke into regional kingdoms - the Shungas at home, new powers at the gates - and the map diversified. **In the exam:** in post-Mauryan questions, date the break first, then read each successor kingdom as one shard of the map. **Check yourself:** watch the walkthrough's team split into clubs - each new crest is a shard of the old single jersey. **Why students miss it:** students mourn the empire as erased - the shards are a no to that; the pieces carried on, smaller and many. Rehearse the exam move once more - date, break, shards - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.
