## ELI5
When two fractions claim equality, there is a swift test — and a solver. Cross-multiply: each numerator times the opposite denominator. Equal results, equal fractions. Better still, when one fraction hides an unknown, cross-multiplication turns the claim into an ordinary linear equation, ready for normal solving. It is legal because both sides of the equation were quietly multiplied by the same product of denominators — the same thing done to both sides. Fast, clean, and it turns 'is this proportional?' into school arithmetic.

## DEFINITION
Cross-multiplication is an algebraic method for solving a proportion of the form \( \frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \) (where \( b \neq 0, d \neq 0 \)) by multiplying the numerator of each fraction by the denominator of the other, yielding the equation \( a \times d = b \times c \). This transforms the proportion into a linear equation that can be solved for an unknown variable.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can correctly set up a proportion from a word problem, apply cross-multiplication to form a linear equation, solve for the unknown variable using inverse operations, and interpret the solution in the original context with appropriate units.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers solving proportions with one unknown using cross-multiplication, including problems where the unknown is in the numerator or denominator, and where terms are integers, fractions, or decimals. It does NOT cover inverse proportion, compound proportion, or proportions with more than one unknown variable.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are helping your mother adjust a recipe for 6 people that originally serves 4 people. The recipe needs 2 cups of flour for 4 servings. How much flour for 6 servings? You know the ratio of flour to servings stays constant, so you write \( \frac{2}{4} = \frac{x}{6} \). Instead of finding the unit rate (flour per serving) as in the unitary method, we can directly cross-multiply: \( 2 \times 6 = 4 \times x \). This gives \( 12 = 4x \), so \( x = 3 \) cups. Cross-multiplication works because if two fractions are equal, their cross-products are equal — a property derived from multiplying both sides of the equation by the common denominator \( bd \). This method is faster than the unitary method when the numbers are not easily divisible.

Takeaway: a/b = c/d becomes a × d = b × c — cross-multiplication converts a proportion into a linear equation; solve with inverse operations and translate the answer back into context. Exam format: expect word problems demanding the setup proportion first; the proportion line, the crossed equation, and the contextual answer each carry marks. Verification beat: substitute the solution back into the original proportion and confirm equality. Why students miss it: they cross-multiply a proportion they never set up correctly from the words — the method is only as good as the proportion it starts from, so translation is the first marked step.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
State the cross-multiplication rule for the proportion \( \frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \) and write the resulting equation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The cross-multiplication rule states that if \( \frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \), then \( a \times d = b \times c \). A wrong answer (e.g., writing \( a \times b = c \times d \) or \( a \times c = b \times d \)) reveals the student does not understand which terms to multiply diagonally and is likely memorising a pattern without grasping the "cross" structure.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why cross-multiplication is a valid step when solving \( \frac{3}{5} = \frac{x}{15} \). Use the concept of multiplying both sides by the same quantity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Cross-multiplication is valid because multiplying both sides of \( \frac{3}{5} = \frac{x}{15} \) by the common denominator \( 5 \times 15 \) cancels the denominators: \( \frac{3}{5} \times (5 \times 15) = \frac{x}{15} \times (5 \times 15) \) simplifies to \( 3 \times 15 = x \times 5 \). A wrong answer (e.g., "it's just a rule" or "we multiply top-left by bottom-right") reveals the student sees it as a trick rather than an application of the balance principle of equations.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Solve for \( x \) using cross-multiplication: \( \frac{7}{x} = \frac{21}{36} \). Show all steps.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Cross-multiply: \( 7 \times 36 = x \times 21 \).
Step 2: Compute left side: \( 252 = 21x \).
Step 3: Divide both sides by 21: \( x = \frac{252}{21} = 12 \).
Step 4: Check: \( \frac{7}{12} = \frac{21}{36} \) (both simplify to \( \frac{7}{12} \)).
A wrong answer like \( x = 4 \) (from \( 7 \times 21 = 36 \times x \)) reveals the student multiplied the wrong pairs (horizontal instead of diagonal).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the unitary method and cross-multiplication for solving \( \frac{5}{8} = \frac{x}{24} \). Which method is more efficient here and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Unitary method: Find value for 1 unit (denominator 1): \( 5 \div 8 = 0.625 \), then multiply by 24: \( 0.625 \times 24 = 15 \). Cross-multiplication: \( 5 \times 24 = 8x \rightarrow 120 = 8x \rightarrow x = 15 \). Cross-multiplication is more efficient here because 24 is a multiple of 8, making the division \( 120 \div 8 \) clean, while the unitary method requires decimal division. A student who says unitary is always better or cannot articulate the efficiency trade-off shows they lack strategic flexibility in choosing methods.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student solves \( \frac{x}{9} = \frac{12}{18} \) as follows:
Step 1: \( x \times 9 = 12 \times 18 \)
Step 2: \( 9x = 216 \)
Step 3: \( x = 24 \)
Identify the error and give the correct solution.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The error is in Step 1: the student multiplied the numerator by the denominator of the *same* fraction (horizontal multiplication) instead of cross-multiplying. Correct cross-multiplication: \( x \times 18 = 9 \times 12 \rightarrow 18x = 108 \rightarrow x = 6 \). A student who misses this error confuses the diagonal pattern with horizontal multiplication, indicating they do not visualise the "cross" in cross-multiplication.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-life word problem (Indian context) that requires setting up a proportion with the unknown in the denominator and solving it using cross-multiplication. Provide the solution.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example problem: "A car travels 180 km on 12 litres of petrol. How many litres are needed to travel 300 km at the same mileage?"
Proportion: \( \frac{180}{12} = \frac{300}{x} \) (unknown in denominator).
Cross-multiply: \( 180x = 12 \times 300 \rightarrow 180x = 3600 \rightarrow x = 20 \) litres.
A poor problem would have the unknown in the numerator, use trivial numbers (like doubling), or not require cross-multiplication (e.g., unitary method is obvious). This reveals if the student understands the structure of proportion problems and can design one that specifically exercises the target skill.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often cross-multiply horizontally instead of diagonally, writing \( a \times b = c \times d \) for \( \frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \). For example, in \( \frac{3}{x} = \frac{9}{12} \), they write \( 3 \times x = 9 \times 12 \) (multiplying the two numerators and the two denominators separately) instead of \( 3 \times 12 = x \times 9 \). This happens because they memorise "multiply across" but confuse "across" with "along the fraction bar" rather than "across the equal sign diagonally".
## ON_FAILURE
Draw two fractions side by side with an equal sign. Use a coloured pen to draw an X connecting the numerator of the first to the denominator of the second, and the denominator of the first to the numerator of the second. Say: "Cross-multiplication means multiply along the arms of this X. The equal sign is the centre of the X. Never multiply along the horizontal fraction bars." Practice 5 problems where the student must first draw the X before writing the equation. Then have them verify by checking if the original fractions are equivalent after solving.

## THIS_FORMULA
If \( \frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \) with \( b \neq 0, d \neq 0 \), then \( a \times d = b \times c \).
## FORMULA_VARIABLES
a = numerator of first ratio, b = denominator of first ratio, c = numerator of second ratio, d = denominator of second ratio; one of these is typically the unknown variable.
## FORMULA_UNITS
dimensionless (ratios are unitless, but the unknown variable carries the unit of its corresponding quantity, e.g., rupees, metres, litres)
## COMPUTATION_TYPE
algebraic

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A 2 kg bag of basmati rice costs ₹180. How much will 5 kg cost at the same rate?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify known and unknown. Cost for 2 kg = ₹180. Cost for 5 kg = ₹x. The rate (cost per kg) is constant, so ratios are proportional.
- Step 2: Set up the proportion with consistent order (cost/kg): \( \frac{180}{2} = \frac{x}{5} \).
- Step 3: Apply cross-multiplication: \( 180 \times 5 = 2 \times x \).
- Step 4: Compute left side: \( 900 = 2x \). Divide both sides by 2: \( x = \frac{900}{2} = 450 \).
- Step 5: Verify: Unit cost = ₹90/kg. 5 kg × ₹90 = ₹450. Answer makes sense.
REASONING: Cross-multiplication directly uses the equality of ratios without explicitly finding the unit rate. The proportion \( \frac{180}{2} = \frac{x}{5} \) states that the cost-to-weight ratio is the same for both quantities. Multiplying both sides by \( 2 \times 5 \) eliminates denominators and gives a simple linear equation.
ANSWER: ₹450
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A map scale shows 1 cm represents 25 km. If two cities are 7.5 cm apart on the map, what is the actual distance between them?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Known: map distance 1 cm → real distance 25 km. Unknown: map distance 7.5 cm → real distance x km.
- Step 2: Set up proportion (map/real): \( \frac{1}{25} = \frac{7.5}{x} \). Note unknown is in denominator.
- Step 3: Cross-multiply: \( 1 \times x = 25 \times 7.5 \).
- Step 4: Compute right side: \( 25 \times 7.5 = 25 \times \frac{75}{10} = \frac{1875}{10} = 187.5 \). So \( x = 187.5 \).
- Step 5: Interpret: Actual distance is 187.5 km. Check: 7.5 cm × 25 km/cm = 187.5 km.
REASONING: When the unknown is in the denominator, cross-multiplication still works identically: the product of the means equals the product of the extremes. Here \( 1 \times x = 25 \times 7.5 \) isolates x immediately without needing to take reciprocals.
ANSWER: 187.5 km
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: In a school, the ratio of girls to boys is 3:5. If 12 new girls join and the ratio becomes 1:1, how many boys are in the school? (Hint: The number of boys does not change.)
STEPS:
- Step 1: Let initial girls = 3x, initial boys = 5x (using ratio variable x). Boys remain constant at 5x.
- Step 2: After 12 girls join, new girls = 3x + 12. New ratio girls:boys = 1:1, so \( \frac{3x+12}{5x} = \frac{1}{1} \).
- Step 3: Cross-multiply: \( (3x + 12) \times 1 = 5x \times 1 \) → \( 3x + 12 = 5x \).
- Step 4: Solve linear equation: Subtract 3x from both sides → \( 12 = 2x \) → \( x = 6 \).
- Step 5: Find number of boys: \( 5x = 5 \times 6 = 30 \).
- Step 6: Verify: Initial girls = 18, boys = 30. After 12 girls join: girls = 30, boys = 30. Ratio 1:1 ✓.
REASONING: This combines ratio representation (3x:5x) with proportion solving. The invariant quantity (boys) anchors the problem. Cross-multiplication simplifies the proportion \( \frac{3x+12}{5x} = 1 \) into a linear equation in x, which is then solved using Class 7 algebra skills.
ANSWER: 30 boys

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A car travels 180 km in 3 hours at a constant speed. Using cross-multiplication, find how long it will take to travel 450 km at the same speed. Then, explain why the unitary method (finding km per hour) would also work but might be less efficient if the numbers were 179 km in 3 hours and 451 km target.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying methods
STEPS:
- Step 1: Set up proportion for constant speed: distance/time = constant. \( \frac{180}{3} = \frac{450}{x} \) where x is time in hours.
- Step 2: Cross-multiply: \( 180x = 3 \times 450 \).
- Step 3: Compute: \( 180x = 1350 \) → \( x = \frac{1350}{180} = 7.5 \) hours.
- Step 4: Unitary method: Speed = 180/3 = 60 km/h. Time = 450/60 = 7.5 hours. Both work.
- Step 5: For 179 km in 3 hours, speed = 179/3 ≈ 59.666... km/h. Time for 451 km = 451 / (179/3) = 451 × 3 / 179 = 1353/179 ≈ 7.558... hours. Cross-multiplication directly: \( \frac{179}{3} = \frac{451}{x} \) → \( 179x = 1353 \) → \( x = 1353/179 \). This avoids the repeating decimal of the unit rate and keeps the calculation in exact fractions until the end.
ANSWER: Time = 7.5 hours. Cross-multiplication is more efficient for non-integer unit rates because it avoids intermediate rounding or repeating decimals by keeping the computation as a single fraction.
DIFFICULTY: hard
