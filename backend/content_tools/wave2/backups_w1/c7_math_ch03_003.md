## ELI5
One paisa is the hundredth of a rupee your pocket never notices - and it is the perfect picture of the second decimal place. Slice a whole into ten equal parts and each is a tenth; slice each tenth again into ten and you hold a hundredth, written 0.01. Two seats right of the decimal point, each seat ten times poorer than the last. Shade one square of a 100-square grid and you have the whole story: small, exact, and the reason prices carry two digits after the point.

## DEFINITION
A hundredth part is one of one hundred equal parts of a whole, represented as the fraction $\frac{1}{100}$ or as the decimal $0.01$. It is the second place to the right of the decimal point, representing values ten times smaller than a tenth part ($\frac{1}{10}$).

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at a shop buying a notebook, and the shopkeeper says an eraser costs 1 paisa. Since 1 rupee is equal to 100 paise, how do we write 1 paisa as a fraction of a rupee? Just like we divided a whole into 10 equal strips for tenths, we now divide a whole square grid into 100 equal tiny squares to understand hundredths. Each tiny square represents $\frac{1}{100}$ of the whole, which we write as the decimal $0.01$. Building on our knowledge of tenths ($\frac{1}{10} = 0.1$), the second position to the right of the decimal point is reserved for the hundredths place. By counting these tiny squares, we can express any number of hundredths as a decimal.

**Takeaway:** a hundredth is one of a hundred equal parts: 0.01, the second seat right of the point, ten times smaller than a tenth. **In the exam:** convert fractions out of 100 by placing the numerator in the hundredths seat; in money, paise are the hundredths of a rupee. **Check yourself:** shade one square of a hundred-grid and name the fraction, the decimal and the rupee value it shows. **Why students miss it:** they write five-hundredths as 0.5, promoting a hundredth to a tenth with one misplaced digit.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The fraction is $\frac{7}{100}$ and the decimal is $0.07$. A student who answers $0.7$ confuses tenths with hundredths, failing to realize that $0.7$ represents 70 hundredths ($\frac{70}{100}$), whereas 7 hundredths requires a zero in the tenths place.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is the number $0.35$ read as "thirty-five hundredths" rather than "three tenths and five"? Explain how its place value structure works.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Express the fraction $\frac{48}{100}$ as a decimal and calculate what value is added to it to make a whole ($1.0$).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Write the fraction $\frac{48}{100}$ as a decimal by placing the numerator in the hundredths place: $0.48$. Step 2: Set up the subtraction equation to find the missing part: $1.00 - 0.48$. Step 3: Perform subtraction with regrouping: $1.00 - 0.48 = 0.52$. Step 4: Verify by adding $0.48 + 0.52 = 1.00$. A student who answers $0.58$ likely made an error in subtraction by not borrowing correctly across the decimal places.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the decimals $0.4$ and $0.38$. Which one is larger, and why is a common student error to think $0.38$ is larger because 38 is greater than 4?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
$0.4$ is larger than $0.38$. To compare them properly, convert $0.4$ to an equivalent hundredths form by annexing a zero: $0.40$. Comparing $40$ hundredths to $38$ hundredths clearly shows $40 > 38$. A student who thinks $0.38$ is larger is wrongly comparing the digits after the decimal point as whole numbers (38 vs 4) without equalizing their place value lengths.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student writes the fraction $\frac{5}{100}$ as $0.5$. Identify the error in their reasoning and correct it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The student's error is writing $\frac{5}{100}$ as $0.5$, which actually represents $\frac{5}{10}$ or 5 tenths. To correct this, remember that hundredths must occupy the second decimal place, so $\frac{5}{100}$ must be written with a zero holder in the tenths place as $0.05$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a decimal number that has a 2 in the hundredths place, a 7 in the tenths place, and a 4 in the ones place. Then, write this number as a sum of its place values using fractions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The number is $4.72$. As a sum of place values, it is written as $4 + \frac{7}{10} + \frac{2}{100}$. A poor answer would mix up the place value positions (e.g., writing $4 + \frac{2}{10} + \frac{7}{100}$), showing a failure to map digit positions correctly to tenths and hundredths.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently misinterpret the place value of zeros in decimals, writing $\frac{6}{100}$ as $0.6$ instead of $0.06$. They treat the numerator 6 as if it belongs directly next to the decimal point, ignoring the empty tenths place that requires a zero placeholder. Another common error is assuming that any number with more digits is larger, leading them to believe $0.125$ is greater than $0.2$ because 125 is greater than 2.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student writes $\frac{6}{100}$ as $0.6$, guide them back to the fraction meaning: ask them "What is $\frac{6}{10}$?" ($0.6$). Then ask "Is $\frac{6}{100}$ ten times smaller than $\frac{6}{10}$?" Show them a 10x10 grid where 6 out of 100 squares are shaded. Emphasize that because there are no full columns (tenths) shaded, we must write a $0$ in the tenths place to hold the spot before writing the $6$ in the hundredths place, giving $0.06$.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Aarav has a ribbon of length 4 metres and 25 centimetres. Express the 25 centimetres part as a fraction of a metre, write it as a decimal, and state the total length of the ribbon in metres using decimal notation.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the relationship between centimetres and metres. We know that 100 centimetres = 1 metre, so 1 centimetre = $\frac{1}{100}$ metre.
- Step 2: Express 25 centimetres as a fraction of a metre: $\frac{25}{100}$ metre.
- Step 3: Convert the fraction $\frac{25}{100}$ into decimal notation by placing 25 in the hundredths position: $0.25$ metre.
- Step 4: Add the whole metres (4) to the decimal part ($0.25$): $4 + 0.25 = 4.25$ metres.
- Step 5: Verify the result by checking place values: 4 is in the ones place, 2 is in the tenths place ($\frac{2}{10}$ or 20 cm), and 5 is in the hundredths place ($\frac{5}{100}$ or 5 cm), total 4 m 25 cm.
REASONING: Since 1 metre is divided into 100 equal parts (centimetres), each centimetre is one hundredth ($\frac{1}{100}$) of a metre. Combining the whole metre units with the hundredth fraction yields a mixed decimal number.
ANSWER: $4.25$ metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Riya saved 75 paise in her piggy bank. Express 75 paise as a fraction of a rupee, convert it to a decimal, and calculate how much more money she needs to reach exactly 2 rupees.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall standard currency conversion where 100 paise = 1 rupee. Therefore, 1 paisa = $\frac{1}{100}$ of a rupee.
- Step 2: Write 75 paise as a fraction of a rupee: $\frac{75}{100}$ rupee.
- Step 3: Convert the fraction to decimal notation: $\frac{75}{100} = 0.75$ rupees.
- Step 4: Set up the subtraction to find the remaining amount needed to reach 2 rupees: $2.00 - 0.75$.
- Step 5: Perform the subtraction: borrow 1 from the ones place, making it $1.99 - 0.74$ or standard regrouping: $2.00 - 0.75 = 1.25$ rupees.
REASONING: Currency conversion relies on base-100 place value systems where every paisa represents one hundredth of the base unit (rupee). Subtracting from 2.00 determines the missing hundredth parts needed.
ANSWER: $1.25$ rupees (or 1 rupee 25 paise)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A rectangular garden plot has a breadth of $3$ metres and $6$ centimetres. A gardener wants to put a fence around it, but first needs to record the breadth purely in metres as a decimal number. Furthermore, if he extends the breadth by 45 centimetres, what will the new breadth be in metres?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Convert the initial breadth component of 6 centimetres into metres: $\frac{6}{100} = 0.06$ metres.
- Step 2: Combine the whole metres and the decimal part for initial breadth: $3 + 0.06 = 3.06$ metres.
- Step 3: Convert the extension of 45 centimetres into metres as a decimal: $\frac{45}{100} = 0.45$ metres.
- Step 4: Add the extension to the initial breadth: $3.06 + 0.45$. Align decimal points:
  $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l} 3.06 \\ + 0.45 \\ \hline 3.51 \end{array}$$
- Step 5: Verify each column addition: hundredths column ($6 + 5 = 11$, write 1, carry 1), tenths column ($0 + 4 + 1 = 5$), ones column ($3 + 0 = 3$), resulting in $3.51$ metres.
REASONING: Combining mixed units requires converting subordinate units (centimetres) into hundredths of the primary unit (metres) before performing standard decimal addition with proper column alignment.
ANSWER: $3.51$ metres
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: In a school sports event, Kabir ran a 100-metre race and finished in a time where his split times were recorded. His friend claimed that finishing with a time of 12.05 seconds is faster than finishing in 12.5 seconds because 5 is smaller than 50. Analyze this claim, identify the mathematical error in comparing the decimal places, and determine which time is actually faster.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying decimal place values in real-world contexts
STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand the context of race times (lower time means faster speed).
- Step 2: Analyze the two decimal numbers given: $12.05$ seconds and $12.5$ seconds.
- Step 3: Align the decimal places or annex zeros to compare equal decimal digits: compare $12.05$ and $12.50$.
- Step 4: Compare tenths place: $12.05$ has a $0$ in the tenths place, while $12.50$ has a $5$ in the tenths place. Since $0 < 5$, $12.05$ is less than $12.50$.
- Step 5: Conclude that a time of $12.05$ seconds is shorter, meaning Kabir finished faster. Explain why the friend's reasoning of comparing 5 to 50 fails due to place value rules.
ANSWER: Kabir's time of $12.05$ seconds is faster. The friend's claim is incorrect because $12.5$ is equivalent to $12.50$ seconds. Comparing tenths reveals that $12.05$ has 0 tenths while $12.50$ has 5 tenths, making $12.05$ smaller (shorter time, hence faster).
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A local artisan is designing decorative tiles. Each tile is a square grid divided into 100 equal small squares. She paints 18 squares red, 35 squares blue, and leaves the rest unpainted. Express the painted portion of the tile as a single decimal fraction. If she decides to paint half of the remaining unpainted squares green, what decimal part of the entire tile will be green?
COMPETENCY: Translating part-whole relationships into hundredth fractions and decimals
STEPS:
- Step 1: Determine total painted squares initially: $18 + 35 = 53$ squares out of 100, which is written as the fraction $\frac{53}{100}$ or decimal $0.53$.
- Step 2: Calculate the number of unpainted squares: $100 - 53 = 47$ unpainted squares.
- Step 3: Calculate half of the remaining unpainted squares: $47 \div 2 = 23.5$ squares.
- Step 4: Express this green-painted portion as a fraction of the total 100 squares: $\frac{23.5}{100}$.
- Step 5: Convert $\frac{23.5}{100}$ into a standard decimal by shifting the decimal point two places to the left: $0.235$ (or understand it as 23 hundredths and 5 thousandths).
ANSWER: The initially painted portion is $0.53$ of the tile. The portion painted green is represented as the decimal $0.235$, which corresponds to 23 and a half hundredths of the total tile.
DIFFICULTY: hard
