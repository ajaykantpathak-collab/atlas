## ELI5
Reflection is light playing catch: rays leave a source, bounce off everything in the room, and the ones that reach your eyes paint the world. A mirror returns the ball in tidy order so your face reads back; a rough desk scatters the catch, so the desk is merely visible. No light, no catch, no seeing - which is why a pitch-dark room hides everything.

## DEFINITION
Reflection is the bouncing of light rays off a surface. We see objects only because light hits them and reflects into our eyes, allowing our brain to perceive their shape and color.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to explain why we see objects using reflection and distinguish between regular reflection (like in a mirror) and diffuse reflection (like off a wall).
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the definition of reflection, the law of reflection (angle of incidence = angle of reflection), and the difference between regular and diffuse reflection. It excludes total internal reflection, polarization, and multiple reflections.
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wondered why you can see your face in a mirror but not in a plain white wall? Start by holding a torch towards a mirror in a dark room; notice the beam of light bouncing off and hitting a spot on the wall. Now, try the same with a piece of cardboard. You will notice that while the mirror sends the light in one clear direction, the cardboard scatters it everywhere. This happens because surfaces have different textures; smooth surfaces create clear images, while rough surfaces scatter light. The key insight is that reflection is happening in both cases, but the *way* the light bounces determines if we see a clear image or just the object's surface.

**Takeaway:** we see objects because light bounces off them into our eyes - smooth surfaces return it in order, rough ones scatter it. **In the exam:** in reflection questions, trace the ray from source to object to eye, then judge the surface's manner. **Check yourself:** shine the walkthrough's torch at the mirror - the returned beam is the tidy catch made visible. **Why students miss it:** students reserve bouncing for mirrors - the visible desk is a no to that; every seen thing has returned light. Rehearse the exam move once more - ray, bounce, eye - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that "reflection" only happens with mirrors. They think that non-shiny objects (like cloth or wood) do not reflect light at all. This happens because they associate "reflection" with the ability to see an image of oneself, rather than the physical act of light changing direction upon impact.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student: "If you are in a dark room, can you see a red ball?" When they say "no," ask "why?" Guide them to realize that the ball needs external light to reflect to their eyes. Explain that everything they see—the wall, the floor, the pen—is reflecting light, but just not in the organized way a mirror does.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A ray of light strikes a smooth mirror at an angle of 45 degrees to the surface normal. If the mirror is tilted, what is the angle of reflection?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given angle of incidence (45 degrees).
- Step 2: Recall the law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
- Step 3: Apply the rule: 45 degrees = angle of reflection.
- Step 4: Confirm that the angle of reflection is 45 degrees.
REASONING: The law of reflection is a constant principle for smooth surfaces; regardless of the mirror's orientation, the reflected ray maintains symmetry with the incident ray.
ANSWER: 45 degrees.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A student shines a torch on a white wall. The light spreads out. Why is this not a "clear image" reflection?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the surface of the wall (rough vs. smooth).
- Step 2: Contrast the light behavior on a smooth mirror (regular reflection) vs. a rough wall (diffuse reflection).
- Step 3: Explain that light scatters in many directions on the wall.
- Step 4: Conclude that because the rays do not stay organized, no image forms.
REASONING: Regular reflection preserves the pattern of light rays, which is necessary to form an image. Diffuse reflection breaks the pattern by sending rays in random directions.
ANSWER: The wall causes diffuse reflection; the light rays scatter in different directions, preventing the formation of a clear image.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A beam of light hits a mirror at 20 degrees to the surface. What is the angle of incidence? (Note: The angle of incidence is measured from the normal, which is 90 degrees to the surface).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the angle to the surface (20 degrees).
- Step 2: Define the normal (90 degrees).
- Step 3: Calculate the angle of incidence: 90 - 20 = 70 degrees.
- Step 4: State the law of reflection (angle of incidence = angle of reflection).
- Step 5: Conclude the angle of reflection is 70 degrees.
REASONING: It is vital to distinguish between the angle relative to the surface and the angle relative to the normal. The physics law specifically uses the normal.
ANSWER: 70 degrees.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You are designing a theatre stage. You want the actors to be clearly visible, but you do not want the audience to be blinded by a direct "glare" (reflection) from the stage floor. Should you polish the floor to a mirror finish or leave it with a matte (rough) finish? Explain why.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define the effect of a mirror finish (regular reflection).
- Step 2: Define the effect of a matte finish (diffuse reflection).
- Step 3: Identify the goal (visibility without glare).
- Step 4: Select the matte finish and justify using the scattering of light.
ANSWER: You should use a matte finish. A mirror finish would create regular reflection, causing the bright stage lights to bounce directly into the audience's eyes (glare). A matte finish causes diffuse reflection, which scatters the light, making the actors visible from all angles without intense, blinding reflections.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A mirror is placed in a corner. A light ray hits the mirror. If you double the angle of incidence, what happens to the angle of reflection? Does the reflected ray move closer to or further from the normal?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions.
STEPS:
- Step 1: State the law of reflection (Angle i = Angle r).
- Step 2: Apply the doubling condition (If i becomes 2i, then r must become 2r).
- Step 3: Conclude that the angle of reflection also doubles.
- Step 4: Determine the direction relative to the normal (Increasing the angle moves the ray further from the normal).
ANSWER: If the angle of incidence is doubled, the angle of reflection also doubles. Because the angles are measured from the normal, increasing the angle means the reflected ray moves further away from the normal line.
DIFFICULTY: hard
