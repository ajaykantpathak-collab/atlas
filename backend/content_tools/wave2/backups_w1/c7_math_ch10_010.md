## ELI5
The canteen loses 50 rupees a day, four days running - and the pattern of losses is a staircase: each day's product steps down by the same amount, marches through zero, and turns positive when the multiplier does. Patterns in integer operations are exactly such staircases: change one factor one step at a time, watch the answers move steadily, and the rule announces itself - including the famous flip where negative times negative turns positive. Spot the step, trust the staircase, predict the landing.

## DEFINITION
Patterns in integer operations involve observing, analyzing, and generalizing regularities or predictable behaviors when applying arithmetic operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division) to positive and negative integers. By recognizing these patterns, students can discover and verify fundamental mathematical properties such as commutativity, associativity, distributivity, and sign rules without performing tedious calculations.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are helping a school canteen manager in Mumbai track their daily profit and loss over a week. If the canteen loses 50 rupees each day (-50) for 4 consecutive days, how do we write that as a mathematical expression, and what pattern emerges if we look at losing money over fewer or more days? To begin, we write out a sequence of calculations: $(-50) \times 3 = -150$, $(-50) \times 2 = -100$, $(-50) \times 1 = -50$, and $(-50) \times 0 = 0$. Connecting this to our prior knowledge of multiplication and number lines, we notice that as the multiplier decreases by 1, the product increases by 50. The key insight is that patterns allow us to predict unknown products and quotients, such as $(-50) \times (-1) = +50$, by extending the established rule of the sequence.

**Takeaway:** integer patterns are staircases: move one factor one step, watch the answers move by a fixed amount. **In the exam:** extend the pattern two steps past zero before claiming the rule; state the rule, then apply it. **Check yourself:** extend the daily-loss pattern by two more days and narrate the product's steady walk. **Why students miss it:** they guess past zero instead of walking the staircase, missing the sign flip entirely.

The next calculation is $(-3) \times (-1) = +3$. A student who answers $-3$ has failed to recognize that as the multiplier decreases by 1, the product *increases* by 3 (moving from 0 toward positive numbers), revealing a misunderstanding of how negative products behave when multiplying by negative integers.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why does the product of two negative integers, such as $(-4) \times (-3)$, always result in a positive integer ($+12$) when analyzed through a decreasing multiplier pattern?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
When we keep the first factor at $-4$ and decrease the second factor from positive to negative ($3, 2, 1, 0, -1, -2, -3$), the products increase by 4 each step ($..., -4, 0, +4, +8, +12$). Thus, extending the pattern past zero naturally lands on positive values. A student who thinks the answer is negative is confusing addition/subtraction sign rules with multiplication sign rules.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the patterns generated by multiplying a fixed negative integer by a decreasing sequence of positive integers versus multiplying it by a decreasing sequence of negative integers. How do the directions of the changes in products differ?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
When multiplying a fixed negative integer (e.g., $-5$) by a decreasing sequence of positive integers ($3, 2, 1$), the products increase (moving closer to zero or positive). When multiplying it by a decreasing sequence of negative integers ($-1, -2, -3$), the products continue to increase into positive numbers at a steady rate. A student who claims the products always decrease fails to analyze the directional shift caused by multiplying two negatives.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the following student claim: "Since $5 + (-5) = 0$ and $4 + (-4) = 0$, if I follow the pattern for addition of opposites, $(-5) + 5$ must equal $-0$." Is this claim mathematically valid? Explain why or why not.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The claim is invalid because zero has no sign; it is neither positive nor negative, so $-0$ is simply equal to $0$. Furthermore, integer addition is commutative ($a + b = b + a$), so $(-5) + 5 = 5 + (-5) = 0$. A student who believes $-0$ exists does not understand that zero is the additive identity and neutral integer.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often assume that operations involving negative numbers always result in negative answers because "negatives make things smaller or worse." For example, when observing a pattern like $(-7) \times 3 = -21$, $(-7) \times 2 = -14$, $(-7) \times 1 = -7$, students frequently predict that $(-7) \times (-1) = -7$ or $-14$, incorrectly applying additive thinking (subtracting 7) instead of recognizing that the pattern increases by $+7$ at each step to yield $+7$.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student makes this error, guide them to write out the full sequence of numbers on a vertical table. Point to the multiplier column and ask: "As the multiplier goes down from 1 to 0, and then to $-1$, what is happening to the values in that column?" Then point to the product column and show mathematically that each step requires *adding* 7 (not subtracting 7), because $-7 + 7 = 0$, and $0 + 7 = +7$. Emphasize that the rule of the pattern dictates the next number, overriding intuition about the word "negative."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A submarine in the Indian Ocean descends at a steady rate of 25 metres per minute. If its current depth is represented as $-25$ metres after 1 minute, use a pattern of multiplication to find its depth after $-3$ minutes (i.e., 3 minutes before it reached the surface reference point).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write the given rate and initial known calculations for positive time: $(-25) \times 3 = -75$, $(-25) \times 2 = -50$, $(-25) \times 1 = -25$.
- Step 2: Identify the pattern rule in the products: as the time multiplier decreases by 1 unit, the product increases by 25 units ($-\mathbf{75} + 25 = -50$, $-\mathbf{50} + 25 = -25$).
- Step 3: Extend the pattern downward to 0 and negative time multipliers: $(-25) \times 0 = 0$, $(-25) \times (-1) = +25$, $(-25) \times (-2) = +50$.
- Step 4: Continue the pattern for the final required step: $(-25) \times (-3) = +50 + 25 = +75$.
- Step 5: Interpret the result in context: mathematically, the pattern reveals $+75$ metres, indicating position relative to a reference frame before descent started.
REASONING: By maintaining a constant multiplier change of $-1$ on the left and applying the corresponding arithmetic adjustment of adding 25 on the right, we preserve the mathematical consistency of the sequence. This proves logically why multiplying a negative integer by a negative integer results in a positive integer.
ANSWER: +75 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Complete the following integer division pattern and find the missing value for step 5: 
Step 1: $32 \div (-8) = -4$
Step 2: $24 \div (-8) = -3$
Step 3: $16 \div (-8) = -2$
Step 4: $8 \div (-8) = -1$
Step 5: $0 \div (-8) = ?$
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine the dividend column: $32, 24, 16, 8, 0$. Notice that the dividend decreases by 8 in each consecutive step.
- Step 2: Examine the divisor column: it remains constant at $-8$.
- Step 3: Examine the quotient column: $-4, -3, -2, -1$. Notice that the quotient increases by $+1$ in each step (since $-4 + 1 = -3$, etc.).
- Step 4: Apply the pattern rule to Step 5 where the dividend is 0: $0 \div (-8)$.
- Step 5: Following the sequence of quotients ($-1$ followed by the next integer increase), $0 \div (-8) = 0$.
REASONING: Patterns in division show that when the dividend decreases by the value of the divisor, the quotient increases by 1. Dividing zero by any non-zero integer always yields zero, which perfectly fits our sequence progression from $-1$ to $0$.
ANSWER: 0
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A game show awards points for correct answers (+10) and deducts points for wrong answers (-10). A contestant starts with 0 points. They get a pattern of 4 consecutive wrong answers. Write this as an integer expression sequence, find the final score, and then determine what their score would have been if the pattern of wrong answers had been "reversed" by $-2$ turns (meaning 2 hypothetical penalty cancellations).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Set up the sequence for 4 wrong answers: $0 + [(-10) \times 1] = -10$, $0 + [(-10) \times 2] = -20$, $0 + [(-10) \times 3] = -30$, $0 + [(-10) \times 4] = -40$.
- Step 2: Identify the score after 4 wrong answers as $-40$ points.
- Step 3: Set up the pattern extension for negative multipliers to simulate reversing 2 penalty turns: $(-10) \times 0 = 0$, $(-10) \times (-1) = +10$, $(-10) \times (-2) = +20$.
- Step 4: Add the reversal value to the starting score: $0 + (+20) = +20$ points.
- Step 5: Verify using algebraic properties: subtracting a penalty is equivalent to adding points, so canceling two $-10$ penalties means adding $+20$ total points.
REASONING: Combining integer multiplication patterns with real-world scoring demonstrates that reversing negative actions (penalties) yields positive outcomes. The step-by-step extension of the table bridges arithmetic operations with logical deduction.
ANSWER: Final score after 4 wrong answers is -40 points; score with 2 reversed penalties is +20 points.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Consider the calculation pattern: $5 \times (-3) = -15$, $4 \times (-3) = -12$, $3 \times (-3) = -9$. 
A student claims: "As the first number decreases, the answers are getting closer to zero, so eventually the answers will become positive numbers like $+3$ and $+6$ if we keep going down to $-1$ and $-2$." Evaluate this claim, identify the error in their reasoning, and provide the correct continuation of the pattern down to multiplier $-2$.
COMPETENCY: Error identification, reasoning justification, and pattern continuation.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the student's premise: They observed that $-15, -12, -9$ are moving closer to zero (increasing).
- Step 2: Identify the flaw: While the numbers increase toward zero, they stop at zero only when multiplied by 0. Multiplying by negative numbers continues the increase *past* zero into positive numbers, not stopping at $+3$ or $+6$ arbitrarily.
- Step 3: Compute the correct next steps: 
  $2 \times (-3) = -6$
  $1 \times (-3) = -3$
  $0 \times (-3) = 0$
  $-1 \times (-3) = +3$
  $-2 \times (-3) = +6$
- Step 4: Formulate the final evaluation showing that the student correctly spotted the upward trend but miscalculated the magnitude or rate of the sequence past zero.
ANSWER: The student's claim is partially correct about the direction (numbers increase toward zero and beyond), but their assumption that it stops or skips values is wrong. The correct values for multiplier $-1$ and $-2$ are $+3$ and $+6$ respectively, because each step adds $+3$.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: An automated weather station in Ladakh records temperature changes. For 5 consecutive hours, the temperature dropped by 2 degrees Celsius each hour, starting from $+4^\circ\text{C}$. 
Part A: Express this sequence using integer multiplication and find the temperature at hour 5. 
Part B: Using the established pattern, calculate what the temperature would have been 3 hours *before* monitoring began (i.e., at time $-3$ hours). Explain how the sign rules for multiplication support your calculation.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical patterns and applying sign rules.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define the starting point ($+4^\circ\text{C}$) and the hourly change rate ($-\mathbf{2}^\circ\text{C}$).
- Step 2: Model Part A as an expression: $+4 + [(-2) \times 5] = +4 + (-10) = -6^\circ\text{C}$.
- Step 3: Model Part B by setting up a multiplication pattern backward in time: $(-2) \times 3 = -6$, $(-2) \times 2 = -4$, $(-2) \times 1 = -2$, $(-2) \times 0 = 0$, $(-2) \times (-1) = +2$, $(-2) \times (-2) = +4$, $(-2) \times (-3) = +6$.
- Step 4: Add this result to the base starting temperature or evaluate the retro-calculation: $4 + (+6) = +10^\circ\text{C}$.
- Step 5: Synthesize how multiplying negative time (hours before) by a negative rate (temperature drop) results in a positive temperature adjustment (+6).
ANSWER: Part A: $-6^\circ\text{C}$. Part B: $+10^\circ\text{C}$. Explanation: Moving backward in time against a cooling trend means the temperature was warmer in the past; multiplying the negative drop rate by negative hours yields a positive temperature offset.
DIFFICULTY: hard
