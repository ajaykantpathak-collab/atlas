## ELI5
The study lamp's secret is a stubborn wire: a tungsten filament so resistive that pushed current heats it white-hot, and heat blooms into light. Electrical energy enters; light and warmth leave. The glass bulb is a bodyguard - keeping oxygen away from the glowing filament so it burns years instead of seconds. Every switched lamp is the same small drama: current fights resistance, and the friction glows.

## DEFINITION
An electric lamp is a device that converts electrical energy into light energy (and thermal energy) through the heating of a high-resistance tungsten filament until it glows white-hot. The physical quantity associated with its operation is electrical power, measured in watts (W), which measures the rate at which electrical energy is converted into other forms of energy in a closed circuit.

## WALKTHROUGH
When you switch on a study lamp at your desk in the evening, you observe that electrical current flows from the battery or wall socket into the bulb, causing it to emit bright light and heat up within a fraction of a second. To understand how this happens, we must examine the inside of the bulb, where electrical energy entering the circuit encounters a thin, coiled metal wire called a filament. Building on our understanding of simple electric circuits and current flow, we know that current must pass through a complete, unbroken path. When this current squeezes through the extremely thin tungsten filament, electrical resistance causes the wire to heat up intensely until it glows. The key principle here is the conversion of electrical energy into light and heat energy through resistive heating.

**Takeaway:** an electric lamp converts electrical energy to light through a high-resistance filament heated white-hot inside a protective bulb. **In the exam:** answer with the conversion chain - electrical to heat to light - and name the filament as the resistance star. **Check yourself:** switch on the walkthrough's study lamp and narrate the filament's evening in three energy stages. **Why students miss it:** students credit the glass with the glow - the flagged misattribution. ## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is the filament of a traditional incandescent electric lamp made of tungsten rather than a thick copper wire?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Tungsten has a very high melting point and high electrical resistance, which allows it to heat up to glowing temperatures without melting. A student who thinks copper should be used confuses good conductivity with heating ability; copper has low resistance and would remain cool, conducting current without glowing or producing light.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A small flashlight bulb has a resistance of $5\,\Omega$ and draws a current of $0.6\,\text{A}$ from a cell. Calculate the electrical power dissipated by the bulb if power is given by the product of current squared and resistance ($P = I^2 \times R$), and state its unit.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Given: Current ($I$) = $0.6\,\text{A}$, Resistance ($R$) = $5\,\Omega$.
Step 1: Identify the formula for electrical power: $P = I^2 \times R$.
Step 2: Substitute the given values with units: $P = (0.6\,\text{A})^2 \times 5\,\Omega$.
Step 3: Square the current: $(0.6\,\text{A})^2 = 0.36\,\text{A}^2$.
Step 4: Multiply by resistance: $0.36 \times 5 = 1.8$. Tracking units ($\text{A}^2 \times \Omega = \text{W}$), we get $1.8\,\text{W}$.
Answer: $1.8\,\text{W}$. A student who gets $3.0\,\text{W}$ likely multiplied $0.6 \times 5$ without squaring the current, showing a failure to follow the correct power formula structure.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze what happens to an electric lamp in a circuit if the thin filament inside the glass bulb breaks (forms a gap). Explain the physical reason using the concept of circuit continuity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
When the filament breaks, the circuit becomes an open circuit because there is a break in the conducting path for the electric current. Since current cannot jump across the air gap in a low-voltage domestic or torch circuit, the current drops to zero, and the lamp immediately stops glowing. A student who believes the lamp will still glow dimly is ignoring the fundamental requirement of a closed, continuous loop for current flow.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the statement: "An electric lamp creates new energy inside the bulb to make light." Is this statement scientifically correct according to the law of conservation of energy?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The statement is incorrect because energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another. The electric lamp does not create energy; it converts electrical energy supplied by the source (cell or battery) into light and heat energy. A student who agrees with the statement fails to understand the principle of energy conservation in electrical appliances.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that an electric lamp "uses up" or "consumes" electricity completely, or that electricity is stored inside the bulb as light. They fail to understand that electrons flow continuously through the filament in a closed loop, transferring energy from the power source to the surroundings as heat and light without any electricity being permanently destroyed or consumed.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this misconception, have the student trace a complete circuit diagram from the positive terminal of a cell, through the lamp filament, and back to the negative terminal. Emphasize that the exact same amount of current entering the lamp leaves the lamp, and explain that energy is transferred (like water turning a waterwheel), not used up like fuel in a fire.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A study lamp connected to a household circuit has a potential difference of $220\,\text{V}$ across its terminals and draws a current of $0.25\,\text{A}$. Calculate the electrical power of the lamp using $P = V \times I$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given quantities with units: Potential difference ($V$) = $220\,\text{V}$, Current ($I$) = $0.25\,\text{A}$, and identify what to find: Power ($P$).
- step 2: Identify the formula and check units: $P = V \times I$, where $V$ is in volts and $I$ is in amperes.
- step 3: Substitute values with units into the formula: $P = 220\,\text{V} \times 0.25\,\text{A}$.
- step 4: Compute the numerical value and track unit cancellation ($\text{V} \times \text{A} = \text{W}$): $220 \times 0.25 = 55$.
- step 5: Verify the answer is physically reasonable for a small desk lamp and state with units: $55\,\text{W}$.
REASONING: Multiplying the voltage (electrical push) by the current (rate of charge flow) gives the total electrical energy converted per second, which is power measured in watts.
ANSWER: $55\,\text{W}$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An experimental small bulb in a school science lab operates at a voltage of $6\,\text{V}$ and has a resistance of $12\,\Omega$. First find the current using Ohm's law ($I = V / R$), and then calculate the power using $P = V \times I$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: $V = 6\,\text{V}$, $R = 12\,\Omega$, and what to find: Current ($I$) and Power ($P$).
- step 2: Calculate current using Ohm's law formula $I = V / R$.
- step 3: Substitute values to find current: $I = 6\,\text{V} / 12\,\Omega = 0.5\,\text{A}$.
- step 4: Substitute voltage and calculated current into the power formula $P = V \times I$: $P = 6\,\text{V} \times 0.5\,\text{A}$.
- step 5: Compute final power and verify units ($\text{V} \times \text{A} = \text{W}$): $3.0\,\text{W}$.
REASONING: By determining the current flowing through the known resistance first, we can find how much electrical energy is being converted into light and heat per second by the bulb.
ANSWER: $3.0\,\text{W}$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A torch bulb is connected to a battery delivering $3\,\text{V}$. If the bulb operates for $60\,\text{seconds}$ and draws a current of $0.4\,\text{A}$, calculate the total electrical energy converted into light and heat during this time, given that Energy ($E$) = Power ($P$) $\times$ time ($t$), where $P = V \times I$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given quantities: $V = 3\,\text{V}$, $I = 0.4\,\text{A}$, time ($t$) = $60\,\text{s}$.
- step 2: Calculate electrical power first using $P = V \times I$.
- step 3: Substitute values for power: $P = 3\,\text{V} \times 0.4\,\text{A} = 1.2\,\text{W}$ (or $1.2\,\text{J/s}$).
- step 4: Identify the energy formula $E = P \times t$ and substitute values: $E = 1.2\,\text{W} \times 60\,\text{s}$.
- step 5: Compute final energy and track units ($\text{W} \times \text{s} = \text{J}$): $1.2 \times 60 = 72\,\text{J}$.
- step 6: Verify the answer is physically reasonable for a small torch bulb running for one minute.
REASONING: Power tells us the energy converted per second; multiplying power by total time gives the cumulative electrical energy transformed into light and heat.
ANSWER: $72\,\text{J}$
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Why does an ordinary incandescent bulb become too hot to touch after glowing for a few minutes, even though its main purpose is to produce light?
COMPETENCY: Explaining energy transformations and side effects in electrical devices
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand that electric lamps convert electrical energy into multiple forms.
- step 2: Recognize that the tungsten filament has high electrical resistance, causing intense thermal heating when current passes through it.
- step 3: Conclude that only a small fraction of electrical energy is converted into visible light, while a major portion is converted into heat (thermal energy).
- step 4: Relate this energy loss to the physical temperature rise of the glass bulb.
ANSWER: An electric lamp is not 100% efficient at producing light. Most of the electrical energy is actually converted into thermal energy (heat) due to the resistance of the tungsten filament, causing the bulb to become very hot.

QUESTION: Two bulbs of different filament thicknesses are connected across identical batteries. Bulb A has a thicker tungsten filament, while Bulb B has a much thinner tungsten filament of the same length. Which bulb will glow brighter and why?
COMPETENCY: Analyzing the relationship between filament thickness, resistance, and light emission
STEPS:

- step 2: Apply the rule that a thinner wire has higher electrical resistance than a thicker wire of the same material and length.
- step 3: Understand that higher resistance restricts current more, but also concentrates more heat generation in a smaller volume of metal.
- step 4: Conclude that the thinner filament (Bulb B) reaches a higher temperature more quickly and glows brighter (though it may burn out sooner if pushed beyond its limit).
ANSWER: Bulb B with the thinner filament will have higher resistance, causing it to heat up to a higher temperature and glow brighter under the same voltage, because higher resistance concentrates thermal energy conversion more intensely in the filament wire.
