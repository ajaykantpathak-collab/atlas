## ELI5
More cooks, less time; faster speed, shorter time — when one quantity grows and the other shrinks so their product never budges, that is inverse proportion. Think of a fixed job, say a big box of sweets to pack: fewer workers means more days, double the workers and the days halve, because workers × days stays the same constant k. To test any table, multiply the pairs: constant products mean inverse, not direct. In direct proportion you divide to check; here you multiply — the product is the thing that refuses to change.

## DEFINITION
Two quantities are in inverse proportion if an increase in one quantity causes a proportional decrease in the other quantity, such that their product always remains constant. Mathematically, if \(x\) and \(y\) are inversely proportional, then \(x \times y = k\) (where \(k\) is a non-zero constant), or equivalently \(x \propto \frac{1}{y}\).

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine a contractor tells you: "6 workers can paint a building in 10 days. If I hire 12 workers, will it take 20 days or 5 days?" Most students intuitively say 5 days because more workers should finish faster. This is the hook: in direct proportion (like more workers → more wages), both quantities increase together. Here, one increases while the other decreases. Ask: "What stays the same?" The total work (worker-days). Write the pairs: (6, 10) and (12, 5). Compute products: \(6 \times 10 = 60\), \(12 \times 5 = 60\). The product is constant. That constant product is the signature of inverse proportion. Define: \(x \times y = k\). Contrast with direct proportion where \(x/y = k\). End with the key insight: "In inverse proportion, we multiply to check; in direct proportion, we divide."

Takeaway: to test a table for inverse proportion, multiply each x-y pair; constant products mean inverse, and that constant is k. Exam format: expect 'identify the type of proportion and justify' — write the products row by row, then state xy = k in one line. Verification beat: confirm the product on every row, including the awkward rows with small values. Why students miss it: they check that one quantity rises while the other falls and stop there; many patterns move oppositely without a constant product, and only the product test separates true inverse proportion from its look-alikes.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
State the mathematical condition that must be true for two quantities \(x\) and \(y\) to be in inverse proportion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The product \(x \times y\) must be constant for all corresponding pairs of values. A wrong answer (e.g., "the ratio \(x/y\) is constant") reveals the student is confusing inverse proportion with direct proportion, where the ratio is constant. This is the most fundamental misconception and must be corrected before proceeding.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why the product of two inversely proportional quantities remains constant, using the example of speed and time for a fixed distance.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
For a fixed distance, distance = speed × time. Since distance does not change, if speed doubles, time must halve to keep the product (distance) unchanged. The product remains constant because the two quantities change by reciprocal factors. A wrong answer (e.g., "because they are opposite") shows the student lacks a mechanistic understanding and is relying on vague language rather than the mathematical structure of the relationship.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
The table below shows values of \(x\) and \(y\). Determine if they are in inverse proportion. Show your check.
\(x\): 3, 6, 9, 12
\(y\): 16, 8, 5, 4
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Compute products: \(3 \times 16 = 48\), \(6 \times 8 = 48\), \(9 \times 5 = 45\), \(12 \times 4 = 48\). The products are not all equal (45 ≠ 48), so \(x\) and \(y\) are NOT in inverse proportion. A wrong answer stating they are in inverse proportion reveals the student only checked the first two or last two pairs and did not verify the constant product condition for ALL given pairs.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare these two situations:
(A) The number of pipes filling a tank and the time taken to fill it.
(B) The number of pipes and the total cost of the pipes.
Which situation represents inverse proportion? Justify your answer by describing how the quantities change relative to each other.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Situation (A) is inverse proportion: more pipes → less time, and the product (pipes × time) equals the tank capacity (constant). Situation (B) is direct proportion: more pipes → more cost, and the ratio cost/pipes (price per pipe) is constant. A wrong answer selecting (B) or saying both are inverse reveals the student cannot distinguish contexts where the product is conserved (work, distance, capacity) from contexts where the rate is conserved (price, density).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims: "If \(x\) and \(y\) are inversely proportional, then when \(x\) becomes 3 times larger, \(y\) becomes 3 times smaller." Is this statement correct? Explain your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, the statement is correct. If \(x \times y = k\) and new \(x' = 3x\), then \(x' \times y' = k \Rightarrow 3x \times y' = x \times y \Rightarrow y' = y/3\). The factor of change is exactly reciprocal. A wrong answer (e.g., "y becomes 1/3 of original" without justification, or "y decreases by 3") reveals the student does not understand the multiplicative nature of the relationship and may be thinking additively ("3 times smaller" is ambiguous language; the precise reasoning uses the constant product).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-life word problem (using Indian context: rupees, litres, kg, etc.) that involves inverse proportion. Write the problem clearly and solve it, showing the constant product step.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example problem: "A water tanker supplies 500 litres of water to a village every day. If each family uses 20 litres per day, the water lasts for 25 families. How many families can be served if each family reduces usage to 10 litres per day?" Solution: Total water = 500 L (constant). Families × Usage = 500. Initially: 25 × 20 = 500. New: \(f \times 10 = 500 \Rightarrow f = 50\). 50 families can be served. A poor answer creates a direct proportion problem (e.g., "cost of apples") or fails to identify the constant product, revealing the student cannot synthesize the inverse relationship structure independently.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students apply the direct proportion check (constant ratio) to inverse proportion situations. For example, given \(x = 2, y = 12\) and \(x = 4, y = 6\), they compute \(2/12 = 1/6\) and \(4/6 = 2/3\), see the ratios are not equal, and incorrectly conclude "not in proportion" (neither direct nor inverse). They do not think to compute the product \(2 \times 12 = 24\) and \(4 \times 6 = 24\). The specific wrong step is: **checking \(x/y\) instead of \(x \times y\)** when the problem context implies one quantity increases while the other decreases.
## ON_FAILURE
1. Give the student a table with 3-4 pairs from a known inverse situation (e.g., workers and days: (2,18), (3,12), (6,6)).
2. Ask them to compute BOTH the ratio \(x/y\) and the product \(x \times y\) for each pair.
3. Explicitly point: "Look, the ratio changes (9, 4, 1) but the product stays 36. In inverse proportion, we multiply; in direct, we divide."
4. Have them practice 5 tables where they must first predict "direct or inverse?" from the context, then compute the appropriate check (ratio for direct, product for inverse). Do not proceed until they automatically reach for multiplication when quantities move in opposite directions.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A farmer has enough cattle feed to last 20 cows for 30 days. If he buys 10 more cows, how many days will the feed last?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the two quantities: number of cows and number of days the feed lasts. More cows means feed finishes faster, so they move in opposite directions → likely inverse proportion.
- Step 2: Verify using constant product. Total feed amount (cow-days) is fixed. Initial product = 20 cows × 30 days = 600 cow-days.
- Step 3: New number of cows = 20 + 10 = 30 cows. Let the feed last \(d\) days. By inverse proportion, 30 × \(d\) = 600.
- Step 4: Solve for \(d\): \(d = 600 / 30 = 20\) days.
- Step 5: Interpret: The feed will last 20 days. Check: 30 cows × 20 days = 600 cow-days (constant). Answer makes sense: more cows, fewer days.
REASONING: The total amount of feed is the invariant quantity. Expressing it as "cow-days" makes the constant product visible. The inverse relationship arises because the same total work (or resource) is distributed among a changing number of agents.
ANSWER: 20 days
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A car travelling at 60 km/h takes 4 hours to reach a destination. At what speed must it travel to cover the same distance in 3 hours?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify quantities: speed (km/h) and time (h). For a fixed distance, higher speed means less time → opposite directions → inverse proportion.
- Step 2: The constant product is the distance. Distance = 60 km/h × 4 h = 240 km.
- Step 3: New time = 3 hours. Let required speed = \(s\) km/h. By inverse proportion, \(s \times 3 = 240\).
- Step 4: Solve: \(s = 240 / 3 = 80\) km/h.
- Step 5: Verify: 80 km/h × 3 h = 240 km (same distance). The speed increased from 60 to 80 (factor 4/3), time decreased from 4 to 3 (factor 3/4) – reciprocal factors.
REASONING: Distance is the conserved quantity linking speed and time. The formula distance = speed × time is exactly the constant product \(x \times y = k\). This example shows inverse proportion in a rate context, different from the work/resource context of Example 1.
ANSWER: 80 km/h
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: In a hostel, food provisions for 120 students last for 20 days. After 5 days, 30 more students join the hostel. For how many more days will the remaining food last?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Total food stock = 120 students × 20 days = 2400 student-days (constant product).
- Step 2: Food consumed in first 5 days = 120 students × 5 days = 600 student-days.
- Step 3: Remaining food = 2400 - 600 = 1800 student-days.
- Step 4: New number of students = 120 + 30 = 150 students.
- Step 5: Let remaining days = \(d\). By inverse proportion on remaining food: 150 × \(d\) = 1800.
- Step 6: Solve: \(d = 1800 / 150 = 12\) days. The food will last 12 more days.
REASONING: This combines the "consumption over time" concept (subtracting used stock) with inverse proportion. The constant product applies to the *remaining* resource and the *new* number of consumers. Students often mistakenly apply inverse proportion to the original 20 days instead of the remaining 15 days' worth of food.
ANSWER: 12 days
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A contractor estimates that 6 workers can paint a building in 10 days. He wants to finish the job in 5 days. How many additional workers must he hire? Explain why this is an inverse proportion situation.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and justifying the type of proportion.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand the problem: Workers and days are the quantities. Total work (worker-days) is fixed.
- Step 2: Identify the relationship: More workers → fewer days. Quantities move in opposite directions → inverse proportion.
- Step 3: Compute constant product: 6 workers × 10 days = 60 worker-days.
- Step 4: Find workers needed for 5 days: \(w \times 5 = 60 \Rightarrow w = 12\) workers.
- Step 5: Additional workers = 12 - 6 = 6 workers.
ANSWER: 6 additional workers are needed. This is inverse proportion because the total amount of work (measured in worker-days) remains constant; increasing the number of workers decreases the time required proportionally, keeping the product constant.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A car takes 3 hours to travel a certain distance at 50 km/h. If the speed is increased by 25%, how much time will be saved? Verify that speed and time are inversely proportional for this journey.
COMPETENCY: Applying inverse proportion to multi-step problems involving percentage change and verifying the constant product property.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Find the fixed distance: Distance = Speed × Time = 50 km/h × 3 h = 150 km.
- Step 2: Calculate new speed: 25% increase on 50 km/h = 50 + 12.5 = 62.5 km/h.
- Step 3: Find new time using constant distance: Time = Distance / Speed = 150 km / 62.5 km/h = 2.4 hours.
- Step 4: Convert 2.4 hours to hours and minutes: 2 hours + 0.4×60 = 2 hours 24 minutes.
- Step 5: Time saved = 3 hours - 2.4 hours = 0.6 hours = 36 minutes.
- Step 6: Verify inverse proportion: Original product = 50 × 3 = 150. New product = 62.5 × 2.4 = 150. Products match, confirming inverse proportion.
ANSWER: 36 minutes will be saved. Speed and time are inversely proportional because their product (the distance) remains constant at 150 km.
DIFFICULTY: hard
