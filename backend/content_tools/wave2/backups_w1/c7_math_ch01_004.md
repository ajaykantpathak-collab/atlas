## ELI5
When a school budget jumps from lakhs to ten crore rupees, the place value chart grows one more column: the crore. One crore is a bundle of a hundred lakhs - an eight-digit number written 1,00,00,000 - sitting one full period beyond the ten-lakhs place. On a cheque it appears as 1 followed by seven zeros, grouped the Indian way. Just as a lakh is ten ten-thousands, a crore is a hundred lakhs: each new column is the old bundle gathered ten times over and given a fresh name.

## DEFINITION
A crore is an 8-digit number in the Indian numbering system equal to 100 lakhs or ten million, representing the place value immediately following the ten-lakhs place. It marks the fourth period in the Indian place value chart, written with commas as $1,00,00,000$.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine the government of India announces a massive budget of nearly ten crore rupees to build new schools across every district, how would you write that number in digits on a cheque? To figure this out, we extend our place value chart one step beyond ten lakhs by adding a new column to the left called the "Crores" place. We recall from our work with lakhs that each period groups digits using commas after the first three digits from the right, and then after every two digits. By placing a '1' in the crores column and zeros elsewhere, we create $1,00,00,000$. The key insight is that one crore is exactly ten times ten lakhs, adding another place value to our counting toolkit.

**Takeaway:** a crore is the fourth Indian period: a hundred lakhs bundled together, written with a comma after the first digit alone. **In the exam:** for eight-digit numbers, name the crore digit first, then read lakhs, thousands and ones as periods, comma by comma. **Check yourself:** expand 3,05,40,120 into its periods and say the whole number aloud in one steady breath. **Why students miss it:** they drop a zero while writing 'twenty lakh' or 'five thousand', shifting every later digit one place left.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do we place a comma after the crore digit in an 8-digit number like $5,24,10,632$, unlike the commas we place later?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The first comma from the right is placed after three digits (thousands period), and subsequent commas are placed after every two digits (lakhs period and crores period) to make reading large numbers easier. A student who thinks commas are placed randomly or just every three digits does not understand the distinct periods (ones, thousands, lakhs, crores) of the Indian numbering system.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often write 8-digit numbers by inserting zeros incorrectly when a period is empty. For instance, when asked to write "Three crore four lakh", they write $3,40,00,000$ instead of $3,04,00,000$ because they confuse "four lakh" with "forty lakh" by misplacing the zero within the lakhs period.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A state government allocates funds of 7 crore 45 lakh 12 thousand 300 rupees for building highways. Write this number in standard Indian numeral form with commas and find the sum of the place values of the digits in the crores place and thousands place.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the digits for each period from the given statement: Crores period = 7, Lakhs period = 45, Thousands period = 12, Ones period = 300.
- Step 2: Arrange the digits into the Indian place value chart: $7,45,12,300$.
- Step 3: Determine the place value of the digit in the crores place: Digit 7 is in the crores place, so its place value is $7 \times 1,00,00,000 = 7,00,00,000$.
- Step 4: Determine the place value of the digit in the thousands place: Digit 2 is in the thousands place, so its place value is $2 \times 1,000 = 2,000$.
- Step 5: Add the two place values: $7,00,00,000 + 2,000 = 7,00,02,000$.
REASONING: Translating words to digits requires identifying every period name and ensuring two-digit slots are filled correctly for lakhs and thousands. Adding place values reinforces the additive nature of expanded notation in large numbers.
ANSWER: $7,45,12,300$; Sum of place values = $7,00,02,000$ rupees

STEPS:

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Rahul claims that the number $99,99,999$ plus $1$ results in a 7-digit number because adding 1 to 9 gives 10, which keeps the total digit count the same. Is Rahul correct? Explain why or why not using place value reasoning, and state the correct resulting number with its name.
COMPETENCY: Error identification, mathematical justification, and place value reasoning
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze Rahul's addition: $99,99,999 + 1$.
- Step 2: Perform column addition from right to left, noting the carry-overs across successive place values.
- Step 3: Observe that adding 1 to the ones place creates a chain reaction of carrying over: $9+1=10$ (carry 1), and this propagates through tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands, lakhs, and ten-lakhs.
- Step 4: Recognize that the final carry-over creates a new place value column to the left (the Crores place).
- Step 5: Conclude that the result is $1,00,00,000$, which has 8 digits, proving Rahul incorrect.
ANSWER: Rahul is incorrect. Adding 1 to $99,99,999$ causes a carry-over across every single place value column, turning all nines into zeros and creating a new place value (the crores place). The correct result is $1,00,00,000$, read as "One crore", which is an 8-digit number.
DIFFICULTY: medium
