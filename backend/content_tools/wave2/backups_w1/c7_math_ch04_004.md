## ELI5
Notebooks at 40 rupees, pens at 15: buying x notebooks and y pens costs 40x + 15y - one expression holding a whole shopping trip. Translation is keyword-reading: 'more than' adds, 'times' multiplies, 'each' attaches a rate to a variable, and 'subtract x from 15' flips the order to 15 - x. Fixed parts like a taxi's starting fee stand alone; per-kilometre rates hug their variable. Read the sentence slowly, circle the verbs, and every word problem writes its own expression.

## DEFINITION
Writing algebraic expressions from word problems involves translating verbal statements into mathematical symbols, variables, and operations to represent real-life quantities. An algebraic expression combines variables (represented by letters like $x$ or $n$), numbers, and operations (+, -, $\times$, $\div$) without an equality sign.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you go to a stationery shop in your neighbourhood where notebooks cost ₹40 each and pens cost ₹15 each. If you buy $x$ notebooks and $y$ pens, how can you write a single mathematical statement that tells you the total cost without knowing how many you bought yet? To do this, we use variables as placeholders for unknown quantities and translate everyday words like "more than", "less than", "times", and "shared equally" into arithmetic operations. Building on our understanding of variables and constants from earlier chapters, we break the word sentence into smaller phrases. Finally, we combine these phrases using proper algebraic notation, remembering that multiplication between a number and a variable (like $40 \times x$) is simply written as $40x$.

**Takeaway:** translating words is keyword-mapping: more-than adds, times multiplies, each fixes a rate, and from-subtract flips the order. **In the exam:** write 'let x be...' first, then build the expression clause by clause, bracketing wherever grouping is spoken. **Check yourself:** convert the notebook-and-pen sentence into an expression, then back-translate it into words to test fidelity. **Why students miss it:** they translate in story order, so 'subtract x from 15' becomes x - 15 and the meaning quietly inverts.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If Rahul has $x$ marbles and Priya has 5 more marbles than Rahul, write the algebraic expression for Priya's number of marbles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct expression is $x + 5$. A student who writes $5x$ has confused the phrase "5 more than" (which means addition) with "5 times" (which means multiplication). Similarly, writing $5 - x$ reverses the order of subtraction, ignoring that Priya has *more* than Rahul.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
A shopkeeper packs $m$ mangoes into each box and has 12 loose mangoes left over. If he uses 4 boxes, explain why the total number of mangoes is represented by $4m + 12$ rather than $(m + 12)4$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct expression is $4m + 12$ because $4$ boxes each contain $m$ mangoes, giving $4m$ mangoes in total, to which we add the $12$ loose ones. Writing $(m + 12)4$ implies that *each* box contains both $m$ mangoes and 12 loose ones, which contradicts the problem statement. A student who writes $(m+12)4$ fails to group the items correctly according to the real-world distribution.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A taxi charges a fixed fee of ₹50 plus ₹12 per kilometre travelled. Write an algebraic expression for the total fare in rupees for a journey of $k$ kilometres.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct expression is $12k + 50$. 
Steps:
1. Identify the variable: Let $k$ be the number of kilometres.
2. Identify the rate per kilometre: ₹12 per kilometre translates to $12 \times k$ or $12k$.
3. Identify the fixed cost: ₹50 is a constant added once regardless of distance.
4. Combine them with addition: $12k + 50$.
A student who writes $50k + 12$ has mistakenly applied the fixed fee per kilometre and the variable rate as a one-time charge, reversing the roles of the constant and coefficient.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the expressions $3(x + 4)$ and $3x + 4$. In the context of buying pencils where $x$ is the cost of a pencil, what real-world situation does each expression represent?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The expression $3(x + 4)$ represents buying 3 identical sets, where each set contains one pencil costing $x$ rupees and an eraser costing ₹4. The expression $3x + 4$ represents buying 3 pencils costing $x$ rupees each, plus a single notebook costing ₹4. A student who thinks these two expressions are identical does not understand how parentheses alter the scope of multiplication in word problems.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Ankit evaluates the phrase "subtract $x$ from 15" and writes the expression as $x - 15$. Is Ankit correct? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Ankit is incorrect. The correct expression is $15 - x$. 
Justification: The phrase "subtract $x$ *from* 15" means $x$ is being taken away *starting with* 15. Therefore, 15 must come first in the subtraction statement. A student who writes $x - 15$ confuses the order of operations in English phrasing, mistaking "subtract $A$ from $B$" for $A - B$ instead of $B - A$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create an algebraic expression involving two variables, $p$ and $q$, to represent the following scenario: "Amit scored $p$ runs in a cricket match, and Rohit scored twice as many runs as Amit minus 15 runs." Explain each part of your expression.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The expected expression is $2p - 15$. 
Explanation: "Amit scored $p$ runs" establishes our variable $p$. "Twice as many runs as Amit" translates to multiplication by 2, written as $2p$. "Minus 15 runs" means subtracting 15 from that product. A poor answer would write $p - 15 \times 2$, which ignores standard order of operations or misinterprets "twice as many" as applying only after subtraction.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently misinterpret verbal phrases involving subtraction and division by reversing the order of terms. For example, in the phrase "decrease $x$ by 7", students often write $7 - x$ instead of $x - 7$, or when seeing "divide 20 by $y$", they write $y \div 20$ instead of $\frac{20}{y}$. This happens because students read words sequentially from left to right without analyzing the directional relationship of the mathematical action.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student makes a reversal error, have them test the expression with a concrete number. Ask: "If you have 20 rupees and decrease it by $x$ rupees, let's say $x$ is 5. Does $5 - 20$ give you what you have left, or does $20 - 5$?" Guide them to see that the starting amount must always come first in subtraction and division statements when phrased as "subtract from" or "divide into".

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A library has $b$ books on a bottom shelf. The middle shelf has 3 times as many books as the bottom shelf minus 12 books. The top shelf has 15 more books than the middle shelf. Write an algebraic expression for the total number of books across all three shelves combined.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the variable for the baseline shelf. Let the number of books on the bottom shelf be $b$.
- Step 2: Translate the middle shelf description. "3 times as many books as the bottom shelf minus 12" translates to $3b - 12$.
- Step 3: Translate the top shelf description. "15 more books than the middle shelf" means we take the middle shelf expression $(3b - 12)$ and add 15 to it: $(3b - 12) + 15$.
- Step 4: Combine all three shelves into a total sum expression: $b + (3b - 12) + (3b - 12 + 15)$.
- Step 5: Simplify the expression by grouping like terms. Total = $b + 3b - 12 + 3b - 12 + 15 = (1 + 3 + 3)b + (-12 - 12 + 15) = 7b - 9$.
REASONING: Breaking down the problem shelf by shelf allows us to build dependent quantities step-by-step using the same variable $b$. Combining like terms at the end yields a clean, simplified algebraic expression representing the grand total.
ANSWER: $7b - 9$ books
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Meera bought notebooks for ₹25 each and pens for ₹10 each. She bought 4 more pens than notebooks. If she bought $n$ notebooks, write an algebraic expression for her total expenditure in rupees.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define the primary variable. Let the number of notebooks be $n$.
- Step 2: Express the quantity of the second item. Since she bought 4 more pens than notebooks, the number of pens is $n + 4$.
- Step 3: Calculate the cost of the notebooks. At ₹25 per notebook, the cost is $25 \times n = 25n$.
- Step 4: Calculate the cost of the pens. At ₹10 per pen, the cost is $10 \times (n + 4)$.
- Step 5: Add both costs together and simplify: $25n + 10(n + 4) = 25n + 10n + 40 = 35n + 40$.
REASONING: Real-world purchasing problems require multiplying the quantity of each item by its unit price, then summing the costs. Distributing the ₹10 across the pens expression ensures every pen is accounted for correctly.
ANSWER: ₹$(35n + 40)$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A rectangular vegetable garden has a length that is 5 metres more than twice its breadth. If the breadth is represented by $x$ metres, write and simplify an algebraic expression for the perimeter of the garden.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define the breadth variable. Let breadth $= x$ metres.
- Step 2: Translate the length using the given condition. "5 metres more than twice its breadth" means Length $= 2x + 5$.
- Step 3: Recall the formula for the perimeter of a rectangle: $\text{Perimeter} = 2 \times (\text{Length} + \text{Breadth})$.
- Step 4: Substitute the expressions for Length and Breadth into the formula: $\text{Perimeter} = 2 \times ((2x + 5) + x)$.
- Step 5: Simplify inside the parentheses first: $(2x + x + 5) = (3x + 5)$.
- Step 6: Multiply by 2: $2 \times (3x + 5) = 6x + 10$.
REASONING: Geometric word problems require combining algebraic translation with geometric formulas. By substituting our algebraic representations of length and breadth into the perimeter formula and applying the distributive property, we obtain a single expression in terms of $x$.
ANSWER: $6x + 10$ metres

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two friends, Aarav and Kabir, save money in their piggy banks. Aarav starts with ₹100 and saves ₹20 every week. Kabir starts with ₹40 and saves ₹30 every week. Write an algebraic expression for the total combined savings of both friends after $w$ weeks. After how many weeks will Kabir's savings exceed Aarav's savings? (Hint: Set up the inequality or compare the expressions).
COMPETENCY: Translating multi-variable real-world scenarios and comparing linear expressions
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write Aarav's savings after $w$ weeks: $100 + 20w$.
- Step 2: Write Kabir's savings after $w$ weeks: $40 + 30w$.
- Step 3: Combine both expressions for total savings: $(100 + 20w) + (40 + 30w) = 50w + 140$.
- Step 4: Set up a comparison to find when Kabir's savings exceed Aarav's: $40 + 30w > 100 + 20w$.
- Step 5: Solve the comparison: subtract $20w$ from both sides ($40 + 10w > 100$), subtract 40 ($10w > 60$), divide by 10 ($w > 6$).
ANSWER: The combined savings expression is $50w + 140$. Kabir's savings exceed Aarav's after 6 weeks (i.e., at 7 weeks onwards).
DIFFICULTY: hard
