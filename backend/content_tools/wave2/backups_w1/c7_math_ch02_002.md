## ELI5
Two shops, one bill: 3 notebooks at 40 rupees, 2 pens at 10 rupees, plus 15 rupees delivery - write 3 x 40 + 2 x 10 + 15 and mathematics needs a traffic rule so everyone reaches the same total. BODMAS is that rule: Brackets first, then Orders, then Division and Multiplication left to right, Addition and Subtraction last. Without it, one friend's total differs from another's. The order is not a school quirk - it is the worldwide agreement that an expression means exactly one value.

## DEFINITION
Evaluating an arithmetic expression requires computing its final numerical value by following a strict, universally agreed-upon sequence of operations known as BODMAS/PEMDAS (Brackets, Orders/Exponents, Division and Multiplication from left to right, Addition and Subtraction from left to right). This rule ensures that every expression yields one unambiguous, unique answer regardless of who evaluates it.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at a local stationery shop in Delhi buying notebooks and pens for school. If a packet has 3 notebooks at ₹40 each and 2 pens at ₹10 each, plus an extra delivery charge of ₹15, how do you write and calculate the total bill? You write the expression as $3 \times 40 + 2 \times 10 + 15$. In Class 6, you learned basic operations and properties of integers and fractions, but when multiple operations appear together, computing strictly from left to right can lead to the wrong total! To fix this, mathematicians established the order of operations, often remembered as BODMAS. We begin by evaluating Brackets first, then Orders (powers), followed by Division and Multiplication (evaluated from left to right), and finally Addition and Subtraction (evaluated from left to right). The key insight is that the order is not a matter of preference but a mandatory grammatical rule of mathematics that prevents ambiguity in every calculation.

**Takeaway:** BODMAS is a fixed sequence: brackets, orders, divide and multiply left to right, add and subtract last. **In the exam:** in evaluation questions, show one operation per line - the steps carry marks even when the final digit slips. **Check yourself:** evaluate the delivery-bill expression writing every intermediate line. **Why students miss it:** they multiply before division purely because of the acronym's look, forgetting the left-to-right rule makes them equal partners.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why must multiplication and division be performed before addition and subtraction when evaluating an expression like $5 + 3 \times 4$? Explain the mathematical reason.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Multiplication represents repeated addition (scaling or grouping), so by convention, scaling must be computed before combining quantities through addition. A student who says "because multiplication comes first in the alphabet" or "because it is on the left" misunderstands that order of operations reflects the hierarchical structure of mathematical quantities, where terms (products/quotients) are evaluated before being combined into sums/differences.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Evaluate the following expression step by step: $24 \div 4 + 2 \times 5 - 3$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $13$. Working: First, perform division and multiplication from left to right: $24 \div 4 = 6$ and $2 \times 5 = 10$. The expression becomes $6 + 10 - 3$. Next, perform addition and subtraction from left to right: $6 + 10 = 16$, and $16 - 3 = 13$. A student who gets $27$ likely added $4 + 2$ first or ignored left-to-right priority, exposing a fundamental failure to apply BODMAS correctly.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often evaluate expressions strictly from left to right across all operation types, ignoring the priority of multiplication and division over addition and subtraction. For example, when evaluating $10 + 5 \times 2$, a student incorrectly adds $10 + 5 = 15$ first, then multiplies by $2$ to get $30$, instead of multiplying $5 \times 2 = 10$ first and then adding $10 + 10 = 20$.
## ON_FAILURE

Write out: 'Step 1: Circle all multiplication and division chunks (e.g., $5 \times 2$). Step 2: Calculate those chunks first. Step 3: Rewrite the expression with the new values. Step 4: Now do addition and subtraction from left to right.' Practice this boxing technique with 3 mixed-operation problems.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Riya went to a book fair in Kolkata and bought 4 storybooks at ₹50 each and 3 science comics at ₹30 each. She also had to pay a fixed entry ticket of ₹20, but she received a ₹15 discount coupon on her total bill. Evaluate the expression $4 \times 50 + 3 \times 30 + 20 - 15$ to find out how much she paid in total.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given expression: $4 \times 50 + 3 \times 30 + 20 - 15$.
- step 2: Apply BODMAS by identifying multiplication operations that must be computed first: $4 \times 50$ and $3 \times 30$.
- step 3: Compute the multiplications: $4 \times 50 = 200$ and $3 \times 30 = 90$, so the expression updates to $200 + 90 + 20 - 15$.
- step 4: Perform addition and subtraction from left to right: first compute $200 + 90 = 290$, then $290 + 20 = 310$, and finally $310 - 15 = 295$.
- step 5: Verify the steps and append the correct unit (rupees): Total amount paid is ₹295.
REASONING: Multiplication takes precedence over addition and subtraction because books and comics represent clustered quantities (scaling) that must be evaluated as single terms before combining into a total bill. Operating strictly from left to right without priority would incorrectly mix up item costs with the entry fee.
ANSWER: ₹295
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Evaluate the arithmetic expression involving mixed operations and decimals: $15.5 + 4.5 \times 2 - 12 \div 4$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the expression and locate all operations: addition, multiplication, subtraction, and division.
- step 2: Mark the multiplication and division parts that have higher priority: $4.5 \times 2$ and $12 \div 4$.
- step 3: Calculate the multiplication: $4.5 \times 2 = 9.0$.
- step 4: Calculate the division: $12 \div 4 = 3$.
- step 5: Substitute these computed values back into the expression: $15.5 + 9.0 - 3$.
- step 6: Perform addition and subtraction from left to right: $15.5 + 9.0 = 24.5$, then $24.5 - 3 = 21.5$.
REASONING: Multiplication and division must be completed before addition and subtraction. By treating $4.5 \times 2$ and $12 \div 4$ as independent units, we respect the mathematical hierarchy before combining decimals and integers.
ANSWER: $21.5$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Evaluate the multi-step expression with brackets and multiple operation levels: $60 - [4 \times (3 + 2) - 10] \div 2$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the innermost grouping symbols: the parentheses $(3 + 2)$ inside the square brackets $[ \dots ]$.
- step 2: Evaluate inside the parentheses first: $3 + 2 = 5$, turning the expression into $60 - [4 \times 5 - 10] \div 2$.
- step 3: Evaluate inside the square brackets, prioritizing multiplication over subtraction: compute $4 \times 5 = 20$, yielding $60 - [20 - 10] \div 2$.
- step 4: Complete the operation inside the square brackets: $20 - 10 = 10$, reducing the expression to $60 - 10 \div 2$.
- step 5: Perform division before subtraction: $10 \div 2 = 5$, giving $60 - 5$.
- step 6: Perform the final subtraction: $60 - 5 = 55$.
REASONING: Brackets dictate the primary nesting order, moving from innermost parentheses outward. Once brackets are simplified, standard BODMAS rules apply where division precedes subtraction, ensuring a unique and mathematically rigorous result.
ANSWER: $55$
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two students, Amit and Priya, are asked to evaluate the expression $100 - 20 \times 3 + 10$. 
Amit's work: $100 - 60 + 10 \rightarrow 40 + 10 = 50$.
Priya's work: $80 \times 3 + 10 \rightarrow 240 + 10 = 250$.
Identify who is correct, explain the exact mathematical error made by the incorrect student, and provide the correct step-by-step evaluation.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and justification in order of operations
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze Amit's steps: he performed multiplication first ($20 \times 3 = 60$), then subtracted from 100 ($100 - 60 = 40$), and added 10 ($40 + 10 = 50$).
- step 2: Analyze Priya's steps: she performed subtraction first ($100 - 20 = 80$), which violates BODMAS.
- step 3: Conclude that Amit is correct because multiplication takes precedence over addition and subtraction.
- step 4: State Priya's error: she ignored operation priority and evaluated from left to right across mixed precedence levels.
ANSWER: Amit is correct. Priya's error was performing subtraction ($100 - 20$) before multiplication ($20 \times 3$). The correct evaluation yields $50$ by computing $20 \times 3 = 60$ first, then $100 - 60 + 10 = 50$.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A school science club in Chennai is organizing an exhibition. They purchase 5 chart papers at ₹12 each, 2 boxes of sketch pens at ₹45 each, and a bottle of glue for ₹25. They receive a group subsidy of ₹10, and the total cost is then shared equally among 5 club members. Write a single arithmetic expression representing this situation and evaluate it to find out how much each member must pay.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world word problems into arithmetic expressions and evaluating using BODMAS
STEPS:
- step 1: Translate each purchase into terms: chart papers cost $5 \times 12$, sketch pens cost $2 \times 45$, glue costs $25$.
- step 2: Combine total expenses and subtract the subsidy: $(5 \times 12 + 2 \times 45 + 25 - 10)$.
- step 3: Apply the division for sharing equally among 5 members by enclosing the numerator in brackets: $[(5 \times 12 + 2 \times 45 + 25) - 10] \div 5$.
- step 4: Compute multiplications inside the bracket: $5 \times 12 = 60$ and $2 \times 45 = 90$. Expression becomes $[60 + 90 + 25 - 10] \div 5$.
- step 5: Compute addition and subtraction inside the bracket: $60 + 90 = 150$, $150 + 25 = 175$, $175 - 10 = 165$.
- step 6: Perform final division: $165 \div 5 = 33$.
ANSWER: The expression is $[(5 \times 12 + 2 \times 45 + 25) - 10] \div 5$. Evaluating this gives ₹33 per member.
