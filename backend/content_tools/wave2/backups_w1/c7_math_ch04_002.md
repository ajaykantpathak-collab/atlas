## ELI5
A Delhi stationery bill - 3 notebooks, 2 pens, a 5-rupee entry fee - becomes 3x + 2y + 5, and that line is built from blocks. Addition and subtraction signs split it into terms: 3x, 2y and 5. Inside a term, the number riding in front is the coefficient, the letters are the variables, and a loner like 5 is the constant term. Invisible coefficients count too: x travels with an unwritten one. Terms are the bricks; naming them is the first skill of algebra.

## DEFINITION
An algebraic expression is made up of terms added or subtracted together, where each term consists of a numerical coefficient multiplied by one or more variables, or stands alone as a constant term.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can identify individual terms, numerical coefficients, and constant terms in any given algebraic expression, including those with negative signs and multiple variables.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the identification and separation of terms, coefficients, and constants in linear and multivariable algebraic expressions, but does NOT cover the simplification of like terms or polynomial degree classification.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are buying stationery for school in Delhi: 3 notebooks cost 3 rupees each, 2 pens cost ₹10 each, and you also paid an entry fee of ₹5. If a notebook is represented by $x$ and a pen by $y$, your total bill can be written as $3x + 2y + 5$. To make sense of this mathematical sentence, we need to break it down into its building blocks just like we separate whole numbers into digits using place value. Each distinct piece added together—$3x$, $2y$, and $5$—is called a term. The number multiplying the variable, such as 3 in $3x$, is called the coefficient, and a standalone number with no variable, like 5, is called the constant term.

**Takeaway:** terms are the sign-separated blocks of an expression; each block keeps its coefficient, its variables and its sign together. **In the exam:** when naming parts, never split a term; carry the minus sign along with its term and spot invisible coefficients of one. **Check yourself:** underline the terms in the bill expression with a ruler and circle every coefficient you find. **Why students miss it:** they call the standalone number a coefficient, or forget the hidden one riding on bare variables.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the definition of a "term" in an algebraic expression? Give a brief description.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
A term is a single mathematical expression or a part of an algebraic expression separated by plus (+) or minus (-) signs. A wrong answer usually confuses individual factors inside a multiplication expression (like $3$ and $x$ in $3x$) with separate terms, showing a failure to recognize that multiplication keeps items together as a single term.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
In the algebraic expression $-4x^2 + 7xy - 9$, why is $-9$ considered a constant term while $7$ is a coefficient and not a constant?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
$-9$ is a constant because its value never changes regardless of the value of $x$ or $y$. The number $7$ is a coefficient because it is explicitly attached as a multiplier to the variable part $xy$, changing in total value as $x$ and $y$ change. A wrong answer typically claims all numbers are coefficients or all numbers are constants, showing a lack of understanding of how variables bind to numbers through multiplication.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Identify all the terms, the coefficient of $x$, the coefficient of $y$, and the constant term in the algebraic expression: $5x - 8y + 12$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Identify terms by looking at the addition and subtraction signs: The terms are $5x$, $-8y$, and $12$.
Step 2: Find the coefficient of $x$ by looking at the number multiplying $x$: It is $5$.
Step 3: Find the coefficient of $y$ by looking at the number multiplying $y$, keeping the sign: It is $-8$.
Step 4: Identify the standalone number with no variable: The constant term is $12$.
A wrong answer usually drops the negative sign from $-8y$, writing the coefficient as $8$ instead of $-8$, which ignores the subtraction sign belonging to the term.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the expression $6ab$ and the expression $6 + a + b$. How many terms do each have, and what is the coefficient of $ab$ in each?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The expression $6ab$ has only ONE term because multiplication binds numbers and variables together, and its coefficient of $ab$ is $6$. The expression $6 + a + b$ has THREE terms ($6$, $a$, and $b$), where the coefficient of $ab$ does not apply directly, but the coefficient of $a$ is $1$ and $6$ is a constant term. A wrong answer treats $6ab$ as three separate terms or fails to see that multiplication creates a single unified term.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that in the algebraic expression $x - y + 4$, the coefficient of $x$ is $0$ because no number is written in front of it. Is this student correct? Evaluate and correct their reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The student is incorrect. When no number is explicitly written in front of a variable, the coefficient is understood to be $1$ (since $x = 1x$). Therefore, the coefficient of $x$ is $1$, and the coefficient of $y$ is $-1$. A wrong answer agrees with the student, showing a misconception that an absent visible number means a value of zero instead of the multiplicative identity one.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create an algebraic expression that has exactly four terms, contains variables $p$ and $q$, has a negative coefficient for one of its terms, and includes a constant term of $-15$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An example of a correct expression is $4p - 3pq + 7q - 15$. This expression has four distinct terms ($4p$, $-3pq$, $7q$, $-15$), contains variables $p$ and $q$, features a negative coefficient ($-3$), and includes a constant term of $-15$. A poor answer might create an expression with multiplication of terms into each other or miss the negative coefficient requirement, showing incomplete grasp of term separation rules.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently drop the negative sign when identifying coefficients of terms that follow a subtraction sign, or they confuse the constant term with coefficients. For example, in the expression $3x^2 - 5x + 7$, a student will often say the coefficient of $x$ is $5$ instead of $-5$, ignoring the minus sign that belongs to the term.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that every subtraction sign in an algebraic expression can be rewritten as adding a negative integer, linking back to integer operations. Ask them to circle each term including its preceding sign: for $3x^2 - 5x + 7$, circle $+3x^2$, $-5x$, and $+7$. Emphasize that the sign immediately to the left always belongs to that term.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Rahul goes to a local market in Jaipur and buys $x$ notebooks for ₹25 each, $y$ geometry boxes for ₹60 each, and also pays a packing charge of ₹15. Write the algebraic expression representing his total cost, and list all terms, the coefficient of $x$, the coefficient of $y$, and the constant term.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Translate the word problem into an algebraic expression by multiplying item quantities by their unit prices and adding fees: Cost = $25x + 60y + 15$.
- Step 2: Identify the terms by looking at the separation by plus signs: The terms are $25x$, $60y$, and $15$.
- Step 3: Extract the coefficient of $x$ by observing the number multiplied by $x$, which is $25$.
- Step 4: Extract the coefficient of $y$ by observing the number multiplied by $y$, which is $60$.
- Step 5: Identify the standalone number with no variable attached, which is the constant term $15$.
REASONING: Terms are separated by addition and subtraction operators. The numerical multiplier attached to a variable is its coefficient, while a number standing by itself without any variable is the constant term representing fixed costs.
ANSWER: Expression: $25x + 60y + 15$; Terms: $25x, 60y, 15$; Coefficient of $x$: $25$; Coefficient of $y$: $60$; Constant: $15$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Consider the algebraic expression $-7m^2 + 12mn - 4n - 19$. List every term along with its explicit sign, find the coefficient of $m^2$, the coefficient of $mn$, the coefficient of $n$, and the constant term.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Rewrite or view the expression by treating subtraction as adding negative terms: $-7m^2 + 12mn + (-4n) + (-19)$.
- Step 2: Identify all individual terms including their signs: $-7m^2$, $12mn$, $-4n$, and $-19$.
- Step 3: Find the coefficient of $m^2$ including its sign, which is $-7$.
- Step 4: Find the coefficient of $mn$ including its sign, which is $12$.
- Step 5: Find the coefficient of $n$ including its sign, which is $-4$, and identify the constant term as $-19$.
REASONING: Every term carries the sign immediately to its left. Omitting negative signs is the most frequent error, so explicitly linking subtraction to negative terms ensures correct coefficient identification.
ANSWER: Terms: $-7m^2, 12mn, -4n, -19$; Coefficient of $m^2$: $-7$; Coefficient of $mn$: $12$; Coefficient of $n$: $-4$; Constant: $-19$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Priya has an algebraic expression given by $14 - 3ab + 8a - 9b + 2ab^2$. Rearrange the expression in standard order of terms, identify how many terms it contains, find the coefficient of $ab$, and state the constant term.
STEPS:
- Step 1: List all the terms from the given expression with their correct signs: $14$, $-3ab$, $8a$, $-9b$, and $2ab^2$. Count them to find there are $5$ terms.
- Step 2: Locate the specific term containing the variable combination $ab$, which is $-3ab$.
- Step 3: Extract the numerical factor multiplying $ab$, taking note of its sign, giving a coefficient of $-3$.
- Step 4: Identify the constant term, which is the standalone number $14$.
- Step 5: Verify all components by checking that variables and constants are correctly categorized without mixing variable parts into constants.
REASONING: Counting terms requires acknowledging that a constant is also a term. Coefficients are strictly the numerical factors attached via multiplication to specified variables.
ANSWER: Total terms: 5 ($14, -3ab, 8a, -9b, 2ab^2$); Coefficient of $ab$: $-3$; Constant term: $14$.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A school science club makes and sells two types of science kits. Deluxe kits sold for ₹$x$ each bring in total revenue, and standard kits sold for ₹$y$ each bring in another part, plus a government grant of ₹5000. The total funds expression is given by $100x + 75y - 2000 + 5000$, where $-2000$ represents expenses. A student says the constant terms are $-2000$ and $5000$, and the coefficients of $x$ and $y$ are $100$ and $75$ respectively. Evaluate this statement, identify any errors, and state the true coefficients and constants.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction in algebraic structures
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine the given expression $100x + 75y - 2000 + 5000$.
- Step 2: Verify the student's identification of variable coefficients: $100$ multiplies $x$ and $75$ multiplies $y$, so these are correct.
- Step 3: Examine the numbers without variables: $-2000$ and $5000$ are both constant numbers.
- Step 4: Conclude whether the student is fully correct or if simplification is needed, noting that while both are constants, they can be combined into a single constant term ($3000$).
ANSWER: The student correctly identified the coefficients of $x$ and $y$ as $100$ and $75$. Both $-2000$ and $5000$ are constant terms, but they should be combined into a single net constant of $+3000$ to fully simplify the expression.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students are analyzing the algebraic expression $P = -ab + 6a - 8$. Student A says the coefficient of $ab$ is $1$. Student B says the coefficient of $ab$ is $-1$. Who is correct, and what mathematical principle explains why? 
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying mathematical reasoning
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine the term containing $ab$ in the expression, which is written as $-ab$.
- Step 2: Recall that when a variable or product of variables has no visible number written in front, an invisible factor of $1$ is implied.
- Step 3: Incorporate the negative sign directly attached to the term, making the complete multiplier $-1$.
- Step 4: Conclude that Student B is correct because $-ab$ is equivalent to $(-1)ab$.
ANSWER: Student B is correct. The term $-ab$ has an implied numerical multiplier of $1$ combined with the negative sign, making the coefficient $-1$ (since $-ab = -1 \cdot ab$). Student A missed the negative sign.
DIFFICULTY: hard
