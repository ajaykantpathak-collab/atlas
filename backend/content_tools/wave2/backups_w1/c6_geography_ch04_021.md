## ELI5
One small patch grows rice, wheat and vegetables side by side; the huge next field grows sugarcane and nothing else. The difference is the goal, not the land. Ask of any farm: who is this food for — the table or the market? A family growing a mixed basket to feed itself through the year is subsistence farming: everything raised ends up on their own plates. A farmer planting one massive crop to sell to a factory for profit is commercial farming: the market is the real customer. Same spade, same soil — but the dinner table and the market demand different plans.

## DEFINITION
Subsistence farming is an agricultural method where families grow food primarily to feed themselves, while commercial farming is a large-scale business activity focused on producing crops or animal products for profit in national or international markets.

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever wondered why some farmers in your village have a small patch of land with many different vegetables, while others have huge fields filled with only one type of crop like cotton? To understand this, look at the farmer's "goal." If a family grows rice, wheat, and pulses just to make sure they have enough dinner for the whole year, they are doing subsistence farming. If they plant a massive field of sugarcane to sell to a factory for money, they are doing commercial farming. Start by asking: "Who is this food for—the table or the market?" Once you answer that, you can see how the farmer decides what to grow and how to manage their land.

**Takeaway:** the farm's goal decides its type — food for the family's own table is subsistence farming; a single crop raised for sale in markets is commercial farming. **In the exam:** when asked to classify a farm, quote the purpose first (feeding the family versus selling to a factory) and then name the type — answers that only describe field size lose the mark. **Check yourself:** a small plot with rice, wheat and pulses mixed = subsistence; a huge sugarcane field destined for a sugar mill = commercial. **Why students miss it:** they judge by land size alone, when the definition is about who the produce is for.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that commercial farming only exists in Western countries or that it must involve modern machinery. They fail to realize that commercial farming is defined by the *intent* (selling for profit) and *scale* (large surplus), which is a common practice in many parts of India, such as sugarcane or tea farming.
## ON_FAILURE

Ask them to point to the one that would be "risky" if the market price dropped, and explain that commercial farming is tied to market prices, while subsistence farming is tied to family hunger.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A farmer named Ravi has 2 acres of land and grows rice, dal, and spinach. He keeps most of it for his house but sells 5 bags of rice at the local shop to buy seeds for next year. What type of farming is this?
STEPS:
- 1: Analyze the land size (small, 2 acres).
- 2: Identify the crop variety (diverse: rice, dal, spinach).
- 3: Determine the primary goal (family needs).
- 4: Evaluate the sale (small surplus for household supplies).
- 5: Conclude the type.
REASONING: The focus is clearly on the family's survival, and the sale is secondary (a surplus).
ANSWER: This is subsistence farming.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2

- 2: Identify the crop variety (single, specialized).
- 3: Determine the primary goal (profit).
- 4: Determine the destination (international market).
- 5: Conclude the type.
REASONING: The scale and the profit-driven goal define this as a business enterprise.
ANSWER: This is commercial farming.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A farmer shifts from growing 5 different crops (for food) to growing only 1 crop (for the market) but realizes the market price is unstable. Why is this a major change?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the shift from subsistence to commercial.
- 2: Evaluate the risk of losing variety (loss of family food supply).
- 3: Evaluate the risk of market dependence (unstable income).
- 4: Consider the scale change (likely needed for the single crop).
- 5: Conclude the impact on the family's security.
REASONING: This shows the tradeoff between self-sufficiency and market-based profit.
ANSWER: It is a change from a secure, self-sufficient lifestyle to a high-risk, profit-dependent business model.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a village has a drought, which farmer is likely to be more secure: the one practicing subsistence or the one practicing commercial? Why?
COMPETENCY: Critical Analysis
STEPS:
- 1: Analyze the resource dependency of both.
- 2: Note that subsistence farmers grow diverse crops, some of which might survive.
- 3: Note that commercial farmers depend on a single cash crop that needs specific conditions.
- 4: Compare stability.
ANSWER: The subsistence farmer is likely more secure because their diverse crops serve as an insurance policy, whereas the commercial farmer relies on one crop for their entire income, which could fail completely.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2

COMPETENCY: Conceptual Synthesis
STEPS:

- 2: Explain that in subsistence farming, surplus is an accident or extra.
- 3: Explain that in commercial farming, the "surplus" is the actual target.
- 4: Synthesize the relationship between purpose and the product.
ANSWER: Surplus is key because for a subsistence farmer, it represents "extra" food after the family is fed, whereas for a commercial farmer, the entire harvest is intended to be surplus to be sold for profit.
