## ELI5
Counting to one lakh is like climbing a staircase of ten-thousands: take ten steps of ten thousand each and you stand at 1,00,000 - the lakh. It is the doorway where five-digit numbers end and six-digit numbers begin, which is why the Indian place value chart opens a brand-new lakh column just for it. Think of exchanging ten ten-thousand notes for one bigger bundle: the money is the same, but the bundle now has its own name, and from here every big price or population is counted in lakhs.

## DEFINITION
The lakh is a unit in the Indian numbering system equal to one hundred thousand ($1,00,000$ or $10^5$), representing the threshold where 5-digit numbers transition to 6-digit numbers. Its magnitude signifies a value ten times greater than ten thousand and forms the primary grouping marker in the Indian place value system before the crore.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine saving money in your piggy bank to buy a bicycle that costs ₹1,00,000. When you count past ninety-nine thousand nine hundred ninety-nine, what number comes next, and how do we write it? To answer this, we extend our familiar place value chart past the ten-thousands column into a brand-new column called the 'lakh' place. Recall from Class 6 how each place value is ten times the value of the column immediately to its right ($10 \times 10,000 = 1,00,000$). The key insight here is that one lakh is written as $1,00,000$ with commas separating the periods according to the Indian system, marking the scale of large real-world quantities like town populations or vehicle costs.

**Takeaway:** one lakh is the six-digit threshold: ten ten-thousands bundled together into one brand-new place. **In the exam:** write 1,00,000 with the Indian comma and name the lakh column before reading the rest; magnitude questions want 'ten times ten thousand', not a zero count. **Check yourself:** say how many ten-thousands live inside one lakh, then decompose any six-digit amount into its places aloud, slowly. **Why students miss it:** they add an extra zero and leap straight to ten lakh, or read 1,00,000 digit by digit without naming the periods.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the magnitude of 1 lakh with 1 thousand. How many thousands are equal to one lakh, and what algebraic equation represents this relationship?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
One lakh is equal to 100 thousands because $1,00,000 \div 1,000 = 100$, represented algebraically as $1,000 \times x = 1,00,000$, where $x = 100$. A student who states that 1,000 thousands make a lakh is confusing the number of zeros between a thousand ($1,000$) and a lakh ($1,00,000$), failing to account for the two extra zeros.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5

A student who judges the statement as true does not understand that digit count dictates place value periods in the Indian system.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently misplace commas or miscount zeros when writing and reading numbers involving lakhs, writing numbers like $10,0,000$ or confusing $1,00,000$ with $10,000$ because they forget that a lakh requires five zeros following the digit 1 in the Indian system.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A real estate developer in Jaipur sold plots of land worth a total of ₹ $4,50,000$. Express this amount in terms of lakhs and thousands, and write out its expanded form.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given number and separate it into periods according to the Indian system: $4,50,000$.
- Step 2: Determine the value of the digits in each place: The digit 4 is in the lakh place ($4 \times 1,00,000$), and the digit 5 is in the ten-thousand place ($5 \times 10,000$).
- Step 3: Write the number in words by reading the periods: Four lakh fifty thousand.
- Step 4: Write the expanded form by summing the product of each digit and its place value: $(4 \times 1,00,000) + (5 \times 10,000) + (0 \times 1,000) + (0 \times 100) + (0 \times 10) + (0 \times 1)$.
- Step 5: Verify the total magnitude: $4,00,000 + 50,000 = 4,50,000$ rupees.
REASONING: Breaking a 6-digit number into its expanded form reinforces the magnitude of each column, showing that a lakh is ten times a ten-thousand.
ANSWER: Four lakh fifty thousand; Expanded form: $4,00,000 + 50,000$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: If a local government allocates ₹ $3,00,000$ for building a community park, and spends ₹ $1,25,000$ on plants and landscaping, how much money is left? Perform the calculation using place value alignment.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write down the initial allocation and expenditure aligned by place value columns:
  $$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l} 3,00,000 \\ - 1,25,000 \\ \hline \end{array}$$
- Step 2: Subtract the digits in the ones, tens, hundreds, and thousands columns where borrowing is necessary from the lakhs place.
- Step 3: Borrow 1 lakh ($1,00,000$) as 10 ten-thousands; reduce the 3 in the lakh place to 2, making the ten-thousand place $10$. Then borrow 1 ten-thousand for the thousands place, leaving 9 in ten-thousands and 10 in thousands.
- Step 4: Complete the subtraction column by column: 
  Lakhs: $2 - 1 = 1$, Ten-thousands: $9 - 2 = 7$, Thousands: $10 - 5 = 5$, Hundreds/Tens/Ones: $0$.
- Step 5: Combine the results with proper units and comma placement: ₹ $1,75,000$.
REASONING: Subtraction with large numbers requires precise borrowing across place value boundaries, specifically breaking 1 lakh into lower place value units.
ANSWER: ₹ $1,75,000$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A textile mill in Surat produced $6,82,400$ spools of thread over a year. If they package them in boxes of $1,000$ spools each, how many full boxes of 1,000 can they make, and how many spools are left unpacked?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the total number of spools ($6,82,400$) and the size of each box ($1,000$).
- Step 2: Set up the division or place value extraction: To find how many thousands are in $6,82,400$, look at the thousands period and all periods to its left.
- Step 3: Deconstruct the number: $6,82,400 = (682 \times 1,000) + 400$.
- Step 4: Interpret the factors: The multiplier 682 represents the number of groups of 1,000 (full boxes), and the remainder 400 represents the leftover spools.
- Step 5: Verify by multiplication and addition: $(682 \times 1,000) + 400 = 6,82,000 + 400 = 6,82,400$.
REASONING: Understanding the magnitude of a lakh allows us to quickly scale numbers up or down by powers of 10, connecting multiplication and division to place value grouping.
ANSWER: 682 boxes and 400 spools left unpacked
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A civic body reports that City A has a population of $4,08,200$ and City B has a population of $3,99,900$. A student claims that City B has a larger population because the digit 9 is larger than the digit 4. Analyze this claim, identify the misconception, and determine which city actually has a greater population with mathematical justification.
COMPETENCY: Error identification, place value comparison, and justification
STEPS:
- Step 1: Compare the number of digits in both numbers ($4,08,200$ has 6 digits; $3,99,900$ has 6 digits).
- Step 2: Examine the highest place value column (lakhs place) for both numbers: City A has 4 in the lakh place ($4,00,000$), while City B has 3 in the lakh place ($3,00,000$).
- Step 3: Conclude that $4,00,000 > 3,00,000$, making City A larger regardless of digits in lower place values.
- Step 4: Address the student's error: The student looked at individual digits from right to left or fixated on a single large digit (9) instead of comparing place value columns from left to right.
ANSWER: City A has a greater population. The student's claim is incorrect because place value comparison must always begin from the highest place value (leftmost digit), where 4 lakh is greater than 3 lakh.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: An environmental NGO plants saplings across a state. They set a target of 10 lakh saplings. In the first phase, they plant $4,50,000$ saplings. In the second phase, they plant $3,25,000$ saplings. 
(a) How many total saplings have been planted so far? 
(b) How many more saplings are needed to reach the target of 10 lakh? Express your final answers in both standard numeral form and words using the Indian numbering system.
COMPETENCY: Multi-step problem solving, addition/subtraction with large numbers, and translation to words
STEPS:
- Step 1: Add the saplings planted in phase 1 and phase 2: $4,50,000 + 3,25,000 = 7,75,000$.
- Step 2: Translate the target of "10 lakh" into standard numeral form: $10,00,000$.
- Step 3: Subtract the total planted so far from the target to find the remaining amount: $10,00,000 - 7,75,000$.
- Step 4: Perform the subtraction with proper borrowing across lakhs and ten-thousands: $2,25,000$.
- Step 5: Convert both results into words: $7,75,000$ is Seven lakh seventy-five thousand; $2,25,000$ is Two lakh twenty-five thousand.
ANSWER: (a) $7,75,000$ (Seven lakh seventy-five thousand saplings); (b) $2,25,000$ (Two lakh twenty-five thousand more saplings needed).
DIFFICULTY: hard
