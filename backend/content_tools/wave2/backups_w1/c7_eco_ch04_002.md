## ELI5
A bank is a busy bridge between two crowds: people with extra money and people who need some. Depositors park their savings for safety and a small interest; the bank lends those very rupees to a borrower - an uncle expanding his workshop - at a higher interest. The bank lives on the spread between the two rates. Your deposit does not sleep in a vault; it crosses the bridge to work.

## DEFINITION
A bank is a financial institution licensed to accept deposits from the public and create credit by lending money, acting as a vital bridge between savers and borrowers in the economy.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine Aarav's family saving money from their small shop in Jaipur and depositing it in a neighborhood bank branch for safekeeping and interest. When Aarav's uncle needs a loan to expand his carpentry workshop, he approaches the same bank, which lends him funds pooled from various depositors. Building on Class 6 concepts of money as a store of value, we now examine how institutions manage this pooled money systematically. We begin by observing how banks transform idle savings into productive investments. Finally, we connect these activities to the broader financial infrastructure that keeps the national economy moving.

**Takeaway:** a bank is a licensed bridge between savers and borrowers: it accepts deposits, pays interest, lends at higher interest, and lives on the spread. **In the exam:** in classification questions, sort the bank by who owns it and whom it serves - commercial, cooperative or central. **Check yourself:** walk the walkthrough's Jaipur family deposit across the bridge to the uncle's carpentry loan. **Why students miss it:** students see a vault, not a bridge - every parked deposit is a yes to a borrower somewhere; the spread is the toll. Trace the deposit's journey aloud - saver, teller, ledger, borrower - and the bridge explains itself. Each rate quoted is a yes to one side and a no to the other.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A local commercial bank in Pune has total deposits worth ₹10,00,000 collected from households, paying them 4% annual interest. It lends ₹8,00,000 of this amount to local businesses at 10% annual interest. What is the bank's net annual interest income earned from this transaction?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps: 1. Calculate interest paid to depositors: 4% of ₹10,00,000 = ₹40,000. 2. Calculate interest earned from borrowers: 10% of ₹8,00,000 = ₹80,000. 3. Subtract interest paid from interest earned: ₹80,000 - ₹40,000 = ₹40,000. Answer: ₹40,000. A wrong answer that calculates interest on the full ₹10,00,000 for both sides fails to understand that banks keep a portion as reserves and do not lend out 100% of deposits.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A commercial bank in Chennai receives ₹50,00,000 in total savings deposits from local households, paying an annual interest rate of 3%. The bank retains 20% as cash reserves and lends out the remaining 80% to small business owners at an annual interest rate of 9%. Calculate the bank's total annual interest income from loans, total annual interest expense paid to depositors, and its net interest margin (spread).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify total deposits (₹50,00,000), deposit interest rate (3%), reserve ratio (20%), loan ratio (80%), and loan interest rate (9%).
- Step 2: Calculate total amount lent out = 80% of ₹50,00,000 = 0.80 × 50,00,000 = ₹40,00,000.
- Step 3: Calculate annual interest income earned from loans = 9% of ₹40,00,000 = 0.09 × 40,00,000 = ₹3,60,000.
- Step 4: Calculate annual interest expense paid to depositors = 3% of ₹50,00,000 = 0.03 × 50,00,000 = ₹1,50,000.
- Step 5: Calculate net annual interest income = ₹3,60,000 - ₹1,50,000 = ₹2,10,000.
REASONING: Banks operate by borrowing money from depositors at a lower cost and lending it to borrowers at a higher cost. By calculating interest on the exact amounts mobilized and lent, we determine the core operating profit of financial intermediation.
ANSWER: Net interest income is ₹2,10,000.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A regional cooperative bank in Punjab has 1,000 farmer members. Each member deposits ₹10,000 into the cooperative pool, giving a total deposit base of ₹1,00,00,000. The cooperative pays 4% interest on deposits. It extends agricultural equipment loans totaling ₹80,00,000 at 7% interest. Find the cooperative's gross surplus (net interest income) and determine how much surplus remains after accounting for ₹1,00,000 in administrative operating expenses.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify deposit pool (₹1,00,00,000), deposit interest rate (4%), loan volume (₹80,00,000), loan interest rate (7%), and operating costs (₹1,00,000).
- Step 2: Calculate interest paid on deposits = 4% of ₹1,00,00,000 = ₹4,00,000.
- Step 3: Calculate interest earned on loans = 7% of ₹80,00,000 = ₹5,60,000.
- Step 4: Calculate gross surplus (net interest income) = ₹5,60,000 - ₹4,00,000 = ₹1,60,000.
- Step 5: Subtract administrative expenses from gross surplus = ₹1,60,000 - ₹1,00,000 = ₹60,000 net surplus.
REASONING: Cooperative banks must cover both interest expenses to savers and operational running costs from their loan earnings, with any remaining surplus often reinvested for member welfare.
ANSWER: Net surplus remaining is ₹60,000.
