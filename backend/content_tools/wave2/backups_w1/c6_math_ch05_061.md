Price times count buys any number of notebooks; fix the total and the letter solves for the missing friend. Instead of guessing case by case, write the rule once and let the letter carry every possibility.

## DEFINITION
Algebra uses letters (like $x, y, n$) as placeholders for unknown numbers, allowing us to write general rules or "recipes" for solving real-life problems. By representing unknown quantities with variables, we can create equations to find specific values that fit our constraints.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are buying notebooks for your friends; if each notebook costs 20 rupees, how do you find the total for any number of friends? You begin by choosing a letter, such as '$n$', to stand for the unknown number of friends. You then build your "rule": $20 \times n$ (or $20n$) is the cost for $n$ friends. If you have 100 rupees, you can set up the equation $20n = 100$ to discover that $n = 5$ friends can be treated. The key insight is that algebra is just a shorthand way to write down relationships between things we know and things we don't.

**Takeaway:** a letter stands for the unknown, rules are built around it, and simple equations are undone by inverse operations to name its value. **In the exam:** in word problems, choose the letter, build the rule, and solve the resulting equation with inverse moves. **Check yourself:** price the walkthrough's notebooks - one letter, one fixed rate, and the rule covers any headcount. **Why students miss it:** students guess numbers one case at a time - the walkthrough's rule card is a no to that; the letter solves every case at once. ## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A taxi charges a fixed 20 rupees plus 12 rupees for every additional kilometer. Write an expression for the total cost for $x$ kilometers (where $x > 1$).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The expression is $20 + 12(x-1)$ or $12x + 8$. A student who writes $20 + 12x$ fails to account for the logic of the "first kilometer" already being paid for within the base fee or the specific condition provided in the text.

**A wrong answer (charging every kilometre the full fare) reveals the student missed the included first stretch - the base fee already pays the opening leg, and the expression must respect that.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why do we use letters like $x$ or $y$ instead of just guessing numbers? Compare this to the trial-and-error method.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Letters allow us to solve for any situation at once, whereas trial-and-error is slow and only works for one specific case. If a student says "it's just a rule to make math harder," they have not yet grasped that algebra is a tool for efficiency and generalization.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
If $3x = 300$, can you justify why we divide 300 by 3 to find $x$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
We divide because the equation says "3 times $x$ is 300," so to find one $x$, we must undo the multiplication. A student who tries to subtract 3 instead of dividing fails to understand inverse operations, which is the foundational "balancing" logic of equations.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often treat variables as specific objects (e.g., thinking "$x$" stands for "apple") rather than as numbers. This leads to errors like saying $3x + 2 = 5x$ because they think "$x$" is a label. They fail to realize that $x$ is a number that, once found, makes the mathematical statement true.

Once they see it results in a number (30), show them that the letter is just a placeholder for that value. Use physical counters or blocks to represent the unknown, physically balancing them against the known total to reinforce the concept of equality.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A tailor uses 3 meters of cloth for one shirt. If he has a total of 75 meters of cloth, how many shirts ($s$) can he make?
STEPS:
- Identify: 3 meters per shirt, 75 total meters. Find $s$.
- Formula: $3 \times s = 75$.
- Method: Divide both sides by 3 to isolate $s$.
- Compute: $s = 75 / 3 = 25$.
- Interpret: The tailor can make 25 shirts.
REASONING: We use multiplication to represent the total cloth used for $s$ shirts, and division to undo that relationship to find the count of shirts.
ANSWER: 25 shirts.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A bus ticket costs 10 rupees. If you spent 150 rupees in total on tickets for your family ($f$), how many people are in the family?
STEPS:
- Identify: 10 rupees per person, 150 total spent. Find $f$.
- Formula: $10 \times f = 150$.
- Method: Divide total spent by cost per ticket.
- Compute: $f = 150 / 10 = 15$.
- Verify: 15 people $\times$ 10 rupees = 150 rupees.
REASONING: This follows the $rate \times quantity = total$ structure, common in everyday financial transactions.
ANSWER: 15 people.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A farmer packs 5 kg of apples in every box. He has 200 kg of apples, but he keeps 20 kg for his family first. How many boxes ($b$) can he pack with the rest?
STEPS:
- Identify: Total 200 kg, reserve 20 kg, pack 5 kg per box.
- Subtract reserve: $200 - 20 = 180$ kg available for boxes.
- Formula: $5 \times b = 180$.
- Divide: $b = 180 / 5$.
- Compute: $b = 36$.
REASONING: We must first resolve the constant (the reserved apples) before we can establish the algebraic relationship for the boxes.
ANSWER: 36 boxes.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Your friend says that if 4 notebooks cost 80 rupees, then 8 notebooks will cost 160 rupees. Can you write an algebraic rule for the cost ($C$) based on the number of notebooks ($n$)? How do you know the rule works?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions.
STEPS:
- Identify rate: $80 / 4 = 20$ rupees per notebook.
- Formulate: $C = 20 \times n$.
- Justification: Test with $n=4$: $20 \times 4 = 80$. Test with $n=8$: $20 \times 8 = 160$.
ANSWER: The rule is $C = 20n$. It works because it maintains a constant rate of 20 rupees per notebook regardless of the quantity chosen.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: You have two different taxi services. Service A charges 50 rupees fixed plus 10 rupees per km. Service B charges only 15 rupees per km. For how many kilometers ($k$) would both services cost the same?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying using algebraic equations.
STEPS:
- Service A cost: $50 + 10k$.
- Service B cost: $15k$.
- Set equal: $50 + 10k = 15k$.
- Solve: $50 = 5k$, so $k = 10$.
ANSWER: They cost the same at 10 km. At 10 km, Service A is $50 + 100 = 150$ and Service B is $15 \times 10 = 150$.
DIFFICULTY: hard
