## ELI5
Buy 3 notebooks at 25 rupees and 2 pens at 10 rupees, and your bill 3 x 25 + 2 x 10 is an arithmetic expression - a sentence of numbers and signs that holds one value. Addition and subtraction signs are the weak joints: they split the expression into terms, while multiplication binds its numbers into one inseparable chunk. So the bill has terms 3 x 25 and 2 x 10 - like rice and dal served on one thali, separate items until you total them.

## DEFINITION
An arithmetic expression is a combination of numbers and operational symbols (+, -, ×, ÷) that represents a single numerical value, where individual parts separated by addition or subtraction signs are called terms.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you go to a stationery shop in your neighborhood and buy 3 notebooks for ₹25 each and 2 pens for ₹10 each. When you write down the total cost as $3 \times 25 + 2 \times 10$, you have created an arithmetic expression. In Class 6, you learned how to perform individual operations on whole numbers, integers, and fractions. Now, we group these operations together into a single mathematical phrase called an expression. Each separate chunk being added or subtracted is called a *term*. The key insight is learning to look at a complex mathematical sentence not just as a long string of numbers, but as structured parts called terms that can be evaluated to find a final value.

**Takeaway:** terms are the plus-separated building blocks of an expression; multiplication and division glue their numbers tightly into one single block. **In the exam:** when asked for terms, split only at plus and minus signs, then evaluate each block with BODMAS before totalling. **Check yourself:** box the terms in the shop-bill expression with your pencil before you evaluate anything at all. **Why students miss it:** they split inside products, calling 3 and 25 separate terms, counting operation signs instead of blocks.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is 3 terms ($15$, $4 \times 3$, and $8$). A wrong answer usually shows that the student is counting every number or every operation sign individually rather than recognizing that multiplication binds numbers together into a single term.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct answer is that multiplication and division symbols bind numbers tightly together, forming a single indivisible product that acts as one component before addition and subtraction take place. A wrong answer suggests the student thinks every number separated by *any* symbol is a separate term, confusing operational hierarchy.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Identify the terms in the arithmetic expression $50 - 12 \div 4 + 6 \times 2$ and evaluate the value of the second term.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The expression has three terms: $50$, $12 \div 4$, and $6 \times 2$. The second term is $12 \div 4$, which evaluates to $3$. A wrong answer often comes from failing to treat $12 \div 4$ as a single operational block, or miscalculating division using basic multiplication facts.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the expressions $E_1 = 40 - 5 \times 6$ and $E_2 = (40 - 5) \times 6$. Explain why their terms and final values are completely different.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
In $E_1$, the terms are $40$ and $5 \times 6$, giving $40 - 30 = 10$. In $E_2$, the parentheses alter the structure entirely, making $(40 - 5)$ a single quantity multiplied by $6$, yielding $35 \times 6 = 210$. A wrong answer indicates the student ignores grouping symbols and assumes parentheses do not change term structure.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student evaluates the expression $24 + 8 \div 4 \times 2$ by calculating the first term as $24$, the second term as $8 \div 4 = 2$, and then multiplying by $2$ to get $4$. Find the error in this interpretation of terms and operations.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The student correctly identified the terms as $24$ and $(8 \div 4 \times 2)$, but inside the second term, they must follow left-to-right priority for multiplication and division ($8 \div 4 = 2$, then $2 \times 2 = 4$), making the terms $24$ and $4$, which sum to $28$. A wrong answer shows the student does not understand that multi-operation terms require strict operational rules within themselves.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Construct an arithmetic expression that has exactly four terms, involves addition, subtraction, and multiplication, and evaluates to a total value of 50.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A valid constructed expression is $10 + 20 + 3 \times 5 + 5$. Checking the terms: $10$, $20$, $3 \times 5$ (which is $15$), and $5$. Their sum is $10 + 20 + 15 + 5 = 50$. A poor answer creates an expression where terms are incorrectly bounded or whose evaluation does not equal 50.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often split an expression at every single operation symbol (+, -, ×, ÷), treating every number as its own term. For example, in $20 - 4 \times 3$, a struggling student might mistakenly think there are three terms ($20$, $4$, and $3$) and try to subtract before multiplying.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that addition (+) and subtraction (-) signs act like walls that separate an expression into independent terms. Multiplication (×) and division (÷) symbols act like glue that keeps numbers tightly bonded inside the same term. Show them how to circle multiplication and division chunks first before looking at the addition and subtraction walls.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Riya went to the market and bought 4 notebooks for ₹30 each and 3 pens for ₹15 each, but received a discount of ₹20 on her total bill. Write the arithmetic expression representing this situation, identify its terms, and calculate the final value.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given quantities and translate them into numbers and operations: 4 notebooks at ₹30 each is written as $4 \times 30$, 3 pens at ₹15 each is written as $3 \times 15$, and a discount of ₹20 is represented by subtracting 20.
- Step 2: Combine these into a single arithmetic expression: $4 \times 30 + 3 \times 15 - 20$.
- Step 3: Identify the individual terms separated by addition and subtraction signs: Term 1 is $4 \times 30$, Term 2 is $3 \times 15$, and Term 3 is $20$.
- Step 4: Evaluate each term independently: Term 1 = $120$, Term 2 = $45$, Term 3 = $20$.
- Step 5: Perform the addition and subtraction on the evaluated terms: $120 + 45 - 20 = 165 - 20 = 145$.
REASONING: Multiplication binds the quantity and price together into single terms ($4 \times 30$ and $3 \times 15$), while '+' and '-' separate the expression into distinct financial components. Evaluating terms first before combining ensures accurate calculation of the final bill.
ANSWER: ₹145
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An aquarium has 5 large tanks with 12 fish in each tank, and 3 small tanks with 8 fish in each tank. Later, 6 fish are given away. Express this as an arithmetic expression, list all its terms, and find the total number of fish remaining.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Translate the word problem into mathematical operations: 5 tanks of 12 fish is $5 \times 12$, 3 tanks of 8 fish is $3 \times 8$, and giving away 6 fish is $- 6$.
- Step 2: Write the complete arithmetic expression: $5 \times 12 + 3 \times 8 - 6$.
- Step 3: Identify the terms by looking at the addition and subtraction separators: The terms are $5 \times 12$, $3 \times 8$, and $6$.
- Step 4: Compute the value of each term using multiplication facts: $5 \times 12 = 60$, $3 \times 8 = 24$, and the third term remains $6$.
- Step 5: Substitute the evaluated terms back into the expression and compute: $60 + 24 - 6 = 84 - 6 = 78$.
REASONING: Breaking the word problem into distinct terms allows us to calculate subgroups of items separately before combining them via addition or subtraction.
ANSWER: 78 fish
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Amit has ₹500. He buys 2 storybooks for ₹120 each and 4 sketch pens for ₹25 each. He then finds ₹50 on the road. Write the complete expression for his final amount of money, identify all terms, and compute how much money he has now.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the starting amount and all subsequent transactions: Starting amount is $500$, spending on storybooks is $- 2 \times 120$, spending on sketch pens is $- 4 \times 25$, and finding money is $+ 50$.
- Step 2: Formulate the arithmetic expression combining all parts: $500 - 2 \times 120 - 4 \times 25 + 50$.
- Step 3: Identify all terms separated by '+' and '-' signs: Term 1 is $500$, Term 2 is $2 \times 120$, Term 3 is $4 \times 25$, and Term 4 is $50$.
- Step 4: Evaluate each term: Term 1 = $500$, Term 2 = $240$, Term 3 = $100$, Term 4 = $50$.
- Step 5: Substitute and compute step-by-step from left to right: $500 - 240 - 100 + 50 = 260 - 100 + 50 = 160 + 50 = 210$.
REASONING: Money transactions involve both additions (starting cash, found cash) and subtractions (purchases), where each purchase is represented as a product term. Computing each term first ensures proper tracking of finances.
ANSWER: ₹210
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Consider the arithmetic expression $100 - 10 \times 5 + 20 \div 2$. A student claims that because there are four numbers, there must be four terms: $100$, $10$, $5$, and $20 \div 2$. Is this correct? Identify all actual terms in this expression and compute its final value with full reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Term identification and structural analysis of arithmetic expressions
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the student's claim by checking where addition and subtraction signs are located.
- Step 2: Recognize that multiplication and division bind numbers together into single terms, so $10 \times 5$ is one single term and $20 \div 2$ is another single term.
- Step 3: List the correct terms: $100$, $10 \times 5$, and $20 \div 2$.
- Step 4: Evaluate each term: $100$, $50$, and $10$.
- Step 5: Combine the evaluated terms using the operations: $100 - 50 + 10 = 60$.
ANSWER: The student is incorrect. The expression has 3 terms, not 4, because $10 \times 5$ forms a single product term. The correct terms are $100$, $50$, and $10$, giving a final value of $60$.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students write expressions for the following problem: "A baker makes 50 cupcakes. He sells 12 packs of 3 cupcakes each, and then bakes 15 more." Student A writes: $50 - 12 \times 3 + 15$. Student B writes: $(50 - 12) \times 3 + 15$. Analyze both expressions by identifying their terms and determining which student correctly models the real-world scenario.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations and analyzing term structure
STEPS:
- Step 1: Break down the word problem into actions: Start with 50 cupcakes, subtract the total sold cupcakes ($12 \text{ packs} \times 3 \text{ cupcakes}$), and add 15 newly baked cupcakes.
- Step 2: Analyze Student A's expression ($50 - 12 \times 3 + 15$): Terms are $50$, $12 \times 3$ ($= 36$), and $15$. This correctly isolates the sold quantity as a single term.
- Step 3: Analyze Student B's expression ($(50 - 12) \times 3 + 15$): The parentheses group $50 - 12$ together before multiplying by 3, which incorrectly implies he sold packs from a remaining difference rather than 12 independent packs of 3.
- Step 4: Evaluate Student A's expression: $50 - 36 + 15 = 14 + 15 = 29$.
- Step 5: Conclude which model is correct based on real-world logic.
ANSWER: Student A correctly models the problem. Student A's expression has terms $50$, $36$, and $15$, evaluating to $29$. Student B's parentheses change the term structure entirely and misrepresent the number of cupcakes sold.
DIFFICULTY: hard
