## ELI5
Cut three glass panels identical in every way for a window, and the frame that fits them is an equilateral triangle: all three sides equal, all three angles equal at exactly 60 degrees apiece. Equality of sides forces equality of angles - the triangle's full inner quota split three fair ways. Given one side length with a ruler and compass, the other two sides come from a single compass setting, two arcs and one crossing point. It is fairness you can verify with a single measurement.

## DEFINITION
An equilateral triangle is a special three-sided polygon in which all three sides are equal in length and all three interior angles are equal, measuring exactly 60° each.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can construct an equilateral triangle using a ruler and compass when given a single side length, and can apply the properties of equal sides and equal 60° angles to solve multi-step geometric problems involving perimeter and unknown angles.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the definition, properties (equal sides and 60° interior angles), and compass-and-ruler construction of equilateral triangles. Does NOT cover congruence proofs, area formulas using trigonometry, or circumcircles/incircles of triangles.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are an architect designing a stained-glass window for a school auditorium in Delhi, and you need to cut three glass panels that are identical in every way, forming a perfectly balanced triangle where every corner and edge matches. To begin, you must recall that triangles have three sides and three angles, but an equilateral triangle is unique because its symmetry means all boundary lengths are identical. We start by drawing a base line segment using a ruler, just like measuring cloth with a metre tape. Then, we use a compass opened to the exact length of that base to swing intersecting arcs from both endpoints. The key insight is that the intersection point of these arcs is equidistant from both ends, guaranteeing that all three sides are equal and each interior angle automatically measures 60°.

**Takeaway:** equilateral means all three sides equal and all three angles equal, each a sixth of a full turn. **In the exam:** for perimeter, multiply the side by three; for construction, one compass width serves both arcs. **Check yourself:** check any claimed equilateral with one side comparison and one angle measurement. **Why students miss it:** they accept unequal-side claims with equal angles, forgetting the two equalities lock together.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the measure of each interior angle in an equilateral triangle?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Each interior angle in an equilateral triangle is 60°. If a student answers 90° or 180°, it reveals a confusion with right angles or the sum of all angles in a triangle rather than recognizing that equal sides force the three angles to be equal and sum to 180° ($180^\circ \div 3 = 60^\circ$).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you are given only the length of one side of a triangle as 7 cm, and you are told it is an equilateral triangle, what are the lengths of the other two sides? Explain why.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The other two sides are also 7 cm each because by definition, an equilateral triangle has all three sides equal in length. If a student states the other sides can be any random length, they do not understand the defining characteristic property of equilateral triangles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
An artisan builds a decorative metal frame in the shape of an equilateral triangle. If one side of the frame measures 12.5 cm, what is the total length of metal wire required to make the entire frame?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Identify that an equilateral triangle has 3 sides of equal length.  
Step 2: Note that the given side length $s = 12.5\text{ cm}$.  
Step 3: Set up the perimeter formula for an equilateral triangle: $\text{Perimeter} = 3 \times s$.  
Step 4: Substitute the value: $\text{Perimeter} = 3 \times 12.5\text{ cm} = 37.5\text{ cm}$.  
Answer: 37.5 cm. If a student answers 25 cm, they likely added only two sides (mistaking it for a different shape) or multiplied incorrectly.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare an isosceles triangle and an equilateral triangle. Can every equilateral triangle be classified as an isosceles triangle? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Yes, every equilateral triangle is an isosceles triangle because an isosceles triangle requires at least two sides to be equal, and an equilateral triangle has three equal sides (which satisfies the condition of having at least two equal sides). If a student says no, they mistakenly believe that "isosceles" means "exactly two equal sides" rather than "at least two equal sides."
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that they constructed an equilateral triangle where the side lengths are 5 cm, 5 cm, and 7 cm, and the interior angles are 60°, 60°, and 60°. Evaluate this claim and identify the error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The claim is false and geometrically impossible. While the angles sum to 180°, the side lengths given (5 cm, 5 cm, 7 cm) form an isosceles triangle, not an equilateral triangle. In a true equilateral triangle, all three sides must be equal in length (e.g., 5 cm, 5 cm, 5 cm). If a student believes this is possible, they are separating angle properties from side properties without checking consistency.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that if they draw three lines of any random length that meet at three corners, they can force them to form an equilateral triangle by adjusting the angles to 60° without checking if the compass radius matches the base line segment during construction. For example, when constructing a triangle with a base of 6 cm, students might mistakenly set their compass to 5 cm and draw arcs, resulting in an isosceles or scalene triangle instead of an equilateral one.
## ON_FAILURE
Guide the student back to the definition of compass construction. Say: "Remember that the compass radius represents the side length of your triangle. If your base line segment is AB = 6 cm, your compass must be opened to exactly 6 cm from the scale before you place the pointer at A and swing an arc, and you must keep that exact same compass width when swinging from B." Have them physically remeasure the compass opening with a ruler to verify it matches the base.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Priya wants to make a triangular banner for her school sports day in the shape of an equilateral triangle. If each side of the banner is 45 cm long, calculate the total length of lace required to decorate the entire outer edge of the banner.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given information. The shape is an equilateral triangle, so all 3 sides are equal in length, and side $s = 45\text{ cm}$.
- Step 2: Identify what needs to be calculated. The total outer edge length, which is the perimeter of the triangle.
- Step 3: Write down the formula for the perimeter of an equilateral triangle: $\text{Perimeter} = 3 \times s$.
- Step 4: Substitute the given value into the formula: $\text{Perimeter} = 3 \times 45$.
- Step 5: Compute the final product: $3 \times 45 = 135\text{ cm}$, and verify units.
REASONING: Since an equilateral triangle has three identical sides, multiplying the length of one side by 3 gives the total distance around the triangle (perimeter), which tells Priya exactly how much lace to buy.
ANSWER: 135 cm
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An engineering student constructs a metal plate in the shape of an equilateral triangle. The total perimeter of the plate is measured to be 84 cm. What is the length of each individual side of the plate, and what is the measure of each of its interior angles?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given information: $\text{Perimeter} = 84\text{ cm}$ and the shape is an equilateral triangle.
- Step 2: Recall the relationship between perimeter and side length: $\text{Perimeter} = 3 \times s$, where $s$ is the side length.
- Step 3: Set up an algebraic equation to solve for $s$: $3 \times s = 84$.
- Step 4: Divide both sides by 3 to isolate $s$: $s = 84 \div 3 = 28\text{ cm}$.
- Step 5: State the angle property: In any equilateral triangle, all interior angles are equal to 60° by definition.
REASONING: Dividing the total perimeter equally among the 3 identical sides gives the length of each side, and knowing it is equilateral immediately determines that every interior angle is 60°.
ANSWER: Side length = 28 cm, Each interior angle = 60°
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A triangular park in a housing society is equilateral in shape. A gardener walks around the park 5 times during his morning exercise. If the total distance covered by the gardener in those 5 rounds is 600 metres, find the length of one side of the park and state the measure of one of its corners (angles).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify total distance for 5 rounds = 600 m.
- Step 2: Calculate the distance for 1 round (which is the perimeter of the park): $\text{Perimeter} = 600 \div 5 = 120\text{ metres}$.
- Step 3: Relate perimeter to side length $s$ of the equilateral triangle: $\text{Perimeter} = 3 \times s = 120$.
- Step 4: Solve for $s$ by dividing by 3: $s = 120 \div 3 = 40\text{ metres}$.
- Step 5: Recall the angle property of equilateral triangles: every interior angle is 60°.
REASONING: We first break down the total multi-round distance into a single perimeter using division, then use the 3-equal-sides property to find the side length, and finally apply the defining 60° angle property.
ANSWER: Side length = 40 metres, Interior angle = 60°
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Rahul attempts to draw an equilateral triangle with side length 6 cm. He draws a base line segment $XY = 6\text{ cm}$. Then, from point $X$, he uses his compass opened to 6 cm to draw an arc. From point $Y$, he uses his compass opened to 5 cm to draw an intersecting arc at point $Z$. Will triangle $XYZ$ be an equilateral triangle? Justify your answer with mathematical reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and geometric justification
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the given construction steps and side lengths of triangle $XYZ$: $XY = 6\text{ cm}$, $XZ = 6\text{ cm}$ (from arc radius), and $YZ = 5\text{ cm}$ (from arc radius).
- Step 2: Recall the definition of an equilateral triangle: all three sides must be equal in length ($XY = YZ = ZX$).
- Step 3: Compare the constructed side lengths: $6\text{ cm}, 6\text{ cm}, 5\text{ cm}$. Since not all sides are equal (one side is 5 cm instead of 6 cm), the condition for an equilateral triangle is violated.
- Step 4: Conclude whether the triangle is equilateral and state the correct classification (it is an isosceles triangle).
ANSWER: No, triangle $XYZ$ will not be an equilateral triangle. For a triangle to be equilateral, all three sides must be equal in length. Because Rahul set his compass to 5 cm for the second arc, the side $YZ$ is 5 cm while the other two sides are 6 cm, making it an isosceles triangle instead.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two metallic wires of equal length, 90 cm each, are bent to form two different shapes: Wire A is bent into a regular hexagon (6 equal sides), and Wire B is bent into an equilateral triangle (3 equal sides). Which shape has a longer individual side length, and by how many centimetres? Show all calculations.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying geometric measurements
STEPS:
- Step 1: Find the side length of Wire A (regular hexagon with 6 equal sides): $\text{Side}_A = 90\text{ cm} \div 6 = 15\text{ cm}$.
- Step 2: Find the side length of Wire B (equilateral triangle with 3 equal sides): $\text{Side}_B = 90\text{ cm} \div 3 = 30\text{ cm}$.
- Step 3: Compare the two side lengths: $30\text{ cm}$ (triangle) versus $15\text{ cm}$ (hexagon).
- Step 4: Calculate the difference between the side lengths: $30\text{ cm} - 15\text{ cm} = 15\text{ cm}$.
- Step 5: Conclude which shape has a longer side and state the difference with proper units.
ANSWER: Wire B (bent into the equilateral triangle) has a longer individual side length. Its side is 30 cm, whereas the hexagon's side is 15 cm. The difference in side length is $30\text{ cm} - 15\text{ cm} = 15\text{ cm}$.
DIFFICULTY: hard
