## ELI5
One bank, three doors. The savings door is for households: park money, earn a little interest, withdraw when needed. The current door is for traders: dozens of payments a day, no interest, overdraft when the month runs long. The fixed-deposit door is for patient money: locked for a promised stretch, rewarded with the best interest. Choosing a door is a quiet trade - access given up is interest gained.

## DEFINITION
Banks offer different types of accounts—Savings, Current, and Fixed Deposit—tailored to meet the unique financial needs of individuals, traders, and long-term savers. Savings accounts encourage personal thrift with easy withdrawals, Current accounts facilitate frequent business transactions with no interest, and Fixed Deposits lock in funds for a set period to earn higher interest.

**Takeaway:** bank accounts are three doors: savings for households, current for traders, fixed deposit for patient money - each trading access against interest. **In the exam:** in selection questions, match the door to the user's rhythm - how often they transact and how long the money can wait. **Check yourself:** sort the walkthrough's family - mother's savings, uncle's current, grandfather's fixed deposit - and defend each choice. **Why students miss it:** every door is a yes and a no at once: the current account says no to interest, the fixed deposit says no to access - students miss the trade.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Priya has ₹50,000 in her savings account earning 3% annual interest. If she leaves this amount untouched for an entire year with no deposits or withdrawals, what is her total balance at the end of the year?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given data: Principal $P = ₹50,000$, Annual Interest Rate $R = 3\%$, Time $T = 1\text{ year}$.
- Step 2: Recall the formula for simple interest: $\text{Interest} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$.
- Step 3: Substitute the numbers into the formula: $\text{Interest} = \frac{50000 \times 3 \times 1}{100}$.
- Step 4: Calculate the interest amount: $\frac{150000}{100} = ₹1,500$.
- Step 5: Add the interest to the principal to find the final balance: $50,000 + 1,500 = ₹51,500$.
REASONING: Savings accounts reward individuals for keeping surplus cash in the banking system by paying a modest interest. By calculating 3% of the principal and adding it back, we determine the exact year-end wealth growth.
ANSWER: ₹51,500
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A local textile trader named Rajesh deposits ₹2,00,000 into a Current Account for his shop. At the end of one month, after making 45 business payments and receiving 60 supplier deposits, what is the interest earned on his account balance?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the account type: Current Account used by a trader for frequent business transactions.
- Step 2: Recall the key rule of Current Accounts: they are designed for high liquidity and frequent transactions, and they offer zero interest to the account holder.
- Step 3: Note the transaction count (45 payments, 60 deposits) which confirms it meets business utility requirements without penalty.
- Step 4: Apply the interest rule for Current Accounts: Annual Interest Rate = 0%.
- Step 5: Calculate the final interest earned: ₹2,00,000 $\times 0\% = ₹0$.
REASONING: Current accounts provide high utility through unlimited transactions and overdraft facilities, but banks do not pay interest on these balances because the funds are highly volatile and can be withdrawn at any second.
ANSWER: ₹0 (Zero interest)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Sunita is deciding where to put her savings of ₹1,00,000. Option A is a Savings Account paying 3% interest per year with instant withdrawal. Option B is a 2-year Fixed Deposit paying 7% interest per year with a penalty for premature withdrawal. If Sunita does not need this money for the next 2 years, calculate the total interest earned under Option B and compare it to Option A over the same 2-year period.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate Option A (Savings Account) interest for 2 years: $\text{Interest} = \frac{100000 \times 3 \times 2}{100} = ₹6,000$.
- Step 2: Calculate Option B (Fixed Deposit) interest for 2 years using simple interest: $\text{Interest} = \frac{100000 \times 7 \times 2}{100} = ₹14,000$.
- Step 3: Find the difference in earnings between the two options: $14,000 - 6,000 = ₹8,000$.
- Step 4: Analyze the trade-off: Option B earns ₹8,000 more in interest because Sunita sacrifices liquidity (ability to withdraw anytime without penalty) for 2 years.
- Step 5: Conclude that since Sunita does not need the money for 2 years, Option B maximizes her financial return.
REASONING: Locking money in a Fixed Deposit transfers certainty to the bank, allowing them to lend it out long-term, which is why they reward the saver with a significantly higher interest rate compared to a flexible Savings Account.
Option A total interest: ₹6,000; Option B total interest: ₹14,000; Difference: ₹8,000 more earned in Option B.
ANSWER: Option B earns ₹14,000 in interest, which is ₹8,000 more than Option A.
