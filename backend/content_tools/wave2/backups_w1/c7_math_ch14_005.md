## ELI5
A carpenter in Chennai nailing a shelf plank perfectly level with its partner is constructing parallel lines: through a marked point, draw a transversal across the given line, copy the angle it makes, and the copied angle forces the new line to keep a constant perpendicular distance forever. The compass radius must not change while the arc is transferred - the copied angle's size lives in that width. Different transversal tilts only relocate the construction arcs, never the parallel verdict.

## DEFINITION
Constructing parallel lines using a compass and ruler involves creating a line that remains at a constant perpendicular distance from a given line, achieved by reproducing corresponding angles or alternate interior angles equal to a transversal intersection. This precise geometric construction guarantees that the newly drawn line will never intersect the original line, no matter how far they are extended.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are a carpenter building a wooden shelf in your home in Chennai, and you need to nail a second wooden plank that must run exactly parallel to the first plank so your books do not slide off. To do this on paper with a ruler and compass, you start by drawing your original reference line and marking a point somewhere outside of it. Next, you draw a transversal line passing through your outside point and intersecting your original line at an angle. Then, using your compass, you measure the angle formed at the intersection and transfer that exact same angle opening to the point outside the line. Finally, connecting your outside point through that newly copied arc intersection creates a brand new line that runs perfectly parallel to the first one because matching interior angles force the lines to never meet.

**Takeaway:** parallel lines are copied angles wearing a straight face: equal corresponding angles keep two lines forever equidistant. **In the exam:** draw a transversal through the point, copy its angle at the new point, and join - the copy forces parallel. **Check yourself:** copy an angle at the marked point with a fixed radius and check both lines against a set square. **Why students miss it:** students change the compass width mid-transfer and quietly bend the copied angle. The radius is the angle's fingerprint; keep it fixed and the new line cannot help running parallel, whatever tilt the transversal wore.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often change the compass radius halfway through copying an angle between the transversal intersection and the external point. For example, if they open the compass to 3 cm at the first line intersection, they might accidentally adjust it to 4 cm when drawing the arc from the external point, resulting in intersecting lines instead of parallel lines.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that a compass is a tool for maintaining a fixed distance (radius). Tell them: "Lock your compass screw tightly after measuring the first arc length. Do not touch or squeeze the legs of the compass when moving it from the first line intersection to the external point. The distance from the vertex must stay identical to guarantee our angles are equal."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Construct a line parallel to a given line $XY$ through an external point $Z$ located 6 cm away, using the alternate interior angle method with a compass and ruler.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw a straight reference line $XY$ and mark point $Z$ above it.
- Step 2: Draw a transversal line passing through point $Z$ and intersecting line $XY$ at a point, let us call it $R$.
- Step 3: Place the compass point at $R$, open it to a convenient radius (e.g., 3 cm), and draw an arc that cuts line $XY$ at point $A$ and cuts the transversal line $RZ$ at point $B$.
- Step 4: Keeping the compass radius exactly at 3 cm, place the compass point at $Z$ and draw a similar arc crossing the transversal line $RZ$ at point $C$.
- Step 5: Measure the distance between point $A$ and point $B$ with the compass. Keeping this exact opening, place the compass point at $C$ and draw a small arc that intersects the arc drawn from $Z$ at point $D$.
- Step 6: Use a straightedge to align point $Z$ and point $D$, then draw the line $ZD$ to form the parallel line.
REASONING: By copying the arc distance from $A-B$ to the upper intersection, we ensure that the alternate interior angles formed by transversal $RZ$ are equal. According to the geometric theorem, if alternate interior angles are equal, the two lines $XY$ and $ZD$ must be parallel.
ANSWER: Line $ZD$ is constructed parallel to line $XY$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A carpenter needs to cut a wooden panel with a width of 45 cm. If the bottom edge of the board is line $PQ$ and point $S$ is marked at the top corner, verify how a parallel line can be drawn using the corresponding angle method.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Establish line segment $PQ$ as the base reference edge and locate point $S$ 45 cm above it.
- Step 2: Draw a transversal line starting from point $S$ that cuts across base line $PQ$ at an acute angle, meeting it at point $T$.
- Step 3: Place the compass at vertex $T$, open it to a radius of 4 cm, and draw an arc cutting line segment $TP$ at $U$ and line segment $TS$ at $V$.
- Step 4: With the exact same compass radius of 4 cm, place the compass needle at vertex $S$ and draw an arc that intersects the transversal line segment $TS$ above point $S$ at a new point $W$.
- Step 5: Measure the span between points $U$ and $V$ using the compass. Place the compass needle at point $W$ and swing an arc to locate point $X$ such that the distance $WX$ equals distance $UV$.
- Step 6: Draw a straight line passing through points $S$ and $X$, establishing the required upper boundary edge parallel to $PQ$.
REASONING: Constructing equal corresponding angles at the intersection points $T$ and $S$ guarantees that the corresponding angles are equal, which forces the top edge and bottom edge to remain equidistant at all points.
ANSWER: The upper border line through $S$ is successfully constructed parallel to $PQ$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Two parallel tracks in a model train set are to be laid on a wooden board. The primary track line is drawn as $AB$. A technician wants to construct a parallel maintenance track line passing through an operational station platform located at point $M$, exactly 7 cm away. Detail the 6-step construction process and explain how the alternate angle theorem guarantees zero intersection.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw the primary track line $AB$ on the board, and designate the station platform point as $M$ located off the line.
- Step 2: Draw a transversal line passing through point $M$ that intersects track line $AB$ at an angle at point $N$.
- Step 3: Set the compass width to 5 cm, place the needle at point $N$, and swing an arc that cuts line $AB$ at point $E$ and line segment $NM$ at point $F$.
- Step 4: Maintaining the exact 5 cm compass width, place the needle at point $M$ and swing an arc cutting segment $NM$ above $M$ at point $G$.
- Step 5: Measure the exact chord length between points $E$ and $F$ using the compass. Move the compass to point $G$ and draw an intersecting arc to pinpoint coordinate $H$.
- Step 6: Connect points $M$ and $H$ using a long straightedge to form the second track line $MH$, ensuring constant spacing from $AB$.
REASONING: The alternate interior angles $\angle ENF$ and $\angle GMH$ are constructed to be equal in measure. In Euclidean geometry, lines cut by a transversal that form equal alternate interior angles are proven to be parallel, meaning their perpendicular distance remains invariant across their entire infinite length.
ANSWER: Maintenance track line $MH$ is constructed parallel to track line $AB$.
