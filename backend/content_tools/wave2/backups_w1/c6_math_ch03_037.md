## ELI5
Fractions live on the number line as addresses between the whole numbers. The denominator slices the walk from one whole number to the next into equal steps; the numerator counts how many steps from home. Marking a fraction is a walk: divide the stretch, count the steps, plant the flag. Fractions beyond one whole simply continue the walk into the next stretch of the road.

## DEFINITION
Fractions on the number line represent a specific length or position between whole numbers, where the distance between two consecutive integers (like 0 and 1) is divided into equal segments based on the denominator.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to correctly identify, mark, and order fractions on a number line by dividing the unit interval into the number of parts specified by the denominator.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers representing proper and improper fractions on a number line. It excludes decimal representations, the density of fractions (fractions between fractions), and rational numbers.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are walking along a straight path where the distance from your house to the grocery store is exactly 1 kilometre. If you want to stop to rest after walking exactly half of that distance, where exactly on that path should you stand? To answer this, we treat the path from 0 to 1 as a single unit, then fold or divide that space into equal parts based on the fraction's denominator. If the denominator is 4, you slice the distance between 0 and 1 into 4 equal segments, and counting 3 segments from 0 lands you at the point representing 3/4. The key insight is that the numerator tells you how many of these "jumps" to take from zero.

**Takeaway:** the denominator divides the unit interval into equal segments; the numerator counts segments from the start. **In the exam:** in number-line questions, divide first by the denominator, then count off by the numerator. **Check yourself:** pace the walkthrough's path from house to shop and stop at the fraction of the distance asked. **Why students miss it:** students swap the counting and cutting jobs - the walkthrough's paced walk is a no to that; the cut count comes from the bottom, the step count from the top. Rehearse the exam move once more - divide, count, mark - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If you are asked to represent the fraction 2/5 on a number line, into how many equal parts must you divide the space between 0 and 1?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
You must divide the space into 5 equal parts. A wrong answer (like 2) shows the student is confusing the numerator (how many parts to count) with the denominator (how many total parts the unit is cut into).

**A wrong answer (dividing by the numerator) reveals the student swapped the jobs - the bottom number owns the cutting, the top only counts the steps.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Does the point 4/4 fall to the left, at, or to the right of the point 1 on the number line? Explain why.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
It falls exactly at 1. If a student thinks it falls to the left or right, they do not understand that a fraction with the same numerator and denominator is equal to one whole unit.

**A wrong answer (placing it off the whole) reveals the student misjudged a complete set - a fraction whose top meets its bottom is exactly one whole, landing precisely on the mark.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Mark the fraction 3/4 on a number line that starts at 0 and ends at 1.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Draw a line from 0 to 1. Step 2: Divide the line into 4 equal segments. Step 3: Count 3 segments starting from 0. Step 4: Place a dot at the third mark. A student who marks it at the first or fourth spot fails to understand the "counting" aspect of the numerator.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the positions of 1/3 and 2/3 on the number line. Which one is closer to 0?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
1/3 is closer to 0. If a student says 2/3, they are likely confusing the size of the fraction with the size of the denominator or simply guessing; they need to visualize the segments getting longer as you move right.

**A wrong answer (calling the later fraction closer) reveals the student trusted the bigger top blindly - counting more equal steps from the start necessarily walks further away.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student marks 5/3 on a number line by dividing the interval between 0 and 1 into 3 parts and placing a dot on the 5th mark. Is this correct? Justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, this is incorrect. Since 5/3 is greater than 1 (it equals 1 2/3), the point must be placed in the interval between 1 and 2. The student failed to recognize that improper fractions extend beyond the first whole unit.

**A wrong answer (cramming past one whole) reveals the student overstuffed the first stretch - a fraction beyond one must be planted past the whole-number mark, not squeezed inside.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a challenge: If you were given a number line with only the marks 0 and 1, how would you precisely locate 1/8?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The student should describe halving the distance to get 1/2, halving again to get 1/4, and halving once more to get 1/8. A poor answer (like "just guess") shows a lack of systematic spatial reasoning.

**A wrong answer (guessing the position) reveals the student skipped the systematic fold - repeated halving builds the exact stop without any guesswork.**

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often treat the "marks" on a number line as the numbers themselves rather than the "gaps" between the marks. For example, when asked to mark 3/4, they count the 0 as the "first" mark and place their dot on the 3rd mark total, which is actually 2/4. They fail to realize that the fraction represents the distance (the interval), not the tick mark itself.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to draw a paper strip representing the unit distance between 0 and 1. Have them fold it into 4 equal sections to create clear physical "gaps." Explain that each fold line is a boundary, and the space between the folds represents the fraction. Tell them to count the "windows" or "gaps" they have created, not the lines they have drawn.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student needs to mark 3/5 of a metre on a number line measuring 1 metre. Show the steps.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw a line segment of 1 metre length, labelling the start 0 and end 1.
- Step 2: Divide the 1 metre distance into 5 equal parts using a ruler.
- Step 3: Starting from 0, count 3 of these equal parts towards the right.
- Step 4: Draw a point at the end of the 3rd part.
REASONING: The denominator 5 tells us how many equal segments make up the whole, and the numerator 3 tells us the total distance to travel from the starting point 0.
ANSWER: The point is located at the 3rd tick mark after 0.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Locate 7/4 on a number line.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that 7/4 is greater than 1 because 7 > 4.
- Step 2: Draw a number line extending from 0 to 2.
- Step 3: Divide the interval between 0 and 1 into 4 parts, and the interval between 1 and 2 into 4 parts.
- Step 4: Count 7 segments starting from 0.
- Step 5: Mark the point at the 7th segment (which is 3 segments after 1).
REASONING: Improper fractions cover more than one whole unit, so the number line must be extended to include the next whole number (2).
ANSWER: The point is located at 1 3/4.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A tailor has a 1-metre long ribbon. He needs to mark a point at 1/2 metre and another at 3/4 metre. How can he do this on a number line?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw a line from 0 to 1 representing 1 metre.
- Step 2: Divide the line into 4 equal parts to accommodate the denominator 4.
- Step 3: Mark the 2nd part (2/4), which is equivalent to 1/2.
- Step 4: Mark the 3rd part (3/4).
- Step 5: Verify that 2/4 is indeed the midpoint between 0 and 1.
REASONING: By using the larger denominator (4), both fractions (1/2 and 3/4) can be placed on the same set of grid lines, showing their relative sizes.
ANSWER: Point A at 2/4 (1/2) and Point B at 3/4.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you were given a number line where the distance between 0 and 1 is divided into 6 equal parts, explain how you would locate 4/6. Where would 2/6 be in relation to 4/6?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify 6 as the total segments.
- Step 2: Count 4 segments for 4/6.
- Step 3: Compare by distance: 2/6 is closer to 0 than 4/6.
ANSWER: You count 4 segments from 0. 2/6 is located to the left of 4/6, meaning it is closer to 0 because 2 is less than 4.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A classmate says that 3/2 should be marked between 0 and 1 because "the numerator is small." Critique this statement.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- Step 1: Evaluate the definition of an improper fraction.
- Step 2: Compare 3/2 to 1 (3/2 = 1.5).
- Step 3: Identify the student's error (confusing numerator size with total value).
ANSWER: The classmate is incorrect. Since 3 is greater than 2, the value is greater than 1 whole unit. It should be marked halfway between 1 and 2.
