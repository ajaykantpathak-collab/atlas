## ELI5
Walk along a park's edge and swing round onto an extended path: the turn you make outside the corner is the exterior angle. The property is a bargain - that outside angle equals the two interior opposite corners added together. It is therefore always bigger than either partner alone, since no sum shrinks below its parts. Extend all three sides in turn and the three outside turns total one full rotation. One extended ruler line at any corner unlocks the algebra without computing every interior angle.

## DEFINITION
The exterior angle property of a triangle states that if any side of a triangle is produced (extended), the exterior angle so formed is equal to the sum of the two interior opposite angles. This fundamental geometric rule allows us to find unknown angle measures in complex figures without needing to calculate all interior angles first.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine standing at the corner of a triangular park in your neighborhood, walking along one side, and then turning to continue along an extended path—the angle you turn through is the exterior angle! To begin understanding this mathematically, draw any triangle on your paper and extend one of its sides outward with a straight line. Recall from earlier work with lines and angles that a straight line measures 180 degrees, and triangles have an interior angle sum of 180 degrees. By exploring how adjacent linear pairs relate to the remaining two angles inside the triangle, we discover a powerful shortcut: an exterior angle never depends on its adjacent interior neighbor, but always equals the exact sum of the other two interior angles. This relationship forms a bridge between lines, angles, and algebraic equations.

**Takeaway:** an exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles. **In the exam:** identify the two opposite interior angles first, then equate their sum to the exterior expression. **Check yourself:** extend one side of a drawn triangle and verify the property with a protractor. **Why students miss it:** they subtract instead of add, or pair the exterior with its next-door interior angle. Mark the two interior opposite angles with arcs first, and the property writes its own equation.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
In triangle $PQR$, side $QR$ is produced to point $S$, forming the exterior angle $\angle PRS$. If the two interior opposite angles are $\angle P = 50^\circ$ and $\angle Q = 60^\circ$, what is the measure of exterior angle $\angle PRS$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The measure is $110^\circ$. A student who answers $70^\circ$ has incorrectly subtracted the angles or confused the exterior angle property with the interior angle sum (calculating $180^\circ - 50^\circ - 60^\circ$), failing to recognize that the exterior angle equals the *sum* of the two opposite interior angles ($50^\circ + 60^\circ = 110^\circ$).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Can an exterior angle of a triangle ever be smaller than one of its interior opposite angles? Explain why or why not using mathematical reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, an exterior angle can never be smaller than either of its interior opposite angles. Because the exterior angle is equal to the *sum* of both interior opposite angles, and angle measures in a standard triangle are positive values greater than zero, adding two positive numbers results in a value greater than or equal to each individual part. A student who thinks it can be smaller likely confuses interior adjacent angles with interior opposite angles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
In $\triangle ABC$, side $BC$ is extended to point $D$ to form exterior angle $\angle ACD$. If $\angle A = (3x + 10)^\circ$, $\angle B = (2x + 20)^\circ$, and exterior angle $\angle ACD = 120^\circ$, find the value of $x$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Write down the exterior angle property equation: $\text{Exterior Angle} = \text{Interior Opposite } \angle A + \text{Interior Opposite } \angle B$.
Step 2: Substitute the given algebraic expressions: $120 = (3x + 10) + (2x + 20)$.
Step 3: Combine like terms on the right side: $120 = 5x + 30$.
Step 4: Subtract 30 from both sides using inverse operations: $120 - 30 = 5x \implies 90 = 5x$.
Step 5: Divide by 5 to solve for $x$: $x = \frac{90}{5} = 18$.
A student who gets $x = 14$ likely forgot to group like terms properly or made an arithmetic error when subtracting 30 from 120.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze a triangle where all three exterior angles are formed by extending each side in one direction. What is the sum of these three exterior angles, and how does this relate to the exterior angle property?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The sum of the three exterior angles of any triangle is always $360^\circ$. This can be analyzed by noting that each exterior angle and its adjacent interior angle form a linear pair summing to $180^\circ$; across all three vertices, the total is $3 \times 180^\circ = 540^\circ$, and subtracting the interior angle sum ($180^\circ$) leaves $360^\circ$. A student who states the sum is $180^\circ$ is confusing the interior angle sum property with the exterior angle sum.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently confuse the adjacent interior angle with the interior opposite angles. When given an exterior angle and the interior adjacent angle, they mistakenly add or subtract those two adjacent angles instead of recognizing that the exterior angle equals the sum of the *two remote interior angles* on the opposite side of the triangle. For example, if the interior adjacent angle is $70^\circ$ and the exterior angle is $130^\circ$, a student might try to add $130^\circ + 70^\circ = 200^\circ$ or subtract them incorrectly relative to the wrong vertex.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student makes this error, pause and have them highlight the vertex where the exterior angle is located. Remind them: "Your exterior angle lives on a straight line outside the triangle. Look across the triangle—away from the line you are standing on—to find the *remote* pair of angles." Have them physically cover up the adjacent interior angle with their thumb so they are forced to focus only on the two opposite corners of the triangle.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: In a school triangular courtyard in Jaipur, side $AB$ is produced to point $D$ to form exterior angle $\angle CBD$. If interior angle $\angle A = 45^\circ$ and interior angle $\angle C = 65^\circ$, find the measure of exterior angle $\angle CBD$.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the exterior angle and its two interior opposite angles from the problem description: Exterior angle is $\angle CBD$; interior opposite angles are $\angle A$ and $\angle C$.
- Step 2: State the exterior angle property formula: $\text{Exterior } \angle CBD = \angle A + \angle C$.
- Step 3: Substitute the known numerical values into the equation: $\angle CBD = 45^\circ + 65^\circ$.
- Step 4: Perform the addition computation: $45 + 65 = 110$.
- Step 5: Verify with units and interpret: The exterior angle is $110^\circ$, which is greater than each individual interior opposite angle, confirming geometric feasibility.
REASONING: The exterior angle of a triangle is always equal to the sum of its two interior opposite angles. By identifying $\angle A$ and $\angle C$ as the remote interior angles relative to vertex $B$, we simply add their degree measures together.
ANSWER: $110^\circ$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An engineer designs a triangular roof truss where an exterior angle measures $(5x - 10)^\circ$. The two interior opposite angles are given as $50^\circ$ and $30^\circ$. Find the value of $x$ and the exact measure of the exterior angle.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Set up the algebraic relation using the exterior angle property: $\text{Exterior Angle} = \text{Interior Opposite 1} + \text{Interior Opposite 2}$.
- Step 2: Substitute the algebraic expression and numerical values: $5x - 10 = 50 + 30$.
- Step 3: Simplify the right side of the equation: $5x - 10 = 80$.
- Step 4: Isolate the variable term by adding 10 to both sides: $5x = 80 + 10 \implies 5x = 90$.
- Step 5: Divide by 5 to solve for $x$: $x = \frac{90}{5} = 18$.
- Step 6: Substitute $x$ back into the expression to find the exterior angle: $5(18) - 10 = 90 - 10 = 80^\circ$.
REASONING: Setting up an algebraic equation allows us to find unknown variables when angles are expressed in terms of $x$. We equate the algebraic expression to the numerical sum of the remote interior angles and solve step-by-step using inverse operations.
ANSWER: $x = 18$, Exterior Angle = $80^\circ$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: In $\triangle XYZ$, side $YZ$ is extended to point $W$ to form exterior angle $\angle XZW$. If $\angle Y = 2x^\circ$, $\angle X = (3x + 15)^\circ$, and $\angle XZW = 115^\circ$, find the measure of both interior angles $\angle Y$ and $\angle X$.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the exterior angle and the two interior opposite angles: $\text{Exterior angle } \angle XZW = \angle Y + \angle X$.
- Step 2: Substitute the given algebraic expressions and value: $115 = 2x + (3x + 15)$.
- Step 3: Combine like terms on the right side ($2x + 3x = 5x$): $115 = 5x + 15$.
- Step 4: Isolate the term with $x$ by subtracting 15 from both sides: $115 - 15 = 5x \implies 100 = 5x$.
- Step 5: Divide by 5 to solve for $x$: $x = \frac{100}{5} = 20$.
- Step 6: Substitute $x = 20$ into each interior angle expression: $\angle Y = 2(20) = 40^\circ$ and $\angle X = 3(20) + 15 = 60 + 15 = 75^\circ$.
- Step 7: Verify the result by checking if their sum equals the exterior angle: $40^\circ + 75^\circ = 115^\circ$, which matches the given exterior angle.
REASONING: Complex geometric problems combine algebraic simplification with angle properties. By writing out the sum equation, combining like terms, solving for $x$, and substituting back, we determine all unknown angle values while verifying consistency.
ANSWER: $\angle Y = 40^\circ$, $\angle X = 75^\circ$

QUESTION: A surveyor measures a triangular plot of land. One exterior angle is measured as $135^\circ$. One of its interior opposite angles is three times the other interior opposite angle. Determine the exact measures of both interior opposite angles.
COMPETENCY: Translating word descriptions into algebraic expressions and solving multi-step angle problems.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Let the smaller interior opposite angle be $y$. Then the larger interior opposite angle is $3y$.
- Step 2: Apply the exterior angle property formula: $\text{Exterior Angle} = \text{Interior Opposite 1} + \text{Interior Opposite 2}$.
- Step 3: Set up the equation: $135 = y + 3y$.
- Step 4: Combine like terms: $135 = 4y$.
- Step 5: Solve for $y$: $y = \frac{135}{4} = 33.75^\circ$.
- Step 6: Calculate both angles: $y = 33.75^\circ$ and $3y = 3 \times 33.75^\circ = 101.25^\circ$.
ANSWER: The two interior opposite angles are $33.75^\circ$ and $101.25^\circ$ (or $33^\circ 45'$ and $101^\circ 15'$).

COMPETENCY: Mathematical reasoning, error identification, and justification of geometric constraints.
