## ELI5
Why is your water bottle plastic or metal, never cloth? Because three quick tests tell you what a material can do. Look: does it shine like metal, or sit dull? Press: does it resist your thumb (hard) or change shape (soft)? Stir in water: does it disappear (soluble) or stay whole (insoluble)? Appearance, hardness and solubility — check these and you can predict a material's job before it fails. Cloth fails the bottle test not because it is weak but because it soaks and leaks; plastic passes because it is insoluble and keeps its shape. Match the properties to the purpose and the material picks itself.

## DEFINITION
Materials are substances used to create objects, and they are classified based on properties such as appearance (lustre), hardness, and solubility in water. These properties determine the suitability of a material for a specific purpose.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that if a material is hard, it must be metal, or if it is soft, it cannot be a metal. For example, they might think a sponge is not a "real" material because it is soft, or that all metals must be extremely hard. This happens because they generalize properties of specific objects (like iron nails) to all members of a group.
## ON_FAILURE

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You have a sugar cube, a stone, and a piece of chalk. You place each in separate beakers of water and stir. Identify which is soluble and which is insoluble.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the materials given (sugar, stone, chalk).
- step 2: Define solubility as the ability of a substance to dissolve (disappear) in water.
- step 3: Predict the outcome for each: sugar dissolves, stone and chalk do not.
- step 4: Conclude that sugar is soluble, while stone and chalk are insoluble.
- step 5: Verify by noting that "soluble" means the substance mixes completely with the water.
REASONING: Solubility depends on whether the material particles can spread out uniformly in water. Sugar molecules break apart and mix, while stone and chalk particles are too large or chemically different to do so.
ANSWER: Sugar is soluble; stone and chalk are insoluble.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: You have three objects: a metal spoon, a rubber eraser, and a cotton cloth. Rank them from hardest to softest.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the objects: metal spoon, rubber eraser, cotton cloth.
- step 2: Apply the test of "resistance to scratching/compression."
- step 3: Test the spoon—it cannot be compressed (Hardest).
- step 4: Test the eraser—it can be compressed with force (Medium).
- step 5: Test the cloth—it compresses and folds easily (Softest).
REASONING: Hardness is a measure of a material's resistance to change in shape. The metallic bond in the spoon is strong, the polymer in the eraser is flexible, and the fiber structure of the cloth is soft.
ANSWER: Hardest: Metal spoon; Medium: Rubber eraser; Softest: Cotton cloth.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper wants to store three things: salt, oil, and iron nails. Which of these will disappear if accidentally mixed in a large tank of water?
STEPS:
- step 1: Check if salt is soluble in water (Yes).
- step 2: Check if oil is soluble in water (No, it floats as a layer).
- step 3: Check if iron is soluble in water (No, it sinks and stays solid).
- step 4: Identify which material "disappears."
- step 5: Confirm salt is the only one that dissolves.
REASONING: Solubility is specific to the substance. Salt (NaCl) is ionic and dissolves, whereas iron is a solid metal, and oil is a liquid that does not mix with water (it is immiscible).
ANSWER: Salt will disappear.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A friend says that because a diamond is hard, it must be a metal. How would you test this claim using the concept of lustre and hardness?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand the claim (Hard = Metal).
- step 2: Identify the error (Hardness is not exclusive to metals).
- step 3: Use the property of "lustre" (metals usually reflect light; non-metals like diamonds have a different type of shine but are carbon-based).
- step 4: Explain that hardness is just one property, and testing other properties (like conductivity or chemical makeup) is needed to classify it.
ANSWER: The claim is incorrect because hardness is found in many materials, including non-metals like diamonds or rocks. To correct this, I would explain that one property (hardness) cannot identify a material; we must look at a combination of properties like lustre, solubility, and conductivity.
