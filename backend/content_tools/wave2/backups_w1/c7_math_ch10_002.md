## ELI5
Your board-game token sits at minus 3 and the dice hands you plus 5: walk five steps to the right and you land safely back in credit. Addition on the number line is choreography - start at the first number, then let the second number's sign choose the direction, right for positive, left for negative, with the step count set by its size. Two left-walks simply sink deeper into debt; the plus sign between addends never changes where each one walks.

## DEFINITION
The addition of integers using a number line is a visual method where positive integers are added by moving to the right and negative integers are added by moving to the left from a starting number. This process physically models how positive and negative values combine on a linear scale.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can accurately represent any integer addition expression ($a + b$) on a number line, correctly interpret the starting position, direction of movement, and final landing point, and verify the result algebraically.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers addition of positive and negative integers using horizontal number lines within the range of $-20$ to $+20$. It does not cover multiplication, division, or fractional/decimal integers.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are playing a board game where your score is currently at $-3$ (3 points in debt), and you roll a dice that gives you $+5$ points. Where does your final score land on the scoring track? To solve this, we start at our initial position on the number line and use directional movement: positive numbers mean moving right (forward), and negative numbers mean moving left (backward). This builds directly on comparing and ordering integers from Class 6, where you learned to locate positive and negative numbers on a number line. The key insight is that the operation of addition combined with a negative integer translates physically to a leftward step, exactly opposite to adding a positive integer.

**Takeaway:** number-line addition: start at the first integer, walk right for a positive addend, left for a negative. **In the exam:** draw the start, the direction arrow and the landing point; the picture is the working. **Check yourself:** replay the dice move on a sketch and read the landing square. **Why students miss it:** they let the plus sign between terms mislead the direction of a negative addend. Draw the arrow before moving the token, and the landing point can never surprise you.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3

Step 2: Since we are adding $+5$, move 5 units to the right.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the sign of the integer being added with the operation sign, resulting in wrong directional movement. For example, when evaluating $(-4) + (-3)$, a student might start at $-4$ and move 3 units to the *right* because they see a plus sign between the brackets, completely ignoring that the second number is negative.
## ON_FAILURE
Guide the student by breaking the expression into two parts: "Where do we start?" and "Which way do we walk?" Have them point to the first number on the number line as the starting post. Then, tell them: "Look at the sign attached directly to the second number—if it's a minus sign, face left; if it's a plus sign, face right; then take the number of steps indicated."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A bank account has a balance of ₹$-400$ (an overdraft). The customer deposits ₹$+700$. Use a number line model to find the new balance.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the starting position from the initial value, which is $-400$ on the number line.
- Step 2: Identify the operation and displacement: adding a positive deposit of $+700$ means moving to the right by $700$ units.
- Step 3: Perform the movement: starting from $-400$, move right by $400$ units to reach $0$, and then move another $300$ units to the right.
- Step 4: Write down the mathematical expression and result: $(-400) + (+700) = +300$.
- Step 5: Interpret the final result with units: The new bank account balance is ₹$+300$ (a positive balance).
REASONING: Starting at a negative number represents debt. Adding a positive amount reduces the debt and moves past zero into positive credit, modeled by stepping right past the origin.
ANSWER: ₹$+300$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A deep-sea diver is exploring at an elevation of $-15$ metres relative to sea level. They swim further down by $12$ metres. Use the number line to find their new position.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the starting integer on the vertical/horizontal number line, which is $-15$.
- Step 2: Identify the second integer representing the change: moving down means a negative displacement of $-12$.
- Step 3: Set up the addition expression: $(-15) + (-12)$.
- Step 4: Execute the number line movement: start at $-15$ and move $12$ units further left (in the negative direction), landing at $-15 - 12 = -27$.
- Step 5: Verify with units: The new elevation is $-27$ metres.
REASONING: Moving further down in depth corresponds to adding a negative integer, which requires continuing in the leftward (negative) direction from the starting point.
ANSWER: $-27$ metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: At 5:00 AM, the temperature in a mountain village was $-6^\circ\text{C}$. By afternoon, the temperature rose by $+11^\circ\text{C}$, and by midnight, it dropped again by $-5^\circ\text{C}$. Find the final temperature at midnight using number line reasoning.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Establish the initial position on the number line at $-6$.
- Step 2: Apply the first change (rise of $+11^\circ\text{C}$) by moving $11$ units to the right: $(-6) + (+11) = +5$.
- Step 3: Use the new position $(+5)$ as the starting point for the second change.
- Step 4: Apply the second change (drop of $-5^\circ\text{C}$) by moving $5$ units to the left: $(+5) + (-5) = 0$.
- Step 5: Verify all calculations step-by-step and confirm the final position on the number line.
REASONING: Multi-step integer operations require updating the position sequentially. Each operation uses the previous result as the new starting coordinate on the number line.
ANSWER: $0^\circ\text{C}$
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two students, Aarav and Meera, are trying to solve $(+3) + (-5)$ using a number line. Aarav starts at $+3$ and moves 5 units left, arriving at $-2$. Meera starts at $-5$ and moves 3 units right, arriving at $-2$. Both got $-2$. Explain why both methods work, referencing mathematical properties.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying mathematical methods
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze Aarav's method: starting at $+3$ and adding $-5$ (moving 5 units left).
- Step 2: Analyze Meera's method: starting at $-5$ and adding $+3$ (moving 3 units right).
- Step 3: Connect to the commutative property of addition, which states that $a + b = b + a$.
- Step 4: Conclude that order does not change the sum, so both physical paths on the number line yield the same final coordinate.
ANSWER: Both methods are correct because integer addition is commutative. Whether you start at the first addend and displace by the second, or vice versa, the final destination on the number line remains invariant at $-2$.
