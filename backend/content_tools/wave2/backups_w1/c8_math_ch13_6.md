## ELI5
Apples with apples, bananas with bananas — that is the entire law of like terms. Two terms are 'like' when their variable parts match exactly: same letters, same powers, like two identical tiffin boxes that can be stacked. Only the numbers in front may differ, and combining like terms simply means adding or subtracting those front numbers while the shared variable part rides along unchanged. Terms with different variables or powers are unlike — a banana never stacks on an apple, however tempting the sum.

## DEFINITION
Like terms are algebraic terms that have exactly the same variable part, meaning identical variables raised to identical powers; only their numerical coefficients may differ. Unlike terms differ in their variable part (different variables or different exponents) and cannot be combined through addition or subtraction.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can correctly identify like and unlike terms in multi-variable expressions (up to three variables, powers up to 3), combine like terms by adding/subtracting coefficients while preserving the variable part, and simplify algebraic expressions by collecting all like terms.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers identification and combination of like terms in algebraic expressions with integer coefficients, multiple variables (up to 3), and whole-number exponents up to 3. Does not cover factorization, expansion of brackets, or solving equations (addressed in later nodes).
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are organizing a school fundraiser: you have 3 boxes of mangoes, 5 boxes of apples, and then you receive 2 more boxes of mangoes and give away 1 box of apples. You naturally group mangoes with mangoes and apples with apples because they are different items. In algebra, terms like \(4x^2y\) and \(-2x^2y\) are "same items" (like terms) because they share the exact variable part \(x^2y\), so we combine their coefficients: \(4 + (-2) = 2\), giving \(2x^2y\). Terms like \(3xy^2\) and \(5x^2y\) are "different items" (unlike terms) and stay separate. Start by circling terms with identical variable parts, then add/subtract their coefficients, and rewrite the simplified expression.

Takeaway: like terms share the identical variable part — same variables, same exponents; combine them by adding or subtracting coefficients only, keeping the variable part untouched; unlike terms stay separate forever. Exam format: expect simplification of long expressions with several variable parts, plus 'identify the like terms' questions involving multiple variables and powers. Verification beat: before merging, place the terms side by side and compare letters and powers character by character. Why students miss it: they merge terms whose variables merely look similar — a squared letter and its cube are different creatures, and so are a product of two variables and a product with a square; identical variable part, not similar appearance, is the test.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Identify the like terms in the expression: \(7a^2b - 3ab^2 + 2a^2b + 5ab - 4ba^2\).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The like terms are \(7a^2b\), \(2a^2b\), and \(-4ba^2\) (since \(ba^2 = a^2b\)). The terms \(-3ab^2\) and \(5ab\) are unlike any other term. A wrong answer such as grouping \(7a^2b\) with \(-3ab^2\) reveals the student ignores the exponent positions and treats \(a^2b\) and \(ab^2\) as the same.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why \(5x^2y\) and \(3xy^2\) are unlike terms, even though both contain \(x\) and \(y\).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
They are unlike because the powers of the variables differ: in \(5x^2y\), \(x\) has exponent 2 and \(y\) has exponent 1; in \(3xy^2\), \(x\) has exponent 1 and \(y\) has exponent 2. The variable parts \(x^2y\) and \(xy^2\) are not identical. A wrong answer claiming they are like because they have the same letters shows the student does not understand that exponents are part of the variable identity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Simplify the expression: \(6p^2q - 4pq^2 + 3p^2q + 2pq^2 - 5pq\).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Identify like terms: \(6p^2q\) and \(3p^2q\) are like; \(-4pq^2\) and \(2pq^2\) are like; \(-5pq\) is alone.
Step 2: Combine coefficients: \(6+3 = 9\) for \(p^2q\); \(-4+2 = -2\) for \(pq^2\).
Step 3: Write simplified expression: \(9p^2q - 2pq^2 - 5pq\).
A wrong answer like \(9p^2q - 2pq^2 - 5pq^2\) (combining \(-5pq\) with \(pq^2\)) reveals the student mistakenly treats \(pq\) and \(pq^2\) as like terms.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the two expressions: \(A = 4x^2y - 2xy^2 + 3x^2y\) and \(B = 4x^2y - 2xy^2 + 3xy\). Which expression has more like terms that can be combined? Explain.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Expression A has two like terms (\(4x^2y\) and \(3x^2y\)) that combine to \(7x^2y\), leaving \(-2xy^2\) separate. Expression B has no like terms because \(4x^2y\), \(-2xy^2\), and \(3xy\) all have different variable parts. So A has more combinable like terms. A wrong answer saying both have the same number of like terms indicates the student does not recognize that \(3xy\) is unlike the others due to missing the square on \(x\).

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create an algebraic expression with exactly four terms that simplifies to \(5x^2y - 3xy^2\) after combining like terms. Your expression must include at least two different variable parts and at least one pair of like terms that cancel each other out (sum to zero).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
One possible expression: \(7x^2y - 2x^2y - 3xy^2 + 4xy - 4xy\). Here \(7x^2y\) and \(-2x^2y\) are like terms combining to \(5x^2y\); \(4xy\) and \(-4xy\) cancel to zero; \(-3xy^2\) remains. The simplified form is \(5x^2y - 3xy^2\). A poor answer that doesn't include a cancelling pair or doesn't simplify to the target shows the student cannot work backwards from a simplified form or doesn't understand that like terms can sum to zero.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often combine terms that have the same letters but different exponents, e.g., adding \(3x^2y + 2xy^2\) to get \(5x^2y^2\) or \(5x^3y^3\). They treat the variable part as a bag of letters ignoring the power of each variable. Another frequent error is mishandling signs when combining: e.g., \(5a^2b - 3a^2b\) becomes \(2a^2b\) (correct) but \(5a^2b - (-3a^2b)\) becomes \(2a^2b\) instead of \(8a^2b\) because they forget the double negative.
## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Have the student write each term as a product of coefficient and variable part (e.g., \(5x^2y = 5 \times x \times x \times y\)). Step 2: Compare variable parts letter-by-letter with exponents; only if every variable and its exponent match exactly are they like. Step 3: For combining, circle the coefficients including their signs, add them using integer rules (revisit integer addition if needed), and attach the common variable part unchanged. Step 4: Practice with a mixed set of terms where some cancel to zero to reinforce sign handling.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Riya buys \(3\) kg of apples at ₹\(x\) per kg, \(2\) kg of oranges at ₹\(y\) per kg, then another \(4\) kg of apples at ₹\(x\) per kg, and returns \(1\) kg of oranges. Write an algebraic expression for the total cost and simplify by combining like terms.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify each purchase/return as a term: \(3x\) (apples), \(2y\) (oranges), \(4x\) (more apples), \(-1y\) (returned oranges).
- Step 2: Write the expression: \(3x + 2y + 4x - y\).
- Step 3: Group like terms: terms with \(x\) are \(3x\) and \(4x\); terms with \(y\) are \(2y\) and \(-y\).
- Step 4: Combine coefficients: \(3 + 4 = 7\) for \(x\); \(2 + (-1) = 1\) for \(y\).
- Step 5: Write simplified expression: \(7x + y\). This means total cost = ₹\(7x + y\).

REASONING: We treat each fruit type as a distinct variable part. Apples (\(x\)) and oranges (\(y\)) are unlike terms, so they stay separate. Combining coefficients follows integer addition rules. The simplified expression gives the net cost in terms of the per-kg prices.

ANSWER: \(7x + y\) rupees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Simplify the expression: \(5a^2b - 3ab^2 + 2a^2b - 4ab + 6ab^2 - a^2b\).

STEPS:
- Step 1: List all terms with their variable parts: \(5a^2b\) (var part \(a^2b\)), \(-3ab^2\) (var part \(ab^2\)), \(2a^2b\) (var part \(a^2b\)), \(-4ab\) (var part \(ab\)), \(6ab^2\) (var part \(ab^2\)), \(-a^2b\) (var part \(a^2b\)).
- Step 2: Group like terms: Group 1 (\(a^2b\)): \(5a^2b, 2a^2b, -a^2b\); Group 2 (\(ab^2\)): \(-3ab^2, 6ab^2\); Group 3 (\(ab\)): \(-4ab\).
- Step 3: Combine coefficients in each group: Group 1: \(5 + 2 + (-1) = 6\); Group 2: \(-3 + 6 = 3\); Group 3: \(-4\) (no change).
- Step 4: Reconstruct expression with combined terms: \(6a^2b + 3ab^2 - 4ab\).
- Step 5: Verify no further like terms exist (all variable parts distinct) and write final answer.

REASONING: The variable parts \(a^2b\), \(ab^2\), and \(ab\) are all different because the exponents of \(a\) and \(b\) differ. We only add coefficients of terms with exactly matching variable parts. The order of terms in the final expression does not matter, but conventionally we write higher powers first.

ANSWER: \(6a^2b + 3ab^2 - 4ab\)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: The perimeter of a triangle is given by the expression \((2x^2y + 3xy^2 - 4xy) + (5x^2y - 2xy^2 + 6xy) + (-x^2y + xy^2 - 2xy)\). Simplify the expression to find the perimeter in simplest form.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Remove parentheses (all addition, so signs stay): \(2x^2y + 3xy^2 - 4xy + 5x^2y - 2xy^2 + 6xy - x^2y + xy^2 - 2xy\).
- Step 2: Identify variable parts: \(x^2y\), \(xy^2\), \(xy\).
- Step 3: Group like terms: \(x^2y\): \(2x^2y, 5x^2y, -x^2y\); \(xy^2\): \(3xy^2, -2xy^2, xy^2\); \(xy\): \(-4xy, 6xy, -2xy\).
- Step 4: Combine coefficients: \(x^2y\): \(2 + 5 - 1 = 6\); \(xy^2\): \(3 - 2 + 1 = 2\); \(xy\): \(-4 + 6 - 2 = 0\).
- Step 5: Write simplified expression: \(6x^2y + 2xy^2 + 0xy = 6x^2y + 2xy^2\).
- Step 6: Interpret: The perimeter simplifies to \(6x^2y + 2xy^2\) (the \(xy\) terms cancelled out).

REASONING: This combines the concept of adding multiple algebraic expressions (from sibling node c8_math_ch13_7) with like-term combination. The cancellation of the \(xy\) terms shows that like terms can sum to zero, effectively removing that variable part from the final expression.

ANSWER: \(6x^2y + 2xy^2\)
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student claims that in the expression \(4p^2q - 2pq^2 + 3p^2q - 5pq\), the terms \(4p^2q\) and \(-2pq^2\) are like terms because both contain \(p\) and \(q\). Do you agree? Explain why or why not, and then simplify the expression correctly.

COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction; reasoning about variable parts and exponents.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand the student's claim: they think same letters mean like terms.
- Step 2: Identify the variable parts: \(4p^2q\) has \(p^2q\); \(-2pq^2\) has \(pq^2\). The exponents differ.
- Step 3: Explain that like terms require identical variable parts (same variables with same exponents).
- Step 4: Correctly group and combine: \(4p^2q + 3p^2q = 7p^2q\); \(-2pq^2\) stays; \(-5pq\) stays.
- Step 5: Write final simplified expression.

ANSWER: I disagree. Like terms must have the exact same variables raised to the exact same powers. \(p^2q\) (p squared times q) and \(pq^2\) (p times q squared) are different variable parts. The correct simplification is \(7p^2q - 2pq^2 - 5pq\).

DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: The area of a rectangular garden is represented by \(A = (3x^2y + 2xy^2 - 5xy) + (4x^2y - 3xy^2 + 7xy) - (2x^2y + xy^2 - 4xy)\). Simplify the expression for the area. Then, if \(x = 2\) m and \(y = 3\) m, calculate the numerical area in square metres.

COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions; multi-step algebraic simplification and substitution.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Remove parentheses, distributing the negative sign in the subtraction: \(3x^2y + 2xy^2 - 5xy + 4x^2y - 3xy^2 + 7xy - 2x^2y - xy^2 + 4xy\).
- Step 2: Group like terms: \(x^2y\): \(3 + 4 - 2 = 5\); \(xy^2\): \(2 - 3 - 1 = -2\); \(xy\): \(-5 + 7 + 4 = 6\).
- Step 3: Write simplified algebraic expression: \(5x^2y - 2xy^2 + 6xy\).
- Step 4: Substitute \(x = 2\), \(y = 3\): \(5(2)^2(3) - 2(2)(3)^2 + 6(2)(3)\).
- Step 5: Compute: \(5 \times 4 \times 3 = 60\); \(-2 \times 2 \times 9 = -36\); \(6 \times 2 \times 3 = 36\). Sum: \(60 - 36 + 36 = 60\).

ANSWER: Simplified expression: \(5x^2y - 2xy^2 + 6xy\). Area = \(60\) m².

DIFFICULTY: hard
