## ELI5
Hold a small ball between a table lamp and a wall: shadow appears only when all three sit in a straight line. Eclipses are the sky's version - sun, Earth and moon aligned so one casts its shadow on another. Yet no eclipse monthly: the moon's orbit tilts slightly out of Earth's, so the usual new moon slides above or below the shadow line. Eclipses are alignment luck on a tilted rail.

## DEFINITION
An eclipse occurs when one celestial body moves into the shadow of another, which requires a precise alignment of the Sun, Earth, and Moon in a nearly straight line. Because the Moon's orbital plane is tilted at an angle of about 5 degrees relative to Earth's orbital plane, eclipses do not happen every month, but only when the Moon crosses the orbital plane during a new moon or full moon.

## WALKTHROUGH
When you hold a small ball between a table lamp and a wall, you can easily make a shadow on the wall, but only when your eye, the ball, and the lamp are lined up straight. In space, the Sun acts as the large lamp, and both Earth and the Moon cast long shadows because light travels in straight lines. However, if you move the small ball slightly up or down, the shadow misses the target entirely, which is why we do not see shadows in space every single day. Connecting this to our understanding of Earth's revolution and the Moon's orbit, the Sun, Earth, and Moon must line up perfectly at specific intersection points called nodes. By analyzing the tilt of the Moon's orbit, we discover that eclipses happen only during specific alignment seasons when the new or full moon matches these intersection points.

**Takeaway:** eclipses demand straight-line alignment, and the moon's tilted orbit is why every new moon is not an eclipse. **In the exam:** in why-not-monthly answers, name the tilt explicitly and describe the moon missing the shadow line above or below. **Check yourself:** run the walkthrough's ball-lamp-wall demo and then tilt the ball slightly - watch the wall's shadow vanish. **Why students miss it:** students assume alignment comes free - the flagged monthly puzzle. The tilt is the gatekeeper. Demonstrate the near-miss with the ball, and the rarity of eclipses becomes geometry instead of mystery.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that eclipses happen every month because the Moon orbits Earth regularly, confusing the monthly lunar cycle with the rare alignment of orbital nodes. For example, they expect a solar eclipse every new moon because the Moon is between the Sun and Earth in 2D diagrams, ignoring the 3D tilt of the orbit.

PROBLEM: A student observes that during a particular year, the Moon crosses Earth's orbital plane (the node) exactly during a new moon phase in the months of March and September. If the orbital period of the Moon is 29.5 days, calculate how many days pass between these two eclipse seasons.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given information: Node crossings happen in March and September, which are separated by approximately 6 months.
- Step 2: Identify the relationship between lunar months and eclipse seasons: Eclipse seasons occur roughly every 6 months (half an orbit of Earth around the Sun).
- Step 3: Compute the approximate number of days for half a year: $6 \text{ months} \times 30 \text{ days/month} = 180 \text{ days}$ with units of days.
- Step 4: Track unit cancellation: $\text{months} \times (\text{days} / \text{month}) = \text{days}$.
- Step 5: Verify physical reasonableness: 180 days represents roughly half of Earth's 365-day revolution, which is correct for node alignment intervals.
REASONING: The Earth-Sun-Moon alignment conditions only recur when Earth's orbital position intersects the line of nodes, which happens twice a year approximately 6 months apart.
ANSWER: 180 days

STEPS:

- Step 2: Identify the condition for partial eclipse initiation: The sum of the radii of both bodies determines the maximum allowable offset from perfect alignment.
- Step 3: Calculate the maximum allowable center-to-center offset: $\text{Offset} = (\text{Sun's size})/2 + (\text{Moon's size})/2 = 0.25^\circ + 0.25^\circ = 0.5^\circ$.
- Step 4: Track unit cancellation and addition: degrees add directly as angular measures ($0.25^\circ + 0.25^\circ = 0.5^\circ$).
- Step 5: Verify reasonableness: An offset within $0.5^\circ$ allows contact of the disk edges, which is physically consistent with angular measurements in astronomy.
REASONING: Eclipses occur not only at the exact nodal point but within a small angular window where the edges of the solar and lunar disks overlap.
