## ELI5
A price tag reading 45.75 rupees is the place value chart crossing to the right of the point: the old columns grow by tens going left, and past the point each seat shrinks by tens going right - tenths, hundredths, thousandths. A decimal is simply a fraction whose denominator is a power of ten wearing a dot. Compare by lining up the points and padding zeros, never by counting digits; and every decimal folds back into a fraction whenever you ask.

## DEFINITION
Decimals are an extension of our place value system used to represent numbers smaller than one by utilizing powers of ten in the denominator, separated from the whole number part by a decimal point. Each position to the right of the decimal point represents a fractional value divided by 10, 100, 1,000, and so on.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at a vegetable market in India buying 2 kilograms and 350 grams of potatoes, and the shopkeeper writes the price tag as ₹45.75. How do we understand what the .75 actually means in terms of parts of a whole? We begin by recalling that our place value system grows larger by multiplying by 10 as we move left, so it must shrink by dividing by 10 as we move right past the ones place. Connect this to fractions, where tenths ($\frac{1}{10}$) and hundredths ($\frac{1}{100}$) are just another way of writing parts of a whole. The key insight is that the decimal point is a fixed anchor: digits to the left are whole numbers (ones, tens, hundreds), while digits to the right are fractional parts (tenths, hundredths, thousandths).

**Takeaway:** decimals extend place value rightward: tenths, hundredths, thousandths, each seat a tenth of the last. **In the exam:** line up points and pad zeros before comparing; convert to fractions by reading the last place's name. **Check yourself:** read the market price aloud place by place - rupees, tenths, hundredths. **Why students miss it:** they read the digits after the point as one whole number, forgetting each place's shrinking value.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Convert the mixed fraction $5 \frac{3}{8}$ into a decimal number by finding an equivalent fraction with a denominator of $1,000$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $5.375$. Working steps: 
1. Identify the fractional part as $\frac{3}{8}$.
2. Determine what to multiply $8$ by to get $1,000$ ($8 \times 125 = 1,000$).
3. Multiply numerator and denominator by $125$: $\frac{3 \times 125}{8 \times 125} = \frac{375}{1,000}$.
4. Convert fraction to decimal: $5 + 0.375 = 5.375$. 
A student who gets $5.38$ likely rounded prematurely or divided incorrectly.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the number $8.452$. How many times larger is the value of the digit $4$ in the tenths place than the value of the digit $2$ in the thousandths place?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The correct answer is $200$ times larger. The value of $4$ in the tenths place is $0.4$ (or $\frac{400}{1000}$), and the value of $2$ in the thousandths place is $0.002$ ($\frac{2}{1000}$). Dividing $0.4$ by $0.002$ yields $200$. A student who says $2$ times has only looked at the face values ($4$ vs $2$) instead of their actual place values.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often assume that the longer a decimal number is, the larger its value (e.g., believing $0.452$ is greater than $0.5$ because $452$ is greater than $5$). They ignore the place value of the first digit after the decimal point (the tenths place) and treat the decimal part as a standalone whole number.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to pad shorter decimals with zeros on the right so that every number being compared has the same number of decimal places (e.g., comparing $0.5$ and $0.452$ becomes comparing $0.500$ and $0.452$). Ask them: "Which is larger, $500$ parts out of $1,000$ or $452$ parts out of $1,000$?" Emphasize that we always compare place values from left to right, starting with the tenths place.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Rahul bought a notebook for ₹35.5, a pen for ₹12.75, and an eraser for ₹2.025. Write the total amount in words and express the sum of the paisa parts as a fraction of a rupee in its lowest terms.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Align the decimal numbers by their decimal points to add them properly: $35.500 + 12.750 + 2.025$.
- Step 2: Add the thousandths place: $0 + 0 + 5 = 5$.
- Step 3: Add the hundredths place: $0 + 5 + 2 = 7$; add the tenths place: $5 + 7 + 0 = 12$ (write $2$, carry over $1$ to the ones place).
- Step 4: Add the whole number part with the carry over: $35 + 12 + 2 + 1 = 50$, giving a total of $50.275$ rupees.
- Step 5: Express the decimal fraction part ($0.275$) as a fraction: $\frac{275}{1,000}$, and simplify by dividing numerator and denominator by their HCF ($25$): $\frac{275 \div 25}{1,000 \div 25} = \frac{11}{40}$.
REASONING: Aligning decimal points ensures that we add like place values together (tenths with tenths, hundredths with hundredths). Converting the decimal remainder to a fraction connects our place value representation back to fractional parts of a whole rupee.
ANSWER: ₹50.275 (Fifty rupees and two hundred seventy-five thousandths of a rupee, with the fractional part being $\frac{11}{40}$)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Arrange the following measurements in descending order: $4.05\text{ m}$, $4.5\text{ m}$, $4.452\text{ m}$, and $4.005\text{ m}$.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify all numbers and count their decimal places to find the maximum length, which is three decimal places.
- Step 2: Pad each number with trailing zeros so all have three decimal places: $4.050\text{ m}$, $4.500\text{ m}$, $4.452\text{ m}$, and $4.005\text{ m}$.
- Step 3: Compare the whole number parts first (all are $4$), then compare the tenths place: $5 > 4 > 0$, so $4.500$ is the largest.
- Step 4: Compare numbers with a tenths digit of $4$ ($4.452$) and tenths digit of $0$ ($4.050$ and $4.005$). Between $4.050$ and $4.005$, compare the hundredths place: $5 > 0$, so $4.050 > 4.005$.
- Step 5: Order from largest to smallest: $4.5\text{ m} > 4.452\text{ m} > 4.05\text{ m} > 4.005\text{ m}$.
REASONING: Padding with zeros creates uniformity in length, preventing the common error where shorter strings of digits are mistakenly judged by whole-number intuition rather than place value magnitude.
ANSWER: $4.5\text{ m}, 4.452\text{ m}, 4.05\text{ m}, 4.005\text{ m}$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A science experiment requires mixing water until a beaker weighs exactly $6 \frac{3}{25}\text{ kg}$. Priya reads her digital balance and it shows $6.115\text{ kg}$. Find the difference between the required weight and Priya's reading, and state which place value contributes most to this difference.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Convert the mixed fraction $6 \frac{3}{25}$ into a decimal by making the denominator $100$: $\frac{3 \times 4}{25 \times 4} = \frac{12}{100} = 0.12$, so the target weight is $6.12\text{ kg}$ (or $6.120\text{ kg}$).
- Step 2: Set up the subtraction of Priya's reading from the target weight: $6.120 - 6.115$.
- Step 3: Subtract thousandths: $10 - 5 = 5$ (borrowing $1$ hundredth as $10$ thousandths).
- Step 4: Subtract hundredths: $1 - 1 = 0$; subtract tenths: $1 - 1 = 0$; whole numbers: $6 - 6 = 0$, yielding a difference of $0.005\text{ kg}$.
- Step 5: Analyze place values of the difference $0.005$: tenths = $0$, hundredths = $0$, thousandths = $5$. Therefore, the thousandths place contributes most (and exclusively) to the difference.
REASONING: Converting mixed fractions to decimals with matching place values allows direct vertical subtraction. Analyzing the resulting difference place-by-place reveals the exact magnitude of error in Priya's reading.
ANSWER: Difference is $0.005\text{ kg}$, and the thousandths place contributes most.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A school running track timer records three students' 100m sprint times: Amit: $14.2\text{ s}$, Sneha: $14.05\text{ s}$, and Rohan: $14.125\text{ s}$. 
(a) Who won the race (recorded the lowest time)? 
(b) Sneha claims she beat Amit by $0.25$ seconds because $20$ (from $14.20$) minus $05$ (from $14.05$) is $15$, wait—explain the error in her subtraction reasoning and calculate the correct time difference between Amit and Sneha.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying decimal magnitudes and error identification
STEPS:
- Step 1: Line up the times with equal decimal places: Amit = $14.200\text{ s}$, Sneha = $14.050\text{ s}$, Rohan = $14.125\text{ s}$.
- Step 2: Compare whole and decimal parts to find the minimum time: $14.050 < 14.125 < 14.200$, so Sneha won.
- Step 3: Analyze Sneha's error: She incorrectly subtracted the decimal digits as whole numbers ($20 - 5 = 15$) instead of respecting place value columns ($14.200 - 14.050$).
- Step 4: Compute the correct difference: $14.200 - 14.050 = 0.150\text{ seconds}$.
ANSWER: (a) Sneha won the race. (b) Sneha's error was treating the tenths and hundredths digits as a whole number $20$ and $5$ without aligning place values; the correct difference is $0.15\text{ seconds}$.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A chemist measures three liquid chemical samples in graduated cylinders: Sample A is represented as $3 + \frac{4}{10} + \frac{7}{1,000}$, Sample B is $3.42\text{ L}$, and Sample C is three litres and forty-five hundredths of a litre. 
(a) Write each sample in standard decimal notation. 
(b) Order the samples from greatest to least volume. 
(c) If the chemist needs to add water to the smallest sample so that its volume equals the largest sample, how much water must be added?
COMPETENCY: Translating between expanded fractional forms, standard decimal forms, and multi-step problem solving
STEPS:
- Step 1: Convert Sample A to standard decimal: $3 + 0.4 + 0.007 = 3.407\text{ L}$.
- Step 2: Write Sample B in standard decimal: $3.420\text{ L}$.
- Step 3: Convert Sample C to standard decimal: $3 + \frac{45}{100} = 3.450\text{ L}$.
- Step 4: Order from greatest to least by comparing place values: $3.450 > 3.420 > 3.407$, so Sample C > Sample B > Sample A.
- Step 5: Find the required water addition for the smallest sample (Sample A) to reach the largest (Sample C): $3.450 - 3.407 = 0.043\text{ L}$.
ANSWER: (a) Sample A = $3.407\text{ L}$, Sample B = $3.420\text{ L}$, Sample C = $3.450\text{ L}$. (b) Order: Sample C, Sample B, Sample A. (c) $0.043\text{ L}$ must be added.
DIFFICULTY: hard
