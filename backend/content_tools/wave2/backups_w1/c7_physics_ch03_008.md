## ELI5
Put a wet plate in the courtyard sun: within the hour the water has gone - solar radiation did the lifting, evaporation sent it skyward. That is the water cycle's opening move: sun radiates, oceans and puddles evaporate, vapour rides convection currents upward, cools, condenses into clouds, and falls home as rain. Heat transfer in the sky, water on a round trip - the planet's oldest courier service, powered entirely by the sun.

## DEFINITION
The water cycle is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the Earth's surface, driven primarily by heat transfer from the Sun through radiation, evaporation, convection, and condensation. There are no specific SI units for the water cycle as a whole, but the rates of evaporation and precipitation are measured in units like millimeters per day ($mm/day$) or liters per square meter ($L/m^2$).

Building on our prior understanding of how heat transfers through convection and radiation, we can now see how these thermodynamic principles power the entire global water cycle. ## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which method of heat transfer is primarily responsible for the Sun heating the Earth's oceans to start the water cycle?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Radiation is responsible for transferring heat from the Sun through the vacuum of space to the Earth's oceans. A wrong answer like "conduction" or "convection" reveals a failure to remember that heat needs a physical medium to travel via conduction or convection, whereas radiation can travel through empty space.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why does warm water vapor rise into the upper atmosphere after evaporating from the sea?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Warm water vapor rises because heating makes the air and vapor less dense, causing it to ascend through convection currents. A wrong answer stating that vapor rises because "light things always float upward without any force or heat" shows a misconception that ignores the role of density differences caused by thermal expansion and convection.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the heat transfer process happening during the evaporation of surface water with the process happening during cloud formation high in the sky.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
During evaporation at the surface, liquid water absorbs heat energy from the surroundings to turn into vapor. Conversely, during cloud formation (condensation) high in the sky, water vapor cools down and releases heat energy into the atmosphere. A wrong answer claiming that both processes absorb heat shows confusion between vaporization (which absorbs heat) and condensation (which releases heat).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that clouds float in the sky because they are made of gas that is naturally lighter than air. Evaluate this statement based on heat transfer and states of matter.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The statement is incorrect because clouds are not made of gas; they are composed of tiny liquid water droplets and ice crystals formed when water vapor condenses. They stay aloft temporarily because rising convection currents of warm air support these microscopic droplets against gravity. A wrong answer accepting the student's claim shows a failure to recognize that phase change produces liquid droplets, not a lighter-than-air gas.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple model using everyday kitchen items to demonstrate both evaporation (driven by heat) and condensation in a closed container.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good design involves placing a small bowl of warm colored water inside a larger, wide bowl, covering the top tightly with plastic wrap, and placing a small pebble in the center of the plastic wrap right above the small bowl. When placed in sunlight, radiation heats the water, causing evaporation; the vapor rises, hits the cool plastic wrap (condensation), and drips back down into the bowl like rain. A poor design omits the heat source or the cooling surface, showing a lack of understanding of how phase change requires both thermal energy input and removal.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that clouds are filled with hot steam or gas because they see "rising vapor," leading them to think clouds float entirely due to high temperature. In reality, clouds consist of condensed liquid droplets and ice crystals that have cooled down significantly compared to the surface air, and they stay afloat only due to upward convective air currents and their extremely small size.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A shallow puddle containing $200\ g$ of water absorbs solar radiation at a constant rate, causing it to evaporate completely in $5\ hours$. Calculate the average mass of water evaporated per minute ($g/min$).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given quantities with units: Total mass of water $m = 200\ g$, Total time in hours $t_{hours} = 5\ h$.
- Step 2: Convert time from hours to minutes because the requested unit is $g/min$: $t = 5 \times 60\ min = 300\ min$.
- Step 3: Set up the formula for average rate of evaporation ($R$): $R = \frac{m}{t}$.
- Step 4: Substitute the values with units into the formula: $R = \frac{200\ g}{300\ min}$.
- Step 5: Compute the division, cancel units, and verify reasonableness: $R = 0.67\ g/min$, which is a physically reasonable rate for a small puddle evaporating over a few hours.
REASONING: Converting units correctly ensures the final rate matches the requested physical dimensions of mass per minute rather than mass per hour. Dividing total evaporated mass by total elapsed time gives the average rate over the entire duration.
ANSWER: $0.67\ g/min$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: During a demonstration, $50\ mL$ of water vapor condenses on a cold metal plate, releasing thermal energy. If the density of water is $1.0\ g/mL$, calculate the mass of water collected in kilograms ($kg$).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given volume ($V = 50\ mL$) and density ($\rho = 1.0\ g/mL$), and what to find (mass in $kg$).
- Step 2: Recall the formula relating mass, volume, and density: $m = V \times \rho$.
- Step 3: Substitute the given values with units: $m = 50\ mL \times 1.0\ g/mL = 50\ g$.
- Step 4: Convert the mass from grams to kilograms by dividing by $1000\ g/kg$: $m = \frac{50\ g}{1000\ g/kg}$.
- Step 5: Compute the final value and track unit cancellation: $m = 0.05\ kg$, which is a sensible mass for a small collected sample of condensation.
REASONING: Density connects volume to mass. Once mass is found in grams, a standard unit conversion factor is applied to reach the SI unit for mass ($kg$).
ANSWER: $0.05\ kg$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A solar water heater basin in a village receives thermal energy from the Sun at a rate of $200\ Joules\ per\ second\ ($W$). Over a period of $1\ hour$, how much total heat energy (in kilojoules, $kJ$) is absorbed by the water basin to drive evaporation?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given power (rate of heat absorption) $P = 200\ J/s$ and time $t = 1\ h$.
- Step 2: Convert time $t$ from hours to seconds: $t = 1\ h \times 3600\ s/h = 3600\ s$.
- Step 3: Identify the energy formula relating power, energy ($E$), and time: $E = P \times t$.
- Step 4: Substitute the values with units: $E = 200\ J/s \times 3600\ s$.
- Step 5: Compute the energy in Joules and track unit cancellation: $E = 720,000\ J$.
- Step 6: Convert Joules to kilojoules by dividing by $1000$: $E = \frac{720,000\ J}{1000\ J/kJ} = 720\ kJ$, which is a realistic amount of thermal energy transferred over an hour.
REASONING: Power represents energy transferred per unit second. Multiplying power by total time in seconds yields total energy absorbed, which is then converted into convenient engineering units ($kJ$).
ANSWER: $720\ kJ$
