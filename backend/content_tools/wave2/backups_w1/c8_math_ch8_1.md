## ELI5
Per cent simply means per hundred. Say a class scores seventy-two per hundred in a test and every listener instantly pictures the same thing — that is the power of the percentage: it drags every fraction onto the same measuring scale of one hundred, so unlike things become comparable. The symbol % is shorthand for divided by one hundred. Scores, discounts, battery levels, ingredients — all become apples-to-apples once each is expressed as its share of a hundred.

## DEFINITION
Percentage is a way of expressing a number as a fraction of 100. The symbol % means "per hundred", so x% = x/100. It represents a part-to-whole relationship where the whole is always considered as 100 equal parts.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can explain the meaning of the % symbol, interpret percentage statements in real-world contexts (e.g., "20% discount" means ₹20 reduction for every ₹100), and represent a given fraction or ratio as a percentage by scaling to a denominator of 100.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the definition of percentage as "per hundred", the % symbol, and the conceptual link between fractions/ratios and percentages. It does NOT cover procedural conversion algorithms (e.g., multiply by 100), finding percentage of a quantity, percentage increase/decrease, or profit/loss calculations — those are addressed in subsequent nodes.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you and your friend take different tests: you score 36 out of 50, your friend scores 72 out of 100. Who did better? Comparing 36/50 and 72/100 directly is hard because the wholes (50 vs 100) are different. But if we scale your score to a base of 100 — multiply numerator and denominator by 2 — you get 72/100. Now both scores are out of 100: you both got 72 per hundred. That "per hundred" is what percentage means. The % symbol is just shorthand for "divided by 100". So 72% = 72/100. This common base of 100 lets us compare any two quantities fairly, whether it's test scores, discounts, or ingredient proportions.

Takeaway: x% = x/100 — the symbol is a standing division by a hundred, and percentages exist to put different wholes on one comparable scale. Exam format: expect 'interpret the statement' and 'express the fraction as a percentage' — interpret means translate to per-hundred language in a sentence. Verification beat: check any conversion by asking 'out of one hundred, how many?'. Why students miss it: they treat % as a decoration rather than an operation — reading % as ÷100 turns interpretation and conversion into the same single move.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What does the symbol % mean? Write the meaning in words and as a mathematical expression.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The symbol % means "per hundred". Mathematically, x% = x/100. For example, 25% = 25/100 = 1/4. A wrong answer like "% means divide by 10" or "% is just a label" reveals the student treats % as a decorative symbol rather than a mathematical operator meaning "out of 100".
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
A shopkeeper says, "This shirt has a 50% discount." A student says, "That means the price becomes half." Is the student correct? Explain why or why not using the definition of percentage.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Yes, the student is correct. 50% means 50/100 = 1/2, so the discount is half the original price. The new price is original minus half = half the original. A wrong answer (e.g., "50% means ₹50 off") reveals the student thinks percentage is an absolute amount, not a relative fraction of the whole. They miss that % always refers to a part of 100 parts of the whole quantity.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
In a class of 40 students, 30 are present today. What percentage of the class is present? Show your steps using the definition of percentage.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Fraction present = 30/40. Step 2: Simplify to 3/4. Step 3: Convert to denominator 100: (3 × 25)/(4 × 25) = 75/100. Step 4: By definition, 75/100 = 75%. So 75% of the class is present. A wrong answer like 30% (using numerator only) or 7.5% (dividing 30 by 4) reveals the student doesn't grasp that percentage requires scaling the fraction to a denominator of 100.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the two statements: (a) "20% of the students in Class A are girls." (b) "The ratio of girls to boys in Class B is 1:4." Which class has a higher percentage of girls? Explain without calculating the exact percentage for Class B.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Class A has 20% girls. In Class B, ratio girls:boys = 1:4 means for every 1 girl there are 4 boys, so total parts = 5. Girls are 1 out of 5 parts = 1/5 = 20/100 = 20%. Both have the same percentage of girls (20%). A wrong answer (e.g., "Class B has more because 1:4 looks bigger") reveals the student cannot translate a part-to-part ratio into a part-to-whole fraction and then to a percentage. They treat ratio and percentage as unrelated forms.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Rahul solved: "Convert 3/5 to percentage." His working: 3/5 = 0.6 = 0.6%. Is his answer correct? If not, identify the error and give the correct percentage.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Rahul is incorrect. 3/5 = 0.6 as a decimal, but 0.6% means 0.6/100 = 0.006, not 0.6. The error: he wrote the decimal form but kept the % symbol without multiplying by 100. Correct: 3/5 = 60/100 = 60%. A wrong evaluation (e.g., "Rahul is correct because 0.6 = 0.6%") reveals the student confuses decimal representation with percentage representation and doesn't understand that % means divide by 100.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-life situation (using rupees, metres, litres, or school context) where using percentage is more useful than using a fraction or decimal. Explain why percentage is better in your example.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: "A 1-litre juice bottle says 'Contains 12% fruit juice'." This is better than saying "Contains 3/25 fruit juice" or "Contains 0.12 litres fruit juice" because percentage instantly tells a buyer that in every 100 ml, 12 ml is real juice — easy to compare across brands. A poor answer (e.g., "50% off sale" without explanation) just states a percentage without justifying why it's more useful than "half price" or "0.5 discount". It shows the student can't articulate the communicative advantage of a standard base of 100.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students treat % as a unit label (like "cm" or "kg") rather than an operator meaning "divided by 100". For example, they write 0.5% = 0.5 (dropping the % but keeping the value) or think 120% is impossible because "you can't have more than 100%". The exact wrong step: when converting 0.75 to percentage, they write 0.75% instead of 75%, because they append % without multiplying by 100. They fail to see that 0.75 = 75/100 = 75%.
## ON_FAILURE
Use a 10×10 grid (100 squares). Shade 1 square → 1/100 = 1%. Shade 25 squares → 25/100 = 25%. Shade 75 squares → 75/100 = 75%. Then ask: "If I shade all 100 squares, what percentage? 100%. If I shade 120 squares (needs another grid), what percentage? 120%." This visual proves % means "number of shaded squares per 100 squares". Then practice: write 0.6 as 60/100 = 60%, not 0.6%. Emphasise: "To change a decimal to %, multiply by 100 and add %."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: In a school assembly, 45 out of 60 students are wearing spectacles. What percentage of the students are wearing spectacles?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the part (students wearing spectacles = 45) and the whole (total students = 60). Write the fraction: 45/60.
- Step 2: Simplify the fraction to lowest terms: divide numerator and denominator by 15 → 3/4.
- Step 3: Use the definition of percentage: we need an equivalent fraction with denominator 100. Multiply numerator and denominator of 3/4 by 25 → (3 × 25)/(4 × 25) = 75/100.
- Step 4: By definition, 75/100 = 75%. So 75% of the students are wearing spectacles.
- Step 5: Verify: 75% of 60 = (75/100) × 60 = 45. Matches the given part. Answer makes sense.
REASONING: Percentage is defined as a fraction with denominator 100. By simplifying 45/60 to 3/4 and then scaling to 75/100, we directly apply the definition. The verification step uses the meaning "x% of y = (x/100) × y" to close the loop.
ANSWER: 75%
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A recipe for 5 people uses 250 g of flour. If you want to express the flour quantity as a percentage of the total recipe weight (assume total weight is 1 kg = 1000 g), what percentage is flour?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the part (flour = 250 g) and the whole (total recipe weight = 1000 g). Ensure same units: both in grams.
- Step 2: Write the fraction: 250/1000.
- Step 3: Simplify the fraction: divide numerator and denominator by 250 → 1/4.
- Step 4: Convert to denominator 100: multiply numerator and denominator by 25 → 25/100.
- Step 5: By definition, 25/100 = 25%. So flour is 25% of the total recipe weight.
REASONING: The definition of percentage requires the part and whole in the same units. Here both are in grams, so the fraction 250/1000 directly gives the proportion. Simplifying to 1/4 and scaling to 25/100 applies the "per hundred" meaning.
ANSWER: 25%
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: The ratio of boys to girls in a class is 3:2. If 5 more girls join the class, the ratio becomes 3:3 (i.e., 1:1). What was the original percentage of boys in the class? What is the new percentage of boys?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Let original number of boys = 3x, girls = 2x (from ratio 3:2). Total = 5x.
- Step 2: Original percentage of boys = (3x / 5x) × 100% = (3/5) × 100% = 60/100 × 100% = 60%.
- Step 3: After 5 girls join, new girls = 2x + 5. New ratio boys:girls = 3x : (2x + 5) = 1:1.
- Step 4: Set up equation: 3x = 2x + 5 → x = 5.
- Step 5: Original total = 5x = 25 students. Original boys = 15, girls = 10. Check: 15/25 = 60%.
- Step 6: New girls = 10 + 5 = 15. New total = 25 + 5 = 30. New percentage of boys = (15/30) × 100% = 50%.
REASONING: This problem combines ratio (prior concept) with percentage definition. The ratio gives a part-to-part comparison; percentage requires part-to-whole. We used algebra to find actual numbers, then applied the percentage definition (fraction with denominator 100). The change in percentage (60% → 50%) shows how adding to the whole changes the proportion.
ANSWER: Original percentage of boys = 60%; New percentage of boys = 50%

QUESTION: Rahul says, "0.5% and 50% are the same because both have the digits 5 and 0." Is Rahul correct? Explain your reasoning using the definition of percentage.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall the definition: x% = x/100.
- Step 2: Write 0.5% as a fraction: 0.5/100 = 0.005.
- Step 3: Write 50% as a fraction: 50/100 = 0.5.
- Step 4: Compare: 0.005 ≠ 0.5. They are not the same.
- Step 5: Explain Rahul's error: he confused the decimal representation (0.5) with the percentage representation (0.5%). The % symbol means divide by 100, so 0.5% is much smaller than 50%.
ANSWER: Rahul is incorrect. 0.5% = 0.5/100 = 0.005, while 50% = 50/100 = 0.5. The % symbol changes the value by a factor of 100.
DIFFICULTY: easy
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A bowl contains 20 red marbles and 30 blue marbles. If 10 more red marbles are added, does the percentage of red marbles increase, decrease, or stay the same? Justify your answer without calculating the exact percentage.
COMPETENCY: Relational reasoning without computing full values
STEPS:
- Step 1: Original: red = 20, total = 50. Fraction red = 20/50 = 2/5.
- Step 2: After adding 10 red: red = 30, total = 60. Fraction red = 30/60 = 1/2.
- Step 3: Compare 2/5 and 1/2 without converting to percentage: cross-multiply → 2×2 = 4, 5×1 = 5. Since 4 < 5, 2/5 < 1/2.
- Step 4: Therefore, the fraction of red marbles increased. Since percentage is just the fraction scaled to denominator 100, the percentage also increased.
- Step 5: Reason conceptually: Adding only red marbles increases the proportion of red in the whole. The percentage must increase.
ANSWER: The percentage of red marbles increases. Originally red was 2/5 (40%) of the marbles; after adding only red marbles, red becomes 1/2 (50%) of the marbles. Adding to the numerator only (part) while the denominator (whole) also increases but by a smaller proportion raises the fraction.
DIFFICULTY: medium
