## ELI5
Finding 3.4 on a ruler is a two-step walk: stand at 3, then slide four small ticks to the right, because the gap between whole numbers is cut into ten equal tenths. Each tick is one-tenth of the whole, so the fourth tick past 3 lands exactly on 3.4 - still closer to 3 than to the halfway mark. Zoom further in and the same gap splits into hundredths. A number line is a ruler without the wood: divide the gap, count the ticks, and every decimal finds its exact seat.

## DEFINITION
Locating decimals on the number line involves identifying the precise position of a decimal number between two integers by dividing the unit distance into ten equal tenths segments and further into hundredths segments. This visual representation extends our ability to order and compare decimal values beyond whole numbers.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can accurately draw, divide, and locate any given decimal number up to two decimal places (tenths and hundredths) on a standard number line, and can correctly identify a decimal represented by a specific point marked on a number line.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the representation and location of terminating decimal numbers up to hundredths on a number line between integers or between two decimals. It does NOT cover irrational numbers or repeating decimals.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are measuring a piece of ribbon for your craft project, and it measures 3.4 cm long. When you look at your plastic ruler, you see whole centimeter marks (0, 1, 2, 3, 4) and smaller ticks between them. How do we show 3.4 on a number line? Just like we learned to place fractions on a number line by dividing the space between whole numbers into equal parts, we do the exact same thing for decimals. Because our decimal system is based on powers of ten, we divide the unit distance between whole numbers (like 3 and 4) into 10 equal smaller intervals, where each tiny mark represents one-tenth ($0.1$). To locate $3.4$, we start at whole number $3$ and move forward $4$ small tick marks to the right. The key insight is that every decimal is simply a fraction with a denominator of $10$ or $100$, so decimal placement follows the exact rules of fractional number line division.

**Takeaway:** a number line shows decimals as exact addresses: split the unit gap into ten equal tenths, then count ticks. **In the exam:** to plot, name the two neighbouring integers, divide the gap, then count tick marks; to read a marked point, count back. **Check yourself:** draw the gap between two whole numbers, mark the tenths, and place 3.4 with your own hand. **Why students miss it:** they count ticks from the wrong integer or forget the tenths division and park decimals between marks.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The interval must be divided into $10$ equal subdivisions because the first decimal place represents tenths ($\frac{1}{10}$), requiring the whole unit to be split into ten equal parts. A student who says $100$ is confusing tenths with hundredths, and a student who says $4$ is mistakenly looking at the digit in the tenths place as the number of divisions rather than its fractional value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A point $P$ is marked exactly $7$ small tick marks to the right of $4$ on a number line divided into tenths between each whole number. What decimal number does point $P$ represent?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The decimal number represented is $4.7$. Working steps: 1) Identify the starting whole number integer, which is $4$. 2) Count the number of subdivision steps to the right, which is $7$ tenths ($0.7$). 3) Add the parts together: $4 + 0.7 = 4.7$. A student who answers $7.4$ has swapped the whole number and decimal parts, while a student who answers $4.07$ has confused tenths with hundredths place value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the locations of $3.2$ and $3.02$ on a number line. Which one appears further to the right, and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
$3.2$ appears further to the right than $3.02$. Working/Reasoning: $3.2$ is equivalent to $3.20$, and when comparing $3.20$ and $3.02$, the tenths digit of $3.20$ is $2$, whereas the tenths digit of $3.02$ is $0$, making $3.2$ larger and thus positioned further right. A student who answers $3.02$ is misled by the length of the digits, thinking more digits mean a larger number without aligning place values.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often miscount the tick marks on a number line because they count the *lines* (lines drawn) rather than the *spaces* (intervals between lines), or they confuse the start of the interval. For example, when locating $1.3$ between $1$ and $2$, a student might count the tick for $1$ as "one" and end up placing $1.3$ on the mark meant for $1.2$ or $1.4$, failing to realize that the starting integer $1$ is the baseline ($1.0$) and the first tick to the right is $1.1$.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to use a "hop" method instead of counting tick marks directly. Say: "Put your pencil tip on the whole number $1$. Do not count that line as a jump. As you move your pencil to the first small line to the right, say 'one hop, that's $1.1$'. Move to the next line, say 'two hops, that's $1.2$'. Move to the third line, say 'three hops, that's $1.3$'." This finger-tracking movement ensures they count the intervals (spaces) rather than the vertical tick marks.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Priya wants to locate the point representing $2.8$ kg of rice on a number line marked in whole kilograms. Describe the step-by-step process to mark this point.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the two whole numbers between which $2.8$ lies, which are $2$ and $3$.
- Step 2: Divide the distance between $2$ and $3$ on the number line into $10$ equal sub-intervals representing tenths ($0.1$ each).
- Step 3: Start at the integer $2$ (representing $2.0$) and count $8$ subdivision tick marks to the right.
- Step 4: Mark the exact position at the $8$th tick mark and label it as $2.8$.
- Step 5: Verify that $2.8$ is $2$ whole units plus $\frac{8}{10}$ of a unit, which is just $2$ ticks short of $3.0$.
REASONING: Decimals represent fractional parts of a whole. By splitting the unit interval into 10 equal parts, each tick represents one-tenth, allowing us to count up the exact decimal value from the lower integer bound.
ANSWER: Point located at $8$ tenths to the right of $2$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Rahul is examining a zoomed-in section of a number line between $4.5$ and $4.6$. He needs to locate the decimal $4.53$. How should he proceed?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recognize that $4.53$ has a hundredths component, so it lies between $4.5$ ($4.50$) and $4.6$ ($4.60$).
- Step 2: Imagine or draw a magnified interval between $4.5$ and $4.6$ divided into $10$ equal sub-parts, where each tick represents one-hundredth ($0.01$).
- Step 3: Start at $4.50$ and move forward by $3$ tiny tick marks to represent the $3$ hundredths ($0.03$).
- Step 4: Compute the position algebraically: $4.5 + 3 \times 0.01 = 4.5 + 0.03 = 4.53$.
- Step 5: Verify the position is $3$ small divisions past $4.5$ and $7$ small divisions before $4.6$.
REASONING: When a decimal has two decimal places, we must zoom in between the two tenths values and subdivide that tenth into ten hundredth parts.
ANSWER: Point located at $3$ hundredths divisions to the right of $4.5$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A thermometer scale acts like a vertical number line. The mercury level is resting exactly $4$ small tick marks below $14.0$ degrees, where each unit between integers is divided into $10$ parts. What decimal temperature does the mercury show?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the upper integer anchor point, which is $14.0$ degrees.
- Step 2: Note that the reading is *below* (to the left or lower on the scale) $14.0$, requiring subtraction.
- Step 3: Determine the value of $4$ small tick marks: $4 \times 0.1 = 0.4$ degrees.
- Step 4: Subtract this value from the anchor point: $14.0 - 0.4 = 13.6$.
- Step 5: Verify the calculation by counting up: $13.6$ plus $0.4$ equals $14.0$.
REASONING: When locating decimals moving downwards or backwards from a whole number on a number line, we subtract the decimal value of the tick intervals from the upper integer bound.
ANSWER: $13.6$ degrees Celsius.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two students, Amit and Sneha, are asked to locate $7.35$ on a number line. Amit says, "I will go to $7.3$ and then take $5$ big jumps to the right." Sneha says, "I will go to $7.3$, look between $7.3$ and $7.4$, and take $5$ tiny jumps representing hundredths." Who is correct, and what is the mathematical flaw in the other student's method?
COMPETENCY: Evaluating reasoning and error identification in decimal representation
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze Amit's method: taking $5$ big jumps from $7.3$ would mean adding $5$ units (or $5$ tenths depending on interpretation), which moves way past $7.35$.
- Step 2: Analyze Sneha's method: looking between $7.3$ and $7.4$ (which are $7.30$ and $7.40$) and moving $5$ hundredths ($0.05$) gives $7.30 + 0.05 = 7.35$.
- Step 3: Conclude that Sneha is correct because $7.35$ has a hundredths digit that requires subdividing the interval between tenths, not whole integers.
- Step 4: Formulate the final explanation highlighting place value scaling.
ANSWER: Sneha is correct. Amit's method treats the hundredth digit $5$ as whole units or tenth units, which would land the point at an incorrect position like $7.8$ or $12.3$, whereas $7.35$ requires zooming in between the tenths $7.3$ and $7.4$.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A hiking trail marker in the mountains is located at kilometer $12.7$. A rest stop is built exactly halfway between kilometer $12$ and kilometer $13$. A water fountain is placed at kilometer $12.75$. Without drawing the entire scale, explain the exact sequential order from left to right in which a hiker walking from kilometer $12$ to $13$ will encounter these three landmarks, and justify their relative positions using decimals.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world milestones into ordered decimal number line positions
STEPS:
- Step 1: Assign decimal coordinate values to all three landmarks: Rest stop = $12.5$ (halfway between $12$ and $13$), Trail marker = $12.7$, Water fountain = $12.75$.
- Step 2: Convert all decimals to equivalent hundredths for precise comparison: Rest stop = $12.50$, Trail marker = $12.70$, Water fountain = $12.75$.
- Step 3: Compare the tenths digits: $12.50$ has a tenths digit of $5$, while both $12.70$ and $12.75$ have a tenths digit of $7$, meaning the rest stop comes first.
- Step 4: Compare the two landmarks at $7$ tenths: $12.70$ (Trail marker) comes before $12.75$ (Water fountain).
- Step 5: Write the final ordered sequence from left to right.
ANSWER: The hiker will encounter them in this order from left to right: Rest stop ($12.5$), Trail marker ($12.7$), and Water fountain ($12.75$). This order is justified because comparing their decimal values ($12.50 < 12.70 < 12.75$) places them sequentially along the number line axis.
DIFFICULTY: hard
