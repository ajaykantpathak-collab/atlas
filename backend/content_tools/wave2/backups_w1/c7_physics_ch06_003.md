## DEFINITION
The revolution of the Earth is the movement of the Earth in a nearly circular or elliptical orbit around the Sun, completing one full circuit in approximately 365.25 days (one year). This orbital motion, combined with Earth's axial tilt, is measured in time units such as days and years, and is responsible for the changing seasons.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine walking in a complete large circle around a bonfire in your school courtyard, returning exactly to your starting point after one complete lap. In a similar way, the Earth travels along a fixed path called an orbit around the Sun. To understand this motion, we first observe how the calendar year is marked by the Sun's changing position relative to background stars over many months. Connecting this to our prior study of time measurement and motion, the Earth's trip is a regular, repeating journey. The key principle to remember is that one complete revolution around the Sun defines one Earth year.

**Takeaway:** revolution is the year: one lap around the sun in about 365.25 days - the quarter-day that leap years sweep up. **In the exam:** distinguish the two motions explicitly - spin gives the day, orbit gives the year - before answering any calendar question. **Check yourself:** complete the walkthrough's bonfire circuit and ask what, in Earth's case, marks the return to the start. **Why students miss it:** students round the year to 365 and lose the ledger - the flagged approximation. The quarter-day is real and compound interest: four of them demand a leap day. Keep the fraction visible and the calendar's odd rule becomes obvious arithmetic.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do we say the Earth follows an orbit rather than moving in a straight line through space?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
If a space probe travels along the Earth's orbit at an average speed of about 30 km/s, what distance does it cover in 2 seconds?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps: 
1. Identify given values: Speed ($v$) = 30 km/s, Time ($t$) = 2 s. 
2. Identify the formula: Distance ($d$) = $v \times t$. 
3. Substitute values with units: $d = 30 \text{ km/s} \times 2 \text{ s}$. 
4. Compute and track unit cancellation: $d = 60 \text{ km}$ (seconds cancel out). 
5. Verify physical reasonableness: 60 km in 2 seconds at orbital speeds is correct. 
Answer: 60 km. A student who divides speed by time confuses the fundamental relationship between distance, speed, and time.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
How does the Earth's revolution around the Sun differ fundamentally from its rotation on its axis?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Revolution is the Earth's movement travelling around the Sun in an external orbit taking 365.25 days, which causes the year, whereas rotation is the spinning of Earth on its own internal imaginary axis taking 24 hours, which causes day and night. A student who claims both take 24 hours confuses the daily rotation cycle with the yearly revolution cycle.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the statement: "If the Earth's distance from the Sun suddenly doubled while maintaining the same orbital speed, a year would still last exactly 365 days." Is this correct or incorrect, and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
This statement is incorrect. If the Earth were farther from the Sun, the circumference of its orbit would be larger, and weaker gravitational pull would result in a slower orbital speed, meaning a complete revolution would take much longer than 365 days. A student who says it remains the same ignores the geometric relationship between orbital radius, circumference, and speed.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently confuse Earth's rotation (spinning on its axis) with its revolution (orbiting the Sun), often believing that day and night are caused by the Earth traveling around the Sun rather than by its rotation. Another common error is thinking that the Earth's orbit is a long, stretched-out oval (extreme ellipse) that brings it drastically closer to the Sun in summer and farther in winter, whereas the orbit is actually very nearly circular.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this misconception, take a globe and first spin it in place while holding a torch stationary to show how light hits one side (Rotation/Day-Night). Then, walk the spinning globe in a large circle around the torch (Revolution/Year). Emphasize that spinning on the spot creates the 24-hour day, while traveling around the central light creates the 365-day year.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: The Earth travels in an approximately circular orbit of radius $1.5 \times 10^8 \text{ km}$ around the Sun. If its average orbital speed is $30 \text{ km/s}$, calculate the approximate time in seconds for one complete revolution, using $\pi = 3$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: radius $r = 1.5 \times 10^8 \text{ km} = 150,000,000 \text{ km}$, speed $v = 30 \text{ km/s}$, and $\pi = 3$. We need to find time $T$.
- step 2: Identify the formula for the circumference of the orbit $C = 2 \pi r$ and time $T = \frac{C}{v}$.
- step 3: Substitute values to find circumference: $C = 2 \times 3 \times 150,000,000 \text{ km} = 900,000,000 \text{ km}$.
- step 4: Substitute circumference and speed into the time formula: $T = \frac{900,000,000 \text{ km}}{30 \text{ km/s}}$.
- step 5: Compute and track unit cancellation: $T = 30,000,000 \text{ s}$ (the km units cancel out, leaving seconds in the numerator).
REASONING: By treating the Earth's orbit as a circle, we first calculate the total path length (circumference) using geometry. Dividing this total distance by the constant orbital speed gives the total time required to complete the journey.
ANSWER: 30,000,000 s (approximately 365 days)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A satellite orbiting close to Earth travels at $8 \text{ km/s}$ along a circular orbit of circumference $48,000 \text{ km}$. Calculate the time taken for one complete orbit in seconds.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given quantities with units: circumference $C = 48,000 \text{ km}$, speed $v = 8 \text{ km/s}$. Find time $T$.
- step 2: Select the appropriate formula linking distance, speed, and time: $T = \frac{C}{v}$.
- step 3: Substitute the given values into the formula with their units: $T = \frac{48,000 \text{ km}}{8 \text{ km/s}}$.
- step 4: Perform the division and cancel matching units: $T = 6,000 \text{ s}$ (km divided by km/s leaves seconds).
- step 5: Verify the answer is physically reasonable (6000 seconds is 100 minutes, which is a realistic orbital period for a low-Earth satellite).
REASONING: The time taken to complete a closed circuit is simply the total distance of the path divided by the speed at which the object travels along that path.
ANSWER: 6,000 s
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: An asteroid follows an elongated orbit around the Sun where it travels a total distance of $1.8 \times 10^9 \text{ km}$ in one revolution. If it takes 2 years ($6.3 \times 10^7 \text{ s}$) to complete one revolution, calculate its average orbital speed in km/s.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given quantities: total distance $d = 1.8 \times 10^9 \text{ km} = 1,800,000,000 \text{ km}$, time $t = 6.3 \times 10^7 \text{ s}$. Find speed $v$.
- step 2: Select the formula for average speed: $v = \frac{d}{t}$.
- step 3: Substitute the numerical values and units into the formula: $v = \frac{1,800,000,000 \text{ km}}{63,000,000 \text{ s}}$.
- step 4: Simplify the fraction by cancelling zeros and computing: $v = \frac{1800}{63} \text{ km/s} \approx 28.57 \text{ km/s}$.
- step 5: Verify the answer: $28.57 \text{ km/s}$ is close to planetary orbital speeds, confirming the calculation is physically sound.
REASONING: Average speed in an orbit is found by dividing the total orbital distance traveled by the total time taken for that revolution, maintaining correct unit tracking of kilometers per second.
ANSWER: 28.57 km/s
