## ELI5
The video game rewards walking backwards from a trap, and the scorekeeper reverses penalties: two reversals land you in profit. That is negative times negative - each negative flips the direction once, and two flips face forward again, so the product of two negatives is positive. The pattern proof is a staircase: as the multiplier descends one rung at a time, the product rises by equal steps, crossing zero without pausing. Multiply the sizes, award the plus sign, and the debt becomes a credit.

## DEFINITION
The multiplication of two negative integers always yields a positive product because multiplying opposite directions on a number line twice reverses the orientation back to the positive side. Formally, for any positive integers $a$ and $b$, $(-a) \times (-b) = +(a \times b)$.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are playing a video game where walking backwards (negative direction) away from a trap is rewarded, but your scorekeeper reverses your penalties twice! When you multiply two negative quantities, think of it as reversing a debt twice, which turns it into a credit. We start by reviewing Class 6 multiplication of positive and negative numbers. Then, we look at the pattern of products as multipliers decrease: $(-3) \times 3 = -9$, $(-3) \times 2 = -6$, $(-3) \times 1 = -3$, $(-3) \times 0 = 0$, following this pattern, $(-3) \times (-1) must be $+3$. The key rule is: negative times negative equals positive.

**Takeaway:** negative times negative is positive: two direction flips face forward again. **In the exam:** justify with the descending-multiplier pattern, then multiply absolute values and attach the positive sign. **Check yourself:** walk the pattern staircase down to the crossing of zero and read the signs turning. **Why students miss it:** they carry the addition rule across, where two negatives only deepen the negative. Walk the staircase on paper once, and the crossing of zero explains the plus forever. The staircase drawing, kept in the margin of the page, argues the plus sign better than any memorised slogan.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often carry over the addition rule for integers—where adding two negative numbers results in a more negative number—into multiplication. For example, they evaluate $(-6) \times (-4)$ as $-24$ instead of $+24$, treating the negative signs as if they just combine to make the answer more negative.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to check the operation: addition combines debts, but multiplication by a negative represents reversing a direction or a debt. Write out the pattern starting from a positive multiplier decreasing through zero to negative multipliers to show the product climbing into positive numbers. Have them circle both negative signs and cross them out to form a plus sign: $(-)\times(-) = (+)$.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A stock market investor records a loss of ₹15 per share on 4 specific shares, represented as $-15$ per share. Due to a market correction error, the brokerage reverses the transaction twice, represented by multiplying by $-2$. Calculate the final integer value representing this double reversal.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given integers from the problem: $-15$ (loss per share) and $-2$ (the double reversal factor).
- Step 2: Set up the multiplication expression: $(-15) \times (-2)$.
- Step 3: Multiply the absolute values of the numbers: $15 \times 2 = 30$.
- Step 4: Apply the sign rule for multiplying two negative integers, which changes the product to positive: $+30$.
- Step 5: Interpret the result: The double reversal turns the negative transaction into a positive credit of ₹30.
REASONING: Multiplying a negative integer by another negative integer cancels out the negative directions, resulting in a positive product.
ANSWER: +30 (or ₹30 credit)

- Step 2: Set up the multiplication problem: $(-6) \times (-5)$.

- Step 4: Apply the rule that the product of two negative integers is positive: $+30$.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Simplify and evaluate the multi-step integer expression: $((-8) \times (-3)) + ((-5) \times (-4)) - 10$.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the two negative multiplication groups in the expression: `((-8) \times (-3))` and `((-5) \times (-4))`.
- Step 2: Evaluate the first product by multiplying absolute values and applying the rule: $(-8) \times (-3) = +24$.
- Step 3: Evaluate the second product similarly: $(-5) \times (-4) = +20$.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student claims that multiplying any three negative integers will always result in a positive product because "two negatives make a positive". Evaluate this statement. Provide an example to support your explanation.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and mathematical justification
STEPS:
- Step 1: Test the student's rule by taking a product of three negative integers, e.g., $(-2) \times (-3) \times (-4)$.
- Step 2: Multiply the first two negative integers: $(-2) \times (-3) = +6$.
- Step 3: Multiply that positive result by the third negative integer: $(+6) \times (-4) = -24$.
- Step 4: Conclude whether the student's rule holds for an odd number of negative factors and write the justification.
ANSWER: The student's claim is incorrect. While any *pair* of negative integers yields a positive product, multiplying an *odd* number of negative integers (like three) results in a negative product because the third negative factor flips the sign back to negative. For example, $(-2) \times (-3) \times (-4) = -24$.
DIFFICULTY: medium
