## ELI5
A parcel drops from a balcony 15 metres above ground into a basement 5 metres below: the integers do the bookkeeping. Words become signs - 'above' is plus, 'below' is minus, a rise adds, a fall subtracts - and then the ordinary rules finish the sum. Read twice, translate once: every elevator, submarine and hill frost is the same signed story, and the final answer must wear its sign and its unit like a name badge.

## DEFINITION

The final result must be interpreted back into the real-world context with proper units.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are standing on a balcony 15 metres above ground level, and you drop a heavy parcel down into an underground parking basement that is 5 metres below ground level. How would you calculate the total vertical distance the parcel travels? To solve this, we must first translate words like "above" into positive integers (+15) and "below" into negative integers (-5). We connect this to our prior knowledge of integers on a number line and integer subtraction rules. The key insight is that words like "rise," "gain," "above," and "deposit" represent positive integers, while "drop," "loss," "below," and "withdrawal" represent negative integers.

**Takeaway:** integer word problems are translation jobs: words to signs, signs to a sum, sum to a signed answer with units. **In the exam:** underline each movement word, mark its sign, then compute with the standard integer rules. **Check yourself:** retell the parcel drop in signs - plus 15, minus 5 - and compute the total travel. **Why students miss it:** they translate 'dropped' as subtraction of a negative and double-flip the direction. Sign, size, unit - the answer badge the examiner reads first. Underline every movement word in the question twice, and the signs write themselves without help.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A miner is working in a coal mine at an elevation of -65 metres. If he climbs up a ladder to a platform that is 25 metres above his current position, what is his new elevation?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is -40 metres. 
Working steps:
1. Identify the starting position: -65 metres.
2. Identify the movement: climbing up means adding, so add +25 metres.
3. Set up the expression: -65 + 25.
4. Compute: |-65| - |25| = 40, and take the sign of the number with the larger absolute value (-), resulting in -40 metres.
A student who answers +40 metres has ignored the initial negative sign of the starting position, treating -65 as a positive distance.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently misinterpret directional words in word problems, confusing words like "below sea level" or "temperature drop" with standard subtraction or ignoring the negative sign entirely. For example, when told a diver descends 50 metres from a boat at sea level (0 metres) and then drops another 20 metres, students often write 50 + 20 instead of recognizing that descents are represented by negative integers (-50 - 20 = -70), or they treat the resulting negative value as a positive distance because distances are usually perceived as positive.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this, guide the student to create a vertical number line on their page right beside the word problem. Tell them to circle the starting point (e.g., mark -50 for 50 metres below sea level). Ask them explicitly: "Does 'descending' mean moving UP towards the sky or DOWN towards the earth?" Once they identify DOWN, instruct them to draw an arrow pointing down and write a minus sign before the next number, turning the sentence into a clear mathematical expression before doing any calculation.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: At 5:00 AM, the temperature in a mountain village was -8 °C. By 2:00 PM, the temperature rose by 12 °C. However, by midnight that same night, a cold wind caused the temperature to drop by 15 °C. What was the final temperature at midnight?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the initial temperature at 5:00 AM as an integer, which is -8 °C.
- step 2: Account for the first change (rise of 12 °C), which is represented by adding +12.
- step 3: Compute the afternoon temperature: -8 + 12 = +4 °C.
- step 4: Account for the second change (drop of 15 °C), which is represented by subtracting 15 or adding -15.
- step 5: Compute the final midnight temperature: +4 - 15 = -11 °C.
REASONING: We translate "rise" into addition and "drop" into subtraction. Working sequentially through the timeline allows us to combine signed numbers step-by-step using standard integer addition rules.
ANSWER: -11 °C
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A research submarine is exploring an ocean trench. It starts at an elevation of -200 metres. It descends 350 metres to collect samples, and then ascends 150 metres to avoid an underwater ridge. What is the final elevation of the submarine?
STEPS:
- step 1: Write down the starting position as a signed integer: -200 metres.
- step 2: Translate the first movement ("descends 350 metres") into a negative change: -350 metres.
- step 3: Translate the second movement ("ascends 150 metres") into a positive change: +150 metres.
- step 4: Combine all terms into a single expression: -200 + (-350) + 150.
- step 5: Evaluate the expression step by step: (-200 - 350) = -550; then -550 + 150 = -400 metres.
REASONING: Elevation below sea level is negative. Descending moves further negative (subtraction), while ascending moves in the positive direction (addition).
ANSWER: -400 metres (or 400 metres below sea level)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A deep-water well digger is drilling into the earth. The starting platform is at +15 metres above ground level. The drill goes down through the earth at a rate of 4 metres per hour. If the drill operates continuously for 7 hours, what is the final elevation of the drill bit?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the starting position (elevation) of the platform: +15 metres.
- step 2: Determine the total depth drilled by multiplying the rate of descent by the time: 4 metres/hour × 7 hours = 28 metres.
- step 3: Translate the downward movement into a negative integer representing total change: -28 metres.
- step 4: Add the total change to the initial starting elevation: +15 + (-28).
- step 5: Compute the result using absolute value subtraction: |28| - |15| = 13, and apply the negative sign of the larger absolute value: -13 metres.
REASONING: Multiplication of a positive rate by time gives total distance moved, which must be given a negative sign because the movement is downward (below ground level). This is then combined with the initial positive elevation.
ANSWER: -13 metres
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Priya notes down her bank account balance over three months. In January, she deposited ₹500 (recorded as +500). In February, due to fees, her account had a withdrawal of ₹700 (recorded as -700). In March, her balance was affected by a transaction such that her total balance across all three months combined became -₹150. What was the exact integer value of the transaction in March?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world financial transactions into multi-step integer equations and solving for an unknown change.
STEPS:
- step 1: Assign integers to each monthly change: January = +500, February = -700, March = unknown ($x$).
- step 2: Set up an equation representing the sum of all changes equal to the final combined balance: (+500) + (-700) + $x$ = -150.
- step 3: Simplify the known part of the equation: 500 - 700 = -200, so -200 + $x$ = -150.
- step 4: Solve for $x$ by adding 200 to both sides: $x$ = -150 + 200 = +50.
ANSWER: The transaction in March was +₹50, meaning she deposited ₹50.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two weather stations record temperature fluctuations. Station A starts at -5 °C, increases by 8 °C at noon, and drops by 11 °C at night. Station B starts at +4 °C, drops by 9 °C at noon, and rises by 2 °C at night. Which station records the lower final temperature, and what is the exact numerical difference between their final temperatures?
COMPETENCY: Comparing multi-step integer outcomes from different real-world contexts and finding the absolute difference.
STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate Station A's final temperature: -5 + 8 - 11 = 3 - 11 = -8 °C.
- step 2: Calculate Station B's final temperature: +4 - 9 + 2 = -5 + 2 = -3 °C.
- step 3: Compare the two final temperatures (-8 °C and -3 °C) to determine which is lower: -8 °C is lower than -3 °C.
- step 4: Find the numerical difference between the two temperatures by subtracting them: |-8 - (-3)| = |-8 + 3| = |-5| = 5 °C.
ANSWER: Station A records the lower final temperature (-8 °C), and the absolute difference between their final temperatures is 5 °C.
DIFFICULTY: hard
