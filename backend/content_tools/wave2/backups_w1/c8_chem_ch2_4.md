## ELI5
Alone, most atoms are restless and reactive — they prefer company. Atoms link up in fixed groups called molecules: hydrogen always joins oxygen in the same pattern to make water, two oxygen atoms pair off as oxygen gas, one carbon holds two oxygens as carbon dioxide. A molecule is the smallest piece of a substance that can exist independently and still be that substance. Count the atoms, write the symbols, and you have the chemical formula — the molecule's exact membership list.

## DEFINITION
A molecule is a group of two or more atoms chemically combined together in a fixed ratio, representing the smallest unit of a pure substance that can exist independently and retain its chemical properties. For example, water ($H_2O$), oxygen gas ($O_2$), and carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) are molecules formed by the combination of identical or different types of atoms.

## WALKTHROUGH
When you open a sealed bottle of carbonated water in your kitchen or observe bubbles rising in boiling water, you are witnessing matter in motion at the microscopic level. In previous classes, you learned about individual atoms as the basic building blocks of matter (c8_chem_ch2_3). However, most atoms are extremely reactive on their own and prefer to link up with other atoms to achieve stability. By observing how hydrogen and oxygen combine to form water, or how carbon and oxygen form carbon dioxide, we discover that atoms bond in definite proportions. We begin by counting the constituent atoms in a given substance and then use chemical symbols to represent their combination, leading us directly to the concept of chemical formulas like $H_2O$, $O_2$, and $CO_2$.

Takeaway: atoms rarely travel alone — molecules are bonded teams in fixed ratios, from oxygen's pair to water's two-hydrogens-one-oxygen, and the formula is the team sheet. Exam format: expect atom counting in given formulas, formula writing, and element-molecule versus compound-molecule sorting. Verification beat: read the formula aloud as a count — two H, one O. Why students miss it: they confuse molecule with compound — oxygen's pair is a molecule of an element; water's team is a molecule of a compound, and the difference is whether the team is all one kind.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the primary difference between an atom and a molecule?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
An atom is the smallest single unit of an element that may or may not exist independently, whereas a molecule is formed by the chemical combination of two or more atoms and can exist independently while retaining its chemical properties. A wrong answer reveals that the student is confusing the fundamental building block (atom) with its stable combined state (molecule).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do oxygen atoms in the air exist as diatomic molecules ($O_2$) instead of single, isolated oxygen atoms?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Single oxygen atoms are chemically unstable due to incomplete outer electron shells, so two oxygen atoms share electrons to form a stable diatomic molecule ($O_2$). A wrong answer shows a misunderstanding of atomic stability and the driving force behind why atoms combine to form molecules.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A chemistry student analyzes a gas sample and finds its molecules are made of one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms bonded together. What is the chemical formula of this substance, and is it a molecule of an element or a compound?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps:
1. Identify constituent atoms: 1 Carbon (C) atom and 2 Oxygen (O) atoms.
2. Combine symbols to write the formula: $CO_2$.
3. Determine if atoms are identical or different: The substance contains different types of atoms (carbon and oxygen).
4. Classify the substance: Since it contains different elements combined chemically, it is a molecule of a compound.
Answer: The formula is $CO_2$, and it is a molecule of a compound. A wrong answer indicates confusion between molecules of elements (same atoms) and molecules of compounds (different atoms).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the formulas $O_2$, $H_2O$, and $CO_2$. How do the atom ratios differ between the molecule of an element and the molecules of compounds?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
$O_2$ is a molecule of an element because it consists of two atoms of the exact same element (oxygen). In contrast, $H_2O$ and $CO_2$ are molecules of compounds because each contains fixed ratios of two *different* elements ($H_2O$ has 2 hydrogen to 1 oxygen; $CO_2$ has 1 carbon to 2 oxygen). A wrong answer reveals an inability to differentiate elemental molecules from compound molecules based on their constituent atom types.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that a beaker containing a mixture of hydrogen gas ($H_2$) and oxygen gas ($O_2$) at room temperature is chemically the same as a beaker of water ($H_2O$). Evaluate this claim using your knowledge of molecules.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The claim is incorrect. In a mixture of $H_2$ and $O_2$, the hydrogen and oxygen atoms are physically mixed but not chemically bonded, meaning they retain their individual properties as gases. In water ($H_2O$), the hydrogen and oxygen atoms have undergone a chemical combination into fixed molecules with entirely new physical and chemical properties. A wrong answer demonstrates a failure to distinguish between physical mixtures and chemically combined molecules.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple model using everyday kitchen items (like different colored clay balls or pulses and toothpicks) to represent how one carbon atom combines with oxygen atoms to form carbon dioxide, and explain what your model shows about atomic ratios.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The student should use one large clay ball for carbon and two smaller clay balls of a different color for oxygen, connected by toothpicks in a 1:2 ratio. The model must visually demonstrate that a single molecule of $CO_2$ always maintains a fixed ratio of one carbon atom to two oxygen atoms. A poor answer fails to show the correct 1:2 atom ratio or confuses physical attachment with chemical combination rules.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the subscript number in a chemical formula with the number of molecules, thinking that $2H_2O$ means two separate water molecules while $H_2O_2$ just means more water. Specifically, they fail to recognize that subscripts (like the '2' in $H_2O$) apply *only* to the specific atom immediately preceding them, defining the fixed atomic ratio within a single molecule, whereas coefficients in front of formulas indicate the total number of molecules.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this error, use visual particle cards: draw one large oxygen circle bonded to two small hydrogen circles to explicitly show that the '2' in $H_2O$ means *two hydrogen atoms are bound inside one single water molecule*. Next, compare $H_2O$ (water) with $H_2O_2$ (hydrogen peroxide) to demonstrate how changing the subscript completely changes the identity and chemical properties of the substance. Have the student physically count the atoms in diagrams for $O_2$, $H_2O$, and $CO_2$ multiple times until the distinction between internal subscripts and external coefficients is clear.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A sample of pure water taken from a kitchen tap in Mumbai contains hydrogen and oxygen. Chemical analysis reveals that every single water molecule is composed of 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom. Write the chemical formula for water and calculate the total number of atoms present in 3 molecules of water.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the constituent atoms per molecule from the problem description: 2 Hydrogen (H) atoms and 1 Oxygen (O) atoms.
- Step 2: Write the chemical formula for a single molecule by combining the symbols with their respective subscripts: $H_2O$.
- Step 3: Calculate the total number of atoms in one molecule: 2 (from hydrogen) + 1 (from oxygen) = 3 atoms per molecule.
- Step 4: Multiply the atoms per molecule by the total number of molecules given (3 molecules): 3 molecules $\times$ 3 atoms/molecule = 9 total atoms.
- Step 5: Verify the calculation and state the final result clearly with appropriate units.
REASONING: Subscripts in a chemical formula tell us the exact atom count in one molecule, while a coefficient multiplies the entire molecule. By finding atoms per molecule first and multiplying by the molecule count, we determine the total particle count accurately.
ANSWER: Chemical formula is $H_2O$; total number of atoms in 3 molecules is 9 atoms.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Carbon dioxide gas ($CO_2$) is exhaled during respiration. If a student has 4 molecules of carbon dioxide, determine: (a) the chemical formula, (b) the total number of carbon atoms, and (c) the total number of oxygen atoms in the sample.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the element symbols and their ratio in carbon dioxide from the name and prior knowledge: Carbon (C) and Oxygen (O).
- Step 2: Write the chemical formula for carbon dioxide showing 1 carbon and 2 oxygen atoms: $CO_2$.
- Step 3: Determine the count of carbon atoms in 4 molecules: Since each molecule has 1 carbon atom, 4 molecules $\times$ 1 = 4 carbon atoms.
- Step 4: Determine the count of oxygen atoms in 4 molecules: Since each molecule has 2 oxygen atoms (subscript 2), 4 molecules $\times$ 2 = 8 oxygen atoms.
- Step 5: Verify atom counts by summing carbon and oxygen atoms (4 + 8 = 12 total atoms) and check against the formula multiplier ($4 \times 3 \text{ atoms per } CO_2 = 12$).
REASONING: The subscript '2' next to oxygen means every single $CO_2$ molecule contains two oxygen atoms. Multiplying the coefficient (4) by each subscript gives the exact count of each type of atom.
ANSWER: (a) $CO_2$, (b) 4 carbon atoms, (c) 8 oxygen atoms.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Photosynthesis in green plants produces oxygen gas ($O_2$) and glucose. A biology experiment collects 5 molecules of oxygen gas ($O_2$) and 2 molecules of carbon dioxide ($CO_2$). Calculate the total number of oxygen atoms present across all these collected molecules.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the chemical formulas given in the problem: Oxygen gas is $O_2$ and carbon dioxide is $CO_2$.
- Step 2: Calculate the number of oxygen atoms in the oxygen gas sample: 5 molecules of $O_2$ means $5 \times 2 = 10$ oxygen atoms.
- Step 3: Calculate the number of oxygen atoms in the carbon dioxide sample: 2 molecules of $CO_2$ means $2 \times 2 = 4$ oxygen atoms (since each $CO_2$ contains 2 oxygen atoms).
- Step 4: Add the oxygen atoms from both sources together: 10 oxygen atoms (from $O_2$) + 4 oxygen atoms (from $CO_2$) = 14 total oxygen atoms.
- Step 5: Verify the calculation by checking each molecular contribution separately and ensuring atom ratios from subscripts were correctly multiplied.
REASONING: When combining different molecular samples, we must evaluate the oxygen contribution from each formula independently by multiplying the number of molecules (coefficient) by the oxygen subscript, then summing the results.
ANSWER: 14 total oxygen atoms.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student mixes hydrogen gas ($H_2$) and oxygen gas ($O_2$) in a closed glass container at room temperature. No chemical reaction occurs spontaneously. The student claims that because both gases contain molecules, the mixture is chemically identical to liquid water ($H_2O$). Critically evaluate this claim, explaining why the molecular combination makes water chemically distinct from a mixture of its constituent gases.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating chemical claims and distinguishing mixtures from compounds based on molecular structure
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine the composition of the gas mixture ($H_2$ and $O_2$ molecules exist separately as elements).
- Step 2: Examine the composition of water ($H_2O$ molecules where hydrogen and oxygen atoms are chemically bonded).
- Step 3: Compare properties: in a mixture, gases retain their individual explosive/combustive properties; in a compound, water extinguishes fire.
- Step 4: Conclude that chemical combination creates a new substance with entirely different properties, proving the student's claim false.
ANSWER: The student's claim is false. In a mixture of $H_2$ and $O_2$, the atoms are not chemically bonded and the gases retain their individual properties. In water ($H_2O$), hydrogen and oxygen atoms are chemically combined in a fixed 2:1 ratio, creating a new substance with unique chemical properties that differ completely from the reactant gases.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Industrial synthesis of dry ice involves solidifying carbon dioxide ($CO_2$). If an industrial cylinder contains a total of $3.0 \times 10^23$ carbon dioxide molecules, determine how many total atoms (both carbon and oxygen) are present inside the cylinder, and explain how the fixed ratio rule applies to this macroscopic sample.
COMPETENCY: Applying atomic ratios and molecular formulas to large-scale quantities with chemical reasoning
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the constituent atoms in one molecule of carbon dioxide ($CO_2$): 1 carbon atom + 2 oxygen atoms = 3 atoms per molecule.
- Step 2: Recognize that the fixed ratio rule dictates every single molecule of $CO_2$ maintains this exact 1:2 ratio regardless of sample size.
- Step 3: Multiply the total number of molecules by the number of atoms per molecule: $3.0 \times 10^23 \text{ molecules} \times 3 \text{ atoms/molecule}$.
- Step 4: Calculate the final total atom count: $9.0 \times 10^23$ total atoms ($3.0 \times 10^23$ carbon atoms and $6.0 \times 10^23$ oxygen atoms).
- Step 5: Interpret chemically: confirm that the fixed ratio of 1C:2O is preserved across the entire macroscopic sample.
ANSWER: There are $9.0 \times 10^23$ total atoms in the cylinder ($3.0 \times 10^23$ carbon atoms and $6.0 \times 10^23$ oxygen atoms). The fixed ratio rule ensures that every individual $CO_2$ molecule strictly maintains a 1:2 carbon-to-oxygen atom ratio, scaling proportionally across the entire sample.
DIFFICULTY: hard
