## ELI5
Ratio and proportion are the kitchen's scaling rules. The ratio is the recipe's strength - sugar to feeds so many; proportion is the promise that a bigger pot keeps the same strength. Double the guests, double everything, taste unchanged. Maps are ratios drawn in ink, and the unit rate - the cost or strength of one - is the key that unlocks any batch size.

## DEFINITION
Ratio is a mathematical way of comparing two quantities of the same kind by division, showing how many times one quantity contains the other. A proportion is an equality between two ratios, indicating that two relationships are equivalent.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers basic ratio representation and simple proportion problems using cross-multiplication or unitary method. It excludes compound proportions, complex multi-step word problems, and algebraic variables beyond simple linear equations.
## WALKTHROUGH
If your mother uses 2 cups of sugar for a cake that serves 4 people, how much sugar do you need for 8 people? Start by writing the relationship as a ratio: 2 cups : 4 people. To keep the recipe consistent, the second ratio must represent the same "strength" or relationship. If you double the people, you must double the sugar, leading to 4 cups : 8 people. The key insight is that in a proportion, the ratio of the parts remains constant, allowing us to find unknown quantities by keeping the relationship balanced.

**Takeaway:** ratios compare same-kind quantities by division, and proportions hold two ratios equal - scaling preserves the strength. **In the exam:** in scaling questions, simplify the given ratio or find the unit rate, then build the requested amount. **Check yourself:** reread the walkthrough's cake recipe - double the guests and every ingredient must follow in step. **Why students miss it:** students add to both sides while scaling - the walkthrough's doubled cake is a no to that; recipes scale by multiplication or the taste dies, and the unit rate is the check. Rehearse the exam move once more - simplify, scale, check - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to solve proportion problems by adding the same number to both sides of a ratio instead of multiplying/dividing. For example, if 2:3 = 4:x, they might think x = 5 (adding 2 to each side). This ignores the multiplicative nature of ratios.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to draw the ratio as groups of items. For 2:3, draw two circles and three squares. If they need to reach 4 circles, ask them how many groups of the "original" ratio they now have. Seeing that the entire "set" is doubled helps them realize they must also double the squares to reach six.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A recipe for 6 people requires 300 grams of rice. How many grams of rice are needed for 15 people?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given ratio (300g : 6 people).
- step 2: Find the amount per person (300 ÷ 6 = 50g per person).
- step 3: Multiply the amount per person by the new number of people (50g × 15).
- step 4: Compute the total (750g).
- step 5: Interpret: 15 people need 750 grams of rice.
REASONING: By finding the unit rate (rice per person), we establish a baseline that allows us to scale the requirement to any number of people.
ANSWER: 750 grams
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A scale on a map is 1:20000. The distance between two cities on the map is 4 cm. Find the actual distance in kilometres.
STEPS:
- step 1: Write the proportion: 1 cm map : 20000 cm real.
- step 2: Calculate actual distance in cm: 4 × 20000 = 80000 cm.
- step 3: Convert cm to metres: 80000 ÷ 100 = 800 metres.
- step 4: Convert metres to kilometres: 800 ÷ 1000 = 0.8 km.
- step 5: Interpret: The actual distance is 0.8 km.
REASONING: Scaling requires keeping the ratio consistent, and unit conversion is necessary to make the large number understandable in real-world terms.
ANSWER: 0.8 km
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Divide 500 rupees between A and B in the ratio 3:2.
STEPS:
- step 1: Add the ratio parts: 3 + 2 = 5 total parts.
- step 2: Find the value of one part: 500 ÷ 5 = 100 rupees.
- step 3: Multiply A's share by the part value: 3 × 100 = 300 rupees.
- step 4: Multiply B's share by the part value: 2 × 100 = 200 rupees.
- step 5: Verify: 300 + 200 = 500 rupees.
REASONING: Totaling the parts allows us to distribute the money fairly according to the specified relationship.
ANSWER: A gets 300, B gets 200 rupees.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you have a mixture of paint with a ratio of 2 parts blue to 3 parts yellow, and you have 10 litres of blue paint, how much yellow paint is needed to maintain the color?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the ratio: 2 blue : 3 yellow.
- step 2: Set up proportion: 2/3 = 10/x.
- step 3: Solve for x: 2x = 30; x = 15.
- step 4: Interpret: 15 litres of yellow paint are needed.
ANSWER: 15 litres. Because the blue paint was multiplied by 5 to get 10, the yellow paint must also be multiplied by 5.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two rectangles have the same area. Can we say their dimensions must be in the same ratio? Give an example to support your answer.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- step 1: Define rectangle area: length × breadth = area.
- step 2: Pick an area, e.g., 24.
- step 3: Rectangle A (6×4, ratio 3:2); Rectangle B (8×3, ratio 8:3).
- step 4: Compare ratios: 3:2 ≠ 8:3.
- step 5: Conclude: No, different dimensions can produce the same area.
ANSWER: No. For an area of 24, a 6x4 rectangle has a 3:2 ratio, while an 8x3 rectangle has an 8:3 ratio. Therefore, the ratio of sides does not need to be the same even if areas are equal.
DIFFICULTY: hard
