## ELI5
Price tags and measuring tapes are where decimals earn their keep. One rupee splits into a hundred paise, one metre into a hundred centimetres, so the point separates wholes from parts in every shop and tailor's stall. Read the tail as hundredths, keep points aligned when totalling, and the bill, the change and the cloth all compute honestly.

## DEFINITION
Decimals in real life are a way of expressing parts of a whole unit, such as money (where 1 rupee = 100 paise) or measurements (where 1 metre = 100 centimetres), using a decimal point to separate the whole number from the fractional part. It allows for precise quantification of quantities that are not perfectly whole.

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever looked at a price tag that says ₹99.50 and wondered what the ".50" actually means? To understand this, think of the decimal point as a bridge between the "whole" (rupees) and the "part" (paise). Whenever you have 100 small units (like 100 paise in a rupee or 100 cm in a metre), each small unit is 1/100th of the whole, written as 0.01. If you have 50 paise, you simply write it after the decimal point as .50. When you add items, just align the decimal points vertically like buttons on a shirt, and add the numbers column by column. The key insight is that the decimal point acts like a fixed anchor—always keep it in the same spot, and you will never lose track of your totals.

**Takeaway:** real-life decimals express parts of whole units - paise as hundredths of a rupee, centimetres as hundredths of a metre. **In the exam:** in money and measure questions, convert the part into its place value, align the points, and compute with units named. **Check yourself:** split the walkthrough's price tag at the point - rupees on the left, the paise part as hundredths on the right. **Why students miss it:** students read the tail as a fresh whole number - the walkthrough's tag is a no to that; the tail is a fraction of the unit, in hundredths. ## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2

**A wrong answer (mixing places in the columns) reveals the student let rupees and paise mingle - aligned points keep each place with its own kind.**

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often treat the digits after the decimal point as whole numbers, such as thinking 0.50 is "fifty" and 0.5 is "five," leading them to believe 0.50 is larger than 0.5. This happens because they view the decimal part as an independent whole number rather than a fraction of the unit.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student struggles, draw a 10x10 square grid on paper. Shade 50 small squares for 0.50 and 5 columns (each with 10 squares) for 0.5. Show them that both represent the same amount of the total square, explaining that the zero at the end is just a placeholder and does not change the quantity.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Meena has ₹200. She buys a bag of flour for ₹85.75 and a carton of milk for ₹42.50. How much change should she receive?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the total cost by adding ₹85.75 and ₹42.50.
- Step 2: Align the decimals: 85.75 + 42.50.
- Step 3: Compute total cost: 5+0=5, 7+5=12 (carry 1), 5+2+1=8, 8+4=12. Total = ₹128.25.
- Step 4: Subtract total cost from the budget: 200.00 - 128.25.
- Step 5: Perform subtraction by borrowing across the zeros.
REASONING: We treat the budget as 200.00 to align with the two decimal places of the total cost, ensuring we subtract hundredths from hundredths.
ANSWER: ₹71.75
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A tailor has a fabric roll of 10 metres. He cuts a piece of 3.45 metres and another of 2.8 metres. How much fabric is left?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate the total fabric used: 3.45 + 2.80 (add a zero for alignment).
- Step 2: Align and add: 3.45 + 2.80 = 6.25 metres.
- Step 3: Identify remaining fabric: 10.00 - 6.25.
- Step 4: Subtract: 10.00 - 6.25 = 3.75 metres.
REASONING: By converting 2.8 to 2.80, we avoid the error of adding 3.45 and 2.8 as 3.73, maintaining proper place value alignment.
ANSWER: 3.75 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A box of apples weighs 5.5 kg. A box of oranges weighs 3.25 kg. If you remove 0.5 kg of apples, what is the total weight of both boxes?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Update the apple weight: 5.50 - 0.50 = 5.00 kg.
- Step 2: Identify the weights to sum: 5.00 kg (apples) and 3.25 kg (oranges).
- Step 3: Align decimal points: 5.00 + 3.25.
- Step 4: Add the values: 5 + 3 = 8, 00 + 25 = 25.
- Step 5: Final weight: 8.25 kg.
REASONING: We first perform the subtraction to find the new weight of the apples, then combine it with the weight of the oranges using decimal addition rules.
ANSWER: 8.25 kg
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have two lengths: 1.4 metres and 1.40 metres. Are they equal? Explain your reasoning using the concept of place value.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the values as decimals.
- Step 2: Convert to fractions to compare.
- Step 3: Recognize that 1.4 = 14/10 and 1.40 = 140/100.
- Step 4: Simplify 140/100 to 14/10.
ANSWER: Yes, they are equal because 1.4 is 14 tenths, and 1.40 is 140 hundredths. Since 140/100 is equivalent to 14/10, the values are the same.
