## ELI5
Two forty-rupee notebooks from the same shop stack so perfectly that no edge peeks out - that flawless overlap is congruence: same shape AND same size. Same shape alone is only similarity, the small photo and its poster. The tests are practical: trace one figure and slide, flip or spin it over the other; or match corresponding sides and angles one by one. Congruent figures even share perimeters and areas - every measurement agrees, because they are the same figure in two places.

## DEFINITION
Congruent figures are geometric shapes that have the exact same shape and the exact same size, meaning one can be placed precisely over the other so that they cover each other completely through rigid transformations like translation, rotation, or reflection.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you buy two identical brand-new exercise notebooks from a stationery shop in your market for ₹40 each. When you stack them on top of your desk, the cover of the top book fits the bottom book so perfectly that no edges stick out. In geometry, when two objects match up completely in both shape and size, we call them **congruent figures**. Building on our earlier work with lines and basic shapes from Class 6, we now look closely at how geometric twins match identically. To test if two figures are congruent, we can imagine lifting one figure and placing it onto the other—if all sides and angles match up cell for cell and line for line, they are congruent. The key insight is that congruence means exact equality in every dimension, not just looking similar.

**Takeaway:** congruence means identical shape and identical size, proven by superimposing or matching all corresponding parts. **In the exam:** compare side-by-side: equal corresponding sides and angles certify congruence; matching perimeters alone never do. **Check yourself:** trace one notebook cover onto the other and slide, flip and spin to test the overlap. **Why students miss it:** they bless look-alike figures as congruent, forgetting that size must match too.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Two triangles have identical interior angles measuring $60^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $60^\circ$. One triangle has sides of length $4\text{ cm}$, and the other has sides of length $8\text{ cm}$. Are these two triangles congruent? Explain why or why not.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, they are not congruent. While their shapes are identical (both are equilateral triangles), their sizes are different because their side lengths ($4\text{ cm}$ vs $8\text{ cm}$) do not match. A student who says "yes" mistakenly thinks that having the same angles is enough to guarantee congruence.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Line segment $AB$ has a length of $7.5\text{ cm}$. Line segment $CD$ has a length of $7.5\text{ cm}$. Are line segments $AB$ and $CD$ congruent? If you place $AB$ on top of $CD$ such that point $A$ coincides with point $C$, what happens to point $B$ and point $D$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Yes, line segments $AB$ and $CD$ are congruent because their lengths are equal ($7.5\text{ cm} = 7.5\text{ cm}$). When placed on top of each other with point $A$ matching point $C$, point $B$ will coincide exactly with point $D$. A student who says they might not coincide assumes line segments can curve or shift, forgetting that straight line segments are determined entirely by their fixed length.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If figure P and figure Q are congruent, what can you deduce about their perimeters and areas? Compare them and explain your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Their perimeters must be exactly equal, and their areas must also be exactly equal. Since congruent figures have identical corresponding sides and angles, the total boundary length (perimeter) and the total space enclosed (area) must be identical. A student who thinks congruent figures can have different areas does not understand that size includes total surface coverage.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Rohan claims that if you cut any rectangle of area $24\text{ cm}^2$ into two pieces, the pieces will always be congruent to each other. Evaluate Rohan's claim and give a counterexample.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Rohan's claim is false. For example, a rectangle of dimensions $6\text{ cm} \times 4\text{ cm}$ can be cut into two rectangles of $3\text{ cm} \times 4\text{ cm}$ (which are congruent), but it can also be cut into a $6\text{ cm} \times 3\text{ cm}$ piece and a $6\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm}$ piece along the length, which are clearly not congruent. A student who accepts Rohan's claim confuses having the same total area with having identical dimensions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Draw or describe two different irregular 4-sided polygons (quadrilaterals) that are NOT congruent to each other, yet both have a total perimeter of $20\text{ cm}$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The expected response should describe two quadrilaterals with different side length combinations that add up to $20\text{ cm}$, such as one with sides $4\text{ cm}, 5\text{ cm}, 6\text{ cm}, 5\text{ cm}$ and another with sides $2\text{ cm}, 8\text{ cm}, 2\text{ cm}, 8\text{ cm}$. A poor answer would provide two shapes with the same side lengths rearranged, which would actually make them congruent, failing to understand that different shapes can share a perimeter without being congruent.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse "similar" figures with "congruent" figures, assuming that if two objects look alike (like a small photo and a large poster of the same picture), they are congruent. For example, they might look at an equilateral triangle with $3\text{ cm}$ sides and an equilateral triangle with $6\text{ cm}$ sides and state they are congruent because "they are both triangles with equal angles."
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that congruence means "geometric twins"—they must match in size as well as shape. Ask them to imagine trying to cover a large textbook with a small pocket notebook; even though both are rectangular, one cannot cover the other. Tell them: "Same shape AND same size means every matching side and every matching angle must measure identically."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Priya has two rectangular garden plots at her school in Jaipur. Plot A has a length of $12\text{ m}$ and a breadth of $5\text{ m}$. Plot B has a length of $12\text{ m}$ and a breadth of $5\text{ m}$. Determine whether Plot A and Plot B are congruent, and calculate their perimeters and areas to verify.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given dimensions for Plot A ($l_1 = 12\text{ m}$, $b_1 = 5\text{ m}$) and Plot B ($l_2 = 12\text{ m}$, $b_2 = 5\text{ m}$).
- step 2: Compare corresponding dimensions: length of Plot A equals length of Plot B ($12\text{ m} = 12\text{ m}$) and breadth of Plot A equals breadth of Plot B ($5\text{ m} = 5\text{ m}$).
- step 3: Conclude that since all corresponding sides are equal, the shapes are congruent.
- step 4: Calculate perimeter for both plots using formula $P = 2(l + b)$. For Plot A: $P_A = 2(12 + 5) = 2(17) = 34\text{ m}$. For Plot B: $P_B = 2(12 + 5) = 2(17) = 34\text{ m}$.
- step 5: Calculate area for both plots using formula $A = l \times b$. $A_A = 12 \times 5 = 60\text{ m}^2$ and $A_B = 12 \times 5 = 60\text{ m}^2$. Since dimensions, perimeters, and areas match, the plots are congruent.
REASONING: Congruent figures possess identical dimensions in every corresponding part. By verifying that lengths, breadths, perimeters, and areas are equal, we confirm that superimposing Plot A onto Plot B results in an exact match.
ANSWER: The plots are congruent; Perimeter = $34\text{ m}$, Area = $60\text{ m}^2$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Amit cuts two triangular cardboard pieces for a craft project. Triangle ABC has sides $AB = 6\text{ cm}$, $BC = 8\text{ cm}$, and $AC = 10\text{ cm}$. Triangle XYZ has sides $XY = 8\text{ cm}$, $YZ = 6\text{ cm}$, and $XZ = 10\text{ cm}$. Are Triangle ABC and Triangle XYZ congruent? State the corresponding vertices if they are congruent.
STEPS:
- step 1: List the side lengths of Triangle ABC: $AB = 6\text{ cm}$, $BC = 8\text{ cm}$, $AC = 10\text{ cm}$.
- step 2: List the side lengths of Triangle XYZ: $XY = 8\text{ cm}$, $YZ = 6\text{ cm}$, $XZ = 10\text{ cm}$.
- step 3: Match the sides by length: $AB = 6\text{ cm}$ matches $YZ = 6\text{ cm}$; $BC = 8\text{ cm}$ matches $XY = 8\text{ cm}$; $AC = 10\text{ cm}$ matches $XZ = 10\text{ cm}$.
- step 4: Align the corresponding vertices based on equal side lengths: Vertex $A$ corresponds to $Z$ ($AZ$ not side, check sharing: vertex $A$ is between sides of length $6$ and $10$, so $A$ corresponds to $Z$ where sides of length $6$ and $10$ meet, wait: $AB=6, AC=10 \implies A$ is between $6$ and $10$; in $\triangle XYZ$, $YZ=6, XZ=10 \implies Z$ is between $6$ and $10$, so vertex $A \leftrightarrow Z$). Let's re-verify: $AB(6) \leftrightarrow YZ(6)$, $BC(8) \leftrightarrow XY(8)$, $AC(10) \leftrightarrow XZ(10)$. Thus vertex $B \leftrightarrow Y$, $A \leftrightarrow Z$, $C \leftrightarrow X$.
- step 5: Conclude that Triangle ABC is congruent to Triangle ZYX (written as $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle ZYX$) because all three corresponding side pairs are equal in length.
REASONING: Two triangles are congruent if their three sides are respectively equal to the three sides of another triangle. Matching corresponding vertices correctly ensures that the superposition lines up every vertex and side precisely.
ANSWER: Yes, they are congruent; $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle ZYX$ with corresponding vertices $A \leftrightarrow Z$, $B \leftrightarrow Y$, $C \leftrightarrow X$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A tile designer creates two metallic tiles in the shape of a parallelogram. Tile 1 has side lengths $7\text{ cm}$ and $10\text{ cm}$, and one interior angle of $70^\circ$. Tile 2 has side lengths $7\text{ cm}$ and $10\text{ cm}$, and an interior angle of $110^\circ$ adjacent to the $7\text{ cm}$ side. Are Tile 1 and Tile 2 congruent? Justify your reasoning using side and angle properties.
STEPS:
- step 1: Examine the given side lengths for both tiles: both have sides of $7\text{ cm}$ and $10\text{ cm}$.
- step 2: Examine the given interior angles: Tile 1 has an angle of $70^\circ$; Tile 2 has an adjacent angle of $110^\circ$.
- step 3: Recall that adjacent angles in a parallelogram are supplementary (add up to $180^\circ$). If Tile 1 has an angle of $70^\circ$, its adjacent angle is $180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ$.
- step 4: Compare Tile 1's full set of angles ($70^\circ, 110^\circ, 70^\circ, 110^\circ$) with Tile 2's angles. Since Tile 2 is specified to have an angle of $110^\circ$ adjacent to the $7\text{ cm}$ side, its angles must also be $110^\circ, 70^\circ, 110^\circ, 70^\circ$.
- step 5: Conclude that since all four corresponding sides ($7\text{ cm}, 10\text{ cm}, 7\text{ cm}, 10\text{ cm}$) and all corresponding interior angles ($70^\circ, 110^\circ, 70^\circ, 110^\circ$) are equal, Tile 1 and Tile 2 are congruent.
REASONING: Congruence requires both identical side lengths and identical angle measures. By using the geometric property of supplementary adjacent angles in a parallelogram, we proved that both shapes share identical internal geometry, allowing them to be superimposed completely.
ANSWER: Yes, Tile 1 and Tile 2 are congruent because all corresponding sides and interior angles match completely.

QUESTION: Rahul cuts out two paper stars from a template. Star 1 has 5 points with all outer edge segments measuring exactly $3\text{ cm}$ each. Star 2 also has 5 points with all outer edge segments measuring $3\text{ cm}$ each. Rahul states: "Since all outer edge segments of both stars are equal to $3\text{ cm}$, the two stars must be congruent." Is Rahul correct? Analyze the properties of complex multi-vertex polygons to justify your answer.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction in geometric shape comparison
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand Rahul's claim: equal outer edge segment lengths imply congruence for complex multi-vertex polygons.
- step 2: Analyze polygon properties: a star shape is a complex non-convex polygon whose shape is determined not only by the outer edge segment lengths, but also by the inner vertex angles (re-entrant angles) where the points meet.
- step 3: Test the hypothesis: two stars can have identical outer side lengths of $3\text{ cm}$ while having sharper or wider inner vertex angles, which changes the overall shape and makes superposition impossible.
- step 4: Formulate the final judgment: Rahul is incorrect because equal edge lengths alone do not guarantee identical interior angles or shape structure in complex polygons.
ANSWER: Rahul is incorrect. While the outer side lengths are equal, a star shape's overall geometry depends heavily on its interior and re-entrant angles. If the angles at the center differ, the stars cannot be superimposed, meaning they are not congruent.

Plate Q has sides $5\text{ cm}, 12\text{ cm}, 13\text{ cm}$. A technician wants to weld them together along an edge. Before doing so, he flips Plate Q over (reflects it horizontally) and tries to fit it onto Plate P. Does flipping a figure destroy its congruence? Explain how rigid transformations (translation, rotation, reflection) relate to congruent figures.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and geometric transformations
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given dimensions of both plates: both are right-angled triangles with sides $5\text{ cm}, 12\text{ cm}, 13\text{ cm}$, making them initially congruent.
- step 2: Understand the action performed: the technician reflects (flips) Plate Q horizontally.
- step 3: Recall the definition of congruent figures: figures are congruent if they have the same shape and size, and rigid transformations include translation, rotation, and reflection.
- step 4: Evaluate the effect of reflection: reflecting a 2D shape creates a mirror image, but its side lengths and interior angles remain completely unchanged.
- step 5: Conclude whether congruence is preserved: since side lengths and angles are preserved under reflection, the flipped plate remains congruent to Plate P.
ANSWER: No, flipping (reflecting) a figure does not destroy its congruence. Congruent figures can be moved, turned (rotated), or flipped over (reflected) in a plane. As long as the side lengths and angles do not change, the reflected shape remains congruent to the original.
