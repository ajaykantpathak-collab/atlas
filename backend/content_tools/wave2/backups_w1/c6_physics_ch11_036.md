## ELI5
Light is a straight-line traveller: bend the garden hose and the bulb at the far end vanishes; step behind a wall and the flame is gone. It will not lean around corners, which is why shadows carry the exact argument of the blocker and why three holed cards in a line are the classic honesty test.

## DEFINITION
Light is a form of energy that travels in a straight line, known as rectilinear propagation. This means it cannot bend around obstacles unless it is reflected or refracted.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever tried to look at a light bulb through a garden hose? If the hose is straight, you see the light clearly; if you bend it, the light disappears. This happens because light is very "stubborn"—it only likes to move in a perfectly straight path. To understand this, imagine you are shining a torch through a hole in a piece of cardboard. If you place another piece of cardboard slightly shifted, the light hits the second board instead of passing through. This behavior is the reason why we see shadows whenever an object blocks the path of light.

**Takeaway:** light travels in straight lines - obstacles block it into shadows, and only a straight, aligned path carries the view. **In the exam:** in light questions, draw the ray as a ruled line and let the blocked line explain the shadow or the hidden view. **Check yourself:** peek down the walkthrough's hose - the straight pipe shows the flame, the bent one hides it. **Why students miss it:** students curve light around corners - the hidden flame behind the bend is a no to that; rays never lean. Rehearse the exam move once more - straight ray, blocked line, shadow - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you hold a straight pipe and a curved pipe to look at a candle, which one allows you to see the flame and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Only the straight pipe works because light travels in a straight line from the candle to your eye. If the pipe is curved, the light hits the inner wall of the pipe and stops, which reveals a student's failure to connect the path of light with the physical shape of the object.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple experiment to prove light travels in a straight line using only three pieces of cardboard, a candle, and a pin.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The student should describe making a tiny hole in the center of all three cards, aligning them perfectly, and checking if the candle flame is visible through all three holes. If the candle is blocked when one card is moved, it proves the path must be perfectly straight. A poor answer fails to ensure the "alignment" of the holes.

**A wrong answer (stacking the holes carelessly) reveals the student broke the alignment trick - the candle's view survives only when all three holes sit on one ruled line.**

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe light "fills" a room like water fills a bucket, assuming it can go anywhere. They struggle to realize that a shadow is actually a "hole" in the light created because the light rays were stopped. For example, they might think the shadow is an object itself rather than the absence of light.
## ON_FAILURE
First, ask the student to trace a line from a light source to a wall using a ruler. Then, place a pencil in the middle of that line and ask, "Where does the light go now?" Guide them to see that the light stops at the pencil, creating a gap on the wall. Repeat this with a sheet of paper to show that the shadow is exactly the shape of the part of the paper blocking the light.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You have a 50 cm ruler. You place a tall bottle 10 cm from a wall and a torch 20 cm from the bottle. Why do you see a shadow?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the source (torch), the object (bottle), and the screen (wall).
- Step 2: Recognize that light travels in straight lines from the torch.
- Step 3: Note that the bottle is an opaque object blocking the light path.
- Step 4: Conclude that the area behind the bottle receives no light.
REASONING: Since light cannot bend around the bottle, the space on the wall directly behind it is deprived of light. This absence of light is what we call a shadow.
ANSWER: A shadow is formed because the bottle blocks the straight path of light rays.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: If you are standing in a dark room and someone shines a laser pointer at you, how can you find the source of the laser?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Realize the laser light travels in a perfectly straight line.
- Step 2: Understand that if you trace the path of the light beam back, it must point to the source.
- Step 3: Look along the direction the light is coming from.
- Step 4: The source will be located exactly along that straight line.
REASONING: Because light paths are straight, reversing the direction of the light ray will always point directly to the object that emitted it.
ANSWER: By looking along the straight path of the light beam, you can trace it back to the source.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A light source shines on a square card, forming a square shadow. If you rotate the card 45 degrees, does the shadow change?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Visualize the light rays as parallel or diverging straight lines.
- Step 2: The card acts as a mask blocking those rays.
- Step 3: The shadow is the "projection" of the card's silhouette.
- Step 4: Rotating the card changes the silhouette (from a square to a diamond shape).
- Step 5: The shadow will now appear as a diamond.
REASONING: A shadow is a 2D projection of a 3D object's cross-section relative to the light source. Changing the object's orientation changes the cross-section being projected.
ANSWER: Yes, the shadow changes shape to a diamond because the silhouette of the card seen by the light source has changed.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Can a shadow be formed if the light source is behind the object? Explain.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the position of the light source, object, and screen.
- Step 2: Determine if the object is between the source and the screen.
- Step 3: Apply the rule that light travels straight and is blocked by the object.
ANSWER: No, a shadow cannot be formed on the wall if the light is behind the object because the light rays are already moving away from the screen/wall. The object must be placed between the light source and the screen to block the path.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Why does the Sun create a shadow for a tree, but a thin piece of glass does not?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the properties of the object (tree vs. glass).
- Step 2: Define "opaque" (blocks light) vs "transparent" (allows light through).
- Step 3: Explain that shadows only form when light is blocked.
ANSWER: A tree is opaque, so it blocks the straight-line path of sunlight. Glass is transparent, meaning it allows light to pass through it in a straight line without being blocked. Therefore, no shadow is created by the glass because the light reaches the ground behind it.
DIFFICULTY: hard
