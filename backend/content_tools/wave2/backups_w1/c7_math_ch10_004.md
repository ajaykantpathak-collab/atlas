## ELI5
The canteen account bleeds 5 rupees a day for 4 days - a negative times a positive, and the balance duly sinks deeper every day. Multiplication's sign rules are the ledger: like signs multiply to positive, unlike signs to negative. Why should negative times negative turn positive? Watch the pattern walk: each step down in the multiplier raises the product steadily until it crosses zero and keeps climbing. Count the negative factors in a long product - an even count lands positive, an odd count negative.

## DEFINITION
The sign rules for multiplication of integers state that the product of two integers with like signs (both positive or both negative) is always a positive integer, while the product of two integers with unlike signs (one positive and one negative) is always a negative integer.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the rules for multiplying two or more integers (positive and negative), their sign conventions, and basic computational applications. It does NOT cover advanced algebraic proofs of rings/fields or complex numbers.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are managing a school canteen bank account where a fee deduction of 5 rupees per day is recorded as $-5$. If this deduction happens for 4 consecutive days, what is the total change in your account balance? To solve this, we need to multiply a negative integer by a positive integer, building upon our understanding of repeated addition of integers from Class 6. We write down the repeated addition as $(-5) + (-5) + (-5) + (-5) = -20$, which corresponds to the multiplication expression $4 \times (-5)$. The key insight is that multiplication is repeated addition, and extending this to negative numbers gives us the fundamental sign rules: positive times negative is negative, and negative times negative is positive.

**Takeaway:** integer multiplication sign rules: like signs positive, unlike signs negative; magnitude multiplies as usual. **In the exam:** decide the sign first by counting negative factors, then multiply the absolute values. **Check yourself:** extend the daily-deduction pattern one more day and watch the sign logic hold. **Why students miss it:** they see one minus sign and force a negative product, miscounting the negatives in the factors. Count the negatives before touching the digits, and the sign is settled before the sizes multiply. Write the sign verdict above the working first, and the digits fall in line after.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often apply the rules of integer addition (such as "keeping the sign of the larger absolute value") to multiplication. For example, when calculating $(-8) \times 3$, they might look at $-8$ and think the answer must be negative because $8$ is larger, but they correctly get $-24$ by coincidence; however, when calculating $(-8) + 3$, they get $-5$, and then erroneously carry over that additive thinking to multiplication, writing things like $(-5) \times (-2) = -7$ by adding the numbers and attaching a negative sign.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that multiplication is shorthand for repeated addition, not addition itself. Show them that $(-5) \times 2$ means adding $-5$ twice: $(-5) + (-5) = -10$. Emphasize the sign rule memory aid: "Friends of my friends are friends (+), enemies of my enemies are friends (+), friends of my enemies are enemies (-)." Have them circle all negative signs in an expression first: an even number of circles makes a positive answer, and an odd number makes a negative answer.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A stock market tracker records a daily drop of 15 rupees in a share price for 4 consecutive days. Represent this total price change as an integer multiplication expression and calculate the final value.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given values and their signs. A daily drop of 15 rupees is represented as $-15$. The number of days is 4, represented as $+4$.
- Step 2: Set up the multiplication expression representing total change: $4 \times (-15)$.
- Step 3: Apply the sign rule for multiplying a positive integer and a negative integer (+ $\times$ - = -).
- Step 4: Multiply the absolute values: $4 \times 15 = 60$, and apply the negative sign to get $-60$.
- Step 5: Verify the result with units: The total price change is $-60$ rupees (a loss of 60 rupees).
REASONING: Since the drop happens repeatedly over 4 days, we multiply the negative daily change by the positive number of days. The rule states that multiplying numbers with unlike signs yields a negative product.
ANSWER: -60 rupees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An underground excavation machine descends 8 metres every hour. What is its position relative to ground level after $-5$ hours? (Interpret $-5$ hours as 5 hours in the past).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define the direction of movement per hour. Descending 8 metres is represented as $-8$.
- Step 2: Define the time factor. Looking back 5 hours is represented as $-5$.
- Step 3: Set up the expression: $(-5) \times (-8)$.
- Step 4: Apply the sign rule for multiplying two negative integers (- $\times$ - = +).
- Step 5: Multiply the absolute values: $5 \times 8 = 40$, giving a positive result of $+40$ metres.
REASONING: Multiplying a negative time duration (past hours) by a negative rate (descending movement) results in a positive displacement, meaning 40 metres above the current reference or a reversal of position.
ANSWER: +40 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Simplify the multi-step integer expression: $(-3) \times 4 \times (-2) \times (-5)$.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Group the integers into pairs from left to right: $[(-3) \times 4] \times [(-2) \times (-5)]$.
- Step 2: Evaluate the first pair: $(-3) \times 4$. Since one is negative and one is positive, the product is $-12$.
- Step 3: Evaluate the second pair: $(-2) \times (-5)$. Since both are negative, the product is $+10$.
- Step 4: Multiply the two intermediate results: $(-12) \times (+10)$.
- Step 5: Apply the sign rule for unlike signs (- $\times$ + = -) and calculate $12 \times 10 = 120$, yielding $-120$.
REASONING: When multiplying multiple integers, we apply the sign rules sequentially or count the total number of negative signs. Here, there are 3 negative signs (odd number), so the final product must be negative.
ANSWER: -120
