## ELI5
Watch a marble roll smoothly across the floor and a bus lurching through traffic: both move in straight lines, but only one keeps a promise. Uniform linear motion covers equal distances in equal intervals of time - any intervals, however small. The other is non-uniform: equal clocks, unequal gains. Log distance every couple of seconds with a stopwatch and the promise - or the broken promise - shows up in the table.

## DEFINITION
Uniform linear motion is the motion of an object along a straight line where it covers equal distances in equal intervals of time, no matter how small those time intervals are. Non-uniform linear motion occurs when an object covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time along a straight line, meaning its speed is changing. Both are measured using standard SI units of meters per second (m/s) for speed, meters (m) for distance, and seconds (s) for time.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers motion strictly along a straight line (linear motion) with constant speed (uniform) or changing speed (non-uniform). It does NOT cover circular motion, rotational motion, or acceleration calculations involving vector directions changing in two or three dimensions.
## WALKTHROUGH
Observe a ceiling fan running at a constant speed after it reaches its normal operating setting, or watch a marble rolling smoothly down a long, polished plastic slide. If you measure the distance the marble travels every single second, you will notice whether it covers the exact same number of centimeters each time or if the distance grows larger and larger as it speeds up. Building on our understanding of how speed compares fast and slow motion, we now look at *how* that speed behaves over consecutive time intervals. By recording distance at regular time marks—such as every 2 seconds using a stopwatch—we can determine if an object's speed remains steady. The core principle is comparing distance intervals: equal distances in equal times mean uniform motion, while unequal distances in equal times mean non-uniform motion.

**Takeaway:** uniform motion is a promise of equality: equal distances in equal time intervals, checked by logging distance against regular time marks. **In the exam:** in table questions, compute the speed of each interval - equal speeds say uniform; drifting speeds say non-uniform. **Check yourself:** log the walkthrough's marble at regular marks and let the equal columns argue the classification. **Why students miss it:** students judge straightness as uniformity - the flagged conflation. Straight but stumbling is non-uniform; the table, not the lane, decides. Compute interval speeds and the classification defends itself with arithmetic.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A toy train travels along a straight track. It covers 12 meters in the first 3 seconds, 12 meters in the next 3 seconds, and 12 meters in the final 3 seconds. Calculate its speed in each 3-second interval.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps:
1. Identify given values for each interval: Distance ($d$) = 12 m, Time ($t$) = 3 s.
2. Recall the speed formula: Speed ($v$) = $\frac{d}{t}$.
3. Substitute values for the first interval: $v_1 = \frac{12\text{ m}}{3\text{ s}}$.
4. Compute and track units: $v_1 = 4\text{ m/s}$. Since every interval has 12 m in 3 s, $v_2 = 4\text{ m/s}$ and $v_3 = 4\text{ m/s}$.
Answer: The speed in each interval is 4 m/s, confirming uniform motion. A student who incorrectly divides time by distance gets an inverted unit like s/m, showing a failure to connect formula variables correctly.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the following distance-time data for a bicycle moving in a straight line: At t=0s, d=0m; at t=2s, d=10m; at t=4s, d=25m; at t=6s, d=45m. Determine if the motion is uniform or non-uniform and explain your reasoning using interval calculations.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The motion is non-uniform. Interval 1 (0 to 2 s): speed = $\frac{10\text{ m}}{2\text{ s}} = 5\text{ m/s}$. Interval 2 (2 to 4 s): distance covered = $25 - 10 = 15\text{ m}$, time = $2\text{ s}$, speed = $\frac{15\text{ m}}{2\text{ s}} = 7.5\text{ m/s}$. Since the speeds in consecutive intervals are different ($5\text{ m/s} \neq 7.5\text{ m/s}$), the motion is non-uniform. A student who just checks total distance divided by total time without checking intervals misses that speed is changing mid-journey.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that if an object is moving forward in a straight line, its motion is automatically uniform because it is "just moving." They confuse the *fact of motion* with the *rate of motion*, failing to realize that speeding up, slowing down, or stopping means the motion is non-uniform even if the path is straight.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this, have the student take a toy car and push it across a rough carpet. Ask them to measure how far it travels in the 1st second, 2nd second, and 3rd second using a phone stopwatch or wall clock. When they see the car covers 40 cm in the first second but only 10 cm in the third second before stopping, point out that unequal distances in equal time intervals prove the motion is non-uniform.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A metro train on a straight elevated track travels 150 meters in the first 10 seconds, 150 meters in the next 10 seconds, and 150 meters in the third 10 seconds. Is this uniform or non-uniform motion? Calculate the speed for each interval to prove your answer.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given distance ($d$) and time ($t$) for each of the three intervals: Interval 1 ($d_1 = 150\text{ m}, t_1 = 10\text{ s}$), Interval 2 ($d_2 = 150\text{ m}, t_2 = 10\text{ s}$), Interval 3 ($d_3 = 150\text{ m}, t_3 = 10\text{ s}$).
- step 2: State the formula for speed: $v = \frac{d}{t}$ and verify units are in meters and seconds.
- step 3: Substitute the values for each interval: $v_1 = \frac{150\text{ m}}{10\text{ s}}$, $v_2 = \frac{150\text{ m}}{10\text{ s}}$, $v_3 = \frac{150\text{ m}}{10\text{ s}}$.
- step 4: Compute the values and track unit cancellation: $v_1 = 15\text{ m/s}$, $v_2 = 15\text{ m/s}$, $v_3 = 15\text{ m/s}$.
- step 5: Verify if the speeds are equal across all intervals. Since $15\text{ m/s} = 15\text{ m/s} = 15\text{ m/s}$, the motion is uniform linear motion.
REASONING: Equal distances covered in equal time intervals mean the speed never changes, which is the exact definition of uniform linear motion. Calculating individual interval speeds proves that the rate of travel is constant.
ANSWER: Uniform motion; speed in all intervals is 15 m/s.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A cyclist riding in a straight line covers 8 meters in the first 2 seconds, 14 meters in the next 2 seconds, and 18 meters in the final 2 seconds. Classify the motion as uniform or non-uniform by calculating the speed in each interval.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given intervals: Interval 1 ($d_1 = 8\text{ m}, t_1 = 2\text{ s}$), Interval 2 ($d_2 = 14\text{ m}, t_2 = 2\text{ s}$), Interval 3 ($d_3 = 18\text{ m}, t_3 = 2\text{ s}$).
- step 2: State the speed formula $v = \frac{d}{t}$ and check that units match SI requirements.
- step 3: Substitute values into the formula for each respective interval: $v_1 = \frac{8\text{ m}}{2\text{ s}}$, $v_2 = \frac{14\text{ m}}{2\text{ s}}$, $v_3 = \frac{18\text{ m}}{2\text{ s}}$.
- step 4: Compute speeds with unit tracking: $v_1 = 4\text{ m/s}$, $v_2 = 7\text{ m/s}$, $v_3 = 9\text{ m/s}$.
- step 5: Compare the computed speeds: $4\text{ m/s} \neq 7\text{ m/s} \neq 9\text{ m/s}$. Because the speed changes in every interval, conclude that the motion is non-uniform.
REASONING: Since the cyclist covers unequal distances in equal time intervals, the speed is increasing, making the motion non-uniform.
ANSWER: Non-uniform motion; speeds are 4 m/s, 7 m/s, and 9 m/s.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A bus accelerates down a straight highway. In the first 5 seconds, it covers 20 meters. In the next 5 seconds, it covers 40 meters. Assuming the acceleration continues at the same rate, calculate the distance the bus will cover in the third 5-second interval (from $t = 10\text{ s}$ to $t = 15\text{ s}$).
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the given distance increments: Interval 1 ($20\text{ m}$ in $5\text{ s}$), Interval 2 ($40\text{ m}$ in $5\text{ s}$).
- step 2: Calculate the increase in distance between consecutive equal time intervals: $40\text{ m} - 20\text{ m} = 20\text{ m}$ increase.
- step 3: Assume a constant rate of increase in distance for uniform acceleration: Interval 3 distance will increase by another $20\text{ m}$ compared to Interval 2, so $d_3 = 40\text{ m} + 20\text{ m} = 60\text{ m}$.
- step 4: Calculate the speed in the third interval using $v_3 = \frac{d_3}{t_3} = \frac{60\text{ m}}{5\text{ s}}$.
- step 5: Compute the final speed and verify physical reasonableness: $v_3 = 12\text{ m/s}$, which is higher than $v_2 = 8\text{ m/s}$ and $v_1 = 4\text{ m/s}$, confirming realistic acceleration.
REASONING: In this specific non-uniform motion pattern (constant acceleration), the distance covered in equal time intervals increases by a constant amount each time. Tracking this pattern allows us to predict future distance without complex high school formulas.
ANSWER: 60 meters covered in the third 5-second interval.
