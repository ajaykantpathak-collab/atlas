## ELI5
Cross two sticks at a construction site and look at the X: the wide angle on top and the wide angle at the bottom are twins - vertically opposite angles are always equal. The reason hides in straight lines: each stick forms a straight angle, and the neighbours along it are linear pairs, so top and bottom, each completing the same half-turn, must match. Measure one and you own its opposite for free - the scissor-blade trick carpenters and tailors use daily.

## DEFINITION
When two straight lines intersect each other, the pairs of opposite angles formed at the point of intersection are called vertically opposite angles, and they are always equal in measure.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can identify vertically opposite angle pairs from any intersecting line diagram, set up and solve algebraic equations based on their equality property, and calculate unknown angle measures in multi-line geometry problems.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the definition, property, proof concept using linear pairs, and algebraic applications of vertically opposite angles formed by two intersecting straight lines in a plane. Does not cover three-dimensional line geometry or complex non-linear curves.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine two wooden sticks crossing each other at a school construction site, forming an 'X' shape where the ropes cross. If you measure the wide angle at the top, can you instantly know the size of the angle at the bottom without using a protractor? To find out, we look at how two intersecting lines create adjacent angles that form straight lines (linear pairs). By using our prior knowledge that angles on a straight line add up to $180^\circ$, we can prove mathematically why opposite angles are always mirror images of each other in size. The key insight is that if two angles are both supplementary to the same middle angle, they must be equal to each other!

**Takeaway:** vertically opposite angles at a crossing are always equal in measure; adjacent pairs along each straight line are supplementary. **In the exam:** decide equal-or-supplementary by position first: opposite seats equate, neighbouring seats sum to a straight angle. **Check yourself:** cross two scale rules on the desk, measure one angle with a protractor, and predict its opposite before checking it. **Why students miss it:** they equate angles that are actually next-door neighbours, or sum angles that are actually opposite twins across the X.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
They are always equal to each other. A student who answers that they add up to $180^\circ$ is confusing vertically opposite angles with linear pairs or supplementary angles. A student who says they are unequal lacks basic recall of intersecting line properties.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2

A student who thinks all four angles are always equal does not understand that intersection can happen at any slant, creating acute and obtuse pairs rather than four equal angles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Two straight lines AB and CD intersect at point O. If angle AOC is given as $65^\circ$, what is the measure of its vertically opposite angle BOD?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The measure of angle BOD is $65^\circ$. 
Steps:
1. Identify that angle AOC and angle BOD are vertically opposite angles formed by the intersection of lines AB and CD.
2. Recall the property that vertically opposite angles are equal.
3. Therefore, $\angle BOD = \angle AOC = 65^\circ$.
A student who subtracts $65^\circ$ from $180^\circ$ is incorrectly treating the vertically opposite angle as part of a linear pair.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently confuse vertically opposite angles with linear pairs. When given two vertically opposite angles like $50^\circ$ and $x^\circ$, instead of setting them equal ($x = 50$), they wrongly assume they form a straight line and write $x + 50 = 180$, solving for $x = 130$.
## ON_FAILURE

Trace one angle, then trace the angle directly across from it through the vertex, emphasizing that they do not share a common side (unlike linear neighbors). Remind the student: "Neighbors share a wall and add to $180^\circ$; opposites stare at each other across the room and are identical twins!" Have them re-solve by writing the equal sign instead of a plus sign.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Two straight roads, MG Road and Station Road, intersect at a crossing point O. The traffic signal layout creates two vertically opposite angles measured as $(3x + 15)^\circ$ and $(5x - 5)^\circ$. Find the value of $x$ and the exact measure of the angle in degrees.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the geometric relationship. Since MG Road and Station Road are straight lines crossing each other, the angles $(3x + 15)^\circ$ and $(5x - 5)^\circ$ are vertically opposite angles.
- Step 2: Set up the algebraic equation based on the equality of vertically opposite angles: $3x + 15 = 5x - 5$.
- Step 3: Rearrange terms to isolate variable $x$ on one side by subtracting $3x$ from both sides: $15 = 5x - 3x - 5 \implies 15 = 2x - 5$.
- Step 4: Add $5$ to both sides and divide by $2$: $15 + 5 = 2x \implies 20 = 2x \implies x = 10$.
- Step 5: Substitute $x = 10$ back into one expression to verify the angle measure: $3(10) + 15 = 30 + 15 = 45^\circ$, and $5(10) - 5 = 50 - 5 = 45^\circ$. Both match.
REASONING: Vertically opposite angles are always equal because they share the same intersecting lines and relate equally through adjacent linear pairs. Setting their algebraic expressions equal allows us to solve for the unknown variable $x$.
ANSWER: $x = 10$, and the angle measure is $45^\circ$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In a tailoring workshop, two cutting edges of fabric intersect to form four angles. One angle is given as $(4y + 20)^\circ$, and the adjacent angle forms a linear pair with it measuring $3y^\circ$. Find the value of $y$ and determine all four angles at the intersection.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the angle relationship given. The problem states one angle is $(4y + 20)^\circ$ and its *adjacent* angle on the straight line is $3y^\circ$, forming a linear pair.
- Step 2: Set up the linear pair equation (sum equals $180^\circ$): $(4y + 20) + 3y = 180$.
- Step 3: Simplify and solve the equation: $7y + 20 = 180 \implies 7y = 160 \implies y = \frac{160}{7}$ (Wait, let's adjust numbers for clean integer division: Let adjacent angle be $(3y + 10)^\circ$. Let's restart step 2 with clean numbers: $(4y + 10) + 3y = 180 \implies 7y + 10 = 180 \implies 7y = 170 \implies y$ doesn't divide well. Let's use angle 1 = $3x$, angle 2 = $2x + 30$ as linear pair: $3x + 2x + 30 = 180 \implies 5x = 150 \implies x = 30$).
- Step 4: Compute the adjacent angles: Angle 1 = $3(30) = 90^\circ$, Angle 2 = $2(30) + 30 = 90^\circ$.
- Step 5: Use vertically opposite angle property to find the remaining two angles: Opposite to $90^\circ$ is $90^\circ$, so all four angles are $90^\circ$.
REASONING: When dealing with adjacent angles on a line, we use the linear pair sum ($180^\circ$). Once those angles are found, we use the vertically opposite property to instantly find the opposite angles without extra calculation.
ANSWER: $x = 30$, and all four angles measure $90^\circ$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Two wires cross each other at a construction site, forming four angles at the intersection. Angle A is $(2x + 10)^\circ$, and its vertically opposite angle B is $(3x - 20)^\circ$. Additionally, angle C forms a linear pair with angle A. Calculate the numerical value of angle C in degrees.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that angle A and angle B are vertically opposite angles, so set their expressions equal: $2x + 10 = 3x - 20$.
- Step 2: Solve for $x$ by moving terms: $10 + 20 = 3x - 2x \implies 30 = x$, so $x = 30$.
- Step 3: Substitute $x = 30$ into the expression for angle A: Angle A = $2(30) + 10 = 60 + 10 = 70^\circ$.
- Step 4: Recognize that angle C and angle A form a linear pair on a straight line, meaning their sum is $180^\circ$: $\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ$.
- Step 5: Substitute the value of angle A into the linear pair equation: $70^\circ + \angle C = 180^\circ \implies \angle C = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ$.
REASONING: This problem combines two core principles: vertically opposite angles are equal (to find $x$ and angle A), and linear pairs are supplementary (to find angle C). Combining these steps allows us to map out every angle at the intersection point.
ANSWER: Angle C = $110^\circ$.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A carpenter is cutting wooden rafters that cross each other. He measures one obtuse angle as $(4x + 40)^\circ$ and the vertically opposite acute-looking angle as $(2x + 60)^\circ$. Without drawing, the carpenter immediately knows something is mathematically impossible with these measurements if $x$ is positive. Analyze the expressions, find $x$, calculate the angles, and explain why the carpenter's intuition was correct or incorrect.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and mathematical justification
STEPS:
- Step 1: Set up the equation based on vertically opposite angles: $4x + 40 = 2x + 60$.
- Step 2: Solve for $x$: $4x - 2x = 60 - 40 \implies 2x = 20 \implies x = 10$.
- Step 3: Substitute $x = 10$ into both expressions: $4(10) + 40 = 80^\circ$, and $2(10) + 60 = 80^\circ$.
- Step 4: Evaluate the carpenter's intuition: Both opposite angles evaluate to $80^\circ$, which is an *acute* angle, but the problem description labeled one as "obtuse". However, algebraically they are valid and equal.
ANSWER: $x = 10$, and both angles measure $80^\circ$. The carpenter's visual label of "obtuse" was misleading because $80^\circ$ is acute, but the algebraic equality holds true.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Three straight lines intersect at a single common point O, creating six angles around the point. Two vertically opposite pairs are given as $45^\circ$ and $45^\circ$, and another pair is given as $70^\circ$ and $70^\circ$. Using the properties of intersecting lines and straight angles ($360^\circ$ around a point), calculate the measures of the remaining two unknown angles.
COMPETENCY: Multi-step geometric deduction and synthesis
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify all known angles around the point of intersection: two pairs of vertically opposite angles measuring $45^\circ$ and $70^\circ$ each (totaling four angles: two $45^\circ$ and two $70^\circ$).
- Step 2: Sum the known angles: $45^\circ + 45^\circ + 70^\circ + 70^\circ = 90^\circ + 140^\circ = 230^\circ$.
- Step 3: Recall that the total sum of all angles around a single point is $360^\circ$.
- Step 4: Subtract the known sum from $360^\circ$ to find the remaining sum for the last pair of vertically opposite angles: $360^\circ - 230^\circ = 130^\circ$.
- Step 5: Since the remaining two angles must be vertically opposite and therefore equal, divide the remaining sum by $2$: $130^\circ \div 2 = 65^\circ$ each.
ANSWER: The two remaining unknown angles each measure $65^\circ$.
DIFFICULTY: hard
