## ELI5
A scale reading 3.524 kilograms of tomatoes is a small ladder of powers of ten: every seat is ten times richer than the seat to its right and one-tenth of the seat to its left. That single relationship runs the whole show - it explains why shifting the point one step right multiplies by ten, why each decimal digit shrinks as it travels right, and how expanded forms unroll a number into sums of tenths and hundredths. Master the ladder and the point is just a doorway.

## DEFINITION
The decimal system and place value relationships describe how each position in a decimal number is ten times greater than the position immediately to its right and one-tenth of the position immediately to its left, extending the base-10 structure of whole numbers past the decimal point. This relationship allows any decimal to be expressed as a sum of products of digits and powers of ten (both positive and negative).

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at a vegetable market in India buying tomatoes, and the weighing scale shows 3.524 kilograms. When you look at the digits 5, 2, and 4 after the decimal point, do you know how much smaller each one is compared to the whole number 3? To begin understanding this, we write down the place values: ones, tenths, hundredths, and thousandths. We connect this to our prior knowledge of whole number place values where moving left multiplies by 10. The key insight here is that moving to the right divides by 10, meaning each place value is exactly one-tenth ($\frac{1}{10}$) of the place value to its immediate left.

**Takeaway:** the decimal ladder runs on powers of ten: each seat is ten times its right neighbour and one-tenth of its left. **In the exam:** for value-of-digit questions, name the seat first, then the fraction; for point shifts, move the point and state the multiply factor. **Check yourself:** take the tomato reading and say each digit's seat and its value aloud in one steady, unhurried pass. **Why students miss it:** they slide the point the wrong way, shrinking a number when they meant to grow it ten times over - say the direction aloud first.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why the value of the digit 4 in 4.321 is 100 times greater than the digit 4 in 0.041.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct answer is that in 4.321, the digit 4 is in the ones place ($4 \times 1$), whereas in 0.041, the digit 4 is in the hundredths place ($4 \times \frac{1}{100}$). Moving two places to the left multiplies a value by $10 \times 10 = 100$. A wrong answer indicates a failure to understand that place value positions change by powers of 10 for every step crossed.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate the result of multiplying the digit value of 6 in the tenths place of 45.62 by the digit value of 6 in the tens place of 61.4.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is 3.6. 
Step 1: Identify the digit in the tenths place of 45.62, which is 6, giving a value of $6 \times 0.1 = 0.6$.
Step 2: Identify the digit in the tens place of 61.4, which is 6, giving a value of $6 \times 10 = 60$.
Step 3: Multiply the two values: $0.6 \times 60 = 36 \times 0.1 = 3.6$.
A wrong answer usually stems from multiplying the face values ($6 \times 6 = 36$) without adjusting for their actual place value multipliers.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that shifting the decimal point of 5.42 one place to the right makes the number 10 times smaller because the digits move closer to the decimal point. Is this statement correct? Evaluate and justify.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The statement is incorrect. Shifting the decimal point one place to the right actually makes the number 10 times *larger* (e.g., $5.42$ becomes $54.2$). Each digit shifts to a position that has a place value 10 times greater (the ones become tens, the tenths become ones, etc.). A wrong answer shows a misunderstanding of the directionality of place value amplification.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often assume that place values to the right of the decimal point increase in magnitude as the digits get larger numerically, or they treat the decimal part as a separate whole number (e.g., thinking 0.45 is greater than 0.5 because 45 is greater than 5). Another common error is multiplying or dividing by powers of 10 by simply adding or removing zeros without tracking the movement of the decimal point relative to place value columns.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student struggles, stop and draw a place value chart explicitly showing columns for Hundreds, Tens, Ones, . (Decimal Point), Tenths ($\frac{1}{10}$), Hundredths ($\frac{1}{100}$), and Thousandths ($\frac{1}{1000}$). Have them physically place digit cards into the chart and point out that moving one column right means dividing by 10, while moving one column left means multiplying by 10. Reinforce this with fraction equivalents (e.g., $0.1 = \frac{1}{10}$).

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Find the sum of the place values of the digits 7 and 4 in the number 379.248, and express the result in both fraction and decimal forms.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the position and value of the digit 7 in 379.248. The 7 is in the tens place, so its value is $7 \times 10 = 70$.
- Step 2: Identify the position and value of the digit 4 in 379.248. The 4 is in the hundredths place, so its value is $4 \times \frac{1}{100} = \frac{4}{100} = 0.04$.
- Step 3: Add the two place values together: $70 + 0.04$.
- Step 4: Perform algebraic combination: $70 + \frac{4}{100} = 70\frac{4}{100}$ or $70.04$.
- Step 5: Verify units and ensure both decimal and fraction forms are correctly represented.
REASONING: Place value analysis requires isolating each digit according to its column multiplier (tens = 10, hundredths = 0.01) and combining them without altering their relative positional weights.
ANSWER: 70.04 (or $70\frac{4}{100}$)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Riya has a ribbon measuring 45.82 metres. How many times larger is the place value of the digit 5 than the place value of the digit 2 in this measurement?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Locate the digit 5 in 45.82. It is in the ones place, so its place value is $5 \times 1 = 5$.
- Step 2: Locate the digit 2 in 45.82. It is in the hundredths place, so its place value is $2 \times 0.01 = 0.02$.
- Step 3: Set up the ratio to find how many times larger the ones place is compared to the hundredths place: Ratio = $\frac{\text{Value of ones place}}{\text{Value of hundredths place}}$. Wait, the question asks about the place value *itself* (the columns), or the values represented by the digits. Let's compare the column values: Ones place is 1, hundredths place is 0.01. Or compare the digit values: $5$ vs $0.02$. Let's compare the columns: Ones column ($1$) is $100$ times larger than the hundredths column ($0.01$) because $1 \div 0.01 = 100$.
- Step 4: Calculate the multiplier: $1 \div \frac{1}{100} = 1 \times 100 = 100$.
- Step 5: State the final relationship clearly with proper justification.
REASONING: Moving from the ones place to the tenths place divides by 10, and moving from tenths to hundredths divides by another 10. Therefore, the ones place is $10 \times 10 = 100$ times larger than the hundredths place.
ANSWER: 100 times larger

COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying place value arrangement for optimization
STEPS:
- Step 1: Determine Number A (maximum value) by placing the largest available digit in the highest place value (tenths): $A = 0.531$.
- Step 2: Determine Number B (minimum value) by placing the smallest available digit in the highest place value (tenths): $B = 0.135$.
- Step 3: Set up the addition of Number A and Number B: $0.531 + 0.135$.
- Step 4: Perform column-by-column addition: Thousandths ($1 + 5 = 6$), Hundredths ($3 + 3 = 6$), Tenths ($5 + 1 = 6$).
