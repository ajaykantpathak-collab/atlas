## ELI5
Packing for sports day you never count 'three red bottles plus two notebooks': bottles group with bottles, notebooks with notebooks. Algebra does exactly the same. Like terms are terms wearing the same variable part - same letters, same powers - and only they may merge, by adding or subtracting their coefficients. Unlike terms stay politely separate forever, like umbrellas mixed with lunch boxes. Simplify an expression by grouping the families, combining each, and the long messy line shrinks to its shortest honest form.

## DEFINITION
Simplification of algebraic expressions involving like terms is the process of combining terms that contain the exact same variables raised to the exact same powers into a single term by adding or subtracting their numerical coefficients. This reduces a long, complicated expression into its shortest, most compact form without changing its overall value.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are packing bags for a school sports day: you have 3 red water bottles, 5 notebooks, 2 red water bottles, and 4 notebooks. To know how many items of each kind you have, you group the red water bottles together and the notebooks together, saying you have $3 + 2 = 5$ red water bottles and $5 + 4 = 9$ notebooks. In algebra, we do the exact same thing when we encounter expressions with letters representing items, measurements, or unknowns. We begin by scanning the expression from left to right, identifying terms that share the exact same variable parts (like terms). We then rearrange and group these like terms using the commutative property of addition, just like we did with integers in Class 6. Finally, we add or subtract their numerical coefficients while keeping the variable part completely unchanged. The key insight is that variables act like labels or units—you can combine 3 apples and 2 apples to make 5 apples, but you cannot combine apples and bananas directly!

**Takeaway:** like terms are the same variable family; only families may merge, by combining coefficients. **In the exam:** simplify by grouping families first, sign attached to each term, then combine each family in one pass. **Check yourself:** sort the sports-day packing list into families and count each family in one line. **Why students miss it:** they merge constants with variable terms or add exponents, inventing variables that never existed.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which of the following pairs of terms are considered "like terms" in an algebraic expression?
A) $4x$ and $4y$
B) $7x^2$ and $3x$
C) $-5ab$ and $12ba$
D) $2xy$ and $2xz$
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is C) $-5ab$ and $12ba$. Like terms must have the exact same variables, and the order of multiplied variables does not matter because multiplication is commutative ($ab = ba$). A wrong answer choosing A or D reveals that the student confuses different variables ($x, y, z$) as being alike simply because they are letters. A wrong answer choosing B reveals that the student does not realize exponents must also match ($x^2$ and $x$ are unlike terms).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
A student simplifies the expression $8x - 3x + 2$ and writes the answer as $7x$. Explain why this answer is incorrect and identify the misconception.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct simplified expression is $5x + 2$. The student's answer of $7x$ is incorrect because they mistakenly combined a variable term ($3x$) with a constant term ($2$), treating the constant as if it had an $x$ attached to it. Constants and variable terms are unlike terms and cannot be added together into a single term. A wrong answer here indicates that the student fails to distinguish between terms with variables and standalone numbers (constants).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Simplify the following algebraic expression completely: $12a - 5b + 3a + 8b - 4$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $15a + 3b - 4$. 
Step 1: Identify and group the like terms together: $(12a + 3a) + (-5b + 8b) - 4$.
Step 2: Combine the coefficients of the $a$ terms: $12 + 3 = 15$, so we get $15a$.
Step 3: Combine the coefficients of the $b$ terms using integer rules: $-5 + 8 = 3$, so we get $+3b$.
Step 4: Bring down the constant term $-4$ since it has no other like terms to combine with.
Step 5: Write the final combined expression: $15a + 3b - 4$. A wrong answer usually stems from integer sign errors when combining $-5b$ and $+8b$, or mistakenly combining the constant $-4$ with the variable terms.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the two expressions below and determine if they are equivalent for all values of $x$: 
Expression 1: $6x + 9 - 2x + 4$ 
Expression 2: $4x + 13$
Are they equivalent, and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Yes, Expression 1 and Expression 2 are entirely equivalent. 
Analysis: For Expression 1, grouping like terms gives $(6x - 2x) + (9 + 4)$. Simplifying the $x$ terms gives $4x$, and simplifying the constants gives $+13$. This results in $4x + 13$, which matches Expression 2 exactly. A wrong answer reveals that the student either grouped unlike terms together or made an arithmetic error with the constants (e.g., $9 - 4$ instead of $9 + 4$).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the student's work for simplifying: $10x^2 - 4x + 3x^2 + 7x$.
Student's Step 1: Group like terms: $(10x^2 + 3x^2) + (-4x + 7x)$
Student's Step 2: Combine coefficients: $13x^4 + 3x^2$
Is the student's final answer correct? If not, identify the exact step where the error occurred.

The student's final answer is incorrect. The error occurred in Step 2. ## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create an algebraic expression containing at least five terms with two different variables ($x$ and $y$) and at least one constant, such that when fully simplified, the result is $5x - 2y + 6$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An acceptable response is any expression that expands out or rearranges to $5x - 2y + 6$, such as $3x - y + 2x - y + 6$. 
Criteria for evaluation: The student must include terms with $x$, terms with $y$, and constants that, when grouped and combined according to integer rules, yield exactly $5x$, $-2y$, and $+6$. A poor answer shows terms that cannot be simplified to these exact coefficients or mixes up variable powers.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently commit the "exponent addition error" when combining like terms with powers, writing $3x^2 + 5x^2 = 8x^4$ instead of $8x^2$. They mistakenly treat addition of like terms the same way they treat multiplication of terms with exponents, forgetting that variables and their exponents represent scaling factors rather than repeated multiplication when combined additively. Another major error is combining constants with variable terms, such as simplifying $4x + 3$ into $7x$.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student makes these errors, guide them back to physical grouping and units. Ask: "If $x^2$ represents the area of a square tile of side $x$, and you have 3 square tiles plus 5 square tiles, do you suddenly get tiles of area $x^4$, or do you simply have 8 square tiles of area $x^2$?" Emphasize that the variable part acts like a unit tag (like 'metres' or 'kilograms') that stays completely unchanged during addition and subtraction; only the counting number in front (the coefficient) changes.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Riya went to the market and bought $7x$ notebooks and $3y$ pens. Later, she bought another $2x$ notebooks and lost $y$ pens. Write an algebraic expression for the total items and simplify it.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write down the algebraic expression representing all items bought and changed: $(7x + 3y) + (2x - y)$.
- Step 2: Use the commutative and associative properties to group like terms together: $(7x + 2x) + (3y - y)$.
- Step 3: Combine the coefficients of the like terms for $x$: $7 + 2 = 9$, giving $9x$.
- Step 4: Combine the coefficients of the like terms for $y$ (remembering that $-y$ has a coefficient of $-1$): $3 - 1 = 2$, giving $+2y$.
- Step 5: Write the final simplified expression combining both results: $9x + 2y$.
REASONING: Grouping terms with the same variable allows us to collect similar quantities. By treating the coefficients as integers and performing addition and subtraction, we find the net amount of each item without altering the variable labels.
ANSWER: $9x + 2y$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Simplify the algebraic expression: $15a^2 - 8b + 4a^2 + 12b - 6 - 3a^2$.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify all different types of terms in the expression: $a^2$ terms, $b$ terms, and constant numbers.
- Step 2: Group all like terms together using parentheses: $(15a^2 + 4a^2 - 3a^2) + (-8b + 12b) - 6$.
- Step 3: Combine the coefficients of the $a^2$ terms: $15 + 4 - 3 = 16$, resulting in $16a^2$.
- Step 4: Combine the coefficients of the $b$ terms: $-8 + 12 = 4$, resulting in $+4b$.
- Step 5: Bring down the remaining constant term $-6$ and assemble the final expression: $16a^2 + 4b - 6$.
REASONING: Long expressions can be intimidating, but breaking them down into categories (like sorting items into separate baskets) ensures no term is missed. Integer arithmetic rules govern the combination of coefficients.
ANSWER: $16a^2 + 4b - 6$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A rectangular playground has a length of $(5x + 3)$ metres and a breadth of $(2x - 1)$ metres. Find the simplified algebraic expression for its perimeter.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall the formula for the perimeter of a rectangle: $\text{Perimeter} = 2 \times (\text{length} + \text{breadth})$.
- Step 2: Substitute the given algebraic expressions into the formula: $\text{Perimeter} = 2 \times ((5x + 3) + (2x - 1))$.
- Step 3: Simplify inside the inner parentheses by grouping like terms: $5x + 2x + 3 - 1 = 7x + 2$.
- Step 4: Multiply the simplified inner expression by 2: $2 \times (7x + 2) = (2 \times 7x) + (2 \times 2)$.
- Step 5: Compute the final multiplication and present the simplified expression with units: $14x + 4$ metres.
REASONING: Geometric word problems require translating dimensions into algebraic expressions first. Once formulated, perimeter calculation relies entirely on grouping like terms and distributing constants across binomials.
ANSWER: $14x + 4$ metres
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two students, Amit and Priya, simplify the expression $10x - [4x - (3x - 2)]$.
Amit's answer: $9x - 2$
Priya's answer: $3x + 2$
Who is correct? Show your step-by-step simplification to justify your answer.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and systematic simplification of expressions with grouping symbols
STEPS:
- Step 1: Address the innermost parentheses/brackets first by examining signs.
- Step 2: Distribute any negative signs carefully across terms inside brackets.
- Step 3: Combine like terms step-by-step from inside out.
- Step 4: Verify the final coefficient and constant.
ANSWER: Priya is correct. 
Step-by-step working: 
1. Given expression: $10x - [4x - (3x - 2)]$
2. Remove inner brackets: $10x - [4x - 3x + 2]$
3. Simplify inside the square brackets by combining like terms ($4x - 3x = x$): $10x - [x + 2]$
4. Distribute the negative sign outside the square brackets: $10x - x - 2$
5. Combine final like terms ($10x - x = 9x$, wait: $10x - x = 9x$... let us re-evaluate: $10x - x = 9x - 2$). 
Correction check: Amit's answer is $9x - 2$, which is actually correct! Let us re-verify Priya's work or re-calculate: $10x - [4x - 3x + 2] = 10x - [x + 2] = 10x - x - 2 = 9x - 2$. Therefore, Amit is correct and Priya made an error with the subtraction of $x$ from $10x$.
DIFFICULTY: medium

ANSWER:

$= px + qy + 3z - 2px + qy - z$
$= (px - 2px) + (qy + qy) + (3z - z)$
$= -px + 2qy + 2z$ rupees.
