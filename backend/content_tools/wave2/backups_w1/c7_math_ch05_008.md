## ELI5
A footbridge crossing a railway track leaves opposite corners at its two crossings - alternate angles, resting on opposite sides of the transversal in non-adjacent seats, tucked inside as alternate interior or hanging outside as alternate exterior. When the tracks are parallel, the Z-pattern delivers the promise: the alternate pair is strictly equal, each angle a copy of the other shifted along the line. One measured angle immediately prices its alternate - and if the two ever disagree, the lines were never parallel at all.

## DEFINITION
Alternate interior or exterior angles are pairs of angles formed on opposite sides of a transversal line intersecting two other lines, occupying non-adjacent positions. When the two intersected lines are parallel, these alternate interior or exterior angles are strictly equal in measure.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine walking along a straight railway track crossed diagonally by a footbridge overhead; the opposite corners created at the crossings look identical in angle slope. To study this, draw two horizontal lines crossed by a straight slanted line called a transversal. Label the eight resulting angles from 1 to 8. Look for pairs that form a "Z" or "N" shape between the two lines, residing on opposite (alternate) sides of the transversal. When the two horizontal lines are strictly parallel, these alternate pairs share the exact same degree measure.

**Takeaway:** alternate interior or exterior angles sit on opposite sides of the transversal in non-adjacent seats; when the lines are parallel they are equal. **In the exam:** spot the Z-shape for alternates, then equate the expressions; unequal alternates are proof the lines were never parallel. **Check yourself:** draw the Z-shape over two rails and a slant, name the alternate interior pair aloud, and restate the equality logic. **Why students miss it:** they grab same-side interior pairs instead of the opposite-side alternates, and add to a straight angle where plain equality was due. Name the four seats at each crossing of the footbridge, and the Z reveals the exact pair that equality binds together between the two intersections.

The correct answer is alternate interior angles. A student who answers "corresponding angles" is confusing positions on the same side of the transversal, while a student who answers "vertically opposite angles" is missing the transversal concept entirely.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, why are the alternate interior angles equal in measure?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
They are equal because parallel lines maintain a constant distance apart, meaning any intersecting transversal tilts at the exact same angle to both lines, creating congruent rotational symmetry at both intersection points. A wrong answer like "because they add up to 180 degrees" shows confusion with consecutive interior angles (linear pairs or co-interior angles).

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently confuse alternate interior angles with consecutive interior angles on the same side of the transversal. When solving algebraic problems where lines are parallel, they mistakenly set the sum of alternate interior angles to $180^\circ$ (e.g., writing $(3x) + (2x) = 180$) instead of equating them $(3x = 2x)$.
## ON_FAILURE
Draw a clear diagram of two parallel lines cut by a transversal and use a colored highlighter to trace out a large "Z" shape. Show the student that the two corners inside the "Z" sit snugly against each other and must therefore be identical in opening size. Remind them: "Alternate means opposite sides of the road (transversal), so they match; consecutive means same side team sharing the room, so they add up to 180."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Two parallel railway tracks in a Mumbai station yard are intersected by a straight signal wire serving as a transversal. Two alternate interior angles are given as $(4x - 15)^\circ$ and $(2x + 25)^\circ$. Find the value of $x$ and the exact angle measure in degrees.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given angle expressions: $\angle 1 = 4x - 15$ and $\angle 2 = 2x + 25$, and note that alternate interior angles of parallel lines are equal.
- Step 2: Set up the algebraic equation by equating the two expressions: $4x - 15 = 2x + 25$.
- Step 3: Rearrange terms to bring variable terms to one side by subtracting $2x$ from both sides: $4x - 2x - 15 = 25 \implies 2x - 15 = 25$.
- Step 4: Add $15$ to both sides of the equation: $2x = 25 + 15 \implies 2x = 40$.
- Step 5: Divide by $2$ to find $x$: $x = 20$. Substitute $x = 20$ into one expression: $4(20) - 15 = 80 - 15 = 65^\circ$.
REASONING: Because the tracks are parallel, the alternate interior angles must be equal. Setting them equal creates a linear equation in one variable, which is solved step-by-step using balance operations to find the exact numerical value of $x$.
ANSWER: x = 20, angle measure = 65 degrees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In a school window grille, two parallel iron bars are cut by a decorative diagonal rod. Two alternate exterior angles are measured as $(5y + 10)^\circ$ and $(7y - 30)^\circ$. Calculate the value of $y$ and the measure of these alternate exterior angles.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that alternate exterior angles formed by parallel lines are equal: $5y + 10 = 7y - 30$.
- Step 2: Group the variable terms on the right side by subtracting $5y$ from both sides: $10 = 7y - 5y - 30 \implies 10 = 2y - 30$.
- Step 3: Isolate the variable term by adding $30$ to both sides: $10 + 30 = 2y \implies 40 = 2y$.
- Step 4: Divide both sides by $2$ to solve for $y$: $y = 40 / 2 = 20$.
- Step 5: Substitute $y = 20$ back into the first expression to verify: $5(20) + 10 = 100 + 10 = 110^\circ$. (Checking second expression: $7(20) - 30 = 140 - 30 = 110^\circ$).
REASONING: Alternate exterior angles share the same equality property as alternate interior angles when lines are parallel. Equating them and isolating the variable yields the correct angle measure.
ANSWER: y = 20, angle measure = 110 degrees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A carpenter cuts two parallel wooden planks with a transversal measuring strip. One alternate interior angle is given as $(3z + 30)^\circ$, and the consecutive interior angle on the same side is $(2z + 20)^\circ$. Find the value of $z$ and determine all angle measures.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Note that the problem gives one alternate interior angle ($3z + 30$) and one consecutive interior angle ($2z + 20$). Recall that alternate interior angles are equal, and consecutive interior angles are supplementary ($180^\circ$). But wait! Let us relate them using a transversal property: the alternate interior angle is equal to the other alternate interior angle, and its vertically opposite or linear pair can be found. Alternatively, use the fact that an angle alternate to $(3z+30)$ forms a linear pair with $(2z+20)$.
- Step 2: Formulate the relationship: The angle alternate to $(3z+30)$ is also $(3z+30)$, and it lies on a straight line with the consecutive interior angle $(2z+20)$, making them supplementary. Therefore: $(3z + 30) + (2z + 20) = 180$.
- Step 3: Combine like terms on the left side: $5z + 50 = 180$.
- Step 4: Subtract $50$ from both sides: $5z = 180 - 50 \implies 5z = 130$.
- Step 5: Divide by $5$: $z = 130 / 5 = 26$.
- Step 6: Calculate the angle measures: Alternate interior angle = $3(26) + 30 = 78 + 30 = 108^\circ$; Consecutive interior angle = $2(26) + 20 = 52 + 20 = 72^\circ$. Check sum: $108 + 72 = 180^\circ$.
REASONING: This problem combines alternate angle properties with supplementary linear pair/consecutive interior properties. By linking the angles through geometric relationships, we form a single linear equation to solve for $z$.
ANSWER: z = 26, angle measures are 108 degrees and 72 degrees
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Rahul designs a staircase with two parallel steel beams crossed by a slanted support beam. He measures two alternate interior angles as $(6x - 24)^\circ$ and $(2x + 36)^\circ$. 
(a) Set up the equation and solve for $x$.
(b) Rahul's friend claims the beams are not parallel because $x$ is an even number. Is the friend's mathematical reasoning correct? Justify your answer.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and mathematical justification
STEPS:
- Step 1: Equate the two alternate interior angles: $6x - 24 = 2x + 36$.
- Step 2: Solve for $x$ by subtracting $2x$ and adding $24$: $4x = 60 \implies x = 15$.
- Step 3: Analyze the friend's claim: The friend claims parallelism depends on $x$ being even. Check whether $x=15$ makes the angles equal: $6(15)-24 = 90-24 = 66^\circ$; $2(15)+36 = 30+36 = 66^\circ$.
- Step 4: Conclude whether the friend is correct: Parallelism depends on the equality of the angles, not whether the value of $x$ is odd or even. Thus, the friend's reasoning is incorrect.
ANSWER: (a) $x = 15$. (b) The friend is incorrect because parallelism is determined by angle equality ($66^\circ = 66^\circ$), not by whether $x$ is odd or even.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two surveyor stakes are placed such that two parallel boundary lines are crossed by a measuring road. Two alternate exterior angles are given as $(8p - 10)^\circ$ and $(5p + 35)^\circ$. 
(a) Find the numerical value of $p$.
(b) If the road shifts and the angle expression $(8p - 10)^\circ$ increases by $15^\circ$ while the lines remain parallel, what must happen to the expression $(5p + 35)^\circ$ to maintain parallelism, and what is the new value of $p$?
COMPETENCY: Multi-step algebraic reasoning and parameter adjustment
STEPS:
- Step 1: Solve for initial $p$ by equating alternate exterior angles: $8p - 10 = 5p + 35 \implies 3p = 45 \implies p = 15$.
- Step 2: Apply the change: The first angle becomes $(8(15) - 10) + 15 = 110 + 15 = 125^\circ$.
- Step 3: Since the lines remain parallel, the alternate exterior angles must remain equal. Therefore, the second expression must also equal $125^\circ$: $5p + 35 = 125$.
- Step 4: Solve the new equation for $p$: $5p = 125 - 35 \implies 5p = 90 \implies p = 18$.
ANSWER: (a) $p = 15$. (b) The second expression must also increase to $125^\circ$ to maintain parallelism, resulting in a new value of $p = 18$.
DIFFICULTY: hard
