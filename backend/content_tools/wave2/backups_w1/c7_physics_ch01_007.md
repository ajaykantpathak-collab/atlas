## ELI5
Science is a relay race across generations: the torch in your hand runs on electric-cell ideas refined through decades - each inventor starting from the last runner's hand, not from zero. New observations and better tools modify, expand, occasionally revolutionise older models - but rarely erase them. The old idea was not wrong so much as unfinished. Newton's shoulders, Einstein's correction, your torchlight: one long collaborative build.

## DEFINITION
How scientific ideas build on each other is the cumulative process by which new observations, experiments, and technological tools refine, expand, or revolutionize existing scientific models without discarding valid foundational principles. There is no direct SI unit or physical measurement for this conceptual process, as it describes the historical and logical evolution of human knowledge across generations.

**Takeaway:** scientific ideas are cumulative: each generation starts from the last one's conclusions, refining rather than restarting. **In the exam:** in evolution-of-idea questions, trace the chain - original idea, new evidence, refined model - and credit both ends. **Check yourself:** open the walkthrough's torchlight and list what each earlier idea contributed to the cell-and-bulb in your hand. **Why students miss it:** students think revolutions erase predecessors - the flagged drama. ## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student measures the speed of a toy car rolling down an incline in the school laboratory. Trial 1 gives a speed of $v_1 = 2.0\text{ m/s}$ over $t = 1.0\text{ s}$. To build upon this, the student doubles the incline height, resulting in a new speed $v_2 = 4.0\text{ m/s}$ over the same time interval. Calculate the percentage increase in speed to show how changing experimental conditions builds new quantitative data upon old data.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify initial speed $v_1 = 2.0\text{ m/s}$ and final speed $v_2 = 4.0\text{ m/s}$ with units.
- Step 2: Identify the formula for percentage increase: $\text{Percentage Increase} = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{v_1} \times 100\%$.
- Step 3: Substitute the given values into the formula: $\frac{4.0\text{ m/s} - 2.0\text{ m/s}}{2.0\text{ m/s}} \times 100\%$.
- Step 4: Compute the numerical value and track unit cancellation: $\frac{2.0\text{ m/s}}{2.0\text{ m/s}} = 1$ (units cancel out completely), then $1 \times 100\% = 100\%$.
- Step 5: Verify the answer is physically reasonable: doubling the speed represents a 100% increase, which matches standard ratio calculations.
REASONING: By comparing experimental trials under different conditions, students see how scientific knowledge expands from a single baseline measurement into a relational understanding of cause and effect.
ANSWER: 100%
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In a study of heat transfer, a student learns that conduction moves heat through a solid iron rod of length $L = 0.5\text{ m}$ in $t = 50\text{ s}$. Building upon this thermal conduction principle, the student calculates the rate of heat travel (speed of thermal wave propagation) along the rod. Compute this rate in meters per second ($\text{m/s}$).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given distance $L = 0.5\text{ m}$ and time $t = 50\text{ s}$.
- Step 2: Recall the speed formula: $v = \frac{L}{t}$, ensuring SI units are used ($\text{m}$ and $\text{s}$).
- Step 3: Substitute values into the equation: $v = \frac{0.5\text{ m}}{50\text{ s}}$.
- Step 4: Compute the division: $0.5 \div 50 = 0.01\text{ m/s}$, tracking unit division ($\text{m} / \text{s} = \text{m/s}$).
- Step 5: Verify the answer is physically reasonable: thermal energy travels slowly through solid iron, so $0.01\text{ m/s}$ is a logical result.
REASONING: This example demonstrates how a qualitative concept (heat moving through solids) is built upon using mathematical tools (speed formulas) learned in motion chapters to create a cross-disciplinary scientific skill.
ANSWER: $0.01\text{ m/s}$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A solar cooking experiment in an Indian courtyard uses a flat mirror to reflect sunlight onto a vessel, raising the water temperature. The initial reflection angle measured from the normal is $\theta_1 = 30^\circ$. To improve the cooker's efficiency based on cumulative design ideas, the mirror is adjusted so the angle of incidence increases by $\Delta\theta = 15^\circ$. Calculate the new angle of reflection and determine the total deviation of the light ray from its original straight path.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify initial angle of reflection $\theta_1 = 30^\circ$ and the law of reflection (angle of incidence equals angle of reflection).
- Step 2: Calculate the new angle of incidence: $\theta_2 = 30^\circ + 15^\circ = 45^\circ$.
- Step 3: Apply the law of reflection to find the new angle of reflection: $\theta_{r2} = 45^\circ$.
- Step 4: Calculate the total deviation of the light ray, which is given by $D = 180^\circ - 2\theta$ for a single reflection, where $\theta = 45^\circ$.
- Step 5: Compute the deviation: $D = 180^\circ - 2(45^\circ) = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$.
- Step 6: Verify the answer with unit tracking: angles are in degrees ($^\circ$), and a $90^\circ$ deviation is physically reasonable for perpendicular redirection of light.
REASONING: This problem combines the Class 6 concept of reflection of light with cumulative design improvements to show how basic optical laws are applied to build advanced sustainable technologies like solar cookers.
ANSWER: $90^\circ$ deviation
