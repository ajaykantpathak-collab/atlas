## ELI5
Simple interest is rent on money: a fixed percentage of the original loan, paid for each year the money is borrowed, never compounding, never growing. The formula links four friends — principal, rate, time, interest — and if you know any three, the fourth follows. Two traps guard the door: time must speak in years, so months and days must convert first; and rearrange the formula honestly rather than guessing. Banks, loans, deposits — the same four-variable dance everywhere.

## DEFINITION
Simple interest applications involve using the formula \(SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}\) to solve real-world financial problems where interest is calculated only on the original principal amount for the entire duration. In Class 8, this extends to finding any missing variable (P, R, T, or SI) when the other three are known, handling time periods expressed in months or days, and comparing different investment or loan options.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers simple interest applications with annual rates, time periods in years/months/days, and problems requiring rearrangement of the formula to find principal, rate, or time. Does NOT cover compound interest, varying interest rates over time, or installment payments (EMIs).
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine your older sibling wants to buy a second-hand scooter for ₹45,000 but only has ₹30,000 saved. They plan to borrow the rest from a relative at 8% simple interest per year and repay in 18 months. How much extra will they pay? Start by identifying what is given: Principal = ₹15,000, Rate = 8%, Time = 18 months. Convert time to years (18/12 = 1.5 years) because the rate is per annum. Use the simple interest formula \(SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}\). Substitute values and compute step by step. The key insight is that time must always match the rate's time unit — if rate is per year, time must be in years.

Takeaway: SI = P × R × T / 100 with T in years — convert months and days first, then substitute; rearrange the same formula to find any missing one of the four. Exam format: expect word problems with mixed time units and 'find the rate' style rearrangements; the unit-conversion line is a marked step. Verification beat: check that time matches the rate's period — a per-annum rate needs years, not months. Why students miss it: they substitute months as years and inflate the interest twelvefold; writing T = months/12 before touching the formula immunises against it.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the formula for calculating simple interest, and what does each letter represent?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The formula is \(SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}\), where P = Principal (initial amount), R = Rate of interest per annum (in percent), T = Time period in years, and SI = Simple Interest. A wrong answer (e.g., writing \(SI = P \times R \times T\) without dividing by 100, or confusing R as a decimal) reveals the student has memorised the formula incorrectly or does not understand that R is a percentage.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why must time be converted to years when the interest rate is given as "per annum"? Explain with an example.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
"Per annum" means per year, so the rate applies to one full year. If time is given in months (e.g., 6 months) and used directly as 6 in the formula, the interest would be calculated for 6 years instead of half a year, giving an answer 12 times too large. For example, ₹10,000 at 10% for 6 months: correct SI = \(\frac{10000 \times 10 \times 0.5}{100} = ₹500\); incorrect SI (using T=6) = ₹6,000. A wrong explanation shows the student does not grasp the meaning of "per annum" or the proportional relationship between time and interest.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Ravi deposits ₹25,000 in a bank at 6% simple interest per annum. How much interest will he earn in 2 years and 6 months?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Convert time to years: 2 years 6 months = 2 + 6/12 = 2.5 years.
Step 2: Identify P = ₹25,000, R = 6%, T = 2.5 years.
Step 3: Apply formula: \(SI = \frac{25000 \times 6 \times 2.5}{100}\).
Step 4: Compute: \(25000 \times 6 = 150000\); \(150000 \times 2.5 = 375000\); \(375000 / 100 = 3750\).
Answer: ₹3,750.
A wrong answer (e.g., ₹15,000 using T=2, or ₹45,000 using T=6) reveals the student either forgot to convert months to years or made a multiplication error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the interest earned on ₹10,000 at 8% for 3 years versus ₹12,000 at 6% for 2 years. Which gives more interest, and by how much?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Option A: \(SI_A = \frac{10000 \times 8 \times 3}{100} = ₹2,400\).
Option B: \(SI_B = \frac{12000 \times 6 \times 2}{100} = ₹1,440\).
Option A gives more interest by ₹2,400 - ₹1,440 = ₹960.
A wrong answer (e.g., choosing Option B because 12,000 > 10,000, or miscalculating one SI) reveals the student cannot compare two scenarios analytically or ignores the combined effect of P, R, and T.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student solved this problem: "Find the principal that yields ₹1,800 interest at 9% per annum in 2 years." Their working: \(P = \frac{SI \times 100}{R \times T} = \frac{1800 \times 100}{9 \times 2} = \frac{180000}{18} = 10,000\). Is this correct? If not, find the error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The working is correct. The formula rearrangement \(P = \frac{SI \times 100}{R \times T}\) is right, substitution is correct, and arithmetic yields ₹10,000. A student who says it is incorrect might mistakenly think the formula is \(P = \frac{SI}{R \times T}\) (forgetting the 100) or miscompute 1800×100. If they identify a non-existent error, it shows they lack confidence in algebraic manipulation of the formula.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-life word problem involving simple interest where the time is given in days (not months or years), and solve it. Use an Indian context (rupees, bank loan, or savings).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example problem: "Priya borrows ₹50,000 from a friend at 12% simple interest per annum. She repays the loan after 90 days. How much interest does she pay?" Solution: Time in years = 90/365 (or 90/360 if using banker's year). Using 365: \(SI = \frac{50000 \times 12 \times 90}{100 \times 365} = \frac{54000000}{36500} ≈ ₹1,479.45\). A good answer includes correct conversion of days to years, proper formula use, and a realistic context. A poor answer uses T=90 directly, ignores the day-to-year conversion, or creates an unrealistic scenario (e.g., negative interest).
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often use the time value directly without converting months to years (e.g., using T=6 for 6 months instead of T=0.5) OR they forget to divide by 100 when the rate is a percentage (e.g., calculating \(25000 \times 6 \times 2.5 = 375,000\) and stating ₹375,000 as interest). Another specific error: when finding Principal, they write \(P = \frac{SI}{R \times T}\) omitting the 100, because they treat R as a decimal (0.06) but the formula expects percentage (6).
## ON_FAILURE
If a student uses months directly as years: Ask "What does 'per annum' mean? If you keep money for 6 months, is that a full year?" Guide them to write "6 months = 6/12 year = 0.5 year" before substituting. If they forget /100: Ask "Is 6% equal to 6 or 0.06?" Show that \(6\% = \frac{6}{100}\), so the formula has /100 built in. Have them redo the problem writing the rate as a fraction \(\frac{R}{100}\) to see the 100 in the denominator.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Arjun takes a loan of ₹80,000 from a bank at 7.5% simple interest per annum to start a small business. He agrees to repay the loan after 3 years and 4 months. Calculate the total amount he must repay at the end of the period.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given values: Principal P = ₹80,000, Rate R = 7.5% per annum, Time = 3 years 4 months.
- Step 2: Convert time to years: 4 months = 4/12 year = 1/3 year ≈ 0.3333 years. So T = 3 + 1/3 = 10/3 years (or 3.333... years).
- Step 3: Write the simple interest formula: SI = (P × R × T) / 100.
- Step 4: Substitute values: SI = (80000 × 7.5 × 10/3) / 100. Simplify stepwise: 80000 × 7.5 = 600,000; 600,000 × 10/3 = 2,000,000; 2,000,000 / 100 = 20,000. So SI = ₹20,000.
- Step 5: Find total Amount A = P + SI = 80,000 + 20,000 = ₹100,000. Verify: Interest per year = 80,000 × 7.5% = 6,000. For 3 years = 18,000. For 4 months (1/3 year) = 2,000. Total = 20,000. Correct.
REASONING: The key is converting mixed time (years and months) into a single unit (years) because the rate is annual. Using fractions (10/3) avoids decimal rounding errors. The amount is principal plus interest, which is what the borrower repays.
ANSWER: ₹1,00,000 (one lakh rupees)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Meena invested a certain sum in a fixed deposit at 8% simple interest per annum. After 2 years, she received ₹1,280 as interest. How much did she originally invest?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify what is asked: Find Principal P. Given: SI = ₹1,280, R = 8%, T = 2 years.
- Step 2: Choose the rearranged formula for P: P = (SI × 100) / (R × T).
- Step 3: Substitute known values: P = (1280 × 100) / (8 × 2).
- Step 4: Compute denominator: 8 × 2 = 16. Compute numerator: 1280 × 100 = 128,000. Divide: 128,000 / 16 = 8,000.
- Step 5: State answer with units: Principal = ₹8,000. Check: Interest on ₹8,000 at 8% for 2 years = (8000 × 8 × 2)/100 = 128,000/100 = 1,280. Matches.
REASONING: This is a reverse problem where interest is known and principal is unknown. Rearranging the formula before substituting avoids algebraic errors. The division by (R×T) scales the interest back to the principal.
ANSWER: ₹8,000
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A farmer borrows ₹1,20,000 at 9% simple interest per annum on 1st April 2023. He repays the loan on 31st December 2023. Calculate the interest he pays. (Assume 2023 is not a leap year.)
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify P = ₹1,20,000, R = 9% per annum. Time is not given directly; we must calculate the number of days between 1st April and 31st December 2023.
- Step 2: Count days: April (30), May (31), June (30), July (31), August (31), September (30), October (31), November (30), December (31). Total = 30+31+30+31+31+30+31+30+31 = 275 days.
- Step 3: Convert days to years: T = 275/365 years (since 2023 is not a leap year).
- Step 4: Apply formula: SI = (120000 × 9 × 275) / (100 × 365). Simplify: Cancel 100 with 120000 → 1200. SI = (1200 × 9 × 275) / 365. Compute numerator: 1200 × 9 = 10,800; 10,800 × 275 = 2,970,000. Divide: 2,970,000 / 365 ≈ 8136.986... ≈ ₹8,137 (rounded to nearest rupee).
- Step 5: Interpret: The farmer pays ₹8,137 as interest for 275 days. Verify reasonableness: Full year interest = 120000 × 9% = ₹10,800. 275/365 ≈ 0.753 of a year. 0.753 × 10,800 ≈ 8,132. Close.
REASONING: Real-life loans often start and end on specific dates, requiring exact day count. Using 365 days for a non-leap year is standard. The calculation combines date arithmetic with the interest formula, testing multiple skills.
ANSWER: ₹8,137 (approx)
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Rakesh has ₹50,000 to invest for 2 years. He has two options: Option A: Simple interest at 7% per annum. Option B: Simple interest at 6.5% per annum, but the bank offers a bonus of ₹500 at the end of 2 years. Which option gives more total money, and by how much? Explain your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying financial choices using quantitative analysis
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate total for Option A: SI_A = (50000 × 7 × 2)/100 = ₹7,000. Amount_A = 50,000 + 7,000 = ₹57,000.
- Step 2: Calculate total for Option B: SI_B = (50000 × 6.5 × 2)/100 = ₹6,500. Bonus = ₹500. Amount_B = 50,000 + 6,500 + 500 = ₹57,000.
- Step 3: Compare: Both give ₹57,000. They are equal.
- Step 4: Conclude: Neither is better; both yield the same total amount after 2 years.
ANSWER: Both options give the same total amount of ₹57,000. Option A gives ₹7,000 interest; Option B gives ₹6,500 interest + ₹500 bonus = ₹7,000 total extra.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A sum of money becomes ₹12,600 in 2 years and ₹13,500 in 3 years at simple interest. Find the principal and the rate of interest per annum.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and solving for multiple unknowns
STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand that in simple interest, the interest earned each year is constant. The difference between amounts in 3 years and 2 years is the interest for 1 year.
- Step 2: Interest for 1 year = ₹13,500 - ₹12,600 = ₹900.
- Step 3: Interest for 2 years = 2 × ₹900 = ₹1,800. Principal = Amount after 2 years - Interest for 2 years = ₹12,600 - ₹1,800 = ₹10,800.
- Step 4: Use SI formula for 1 year to find rate: 900 = (10800 × R × 1)/100 → R = (900 × 100)/10800 = 90000/10800 = 8.333...% = 8 1/3%.
- Step 5: Verify: In 3 years, SI = (10800 × 25/3 × 3)/100 = (10800 × 25)/100 = ₹2,700. Amount = 10800 + 2700 = ₹13,500. Correct.
ANSWER: Principal = ₹10,800; Rate = 8 1/3% per annum (or 8.33%).
DIFFICULTY: hard
