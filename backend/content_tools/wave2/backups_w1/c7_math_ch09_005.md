## ELI5
Two friends each build a roof truss from sticks of 5, 7 and 10 centimetres - and no matter how one is spun or flipped, the frames are twins. Three matching sides lock a triangle rigid; the corners cannot swivel, so all three angles match too. That is the SSS criterion: three pairs of equal corresponding sides certify congruence. Name the triangles with vertices in matching order, then borrow any part from one twin into the other - sides and angles travel together.

## DEFINITION
Two triangles are congruent by the Side-Side-Side (SSS) congruence criterion if all three sides of one triangle are respectively equal to the three corresponding sides of another triangle. When this condition is met, the two triangles have identical shapes and sizes, meaning their corresponding angles are also equal.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you and your friend are building wooden triangular roof trusses for a model house in your craft class. You both cut three wooden sticks of lengths 5 cm, 7 cm, and 10 cm, and nail them together at their endpoints to form a triangle. Even if your friend pushes or flips their triangle around on the desk, will it look identical to yours, or could they have built a completely different triangle using those exact same three side lengths? To answer this, we recall from Class 6 geometry that line segments of fixed lengths can only form one unique triangle shape because the sides lock the vertices rigidly in place. Today, we formalize this geometric rigidity into the SSS (Side-Side-Side) congruence criterion, which allows us to prove two triangles are identical twins simply by measuring their three pairs of matching sides.

**Takeaway:** SSS congruence: three pairs of equal corresponding sides lock both shape and size. **In the exam:** match the three side pairs, align the vertex order to the matches, then write the congruence statement. **Check yourself:** build both trusses from equal-length sticks and try to bend a corner - the rigidity is the proof. **Why students miss it:** they verify sides but then pair vertices carelessly, breaking the correspondence the statement needs.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often write congruence statements with mismatched vertices, such as writing $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle FED$ when side $AB$ actually corresponds to $FD$ instead of $FE$. For example, if $AB = 5$, $BC = 7$, $AC = 9$ in $\triangle ABC$ and $FE = 5$, $ED = 7$, $FD = 9$ in $\triangle FED$, a student might carelessly write $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$, completely ignoring that vertex $A$ must correspond to vertex $F$, vertex $B$ to vertex $E$, and vertex $C$ to vertex $D$.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student makes vertex-matching errors, tell them: "Pause and trace the side lengths with your fingers on both triangles. If side $AB$ is 5 cm and side $FE$ is 5 cm, then vertex $A$ and vertex $B$ must match up with vertex $F$ and vertex $E$. Always list your letters in the exact order of matching side lengths: write $AB$ first, so write $FE$ first in the second triangle's name!" Have them physically rotate the second triangle on scrap paper until it overlays the first triangle to see the correct vertex alignment.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: In a school craft project, Amit cuts two triangular cardboard pieces. For piece 1 ($\triangle PQR$), the side lengths are $PQ = 6\text{ cm}$, $QR = 8\text{ cm}$, and $PR = 10\text{ cm}$. For piece 2 ($\triangle XYZ$), the side lengths are $XY = 8\text{ cm}$, $YZ = 10\text{ cm}$, and $XZ = 6\text{ cm}$. Prove that the two triangles are congruent and write the correct congruence statement.
STEPS:
- Step 1: List the given side lengths for both triangles: For $\triangle PQR$: $PQ = 6\text{ cm}$, $QR = 8\text{ cm}$, $PR = 10\text{ cm}$. For $\triangle XYZ$: $XY = 8\text{ cm}$, $YZ = 10\text{ cm}$, $XZ = 6\text{ cm}$.
- Step 2: Match the corresponding sides by comparing lengths: $PQ = 6\text{ cm}$ matches $XZ = 6\text{ cm}$ ($PQ = XZ$). $QR = 8\text{ cm}$ matches $XY = 8\text{ cm}$ ($QR = XY$). $PR = 10\text{ cm}$ matches $YZ = 10\text{ cm}$ ($PR = YZ$).
- Step 3: Apply the SSS congruence criterion since all three pairs of corresponding sides are equal.
- Step 4: Write the correct vertex correspondence: Since $PQ = XZ$, $QR = XY$, and $PR = YZ$, vertex $P$ corresponds to $X$, $Q$ to $Z$, and $R$ to $Y$. Therefore, the congruence statement is $\triangle PQR \cong \triangle XZY$.
- Step 5: Verify the result by confirming that every side in $\triangle PQR$ has an equal counterpart in $\triangle XZY$ with correct vertex alignment.
REASONING: By matching the side lengths carefully ($6$ with $6$, $8$ with $8$, $10$ with $10$), we establish that the triangles have identical side dimensions. The SSS criterion guarantees congruence, but the vertex order must reflect which letters pair up based on the shared lengths.
ANSWER: $\triangle PQR \cong \triangle XZY$ by SSS congruence.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Two triangular park lawns in a city have fencing requirements. Lawn A ($\triangle ABC$) has $AB = 12\text{ m}$, $BC = 15\text{ m}$, and $AC = 9\text{ m}$. Lawn B ($\triangle DEF$) has $DE = 12\text{ m}$, $EF = 15\text{ m}$, and $DF = 9\text{ m}$. If it is known that $\angle A = 53^\circ$ and $\angle B = 37^\circ$, find the measure of $\angle D$ and $\angle E$ without measuring Lawn B directly.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given side lengths: $AB = 12\text{ m} = DE$, $BC = 15\text{ m} = EF$, and $AC = 9\text{ m} = DF$.
- Step 2: Establish congruence using SSS criterion: Since all three pairs of sides are equal ($AB=DE$, $BC=EF$, $AC=DF$), $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$ by SSS.
- Step 3: Use the property of congruent triangles (CPCTC - Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent) to match angles: Vertex $A$ corresponds to vertex $D$, so $\angle A = \angle D$. Vertex $B$ corresponds to vertex $E$, so $\angle B = \angle E$.
- Step 4: Substitute the known angle values: Since $\angle A = 53^\circ$, $\angle D = 53^\circ$. Since $\angle B = 37^\circ$, $\angle E = 37^\circ$.
- Step 5: State the final angle measures with proper units.
REASONING: Once SSS congruence is proved, all corresponding interior angles must also be equal. By identifying the correct vertex correspondence ($A \leftrightarrow D$ and $B \leftrightarrow E$), we directly transfer the angle measures from the first triangle to the second without physical measurement.
ANSWER: $\angle D = 53^\circ$ and $\angle E = 37^\circ$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: In a quadrilateral $ABCD$, $AB = CD$ and $AD = BC$. Diagonal $BD$ is drawn, dividing the quadrilateral into two triangles: $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle CDB$. Prove that $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle CDB$ and show that $\angle A = \angle C$.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given information in the two triangles $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle CDB$: Side $AB = CD$ (given) and side $AD = BC$ (given).
- Step 2: Identify the shared side between both triangles: The diagonal $BD$ is common to both $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle CDB$, so $BD = DB$ (reflexive property).
- Step 3: Check all three side equalities for the two triangles: $AB = CD$, $AD = BC$, and $BD = DB$.
- Step 4: Apply the SSS congruence criterion: Since all three pairs of corresponding sides are equal, $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle CDB$.
- Step 5: Use CPCTC to relate corresponding angles: Vertex $A$ corresponds to vertex $C$ in this triangle pairing, therefore $\angle A = \angle C$.
- Step 6: Verify the proof logic: Every step relies on given properties and shared geometric segments, leading logically to the desired angle equality.
REASONING: Proving composite geometric figures congruent often requires recognizing hidden or shared sides (like diagonal $BD$). Once the three side pairs are matched ($AB=CD$, $AD=BC$, $BD=BD$), SSS proves the triangles are identical twins, allowing us to state that their corresponding angles are equal.
ANSWER: $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle CDB$ by SSS congruence, which implies $\angle A = \angle C$ by CPCTC.
