## ELI5
Put a piece of camphor in the winter wardrobe and weeks later it has vanished — no puddle, no wet patch, nothing. Some solids skip melting altogether: their particles pick up a little heat energy and escape straight into the air as gas. Solid to gas directly, no liquid stage in between — that is sublimation. Cool the vapour and it settles back as solid, which is deposition. The disappearing act is really an escape: heat disrupts the crystal lattice so completely that the liquid stage is never seen.

## DEFINITION
Sublimation is the direct phase transition of a substance from its solid state to its gaseous state without passing through the intermediate liquid state. Conversely, the direct deposition of gas back into solid is sometimes termed reverse sublimation or deposition.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine placing a piece of camphor in your winter wardrobe to keep insects away, and noticing weeks later that it has completely vanished without leaving any liquid behind. To understand this, observe how some solids possess weak intermolecular forces that allow their particles to gain enough thermal energy to escape directly into the gas phase when heated. Connect this to your prior understanding of particle movement and inter-particle space in solids and gases. Unlike melting, where particles gain kinetic energy to become a mobile liquid, subliming particles break free of the solid lattice entirely to form a vapor. The key concept is that heat energy disrupts the crystal lattice directly, turning a solid into gas without any liquid phase.

Takeaway: sublimation skips the liquid — camphor, naphthalene, iodine, ammonium chloride and dry ice leap from solid to vapour — and that leap becomes a separation tool when mixed with non-subliming impurities. Exam format: expect the substance list, the particle explanation, and a separation design. Verification beat: name at least three subliming substances from the standard list. Why students miss it: they treat solid-to-gas as two steps — the definition's whole point is the missing liquid, and the separation trick exploits exactly that skip. The classroom demo seals it: cover a warming dish of camphor with a funnel and the vapour re-solidifies on the cool walls — sublimation and its reverse, witnessed in one apparatus.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why does solid dry ice (solid carbon dioxide, $CO_2$) disappear at room temperature without leaving a puddle of liquid on the laboratory table?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Dry ice undergoes sublimation because at normal atmospheric pressure, solid $CO_2$ transitions directly into carbon dioxide gas as it absorbs heat from the surrounding air. A student who thinks it melts first misunderstands the phase behavior of carbon dioxide under standard atmospheric conditions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A mixture contains common salt ($NaCl$) and ammonium chloride ($NH_4Cl$). If you gently heat this mixture in an evaporating dish covered with an inverted funnel, what will happen to the ammonium chloride?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is that ammonium chloride will sublime, turning directly into vapor and depositing on the cooler upper stem of the inverted funnel, leaving the salt behind. Working steps: 1) Identify ammonium chloride as a sublimable solid and salt as a non-sublimable solid. 2) Apply heat to the mixture. 3) Ammonium chloride absorbs thermal energy, breaking its weak intermolecular bonds to form a gas. 4) The gas hits the cool funnel walls and deposits back into solid crystals. A student who says it melts thinks ammonium chloride behaves like ice or wax.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the particle arrangement and inter-particle forces in iodine crystals ($I_2$) to explain why iodine undergoes sublimation upon gentle heating rather than melting into a liquid at standard pressure.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Iodine molecules are held together in the solid crystal by weak London dispersion forces rather than strong ionic or covalent bonds throughout the lattice. When heated, a modest amount of thermal energy is sufficient to completely overcome these weak attractive forces, allowing molecules to escape directly into the gas phase. A student who believes strong chemical bonds are broken is confusing intermolecular forces with intramolecular covalent bonds.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the following statement: "All substances that are solid at room temperature can undergo sublimation if heated strongly enough in an open container." Is this statement scientifically accurate?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The statement is false because most substances (like ice or iron) melt into a liquid phase before vaporizing into a gas at standard atmospheric pressure. Sublimation only occurs readily when the vapor pressure of the solid equals atmospheric pressure below its melting point, which is unique to specific substances like iodine, camphor, and ammonium chloride. A student who agrees fails to realize that melting point and triple point pressures dictate whether a liquid phase must form.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple laboratory procedure using everyday school lab equipment to separate a solid mixture of sand and camphor. Explain how each component is recovered.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
To separate sand and camphor: 1) Place the mixture in an evaporating dish. 2) Cover the dish with an inverted glass funnel and plug the funnel's narrow top with a small piece of cotton. 3) Heat the bottom of the dish gently with a Bunsen burner. 4) Camphor will sublime into a gas, rise, and deposit as pure solid crystals on the cool inner walls of the funnel, while non-volatile sand remains at the bottom of the dish. 5) Allow the apparatus to cool, remove the funnel, and scrape off the pure camphor crystals. A poor answer would suggest dissolving in water without utilizing camphor's unique sublimation property.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse sublimation with evaporation, assuming that because a solid disappears into the air (like camphor or naphthalene balls), it must first melt into an invisible liquid layer on the surface. They do not realize that the solid-to-gas phase transition happens directly without any liquid ever forming.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this misconception, guide the student to observe a piece of iodine or camphor crystals in a sealed glass jar placed on a warm surface. Ask them to look closely at the surface of the solid: point out the complete absence of any wetness or liquid puddle while purple vapors or gas fill the jar. Emphasize that the particles leap straight from a tightly packed solid array into freely moving gas particles.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student takes 15 grams of impure ammonium chloride mixed with sand. After heating the mixture in a China dish for 5 minutes, all the ammonium chloride has turned into vapor and condensed on the funnel, leaving behind 4 grams of pure sand in the dish. Calculate the mass of ammonium chloride present in the original mixture and explain the physical process.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the total mass of the mixture (15 g) and the mass of the residue left behind (sand = 4 g).
- step 2: Recognize that the volatile component is ammonium chloride which undergoes sublimation, while sand is non-volatile.
- step 3: Apply conservation of mass to find the mass of ammonium chloride: Mass of mixture = Mass of sand + Mass of ammonium chloride.
- step 4: Calculate: Mass of ammonium chloride = Total mass - Mass of sand = 15 g - 4 g = 11 g.
- step 5: Interpret the physical process: Heat supplied broke the weak intermolecular bonds of solid ammonium chloride, turning it directly to gas, which then deposited back into solid on the cool funnel walls.
REASONING: Sublimation allows the separation of a volatile solid from a non-volatile solid without forming a liquid phase. Subtracting the insoluble, non-sublimable residue mass from the total mixture mass gives the exact mass of the sublimable component.
ANSWER: 11 grams of ammonium chloride
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In a school chemistry demonstration, 10 grams of iodine crystals are heated in a sealed round-bottom flask. After heating, the flask is filled with purple iodine vapor. When the heat is removed and the upper part of the flask is cooled with ice, shiny dark crystals reappear on the glass surface. What mass of iodine crystals is recovered on the glass, assuming no vapor escaped the sealed flask?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the initial mass of iodine crystals placed in the flask (10 g).
- step 2: Note that the system is completely sealed (no mass enters or leaves).
- step 3: Identify the physical processes occurring: sublimation upon heating (solid to gas) followed by deposition upon cooling (gas back to solid).
- step 4: Apply the Law of Conservation of Mass to phase changes: the total mass of matter remains constant during a physical change.
- step 5: Conclude that the mass of recovered solid iodine crystals equals the initial mass added to the flask, which is 10 grams.
REASONING: Phase transitions like sublimation and deposition are physical changes where intermolecular forces are temporarily overcome, but no atoms or molecules are destroyed or created, preserving total mass in a closed system.
ANSWER: 10 grams of iodine crystals
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A sample containing a mixture of dry ice (solid $CO_2$) and sodium chloride ($NaCl$) weighing 25 grams is left exposed in an open beaker at room temperature for 2 hours. After 2 hours, solid $CO_2$ has completely turned into gas and escaped into the atmosphere. The remaining solid in the beaker weighs 15 grams. What percentage by mass of the original mixture was sodium chloride?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the initial total mass of the mixture (25 g) and the final mass of the remaining solid in the beaker (15 g).
- step 2: Recognize that solid carbon dioxide (dry ice) undergoes sublimation at room temperature and escapes as a gas into the air.
- step 3: Identify that sodium chloride ($NaCl$) is stable, non-volatile, and does not sublime at room temperature, meaning the final solid remaining is pure sodium chloride.
- step 4: Calculate the percentage by mass of sodium chloride using the formula: $(\text{Mass of } NaCl / \text{Initial Total Mass}) \times 100$.
- step 5: Substitute the values: $(15 \text{ g} / 25 \text{ g}) \times 100 = 0.6 \times 100 = 60\%$.
REASONING: Combining sublimation with mass percentage calculations shows how volatile and non-volatile components behave in open systems. Since dry ice sublimes completely into the atmosphere, only the non-sublimable sodium chloride remains behind to be measured.
ANSWER: 60%

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A laboratory technician accidentally mixes naphthalene balls (which sublime easily) with powdered sugar. Design a step-by-step separation strategy using a Bunsen burner, tripod stand, wire gauze, evaporating dish, glass funnel, and cotton plug. Predict what happens to each component during the process.
COMPETENCY: Designing experimental separation techniques based on specific physical properties
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the distinct physical properties: naphthalene undergoes sublimation upon heating, whereas sugar melts and eventually chars or caramelizes at higher temperatures without subliming directly.
- step 2: Set up the apparatus: place the mixture in an evaporating dish on a wire gauze over a tripod stand, cover the dish with an inverted glass funnel, and plug the funnel hole with cotton.
- step 3: Apply gentle heat using the Bunsen burner. Naphthalene particles absorb heat and transition directly from solid to gas, rising up into the funnel.
- step 4: Cool the upper region of the funnel so that naphthalene vapor deposits back into solid crystals, while pure sugar remains behind in the evaporating dish.
- step 5: Turn off the burner, let the setup cool, detach the funnel to collect pure naphthalene crystals, and recover sugar from the dish.
ANSWER: By heating the mixture under an inverted funnel, naphthalene sublimes directly into gas and recrystallizes on the cool walls of the funnel, separating from the sugar which remains in the evaporating dish because it does not sublime.
DIFFICULTY: hard
