## ELI5
A railway announcement saying 4,52,389 passengers travelled today is the place value chart grown one column taller. Every step left multiplies a digit's value by ten: the same digit means five lakhs in one seat and fifty in another. Six digits take you into lakhs, a seventh opens ten lakhs - the way ten ten-rupee notes bundle into one hundred, ten lakh-bundles stack into ten lakhs. Read any big number by naming each digit's column first, and the crowd of digits turns into a story you can say aloud.

## DEFINITION
The place value system extends to 6-digit (lakhs) and 7-digit (ten lakhs) numbers in the Indian numbering system, where each digit's position to the left represents a value ten times greater than the position immediately to its right. This allows us to represent, read, and write very large quantities, such as country populations or national budgets, using the powers of ten: Ones, Tens, Hundreds, Thousands, Ten Thousands, Lakhs, and Ten Lakhs.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine the ticket collector at a busy railway station in Mumbai announces that 4,52,389 passengers traveled across suburban lines today! To understand how massive that number is, we cannot just rely on hundreds or thousands anymore; we need to step into the world of lakhs. Let us begin by recalling how 5-digit numbers use Ten Thousands, and observe how adding one more column to the left opens up the Lakhs and Ten Lakhs places. When writing down a large number, we start from the rightmost digit (Ones) and move left, separating periods with commas according to the Indian system (3 digits, then groups of 2). The key insight is that moving one place to the left multiplies the local value by 10, transforming thousands into ten-thousands, and ten-thousands into lakhs.

**Takeaway:** each step left on the chart is ten times the step before; six digits reach lakhs, seven reach ten lakhs. **In the exam:** for place-value questions, count columns from the right, name the period, then multiply the digit - never eyeball. **Check yourself:** take 47,25,910 and say the place value of each digit aloud in one pass. **Why students miss it:** they count commas instead of columns and land one period short, turning lakhs into ten-thousands.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently misplace commas or misread 6 and 7-digit numbers by treating them like the International system (grouping by threes from the right: millions/billions) instead of the Indian system (grouping by threes first, then groups of two: Thousands, Lakhs, Crores). For example, when reading 4252389, they might write or read it as 4,252,389 instead of the correct Indian grouping 42,52,389. Another common error is omitting zero placeholders when converting expanded numbers like $7,00,000 + 50$ directly into standard form, writing 7,50 instead of 7,00,050.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student struggles with place values or misplaces commas in 6- and 7-digit numbers, guide them back to the basic period chart: draw columns for Ones, Tens, Hundreds | Thousands, Ten Thousands | Lakhs, Ten Lakhs. Have them physically place digits from right to left, noting that the comma separates the periods (the first comma after 3 digits from the right, and subsequent commas after every 2 digits). If they drop zeros in expanded form, have them write out all place values vertically, including those multiplied by zero, to visualize every single column before adding.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A textile mill in Gujarat produced 8,45,612 meters of cloth in a year. Write this number in expanded form and find the difference between the place values of the two digits '5' if another '5' is added in the Ten Lakhs place to make it 58,45,612 meters.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given number and the new 7-digit number: Original is 8,45,612; New number with Ten Lakhs digit is 58,45,612.
- Step 2: Write out the place value expansion for 58,45,612: $5 \times 10,00,000 + 8 \times 1,00,000 + 4 \times 10,000 + 5 \times 1,000 + 6 \times 100 + 1 \times 10 + 2 \times 1$.
- Step 3: Identify the two place values where the digit '5' appears: one is in the Lakhs place ($5 \times 1,00,000 = 5,00,000$) and the other is in the Ten Lakhs place ($5 \times 10,00,000 = 50,00,000$).
- Step 4: Calculate the difference between these two place values: $50,00,000 - 5,00,000 = 45,00,000$.
- Step 5: Verify the result by checking column positions; the Ten Lakhs place is ten times the Lakhs place, so $50,00,000 - 5,00,000$ correctly equals $45,00,000$ meters.
REASONING: Expanding a number reveals the precise value contributed by each digit based on its column position. Subtracting two place values containing the same digit highlights how shifting left multiplies the value by 10.
ANSWER: Expanded form: $5 \times 10,00,000 + 8 \times 1,00,000 + 4 \times 10,000 + 5 \times 1,000 + 6 \text{...}$; Difference: 45,00,000 meters.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Write down both numbers clearly with Indian commas: Number A = 7,42,105 and Number B = 7,24,501.
- Step 2: Compare digits from left to right (highest place value to lowest). Both start with 7 in the Lakhs place ($7,00,000 = 7,00,000$).
- Step 3: Move to the next highest place to the right: Ten Thousands place. Number A has 4 ($40,000$) and Number B has 2 ($20,000$).
- Step 4: Conclude whether Student A's reasoning is correct by evaluating $40,000 > 20,000$.
ANSWER: Student A's reasoning is completely correct. When comparing numbers, we inspect digits from left to right. Since both numbers have the same digit (7) in the Lakhs place, we look at the Ten Thousands place. Number A has 4 ($40,000$) while Number B has 2 ($20,000$). Since 40,000 is greater than 20,000, 7,42,105 is greater than 7,24,501 regardless of the digits that follow.
