## ELI5
Under a leafy peepal tree on a sunny afternoon, the ground wears small circular patches of light - and they are not gaps in the leaves. Each bright circle is the sun's image, cast by every tiny gap between leaves acting as a pinhole camera. Straight-line light from the sun's round disc crosses at each gap and lands inverted - round because the sun is round. The tree is a gallery of pinhole photographs.

## DEFINITION
A pinhole camera is a simple optical device without a lens that forms an inverted (upside-down) real image of a bright object on a screen based on the rectilinear propagation of light. It has no SI unit as it is an instrument, but its dimensions and image distances are measured in meters (m) or centimeters (cm).

## WALKTHROUGH
When you stand outside on a sunny afternoon under a leafy peepal tree, you might notice small circular patches of light on the ground, which are actually pinhole images of the sun formed by spaces between leaves. To understand how this happens, we build a pinhole camera using two concentric cardboard boxes or tubes where light enters only through a tiny pinhole at one end and falls on a translucent tracing paper screen at the other. This demonstrates that light travels in straight lines because rays from the top of an object pass through the straight pinhole to the bottom of the screen, and rays from the bottom pass to the top. By measuring the object distance ($u$), image distance ($v$), object height ($h_o$), and image height ($h_i$), we discover the geometric relationship of similar triangles: $\frac{h_i}{h_o} = \frac{v}{u}$.

**Takeaway:** a pinhole camera proves light's straightness: each small gap projects an inverted real image - the peepal's ground shows the sun's own portrait. **In the exam:** answer why-inverted with the crossing rays, and why-sharp with the small hole - both are the same straight-line fact. **Check yourself:** look down at the walkthrough's light patches and explain their shape before your next step explains their inversion. **Why students miss it:** students call the patches leaf-shaped - the flagged expectation. The screen shows the source, not the hole. Once the pinhole's logic clicks, the camera, the eclipse projection and the tree canopy become one idea.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A candle of height 10 cm is placed 40 cm away from the pinhole of a pinhole camera. If the screen is placed at a distance of 20 cm from the pinhole, what is the height of the image formed on the screen?
A) 20 cm
B) 5 cm
C) 10 cm
D) 2 cm
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is B) 5 cm.
Steps:
1. Identify given values with units: Object height $h_o = 10\text{ cm}$, object distance $u = 40\text{ cm}$, image distance $v = 20\text{ cm}$.
2. Identify the formula for pinhole camera magnification and height relationship: $\frac{h_i}{h_o} = \frac{v}{u}$, where $h_i$ is image height.
3. Substitute values with units: $\frac{h_i}{10\text{ cm}} = \frac{20\text{ cm}}{40\text{ cm}}$.
4. Compute and track unit cancellation: $h_i = 10\text{ cm} \times \frac{20\text{ cm}}{40\text{ cm}} = 10\text{ cm} \times 0.5 = 5\text{ cm}$.
5. Verify the answer is physically reasonable: Since the screen distance (20 cm) is half of the object distance (40 cm), the image height must be half of the object height (10 cm / 2 = 5 cm).
A student who chooses A likely inverted the ratio ($40/20$ instead of $20/40$), while a student who chooses C forgot to apply distance scaling altogether.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that the inverted image inside a pinhole camera is caused by mirrors or lenses hidden inside the box, or they think the image is right-side up because they read text upside-down with difficulty. They fail to trace individual straight light rays from the top and bottom of the object crossing at the pinhole. For example, when observing a tree, they think the top of the tree somehow "bounces" upward onto the screen instead of traveling straight through the aperture to the bottom of the screen.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this misconception, take a cardboard shoebox with a pinhole on one end and a tracing paper screen on the other. Hold a glowing torch in front of it with the bulb pointing up. Have the student use a piece of colored thread or a laser pointer to trace a straight line from the top of the torch bulb through the pinhole to the bottom of the screen, and another line from the bottom of the bulb to the top of the screen. Seeing the physical crossing of straight light paths makes the inversion intuitive and clear.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A school flag pole of height 12 m stands at a distance of 60 m from a large pinhole camera box. If the camera screen is located 0.5 m behind the pinhole, calculate the height of the flagpole's image formed on the screen in centimeters.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: Object height $h_o = 12\text{ m}$, object distance $u = 60\text{ m}$, image distance $v = 0.5\text{ m}$. Convert $h_o$ to cm: $12\text{ m} = 1200\text{ cm}$.
- step 2: Identify the pinhole camera similarity formula: $\frac{h_i}{h_o} = \frac{v}{u}$.
- step 3: Substitute known values with units into the formula: $\frac{h_i}{1200\text{ cm}} = \frac{0.5\text{ m}}{60\text{ m}}$.
- step 4: Compute and track unit cancellation: Note that meters cancel out in $\frac{0.5\text{ m}}{60\text{ m}} = \frac{1}{120}$. Then $h_i = 1200\text{ cm} \times \frac{1}{120} = 10\text{ cm}$.
- step 5: Verify the answer is physically reasonable: An image distance of 0.5 m is extremely small compared to a 60 m object distance, so the image should be a miniature version ($10\text{ cm}$), which makes complete physical sense.
REASONING: Similar triangles formed by intersecting light rays at the pinhole allow us to set up a direct ratio between object and image dimensions. Keeping units consistent ensures correct proportions.
ANSWER: 10 cm (inverted)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An LED light bulb of height 6 cm is placed in front of a pinhole camera. The image formed on the screen has a height of 2 cm. If the screen is positioned at a distance of 10 cm from the pinhole, what is the distance ($u$) of the LED bulb from the pinhole?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values with units: $h_o = 6\text{ cm}$, $h_i = 2\text{ cm}$, $v = 10\text{ cm}$. Find $u$.
- step 2: Write the pinhole camera ratio formula: $\frac{h_i}{h_o} = \frac{v}{u}$.
- step 3: Rearrange the formula to solve for $u$: $u = v \times \left(\frac{h_o}{h_i}\right)$.
- step 4: Substitute values with units: $u = 10\text{ cm} \times \left(\frac{6\text{ cm}}{2\text{ cm}}\right)$.
- step 5: Compute and track unit cancellation: $u = 10\text{ cm} \times 3 = 30\text{ cm}$. Verify that since the object is 3 times taller than the image ($6/2 = 3$), the object distance must be 3 times greater than the image distance ($10\text{ cm} \times 3 = 30\text{ cm}$).
REASONING: Rearranging the similarity formula lets us find unknown distances when object and image heights are known.
ANSWER: 30 cm
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A tall coconut tree is viewed through a pinhole camera. The tree forms an image 15 cm high on a screen placed 20 cm behind the pinhole. If the distance from the pinhole to the tree is increased from 40 m to 80 m, what will be the new height of the image on the screen? (Assume camera dimensions remain unchanged).
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze initial conditions to find the tree's actual height ($h_o$): $h_i = 15\text{ cm}$, $v = 20\text{ cm} = 0.2\text{ m}$, initial $u_1 = 40\text{ m}$.
- step 2: Set up formula for initial state: $\frac{15\text{ cm}}{h_o} = \frac{0.2\text{ m}}{40\text{ m}}$.
- step 3: Solve for $h_o$: $h_o = 15\text{ cm} \times \frac{40\text{ m}}{0.2\text{ m}} = 15\text{ cm} \times 200 = 3000\text{ cm} = 30\text{ m}$.
- step 4: Set up formula for the new condition with new object distance $u_2 = 80\text{ m}$ and constant image distance $v = 0.2\text{ m}$: $\frac{h_{i2}}{3000\text{ cm}} = \frac{0.2\text{ m}}{80\text{ m}}$.
- step 5: Compute new image height $h_{i2}$: $\frac{0.2}{80} = \frac{1}{400}$. Therefore, $h_{i2} = 3000\text{ cm} \times \frac{1}{400} = \frac{30}{4} = 7.5\text{ cm}$.
- step 6: Verify physical reasonableness: Doubling the object distance ($40\text{ m}$ to $80\text{ m}$) halves the image size ($15\text{ cm}$ to $7.5\text{ cm}$), which aligns perfectly with inverse proportionality between object distance and image size.
REASONING: Real objects maintain a constant height while changing position. By calculating the constant object height first, we can predict the new image dimensions under altered distances.
ANSWER: 7.5 cm
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two students build pinhole cameras of different lengths. Student A's camera box is 15 cm long, and Student B's camera box is 30 cm long. Both students point their cameras at the same distant school flagpole from the exact same spot. Compare the sizes and brightness of the images formed in both cameras, and explain the physical reason for any differences.
COMPETENCY: Predicting outcomes from changing variables and geometric reasoning
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand the physical setup: Both cameras have the same object distance ($u$) and object height ($h_o$), but different image distances ($v_A = 15\text{ cm}$, $v_B = 30\text{ cm}$).
- step 2: Apply the pinhole height formula: $h_i = h_o \times \frac{v}{u}$. Since $v_B$ is twice as large as $v_A$, Student B's image height will be twice as large as Student A's.
- step 3: Analyze brightness: Student B spreads the incoming light over a larger area ($4\times$ area or $2\times$ linear dimensions), making Student B's image dimmer (fainter) than Student A's brighter, smaller image.
- step 4: Synthesize conclusion: Longer image distance yields a larger but fainter image.
ANSWER: Student B's image will be twice as tall as Student A's image, but Student A's image will be brighter because the light is concentrated over a smaller area.
DIFFICULTY: medium
