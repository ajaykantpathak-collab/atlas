## ELI5
The mode is the crowd's favourite: whichever value shows up most often in the data wins the title. No sorting, no averaging - just count each value's appearances and crown the busiest. Size is irrelevant; a small score can out-vote a big one simply by turning up more times.

## DEFINITION
The mode is the value in a data set that appears the most often. It represents the most frequent observation or "popular" choice in a collection of data.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify the mode from an unorganized list of data by counting frequencies and confirm the result by showing the count for each value.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers identifying the mode in simple data sets with a single most frequent value. It does not cover multimodal data, grouped data, or finding the mode from a histogram.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If a list of shoe sizes is 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 6, 7, why is 6 the mode?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
6 is the mode because it appears 3 times, which is more frequent than any other size. If a student says "because it is in the middle," they are confusing mode with median.

**A wrong answer (guessing the busiest) reveals the student missed the counting - the winner is whichever size's tally is tallest.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Find the mode of these marks in a test: 10, 15, 10, 20, 10, 15.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
1. List the unique values: 10, 15, 20. 2. Count frequencies: 10 appears 3 times, 15 appears 2 times, 20 appears 1 time. 3. Compare counts: 3 is the largest. 4. Identify mode: 10. A student who says 15 likely miscounted or skipped the last 10.

**A wrong answer (skipping the tally) reveals the student crowned without counting - list the unique values, tally each, and let the tallest stack win.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If a cricket player scores 5, 10, 5, 20, 5, 10 runs in six matches, which score is the mode?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The mode is 5. It appears 3 times, while 10 appears twice and 20 appears once. A student answering 5 and 10 together is confusing the concept with multimodal data, which is outside our current scope.

**A wrong answer (letting a bigger score win) reveals the student confused fame with size - the smaller score's extra appearances out-vote it.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Is the mode always the largest number in a data set? Explain.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
No, the mode is the most frequent number, not the biggest one. For example, in 2, 2, 2, 9, 9, the mode is 2, even though 9 is the largest number. A "Yes" answer reveals a misunderstanding of "frequency" versus "value magnitude."

**A wrong answer (backing the largest) reveals the student mixed the contests - the mode is a popularity prize, never a size prize.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a small data set of 5 numbers where the mode is 4.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A correct set would be 4, 4, 4, 1, 2. A poor answer like 4, 1, 2, 3, 5 shows a failure to ensure 4 appears more frequently than other numbers.

**A wrong answer (building a set with no repeat winner) reveals the student missed the recipe - the demanded value must out-tally every other value in the created set.**

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often choose the highest *numerical value* in the list as the mode instead of the *most frequent value*. For example, in the set 2, 2, 8, 8, 8, 9, they might pick 9 because it is the biggest number, ignoring that 8 appears three times while 9 appears only once.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student picks the largest number, ask them to count how many times each number appears in the list. Have them write a frequency table with two columns: "Number" and "How many times it appears." Once the table is complete, ask them to circle the highest number in the "How many times" column; the corresponding value in the first column is the mode.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper sold different kg of rice over 7 days: 5kg, 8kg, 5kg, 3kg, 5kg, 8kg, 2kg. What is the mode of the rice sales?
STEPS:
- Step 1: List all unique values (2, 3, 5, 8).
- Step 2: Count the frequency of each (2: once, 3: once, 5: three times, 8: twice).
- Step 3: Compare frequencies (3 is the highest frequency).
- Step 4: Identify the value corresponding to the highest frequency (5).
- Step 5: Verify that no other number appears 3 or more times.
REASONING: The mode is defined as the value that appears most often. By listing the counts, we mathematically prove that 5 is the most frequent value.
ANSWER: 5 kg
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: The number of students absent in a class over 6 days were: 1, 0, 1, 2, 1, 0. What is the mode?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify unique values (0, 1, 2).
- Step 2: Tally counts (0 appears 2 times, 1 appears 3 times, 2 appears 1 time).
- Step 3: Determine the highest count (3).
- Step 4: State the mode associated with that count (1).
- Step 5: Check if 1 is indeed the most frequent.
REASONING: Since 1 appears more times than any other number in the set, it qualifies as the most frequent value, which is the mode.
ANSWER: 1 student
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A vegetable seller recorded the number of potatoes sold in 8 different bundles: 12, 15, 12, 18, 15, 12, 20, 12. What is the mode?
STEPS:
- Step 1: List unique values: 12, 15, 18, 20.
- Step 2: Count 12s: it appears 4 times.
- Step 3: Count 15s: it appears 2 times.
- Step 4: Count 18s and 20s: each appears 1 time.
- Step 5: Compare 4, 2, 1, 1. The highest is 4, which belongs to 12.
REASONING: Comparing the total counts shows that 12 is the "most frequent" value, identifying it as the mode.
ANSWER: 12 potatoes
