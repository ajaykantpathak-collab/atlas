## ELI5
A plane mirror plays a polite trick: it posts your image as far behind the glass as you stand before it, same size, standing upright - but printed back-to-front, so your right hand waves as a left. The image cannot be caught on a screen; it lives only where your eyes insist it does. Same distance, same size, reversed sides, uncatchable - the four-line contract.

## DEFINITION
A plane mirror is a flat, smooth reflecting surface that produces an image which appears to be located behind the mirror at the same distance as the object in front. The image is virtual, upright, the same size as the object, and laterally inverted.

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever noticed that when you comb your hair in front of a mirror, your right hand appears to be your left hand? To understand this, stand in front of a mirror and touch your right ear; observe how the mirror image touches its left ear. This happens because the mirror flips the image sideways, a property called lateral inversion. You can also test the distance rule by placing a toy 10 cm from the mirror and observing that the image appears exactly 10 cm behind the glass. The key insight is that even though the image looks real, it is "virtual"—it cannot be caught on a screen like a shadow.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that the mirror image is "inverted" (upside down) like a reflection in a lake. This happens because they confuse the horizontal flipping (lateral inversion) with the vertical flipping seen in water reflections or pinhole camera images.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to stand in front of a mirror and raise their right hand. Ask: "Does the mirror image raise its head or its hand?" Then point to the horizontal side and ask: "Is the hand on the left or right side of the image?" This helps them isolate the movement to a horizontal swap rather than a total inversion.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A boy stands 1.5 metres away from a large wall mirror. He walks 0.5 metres closer to the mirror. How far is he from his own image now?
STEPS:
- step 1: Initial distance is 1.5 m. He moves 0.5 m closer, so the new distance from the mirror is 1.5 - 0.5 = 1.0 m.
- step 2: The image is always the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front.
- step 3: The image is 1.0 m behind the mirror.
- step 4: Total distance between the boy and his image = distance in front + distance behind = 1.0 m + 1.0 m = 2.0 m.
REASONING: Since the image distance equals the object distance, the total separation is always double the distance to the mirror.
ANSWER: 2.0 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An arrow 8 cm long is placed vertically 20 cm in front of a plane mirror. Describe the image's height, orientation, and distance from the mirror.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify size property: Image size = Object size = 8 cm.
- step 2: Identify orientation property: Plane mirror images are upright (not upside down).
- step 3: Identify distance property: Image distance = Object distance = 20 cm.
- step 4: Check for lateral inversion: The left/right sides of the arrow will be swapped.
REASONING: These are the standard properties of plane mirror reflections which remain constant regardless of the object's size.
ANSWER: 8 cm tall, upright, 20 cm behind the mirror, laterally inverted.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student places a notebook 40 cm from a mirror. They want to place a pen at the location of the image to catch it. Where should the pen be placed, and what happens to the size of the image if they move the notebook to 80 cm?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify initial image position: 40 cm behind the mirror.
- step 2: State placement of pen: 40 cm behind the mirror.
- step 3: Identify size property: The image size remains the same regardless of distance.
- step 4: Answer the change: Moving the notebook to 80 cm moves the image to 80 cm behind the mirror, but the height of the image does not change.
REASONING: The image size in a plane mirror is independent of the distance between the object and the mirror.
ANSWER: Pen at 40 cm behind the mirror; image size remains the same at 80 cm distance.
