## ELI5
Srinagar wakes at minus 3 degrees and the night drops it by five more: subtraction of integers handles the plunge. The rule is one move - subtracting a number means adding its opposite - so taking away a positive steps left, and taking away a negative sneaks a step right. Double negatives unfreeze into addition, which is how removing a debt feels like receiving money. Convert every minus-minus, then walk the line; the thermometer always confirms the landing.

## DEFINITION
Subtraction of integers involves finding the difference between two integers by adding the additive inverse of the subtrahend to the minuend, following the algebraic rule $a - b = a + (-b)$ and $a - (-b) = a + b$.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are checking the weather report in Srinagar, where the morning temperature is $-3^\circ\text{C}$ and it drops by $5^\circ\text{C}$ by night. How do we calculate the final temperature using subtraction of integers? We begin by writing down the starting integer and the subtraction operation, just like we did with whole numbers in earlier grades. However, when subtracting a positive or a negative integer, we must remember our prior learning about additive inverses and number lines. The key insight is turning a subtraction problem into an addition problem by changing the sign of the subtrahend: subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite.

**Takeaway:** integer subtraction is addition of the opposite: change the sign, then follow the addition rules. **In the exam:** rewrite every subtraction as add-the-opposite first, then move left and right along the line. **Check yourself:** compute the Srinagar night temperature by adding the opposite of the drop. **Why students miss it:** they flip the wrong sign or stop halfway, leaving a double negative unconverted. Convert first, walk second - the thermometer only confirms the landing point that the algebra already chose for it. Read the rewritten sum aloud, sign by sign, before letting the number line move.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the result of evaluating the expression $-7 - (-4)$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is $-3$. A student who answers $-11$ has incorrectly added the absolute values together or treated the double negative as a single negative sign, failing to apply the rule that subtracting a negative number is equivalent to adding its positive opposite $(-7 + 4 = -3)$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why subtracting a positive integer, such as $8 - 12$, moves us to the left on the number line, whereas subtracting a negative integer, such as $8 - (-12)$, moves us to the right.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Subtracting a positive integer means removing value or moving in the negative direction (left), resulting in $-4$. Conversely, subtracting a negative integer is mathematically equivalent to adding its positive opposite ($8 + 12 = 20$), which moves us to the right on the number line because taking away a deficit is the same as gaining a credit. A student who thinks both operations move left does not understand the sign transformation rule for double negatives.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate the value of $-15 - 8 - (-25)$ step by step.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $2$. Step 1: Rewrite subtraction of a negative as addition: $-15 - 8 + 25$. Step 2: Combine the first two terms: $-15 - 8 = -23$. Step 3: Add the remaining positive integer: $-23 + 25 = 2$. A student who gets $-32$ has incorrectly combined $-15$ and $-8$ as a positive number or added $8$ and $25$ before handling the signs.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the expressions $a - b$ and $b - a$ when $a = -5$ and $b = 12$. What mathematical property of integer subtraction does this relationship illustrate?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Evaluating $a - b$ gives $-5 - 12 = -17$, while evaluating $b - a$ gives $12 - (-5) = 12 + 5 = 17$. They are additive inverses of each other ($a - b = -(b - a)$), which proves that integer subtraction is not commutative. A student who states that they are equal is confusing subtraction with addition or multiplication.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student evaluates $-24 - (-15)$ and writes down the working: $-24 - (-15) = -24 - 15 = -39$. Judge whether this work is correct and identify the exact error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The student's work is incorrect. The error occurs when changing the signs: the student dropped the negative sign of $15$ but kept a subtraction sign instead of changing it to addition ($-\,(-15)$ becomes $+15$). The correct working is $-24 + 15 = -9$. A student who agrees with the original work does not understand that subtracting a negative number adds its positive value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Construct an integer subtraction expression involving at least one negative integer and one set of parentheses that results in a final answer of $-10$. Explain your reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An acceptable response is $-4 - (6) = -10$ or $-15 - (-5) = -10$. A good answer shows clear understanding of how integer sign rules generate a specific target value. A poor answer uses only whole numbers or ignores the integer rules, revealing a lack of comprehension regarding negative values.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently drop or misapply negative signs when converting subtraction to addition, especially when encountering consecutive negative signs like $-a - (-b)$. For instance, in solving $-12 - (-7)$, a student often writes $-12 - 7 = -19$ instead of changing the double negative to a positive addition $-12 + 7 = -5$.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student makes this sign error, pause and ask them to circle the two adjacent signs in the middle of the expression (the subtraction sign and the negative sign of the second number). Remind them of the rule: "Two negatives side by side combine to make a big positive plus sign." Have them rewrite the problem explicitly with the addition sign before calculating: $-12 - (-7)$ becomes $-12 + (-(-7))$ which is $-12 + 7 = -5$.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: At 6:00 AM, the temperature in a mountain town in Himachal Pradesh was $-4^\circ\text{C}$. By 2:00 PM, the temperature rose, but by midnight it dropped $11^\circ\text{C}$ from the 2:00 PM reading. If the 2:00 PM temperature was $7^\circ\text{C}$, what was the midnight temperature? Furthermore, what is the total temperature difference between 6:00 AM and midnight?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given values: 6:00 AM temperature = $-4^\circ\text{C}$, 2:00 PM temperature = $7^\circ\text{C}$, midnight temperature drop from 2:00 PM = $11^\circ\text{C}$.
- step 2: Calculate the midnight temperature by subtracting the drop from the 2:00 PM reading: $7 - 11$.
- step 3: Apply integer subtraction rule: $7 - 11 = 7 + (-11) = -4^\circ\text{C}$.
- step 4: Calculate the temperature difference between 6:00 AM and midnight: $\text{Midnight temperature} - \text{6:00 AM temperature} = -4 - (-4)$.
- step 5: Apply the double negative rule: $-4 - (-4) = -4 + 4 = 0^\circ\text{C}$.
REASONING: Subtraction of integers allows us to find the exact change or new state when dealing with below-zero numbers. Converting subtraction to addition using the additive inverse prevents sign errors during computation.
ANSWER: Midnight temperature is $-4^\circ\text{C}$ and the total difference between 6:00 AM and midnight is $0^\circ\text{C}$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A submarine is cruising at an elevation of $-250\text{ metres}$ (below sea level). It descends further by diving $180\text{ metres}$, and then rises by $90\text{ metres}$. Express the final elevation using integer subtraction and addition, then calculate the difference between its initial and final positions.
STEPS:
- step 1: Write the initial position as an integer: $-250$.
- step 2: Account for the descent (subtracting a positive depth or adding negative movement): $-250 - 180 = -430\text{ metres}$.
- step 3: Account for the rise (adding positive movement): $-430 + 90 = -340\text{ metres}$ (final position).
- step 4: Set up the expression for the difference between initial position and final position: $\text{Final position} - \text{Initial position} = -340 - (-250)$.
- step 5: Evaluate using the subtraction rule: $-340 + 250 = -90\text{ metres}$.
REASONING: Real-world depths and heights are represented by signed integers. Finding the difference between two states always requires subtracting the starting state from the final state.
ANSWER: Final position is $-340\text{ metres}$ and the difference from start to finish is $-90\text{ metres}$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Simplify the complex integer expression: $-85 - [ -32 - ( 15 - 48 ) ] + (-12)$. Show all steps clearly by working from the innermost parentheses outward.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the innermost expression inside parentheses: $(15 - 48)$.
- step 2: Compute $(15 - 48) = 15 + (-48) = -33$.
- step 3: Substitute this result back into the brackets: $-85 - [ -32 - (-33) ] + (-12)$.
- step 4: Simplify the bracketed subtraction with a double negative: $-32 - (-33) = -32 + 33 = 1$.
- step 5: Substitute the bracket result back into the main expression: $-85 - [ 1 ] + (-12)$.
- step 6: Perform the final sequence of additions and subtractions from left to right: $-85 - 1 - 12 = -86 - 12 = -98$.
REASONING: Order of operations applies to integers just as it does to whole numbers. Clearing innermost parentheses and carefully applying the rule for subtracting negative numbers ensures accuracy in multi-step expressions.
ANSWER: $-98$
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Riya has a bank account balance of $-\text{Rs. } 450$ (she owes the bank money). She makes a deposit, bringing her balance to $\text{Rs. } 1200$. Her friend Kabir has a balance of $\text{Rs. } 300$ and withdraws $\text{Rs. } 850$. 
(a) Write an integer subtraction sentence to find the change in Riya's account balance from her initial debt to her final balance.
(b) Whose account experienced a greater numeric change from start to finish? Show your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world financial situations to mathematical expressions and comparing integer changes
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify Riya's start ($-\text{Rs. } 450$) and end ($\text{Rs. } 1200$). Formulate subtraction: $1200 - (-450)$.
- step 2: Compute Riya's change: $1200 + 450 = \text{Rs. } 1650$.
- step 3: Identify Kabir's start ($\text{Rs. } 300$) and end ($\text{Rs. } 300 - 850 = -\text{Rs. } 550$).
- step 4: Compute Kabir's change: $-\text{Rs. } 550 - \text{Rs. } 300 = -\text{Rs. } 850$ (magnitude of change is $850$).
- step 5: Compare the magnitudes of change: $1650 > 850$.
ANSWER: (a) $1200 - (-450) = 1650$. (b) Riya's account experienced a greater numeric change ($\text{Rs. } 1650$ increase compared to Kabir's $\text{Rs. } 850$ decrease).

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two integers $x$ and $y$ are such that $x = -18$ and $y = -35$. 
(a) Calculate $x - y$ and $y - x$. 
(b) Prove algebraically that for any two negative integers where $x > y$, the difference $x - y$ is always a positive integer, while $y - x$ is always a negative integer.
COMPETENCY: Algebraic generalization and proof of integer subtraction properties
STEPS:
- step 1: Substitute values for (a): $x - y = -18 - (-35) = -18 + 35 = 17$, and $y - x = -35 - (-18) = -35 + 18 = -17$.
- step 2: For part (b), let $x$ and $y$ be negative integers such that $x > y$. This means $x$ is closer to zero than $y$ (e.g., $-2 > -5$).
- step 3: Set up the expression $x - y$. Since $x > y$, moving from $y$ to $x$ on the number line requires moving in the positive direction (to the right).
- step 4: Alternatively, rewriting subtraction as addition of the opposite: $x + (-y)$. Since $y$ is negative, $-y$ is positive. Adding a larger positive value than the negative value of $x$ yields a positive result.
- step 5: Conclude that $x - y > 0$ and $y - x < 0$.
ANSWER: (a) $x - y = 17$, $y - x = -17$. (b) Proof demonstrates that subtracting a smaller negative number from a larger negative number results in adding a positive value of greater magnitude, yielding a positive integer difference.
DIFFICULTY: hard
