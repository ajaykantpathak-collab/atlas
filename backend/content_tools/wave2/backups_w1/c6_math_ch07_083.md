## ELI5
Congruent shapes are twins in the strictest sense: same shape, same size, one lying perfectly over the other with edges on edges and corners on corners. Identical bangles stack flawlessly; a stamp and its poster blow-up share the look but not the size, so they are look-alikes only. Fit, not resemblance, is the test.

## DEFINITION
Two geometric shapes are congruent if they have the exact same shape and size, meaning one can be placed perfectly over the other so that all edges and corners match. Congruent figures possess identical corresponding sides and identical corresponding angles.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to visually identify congruent shapes and explain that they "fit" perfectly when superimposed, and identify corresponding parts in congruent triangles.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the fundamental definition of congruence and the criteria for triangle congruence (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS). It excludes formal geometric proofs, similarity, and transformation-based congruence (like rotation or reflection).
## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever noticed that two identical pages from your notebook can be placed perfectly on top of each other? If you take two identical bangles or two 5-rupee coins, you will notice that they have the same size and shape—they are "congruent." To start, visualize picking up one shape and laying it down on the other; if it covers the shape underneath without any part sticking out or any gaps, they are congruent. For triangles, we don't always need to test every single side; we can use "shortcuts" or rules like SSS (Side-Side-Side) or SAS (Side-Angle-Side) to confirm they are identical. The key insight is that congruence is about "sameness" in every dimension.

**Takeaway:** congruent figures match in both shape and size - one superposes exactly on the other, edges and corners aligned. **In the exam:** in congruence questions, test fit, not looks: matching sides and angles together, or the side rules that force them. **Check yourself:** stack the walkthrough's bangles - perfect overlap is the whole definition in one motion. **Why students miss it:** students accept look-alikes as twins - the bangles are a no to that; same angles with different sizes is resemblance, not congruence. Rehearse the exam move once more - shape, size, superpose - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse "congruent" with "similar." They think if shapes look alike (like two triangles of different sizes), they are congruent. This happens because they focus only on the "shape" and ignore the "size" requirement.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to place a small triangle on a larger one. Ask: "Does the small one cover the large one exactly?" When they say "no," explain: "Congruence is a 'perfect fit'—no gaps, no overlaps. If there is a difference in size, it's not congruent."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You have two triangular metal plates. Plate A has sides of 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm. Plate B has sides of 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm. Are they congruent?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the measurements of Plate A (3, 4, 5 cm).
- Step 2: Identify the measurements of Plate B (3, 4, 5 cm).
- Step 3: Compare corresponding sides (3=3, 4=4, 5=5).
- Step 4: Apply the SSS rule for congruence.
- Step 5: Conclude that since all corresponding sides match, they are congruent.
REASONING: The SSS rule explicitly states that if all sides match, the triangles are identical in size and shape.
ANSWER: Yes, they are congruent.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Two rectangles are cut from a paper. Rectangle 1 is 6 cm by 4 cm. Rectangle 2 is 5 cm by 4 cm. Can one be superimposed on the other?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify dimensions of Rectangle 1 (length 6, width 4).
- Step 2: Identify dimensions of Rectangle 2 (length 5, width 4).
- Step 3: Compare corresponding sides (6 vs 5, 4 vs 4).
- Step 4: Note that 6 does not equal 5.
- Step 5: Conclude they cannot be superimposed exactly.
REASONING: Congruence requires ALL corresponding parts to be equal; a discrepancy in one side makes superimposition impossible.
ANSWER: No, they are not congruent.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Triangle X has sides of 6 cm, 8 cm, and an included angle of 90 degrees. Triangle Y also has sides of 6 cm, 8 cm, and an included angle of 90 degrees. Are they congruent?
STEPS:
- Step 1: List parts of Triangle X (6 cm, 8 cm, 90°).
- Step 2: List parts of Triangle Y (6 cm, 8 cm, 90°).
- Step 3: Identify the rule (SAS - Side-Angle-Side).
- Step 4: Verify the angle is between the two sides.
- Step 5: Conclude congruence based on SAS rule.
REASONING: The SAS rule confirms that if two sides and the included angle match, the triangles are congruent.
ANSWER: Yes, they are congruent.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you cut two identical circles from a piece of paper, are they congruent? If you fold one circle in half, is the resulting semicircle congruent to the other semicircle cut from the second circle?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that the two circles have the same radius.
- Step 2: Apply definition of congruence (same shape and size).
- Step 3: Analyze the effect of folding/cutting both circles identically.
- Step 4: Verify if the halves have the same dimensions.
ANSWER: Yes, the two original circles are congruent. After folding and cutting both exactly the same way, the two resulting semicircles are also congruent because they are identical portions of identical circles.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A carpenter has two wooden triangles. Triangle PQR has sides 5cm, 5cm, and 7cm. Triangle XYZ has sides 5cm, 5cm, and 7cm. The carpenter claims they are congruent. Is he correct? What if Triangle XYZ had sides 7cm, 5cm, and 5cm in a different order?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations
STEPS:
- Step 1: Compare sides of PQR (5, 5, 7) and XYZ (5, 5, 7).
- Step 2: Use the SSS rule.
- Step 3: Consider the order of sides (side lengths are the same regardless of order).
- Step 4: Determine if order changes the shape/size.
ANSWER: The carpenter is correct. Even if the order is different, the sides (5, 5, 7) are the same, meaning the triangles can be turned or flipped to fit exactly. They remain congruent.
DIFFICULTY: hard
