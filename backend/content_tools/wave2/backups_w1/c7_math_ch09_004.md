## ELI5
The craft club's star is tiled with wooden triangles whose sides are all equal, and every corner of every tile measures exactly 60 degrees - no protractor needed. The reasoning is a two-step ladder: equal sides throw equal opposite angles, and the three equals must share the triangle's angle budget fairly, landing each on a sixth of a full turn. Extend any side and the outside angle widens to double. Six such tiles meet at a point and complete the circle - the secret behind star patterns and courtyard rangoli grids.

## DEFINITION
An equilateral triangle is a triangle in which all three sides are equal in length, and consequently, all three interior angles are equal to exactly $60^\circ$ each.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can calculate unknown angle measures in equilateral triangles, prove that all angles measure $60^\circ$ using the angle sum property of triangles, and solve multi-step geometric problems involving side lengths and interior angles.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the interior angle properties of equilateral triangles (all angles equal to $60^\circ$) and basic angle calculations in equilateral triangular figures. Does NOT cover trigonometry, vector geometry, or coordinate geometry proofs of equilateral triangles.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are helping your school craft club build a decorative star pattern using wooden triangular tiles, where every tile has sides of equal length. If you pick up one of these tiles, you might wonder how its interior corners relate to one another without using a protractor. In Class 6, you learned that the sum of angles in any triangle is $180^\circ$ and that triangles can be classified by their sides and angles. Building on that foundation, we now explore what happens when all three sides are equal—meaning all three angles must also be equal because equal sides subtend equal angles. Therefore, if we divide the total sum of $180^\circ$ equally among three identical angles, each angle must measure $180^\circ \div 3 = 60^\circ$. This powerful property allows us to instantly identify missing angles in any equilateral triangle structure.

**Takeaway:** equilateral triangles carry 60 degrees in every corner: equal sides force equal angles, and the sum splits fairly. **In the exam:** derive the 60 by pairing the isosceles property with the angle sum; use it for exterior and tiling questions. **Check yourself:** compute one angle of the wooden tile from scratch and confirm the other two match. **Why students miss it:** they accept any three angles summing to the straight-angle total as equilateral, ignoring the all-equal clause.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the exact measure of each interior angle in an equilateral triangle?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Each interior angle measures $60^\circ$. A student who answers $90^\circ$ is confusing equilateral triangles with right-angled triangles, while a student who answers $180^\circ$ is confusing individual angles with the sum of all three interior angles in a triangle.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why must all three angles of an equilateral triangle be equal to each other?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Because all three sides are equal in length, the angles opposite to these equal sides must also be equal (isosceles and equilateral triangle property). A student who says "because they look symmetrical" lacks geometric reasoning, whereas the correct mathematical reason relies on the relationship between side lengths and opposite angle measures.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
In an equilateral triangle $ABC$, side $AB = 5\text{ cm}$. If an exterior angle is formed by extending side $BC$ to a point $D$, what is the measure of this exterior angle?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The measure of the exterior angle is $120^\circ$. 
Steps:
1. Identify that triangle $ABC$ is equilateral, so interior angle $\angle ACB = 60^\circ$.
2. Note that the interior angle $\angle ACB$ and the exterior angle $\angle ACD$ lie on a straight line ($BCD$), forming a linear pair.
3. Set up the equation: $\text{Exterior Angle} + 60^\circ = 180^\circ$.
4. Solve for the exterior angle: $\text{Exterior Angle} = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ$.
A student who computes $60^\circ$ forgot the linear pair supplementary rule, while one who computes $90^\circ$ guessed randomly.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If a triangle has interior angles measuring $60^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $x^\circ$, what type of triangle is it and what is the value of $x$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The triangle is an equilateral triangle, and $x = 60$. 
Steps:
1. Use the angle sum property of triangles: sum of angles = $180^\circ$.
2. Set up the equation: $60^\circ + 60^\circ + x^\circ = 180^\circ$.
3. Simplify the left side: $120^\circ + x^\circ = 180^\circ$.
4. Solve for $x$: $x = 180 - 120 = 60$. Since all three angles are $60^\circ$, all three sides are equal, making it equilateral.
A student who states $x = 120$ incorrectly subtracted instead of using the total angle sum of $180^\circ$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that an equilateral triangle can have interior angles of $50^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $70^\circ$ because it adds up to $180^\circ$. Evaluate this claim and explain why it is incorrect.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The claim is false. While $50^\circ + 60^\circ + 70^\circ = 180^\circ$, an equilateral triangle requires *all* interior angles to be equal. Since $50^\circ \neq 60^\circ \neq 70^\circ$, this is actually a scalene triangle, not an equilateral triangle. A student who agrees with the claim confuses the general angle sum property of any triangle with the specific equal-angle requirement of an equilateral triangle.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a scenario where knowing that all angles of an equilateral triangle are $60^\circ$ helps solve a real-world design problem involving an Indian rangoli pattern made of equilateral triangles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good response describes tiling a courtyard with triangular tiles where six equilateral triangles meet at a central point, noting that $6 \times 60^\circ = 360^\circ$, which forms a complete circle around the point without any gaps or overlaps. A poor response simply states "triangles fit together" without mathematical justification using the $60^\circ$ angle measure.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the side length of an equilateral triangle with its angle measure, attempting to set side lengths equal to angle degrees (e.g., writing $5\text{ cm} = 60^\circ$) or assuming that if side lengths are scaled up (e.g., sides become $10\text{ cm}$ instead of $5\text{ cm}$), the interior angles also increase in size to $120^\circ$.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that side lengths and angle measures have entirely different units (centimetres vs. degrees) and measure different properties of a shape. Draw an equilateral triangle with small sides and one with large sides, and use a protractor to show that regardless of how long the sides are, the opening between the sides (the angle) remains exactly $60^\circ$.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: In a school art project, Priya cuts an equilateral triangular card of side $8\text{ cm}$. She wants to fold it along an altitude to create two smaller right triangles. If the altitude divides the top vertex angle into two equal parts, what are the measures of all interior angles in each of the resulting smaller triangles?

STEPS:
- step 1: Identify that the original card is an equilateral triangle, so its three interior angles are $\angle A = 60^\circ$, $\angle B = 60^\circ$, and $\angle C = 60^\circ$.
- step 2: Note that the altitude creates two right-angled triangles by dropping a perpendicular from the top vertex to the base, forming a $90^\circ$ angle at the base.
- step 3: Calculate the divided top vertex angle: since the altitude bisects the $60^\circ$ angle, the top angle in each smaller triangle is $60^\circ \div 2 = 30^\circ$.
- step 4: Verify the third angle of the smaller triangle using the angle sum property: $180^\circ - (90^\circ + 30^\circ) = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$.
- step 5: Conclude that the interior angles of each smaller right triangle are $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$.
REASONING: Because the original shape is an equilateral triangle, all its base angles are $60^\circ$. Bisecting the vertical angle splits $60^\circ$ into two $30^\circ$ angles, and the perpendicular altitude introduces a $90^\circ$ angle, allowing us to find the remaining angles using the triangle angle sum rule.
ANSWER: $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Rahul builds a triangular flower bed shaped like an equilateral triangle with perimeter $30\text{ m}$. He wants to put a decorative fence post at each corner and run a string across from one vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side (an altitude/median). Find the angle made by this string with the base side.

STEPS:
- step 1: Recognize that the flower bed is an equilateral triangle, meaning all its sides are equal and all its interior angles are $60^\circ$.
- step 2: Understand that the string dropped from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side acts as an altitude and angle bisector in an equilateral triangle.
- step 3: Determine the angle where this string meets the base side by recalling that the altitude of an equilateral triangle is perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to the base.
- step 4: Calculate the angle between the string and the base: since it is perpendicular, the angle is $90^\circ$. (Alternatively, the base angle of the triangle is $60^\circ$, and the vertex angle is bisected to $30^\circ$, forming a $30^\circ-60^\circ-90^\circ$ triangle).
- step 5: State the final angle clearly with units: the string meets the base at $90^\circ$.
REASONING: In an equilateral triangle, drawing a line from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side creates a perpendicular bisector. Therefore, the angle formed at the intersection with the base is always a right angle ($90^\circ$).
ANSWER: $90^\circ$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A metallic frame consists of two equilateral triangles joined side-by-side sharing a common edge, forming a rhombus-like double-triangle shape. If an exterior angle is created at one of the outer vertices by extending the base line by $5\text{ cm}$, what is the measure of that exterior angle?

STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the geometric property of the individual shapes: each component is an equilateral triangle, so every interior angle is $60^\circ$.
- step 2: Locate the specific vertex where the base line is extended to form the exterior angle.
- step 3: Recognize that the interior angle of the equilateral triangle at that vertex is $60^\circ$ and it lies on the same straight line as the extension (linear pair).
- step 4: Set up the linear pair equation: $\text{Interior Angle} + \text{Exterior Angle} = 180^\circ$, which translates to $60^\circ + x = 180^\circ$.
- step 5: Solve algebraically: $x = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ$.
REASONING: Regardless of how many triangles are joined together, any individual equilateral triangle maintains interior angles of $60^\circ$. The exterior angle theorem or linear pair property states that an interior angle and its adjacent exterior angle always sum to $180^\circ$, leading directly to $120^\circ$.
ANSWER: $120^\circ$
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: In a triangular garden shaped as an equilateral triangle $PQR$, a gardener walks from corner $P$ along the side to $Q$, turns along an imaginary line to the interior, and cuts across such that an angle of $30^\circ$ is formed with side $PQ$. What is the angle between this cutting path and side $PR$?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world geometric layouts into angle calculations
STEPS:
- step 1: Draw or visualize equilateral triangle $PQR$ with all interior angles equal to $60^\circ$ ($\angle P = 60^\circ$, $\angle Q = 60^\circ$, $\angle R = 60^\circ$).
- step 2: Identify the new path starting from vertex $P$ that makes a $30^\circ$ angle with side $PQ$. This means the original $60^\circ$ angle at vertex $P$ is divided into a $30^\circ$ angle and another angle ($60^\circ - 30^\circ = 30^\circ$).
- step 3: Consider the newly formed smaller triangle containing vertex $P$, one corner on side $QR$, and the vertex $P$ itself.
- step 4: Use the triangle angle sum property on this new triangle to find the missing angle with side $PR$.
ANSWER: The angle between the cutting path and side $PR$ is $30^\circ$, because the $60^\circ$ angle at vertex $P$ is bisected into two equal $30^\circ$ parts when symmetrical properties are applied.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two identical equilateral triangular tiles, $ABC$ and $ACD$, are joined along their common side $AC$ to form a rhombus $ABCD$. Calculate the measure of angle $\angle BCD$ and explain how the equilateral triangle property helps determine it without a protractor.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying geometric composite shapes
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the composite shape: it is made of two equilateral triangles, $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ACD$, sharing side $AC$.
- step 2: Recall that in equilateral triangle $ABC$, angle $\angle BCA = 60^\circ$.
- step 3: Recall that in equilateral triangle $ACD$, angle $\angle ACD = 60^\circ$.
- step 4: Combine the two adjacent angles to find the total angle $\angle BCD$: $\angle BCD = \angle BCA + \angle ACD = 60^\circ + 60^\circ = 120^\circ$.
- step 5: Conclude that the angle is $120^\circ$ by utilizing the invariant $60^\circ$ interior angle property of equilateral triangles.
ANSWER: The measure of angle $\angle BCD$ is $120^\circ$. This is found by adding the adjacent $60^\circ$ angle from triangle $ABC$ and the $60^\circ$ angle from triangle $ACD$ that together form angle $\angle BCD$.
DIFFICULTY: hard
