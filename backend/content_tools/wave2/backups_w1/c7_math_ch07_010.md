## ELI5
Two beams of 6 and 8 centimetres must meet at an exact 60-degree corner: that is the SAS recipe - two sides and the angle trapped between them. Draw one beam, set the protractor at its end, mark the angle, lay out the second beam along the new ray, then close the triangle. The angle must be the included one - supplied at the wrong corner, the data builds a different triangle or none at all. And a corner of a full half-turn flattens the frame into a line.

## DEFINITION
Constructing a triangle given two sides and the included angle (SAS) involves drawing a unique triangle using a ruler, compass, and protractor when the lengths of two sides and the measure of the angle formed between them are known. The "included angle" is strictly the angle positioned directly between the two given sides.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are a carpenter in your village tasked with building a triangular wooden frame for a decorative roof, and your client tells you two wooden beams must measure 6 cm and 8 cm, and the corner where they meet must form an exact angle of 60°. How do you start drawing this on paper before cutting the wood? First, you draw the longer base line segment using your ruler to establish a firm foundation. Next, you use your protractor at one end of the segment to measure and draw the given included angle precisely. Finally, you measure off the second side length along that new angle ray using a compass, and connect the endpoints to complete the triangle.

**Takeaway:** SAS builds a unique triangle from two sides and the angle included between them. **In the exam:** draw the base, construct the included angle at its endpoint, mark the second side on the ray, join. **Check yourself:** build the carpenter's frame on paper and measure the third side to verify. **Why students miss it:** they accept an angle at any corner, not noticing it must sit between the two given sides. Measure the closed third side afterwards, and the protractor's verdict certifies the whole construction.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What does the term "included angle" mean when constructing a triangle using the SAS criterion?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The included angle is the angle formed specifically at the vertex where the two given side lengths meet. A wrong answer—such as believing it is any of the three angles in the triangle—reveals a failure to recognize that the angle must physically connect the two given sides for the construction to be uniquely determined.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If you are given side $AB = 5\text{ cm}$, side $BC = 7\text{ cm}$, and $\angle A = 50^\circ$, can you uniquely construct this triangle using the SAS method? Why or why not?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, you cannot construct it using SAS because the given angle ($\angle A$) is not the angle *between* the two given sides ($AB$ and $BC$). The included angle between $AB$ and $BC$ is $\angle B$, not $\angle A$. A wrong answer indicates confusion between adjacent and non-included angles in geometric descriptions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Describe the exact second step you must perform after drawing the first side segment $XY = 6\text{ cm}$ when constructing $\triangle XYZ$ with $\angle X = 60^\circ$ and $XZ = 8\text{ cm}$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Draw base line segment $XY = 6\text{ cm}$. Step 2: Place the center of the protractor at vertex $X$, align the baseline with segment $XY$, locate $60^\circ$, make a pencil mark, and draw a ray starting from $X$ passing through that mark. A wrong answer usually suggests measuring the angle from vertex $Y$, which ignores that the angle must be at the vertex shared by both given sides ($X$).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Why is it impossible to construct a unique triangle if you are given two sides of lengths 4 cm and 9 cm and an included angle of $180^\circ$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
An angle of $180^\circ$ forms a straight line, meaning the two sides would lie flat on top of each other instead of opening up to form a closed 3-sided polygon, resulting in a degenerate triangle (a straight line segment of length $13\text{ cm}$). A wrong answer reveals a lack of spatial understanding regarding angle measures and polygon boundaries.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The claim is false because SAS (Side-Angle-Side) guarantees triangle congruence; any accurate construction using the exact same measurements will result in a triangle of identical size and shape, meaning side $QR$ will be identical for everyone. A wrong answer shows a misunderstanding of geometric uniqueness and the properties of congruent triangles.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently measure the second side length along the wrong ray or measure the included angle from the wrong vertex. For example, when given side $AB = 6\text{ cm}$, side $BC = 8\text{ cm}$, and included angle $\angle B = 60^\circ$, students often draw angle $\angle A = 60^\circ$ instead of $\angle B = 60^\circ$, making it impossible to correctly place side $BC$ because vertex $B$ is not being used as the vertex for the angle.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to trace the letters of the given sides: the shared letter in the two side names is always the vertex of the included angle. Say: "Look at your given sides $AB$ and $BC$. Which letter appears in both? It is $B$! That means your protractor must sit on vertex $B$ to measure the $60^\circ$ angle." Have them circle the common vertex before touching their protractor.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Construct $\triangle PQR$ where side $PQ = 7\text{ cm}$, side $PR = 5\text{ cm}$, and the included angle $\angle QPR = 60^\circ$. Find the length of the third side $QR$ by measurement.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given parameters: side $PQ = 7\text{ cm}$ (base line), side $PR = 5\text{ cm}$, and included angle $\angle P = 60^\circ$ located between side $PQ$ and side $PR$.
- Step 2: Draw a baseline segment $PQ$ of length $7\text{ cm}$ using a ruler, marking endpoints $P$ and $Q$.
- Step 3: Place the center of the protractor at vertex $P$, align the baseline with $PQ$, measure an angle of $60^\circ$, make a pencil mark, and draw a long ray starting from $P$ passing through the mark.
- Step 4: Open your compass to a radius of $5\text{ cm}$ using a ruler, place the metal pointer at vertex $P$, and cut an arc on the $60^\circ$ ray to mark vertex $R$.
- Step 5: Connect vertex $R$ to vertex $Q$ with a straight line segment, and measure segment $QR$ with a ruler to find its length, which is approximately $6.2\text{ cm}$.
REASONING: The angle $\angle P$ is the "included" angle because it is formed directly by the meeting of the two known sides $PQ$ and $PR$ at vertex $P$. Constructing the angle first and then marking the exact side length along the ray ensures the triangle is unique and geometrically correct.
ANSWER: Triangle $PQR$ is successfully constructed with $QR \approx 6.2\text{ cm}$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A farmer wants to fence a triangular flower bed shaped like $\triangle ABC$ where side $AB = 6.5\text{ cm}$ on his scale drawing, side $BC = 5.5\text{ cm}$, and the included angle $\angle B = 75^\circ$. Construct this triangle and determine the perimeter of the scale drawing.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given parameters: side $AB = 6.5\text{ cm}$, side $BC = 5.5\text{ cm}$, and included angle $\angle B = 75^\circ$ (vertex $B$ is common to both sides).
- Step 2: Draw baseline segment $AB = 6.5\text{ cm}$ using a ruler.
- Step 3: Place the protractor at vertex $B$, measure $75^\circ$, and draw ray $BX$ from vertex $B$.
- Step 4: Open the compass to $5.5\text{ cm}$, place the pointer at $B$, and cut an arc on ray $BX$ to establish vertex $C$.
- Step 5: Join points $A$ and $C$ to complete $\triangle ABC$, measure side $AC$ (approx. $6.8\text{ cm}$), and calculate perimeter = $6.5 + 5.5 + 6.8 = 18.8\text{ cm}$.
REASONING: Because $B$ is the vertex shared by sides $AB$ and $BC$, it must be the vertex where the $75^\circ$ angle is constructed. Summing all three side lengths gives the total perimeter of the scale model.
ANSWER: Perimeter of the scale drawing is $18.8\text{ cm}$.
