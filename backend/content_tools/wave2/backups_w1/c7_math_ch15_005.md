## ELI5
Amit starts with 15 points and gains 3 a level; Priya starts with 5 and gains 5 a level. At which level do they draw level? Park the variables on one side and the plain numbers on the other - the equation sorts itself the way two runners' scores sort when like is compared with like. Transpose carefully, flip every sign that crosses, and the meeting level steps out of the balance.

## DEFINITION
Equations with variables on both sides are algebraic equations that contain the same unknown variable term on both the Left-Hand Side (LHS) and the Right-Hand Side (RHS). To solve such equations, one must use inverse operations to collect all variable terms on one side and all constant terms on the other side to find the value of the unknown.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can successfully transpose variable terms across the equals sign using inverse operations, simplify both sides of a linear equation, and solve for the unknown variable while correctly handling negative integers and fractional coefficients.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers linear equations in one variable where the variable appears on both sides of the equals sign with integer, fractional, or decimal coefficients. Does NOT cover quadratic equations, systems of simultaneous linear equations, or equations with variables in denominators.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine Amit and Priya are playing a digital game where both are collecting bonus points, but they start with different initial scores and earn points at different rates per level. If Amit starts with 15 points and earns 3 points per level, while Priya starts with 5 points and earns 5 points per level, at which level will they have the exact same total score? To figure this out, we can set up an algebraic equation where the variable (representing the level) appears on both sides: $15 + 3x = 5 + 5x$. Building on our knowledge of balancing equations and systematic solving methods, we treat the equals sign like a physical weighing scale. Our goal is to gather all the variable terms ($x$) on one side and all the constant numbers on the other side by applying inverse operations. The key insight is that whatever operation we perform on one side of the equation to eliminate a term, we must perform the exact same operation on the other side to keep it balanced.

**Takeaway:** collect variables on one side, constants on the other - then solve the simple equation left behind. **In the exam:** transpose with inverse operations and flip the sign of every term that crosses the equals sign. **Check yourself:** ask when Amit's 15-plus-3-a-level catches Priya's 5-plus-5-a-level, and let the balanced equation name the level. **Why students miss it:** sign errors while transposing are the flagged killer - a negative term that forgets to flip poisons every later line. Move one family at a time, variables first and constants second, and the substitution at the end exposes any flip you fumbled before the examiner does.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
An equation with variables on both sides has the unknown letter (like $x$) appearing in expressions on both the left-hand side and the right-hand side of the equals sign, such as $4x - 2 = 2x + 10$. A wrong answer often shows confusion between algebraic expressions and equations, or mistakes an equation with multiple variables (like $x$ and $y$) for an equation with the variable on both sides.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
When solving the equation $7x = 3x + 16$, why is our first strategic goal usually to subtract $3x$ from both sides rather than adding $16$ to both sides?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Subtracting $3x$ from both sides collects all the variable terms onto a single side of the equation ($7x - 3x = 16$), which simplifies it into a standard one-variable equation we already know how to solve. If a student adds $16$ first, they end up with $7x + 16 = 3x + 32$, which still leaves variables on both sides and makes the expression more complicated instead of isolating the unknown.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Solve the equation: $5x - 7 = 2x + 8$. Find the value of $x$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Subtract $2x$ from both sides to gather variables on the left: $5x - 2x - 7 = 8 \implies 3x - 7 = 8$. 
Step 2: Add $7$ to both sides to isolate the variable term: $3x = 8 + 7 \implies 3x = 15$. 
Step 3: Divide both sides by $3$: $x = 15 \div 3 = 5$. 
A wrong answer like $x = -5$ or $x = 3$ usually indicates a sign error when transposing terms or an arithmetic mistake during division.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the two equations: Equation A ($4x + 3 = 4x + 7$) and Equation B ($4x + 3 = 2x + 7$). Without fully solving them, explain how their solutions will differ fundamentally.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
In Equation A, if we subtract $4x$ from both sides, we get $3 = 7$, which is a false statement, meaning Equation A has no solution. In Equation B, the variable coefficients are different ($4$ and $2$), so subtracting $2x$ from both sides leaves a valid variable term ($2x + 3 = 7$), which leads to a unique single solution ($x = 2$). A wrong answer reveals a misunderstanding of what happens to variable terms when they have identical coefficients on both sides.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a linear equation with variables on both sides where the final solution is a negative integer, specifically $x = -4$. Show the step-by-step verification that your created equation works.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A valid equation is $3x + 5 = 2x + 1$. Verification: Substitute $x = -4$ into LHS: $3(-4) + 5 = -12 + 5 = -7$. Substitute into RHS: $2(-4) + 1 = -8 + 1 = -7$. Since LHS = RHS, the equation works. A poor answer provides an equation that does not balance when $x = -4$ is substituted, showing a lack of understanding of how equations are constructed from known solutions.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently make a sign error when transposing a term across the equals sign or when moving a variable term with a negative coefficient. For example, when solving $5 - 3x = 2x - 10$, a student might add $3x$ to the right side but forget to change the sign or incorrectly calculate $-3x + 3x$, or they might drop negative signs when dividing by a coefficient. Another common error is moving terms without applying the inverse operation to both sides simultaneously (e.g., changing $3x$ to $-3x$ on the same side instead of subtracting it from both sides).
## ON_FAILURE
If a student struggles with variables on both sides, pause and use the physical balance scale model. Write the equation and physically draw a dividing line at the equals sign. Instruct the student to write down every single operation explicitly (e.g., write "+ 2x" or "- 5" under both sides of the equation) rather than doing mental transposition. Emphasize the golden rule of algebra: "Whatever you do to one side of the balance to get rid of a term, you must do the exact same thing to the other side."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Riya saves rupees 50 in her piggy bank and adds rupees 20 every week. Ananya saves rupees 20 in her piggy bank and adds rupees 30 every week. After how many weeks will both have saved the exact same amount of money?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given values and let $w$ represent the number of weeks. Riya's total savings = $50 + 20w$. Ananya's total savings = $20 + 30w$.
- step 2: Set up the equation where both savings are equal: $50 + 20w = 20 + 30w$.
- step 3: Subtract $20w$ from both sides to collect the variable terms on the right side: $50 = 20 + 30w - 20w \implies 50 = 20 + 10w$.
- step 4: Subtract $20$ from both sides to isolate the variable term: $50 - 20 = 10w \implies 30 = 10w$.
- step 5: Divide both sides by $10$ to solve for $w$: $w = 30 \div 10 = 3$. Verify by substitution: Riya = $50 + 20(3) = 110$; Ananya = $20 + 30(3) = 110$.
REASONING: By setting the two algebraic expressions equal, we create an equation with variables on both sides. Using inverse operations step-by-step allows us to isolate the variable $w$ and find the exact week when their savings match.
ANSWER: After 3 weeks
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Solve the algebraic equation: $8x - 5 = 3x + 15$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Examine the equation to identify variable terms ($8x$ and $3x$) and constant terms ($-5$ and $15$) on both sides.
- step 2: Subtract $3x$ from both sides of the equation to bring all variable terms to the left-hand side: $(8x - 3x) - 5 = 15 \implies 5x - 5 = 15$.
- step 3: Add $5$ to both sides of the equation using the inverse operation to isolate the variable term: $5x = 15 + 5 \implies 5x = 20$.
- step 4: Divide both sides by the coefficient $5$ to find the value of $x$: $x = 20 \div 5 = 4$.
- step 5: Verify the solution by substituting $x = 4$ into the original equation: LHS = $8(4) - 5 = 32 - 5 = 27$; RHS = $3(4) + 15 = 12 + 15 = 27$. Both sides match.
REASONING: Moving all variable terms to one side first simplifies the multi-variable problem into a standard two-step linear equation, making isolation straightforward.
ANSWER: x = 4
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Solve the equation involving negative integers and fractional coefficients: $7 - 2x = 3x - 13$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify all terms. We have constants $7$ and $-13$, and variable terms $-2x$ and $3x$.
- step 2: Add $2x$ to both sides of the equation to eliminate the negative variable term from the left: $7 = 3x + 2x - 13 \implies 7 = 5x - 13$.
- step 3: Add $13$ to both sides of the equation to isolate the variable term on the right: $7 + 13 = 5x \implies 20 = 5x$.
- step 4: Divide both sides of the equation by $5$ to solve for $x$: $20 \div 5 = x \implies 4 = x$, which means $x = 4$.
- step 5: Verify the solution by substituting $x = 4$ back into the original equation: LHS = $7 - 2(4) = 7 - 8 = -1$; RHS = $3(4) - 13 = 12 - 13 = -1$. Both sides balance correctly.
REASONING: Adding $2x$ instead of subtracting $3x$ prevents working with negative variable coefficients, which minimizes calculation errors and keeps the arithmetic positive until the final division step.
ANSWER: x = 4
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two taxi companies charge different fares. Company A charges a fixed booking fee of rupees 50 plus rupees 15 per kilometre. Company B charges a fixed booking fee of rupees 110 plus rupees 10 per kilometre. At what exact distance in kilometres will the total fare charged by both companies be identical? Set up the equation with variables on both sides and solve it completely.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and solving equations with variables on both sides
STEPS:
- step 1: Define the variable, let $d$ represent the distance in kilometres. Write expressions for both companies: Fare A = $50 + 15d$, Fare B = $110 + 10d$.
- step 2: Set the two fares equal to each other: $50 + 15d = 110 + 10d$.
- step 3: Subtract $10d$ from both sides: $50 + 5d = 110$. Subtract $50$ from both sides: $5d = 60$.
- step 4: Divide by $5$: $d = 12$ kilometres. Verify by substituting $d = 12$ into both expressions.
ANSWER: The fares will be identical at a distance of 12 kilometres (both fares equal rupees 230).
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A rectangular park has a length that is 5 metres more than twice its width. Another triangular garden has a perimeter given by the expression $10x + 10$, where $x$ represents a standard unit length. If the perimeter of the rectangular park is expressed as $6x + 20$, find the value of $x$ where both shapes share the same perimeter value, and calculate that perimeter.
COMPETENCY: Comparing complex algebraic expressions and solving multi-step linear equations with variables on both sides
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given perimeter expressions: Rectangle Perimeter = $6x + 20$, Triangle Perimeter = $10x + 10$.
- step 2: Set the two perimeter expressions equal to each other according to the problem condition: $6x + 20 = 10x + 10$.
- step 3: Subtract $6x$ from both sides to collect variables on the right: $20 = 4x + 10$.
- step 4: Subtract $10$ from both sides to isolate the variable term: $10 = 4x$.
- step 5: Divide by $4$: $x = 10 \div 4 = 2.5$. Substitute $x = 2.5$ into either expression to find the perimeter: $6(2.5) + 20 = 15 + 20 = 35$ metres.
ANSWER: The value of $x$ is $2.5$, and the common perimeter is 35 metres.
DIFFICULTY: hard
