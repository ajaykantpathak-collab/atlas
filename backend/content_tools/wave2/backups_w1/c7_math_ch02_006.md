## ELI5
Four notebooks at 15 rupees plus four pens at 5 rupees can be billed two ways: price each lot separately, or add the prices first and multiply the sum by 4. Both land on 80 rupees, and that twin path is the distributive property: a x (b + c) = a x b + a x c. It is the mind's shortcut for mental math - split an awkward price into friendly parts, multiply each part, then add - and the discipline of checking that the outside factor visits every single term inside the bracket.

## DEFINITION
The distributive property in arithmetic expressions states that multiplying a number by a sum (or difference) is equal to multiplying that number by each term inside the sum (or difference) separately and then adding (or subtracting) the products: $a \times (b + c) = (a \times b) + (a \times c)$.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are buying 4 notebooks for ₹15 each and 4 pens for ₹5 each at the local stationery store. You could calculate the cost of notebooks ($4 \times 15 = 60$) and pens ($4 \times 5 = 20$) separately and add them to get ₹80. Alternatively, you could add the prices first ($15 + 5 = 20$) and then multiply by the quantity ($4 \times 20 = 80$). Both methods give the exact same total because multiplication distributes over addition. To begin using this property in arithmetic, look for a common factor outside or inside parentheses. Connect this to the basic properties of operations learned in earlier grades, such as multiplication and addition rules. The key insight is that $a(b + c) = ab + ac$, which allows us to break down difficult mental calculations into simpler parts.

**Takeaway:** the distributive property lets a factor visit each term: multiply the sum by splitting it. **In the exam:** use it to turn awkward products into friendly parts; in proofs, show both forms give the same total. **Check yourself:** bill the notebooks-and-pens purchase both ways and confirm the rupees agree. **Why students miss it:** they multiply the outside number by the first inside term and forget the second, dropping a whole product.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently apply the multiplier outside the parentheses to only the first term inside while completely forgetting to multiply it by the second term. For example, when evaluating $6 \times (20 + 4)$, a student might write $6 \times 20 + 4$ instead of $(6 \times 20) + (6 \times 4)$, resulting in an incorrect final calculation of $120 + 4 = 124$ instead of $120 + 24 = 144$.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student makes this error, explicitly circle both terms inside the parentheses and draw arrows from the outside multiplier pointing to each term. Say: "The multiplier outside has a ticket for every term inside the brackets, not just the first one. Let's write out both multiplication parts explicitly before doing any arithmetic." Have the student re-solve by writing the expanded sum or difference before computing.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A school store purchased 18 boxes of notebooks. Each box contains 50 ruled notebooks and 10 unruled notebooks. Using the distributive property, calculate the total number of notebooks purchased.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given quantities. Number of boxes = $18$, notebooks per box = $(50 + 10)$.
- Step 2: Set up the arithmetic expression representing total notebooks: $18 \times (50 + 10)$.
- Step 3: Apply the distributive property to expand the expression into two products: $(18 \times 50) + (18 \times 10)$.
- Step 4: Compute each product step by step: $18 \times 50 = 900$ and $18 \times 10 = 180$.
- Step 5: Add the computed products together with units: $900 + 180 = 1080$ notebooks.
REASONING: Breaking down $50 + 10$ allows us to multiply $18$ by clean multiples of ten, making mental arithmetic much simpler than multiplying $18 \times 60$ directly.
ANSWER: 1080 notebooks
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Calculate the value of $35 \times 98$ efficiently by using the distributive property with subtraction, considering $98$ as $(100 - 2)$.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Rewrite the number $98$ as a difference from a base of one hundred: $100 - 2$.
- Step 2: Substitute this into the expression: $35 \times (100 - 2)$.
- Step 3: Apply the distributive property over subtraction: $(35 \times 100) - (35 \times 2)$.
- Step 4: Perform the individual multiplications: $35 \times 100 = 3500$ and $35 \times 2 = 70$.
- Step 5: Subtract the second product from the first: $3500 - 70 = 3430$.
REASONING: Subtraction distribution is exceptionally useful when a number is slightly less than a tidy multiple of 10 or 100, converting complex multiplication into easy subtraction.
ANSWER: 3430
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A community hall arranged 16 rows of chairs for a function. Each row consists of 25 red chairs and 15 blue chairs. Additionally, 5 extra chairs were placed separately. Write the expression representing the total number of chairs and compute the final value using the distributive property.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Set up the expression for chairs in rows: $16 \times (25 + 15)$, and add the extra 5 chairs: $16 \times (25 + 15) + 5$.
- Step 2: Apply the distributive property inside the parentheses: $(16 \times 25) + (16 \times 15) + 5$.
- Step 3: Compute the first multiplication product: $16 \times 25 = 400$.
- Step 4: Compute the second multiplication product: $16 \times 15 = 240$.
- Step 5: Add all components together: $400 + 240 + 5 = 640 + 5 = 645$.
- Step 6: Verify the result by checking order of operations ($25 + 15 = 40$, $16 \times 40 = 640$, $640 + 5 = 645$).
REASONING: Combining the distributive property with subsequent addition tests both grouping expansion and the correct order of operations in multi-step arithmetic expressions.
ANSWER: 645 chairs

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A local bakery makes batches of biscuits. On Monday, they baked 32 trays, where each tray contained 50 butter cookies and 20 coconut cookies. On Tuesday, they baked 30 trays with the exact same contents per tray. Using the distributive property, write a single combined arithmetic expression for the total cookies baked over both days, simplify it, and find the total number of cookies.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and applying advanced properties
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write the expression for Monday's cookies: $32 \times (50 + 20)$.
- Step 2: Write the expression for Tuesday's cookies: $30 \times (50 + 20)$.
- Step 3: Combine both days into one total expression: $32 \times (50 + 20) + 30 \times (50 + 20)$.
- Step 4: Factor out the common binomial term $(50 + 20)$ using the reverse distributive property: $(32 + 30) \times (50 + 20)$.
- Step 5: Evaluate the sums inside parentheses: $62 \times 70$.
- Step 6: Compute the final product: $62 \times 70 = 4340$ cookies.
ANSWER: The combined expression is $(32 + 30) \times (50 + 20)$, which simplifies to $62 \times 70 = 4340$ total cookies.
DIFFICULTY: hard
