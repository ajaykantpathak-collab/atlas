## ELI5
Sodium is kept under kerosene like a difficult guest under house arrest - left in open air it reacts fast, in water it dances dangerously. Reactivity of metals measures how easily and vigorously a metal undergoes chemical change with oxygen, water or acids, forming oxides, hydroxides or salts plus hydrogen gas. Sodium tops the temper scale, gold sulks at the bottom, and iron rusts somewhere in between.

## DEFINITION
The reactivity of metals refers to how easily and vigorously a metal undergoes a chemical change when it comes into contact with substances like oxygen, water, and acids, forming new compounds such as metal oxides, hydroxides, or salts along with hydrogen gas. For example, when magnesium reacts with oxygen, it burns with a dazzling white flame to form magnesium oxide ($2Mg + O_2 \rightarrow 2MgO$).

Covers the chemical reactions of common metals (such as iron, magnesium, copper, and zinc) with oxygen, water, and dilute acids, producing metal oxides, hydroxides/oxides, and salts with the release of hydrogen gas. It does NOT cover advanced periodic trends, standard reduction potentials, or metallurgy processes.
## WALKTHROUGH
When you leave an iron tawa or spade out in the humid courtyard during the monsoon, you notice a reddish-brown flaky coating forming on its surface. This familiar household observation is a slow chemical reaction between the metal, oxygen in the air, and moisture. To understand this deeper, we begin by observing how different metals react at vastly different speeds—some catch fire instantly when heated with oxygen, while others react only with steam or dilute acids. Building on our knowledge of chemical changes from Class 6, we now look at the specific products formed when metals interact with these three key reagents. We will discover that highly reactive metals like sodium react vigorously with cold water, while less reactive metals like copper do not react with water or dilute hydrochloric acid at all.

**Takeaway:** reactivity ranks metals by how vigorously they attack oxygen, water and acids - sodium explosive, iron steady, gold indifferent. **In the exam:** in ranking questions, test against the three partners - air, water, acid - and order the metals by their answers. **Check yourself:** leave the walkthrough's iron tawa in the humid monsoon and explain its stain as moderate reactivity at work. **Why students miss it:** students rank metals by fame - reactivity is measured chemistry, not reputation; each calm noble metal is a no to the assumption that all metals misbehave alike.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What gas is typically released when a reactive metal like zinc reacts with a dilute acid such as hydrochloric acid?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Hydrogen gas is released. A student who answers "oxygen" or "carbon dioxide" is confusing the gas produced in acid-metal reactions with the gas produced during combustion or respiration, showing a lack of recall regarding the products of single displacement reactions between metals and acids.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why does a piece of sodium metal have to be stored immersed in kerosene oil instead of open air or water?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Sodium is an extremely reactive metal that reacts violently and explosively with oxygen and moisture present in the air and water, producing hydrogen gas which can catch fire. A student who says "to keep it clean" or "to prevent it from melting" does not understand the high chemical reactivity of alkali metals toward oxygen and water.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A student drops a clean iron nail into a test tube containing copper sulfate solution. After 30 minutes, the blue color of the solution fades to pale green, and a brown coating appears on the iron nail. Write the balanced chemical equation for this reaction and identify the type of chemical reaction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Steps:
1. Identify reactants: Iron ($Fe$) and copper sulfate ($CuSO_4$).
2. Identify products based on reactivity: Iron is more reactive than copper, so it displaces copper, forming iron(II) sulfate ($FeSO_4$) and copper metal ($Cu$).
3. Write the skeleton equation: $Fe + CuSO_4 \rightarrow FeSO_4 + Cu$.
4. Check balancing: 1 Fe, 1 Cu, 1 S, 4 O on both sides; equation is balanced.
5. Identify reaction type: Displacement reaction.
Answer: $Fe + CuSO_4 \rightarrow FeSO_4 + Cu$; this is a displacement reaction. A student who writes $Fe + CuSO_4 \rightarrow Cu_2SO_4 + Fe$ is using incorrect valencies for iron and copper.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze why aluminium metal, which is quite reactive chemically, does not corrode easily when exposed to rain and air in daily use utensils like pressure cookers.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Aluminium reacts quickly with atmospheric oxygen to form a thin, invisible, and tightly adherent layer of aluminium oxide ($Al_2O_3$) on its surface. This oxide layer acts as a protective shield that prevents further oxygen and moisture from reaching the underlying metal, stopping ongoing corrosion. A student who answers "aluminium is a non-reactive metal like gold" is failing to distinguish between inherent chemical reactivity and the protective physical barrier created by its stable oxide coating.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate whether the following chemical equation is correct and balanced: $Mg + 2H_2O \rightarrow MgO + 2H_2$. Justify your evaluation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The equation is incorrect because magnesium reacts with water (specifically steam) to form magnesium hydroxide or magnesium oxide and hydrogen gas, but the atom count and formula for the reaction of magnesium with steam yields $Mg + H_2O \rightarrow MgO + H_2$ (balanced: 1 Mg, 2 H, 1 O on both sides), whereas liquid water reacts differently to form magnesium hydroxide ($Mg(OH)_2 + H_2$). A student who accepts the given equation as correct is failing to check stoichiometry and proper product formation for metal-water reactions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple laboratory test using three test tubes, iron nails, dilute hydrochloric acid, and a burning splinter to demonstrate that metals react with acids to produce hydrogen gas. Describe what you would observe in each step.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
To design this test: Take a clean test tube, place a few pieces of zinc or iron granules inside, and add 5 mL of dilute hydrochloric acid. Observe effervescence (bubbles forming rapidly). Bring a burning splinter near the mouth of the test tube; it will put out the splinter with a pop sound, confirming the evolution of hydrogen gas. A poor answer would omit safety measures, fail to mention the pop test for hydrogen, or suggest using an unreactive metal like copper, which yields no observation.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often memorize that "all metals react with acids and water to produce hydrogen gas," leading them to write balanced equations for unreactive metals like copper or silver reacting with dilute hydrochloric acid (e.g., $Cu + 2HCl \rightarrow CuCl_2 + H_2$). They fail to realize that metals below hydrogen in the reactivity series cannot displace hydrogen from acids or water.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this misconception, have the student perform a physical comparison test in the lab or review a reactivity series chart. Show them that copper turnings added to dilute hydrochloric acid produce no bubbles at all, proving that position in the reactivity series dictates whether a displacement reaction occurs. Emphasize that chemical equations can only be written for reactions that actually take place in nature.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A science teacher burns a 2.4 g strip of magnesium ribbon in the presence of excess oxygen gas in a crucible in the school laboratory in New Delhi. Calculate the mass of magnesium oxide formed, assuming complete reaction. (Atomic masses: Mg = 24 g/mol, O = 16 g/mol)
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the balanced chemical equation for the combustion of magnesium: $2Mg + O_2 \rightarrow 2MgO$.
- step 2: Determine the molar masses of reactants and products: Molar mass of Mg = 24 g/mol; Molar mass of $MgO = 24 + 16 = 40$ g/mol.
- step 3: Use the stoichiometric coefficients from the balanced equation: 2 moles of Mg produce 2 moles of MgO (or 1 mole of Mg produces 1 mole of MgO).
- step 4: Calculate the moles of magnesium used: $\text{Moles of Mg} = \frac{\text{Given mass}}{\text{Molar mass}} = \frac{2.4 \text{ g}}{24 \text{ g/mol}} = 0.1 \text{ mol}$.
- step 5: Calculate the mass of magnesium oxide produced: Since 1 mole of Mg gives 1 mole of MgO, moles of MgO = 0.1 mol. Mass = $\text{Moles} \times \text{Molar mass} = 0.1 \text{ mol} \times 40 \text{ g/mol} = 4.0 \text{ g}$.
REASONING: The law of conservation of mass and chemical stoichiometry dictate that the mole ratio between reactants and products in a balanced equation remains constant, allowing us to convert mass to moles, apply the reaction ratio, and convert back to mass.
ANSWER: 4.0 g of magnesium oxide ($MgO$)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Sodium metal reacts vigorously with cold water in a beaker in a chemistry demonstration, releasing hydrogen gas and forming an alkaline solution of sodium hydroxide. Write the balanced chemical equation and determine how many moles of hydrogen gas are produced when 4.6 g of sodium reacts completely. (Atomic mass: Na = 23 g/mol)
STEPS:
- step 1: Write the skeleton equation for the reaction of sodium with water: $Na + H_2O \rightarrow NaOH + H_2$.
- step 2: Balance the chemical equation by adjusting coefficients: $2Na + 2H_2O \rightarrow 2NaOH + H_2 \uparrow$.
- step 3: Calculate the number of moles of sodium used: $\text{Moles of Na} = \frac{4.6 \text{ g}}{23 \text{ g/mol}} = 0.2 \text{ mol}$.
- step 4: Use the stoichiometric ratio from the balanced equation: 2 moles of Na produce 1 mole of $H_2$, so 0.2 moles of Na will produce $\frac{0.2}{2} = 0.1 \text{ mol}$ of $H_2$.
- step 5: Verify units and state the final result clearly with chemical formulas.
REASONING: Balancing the equation ensures mass conservation. The mole ratio (2:1 between Na and $H_2$) allows us to scale down the product amount proportionally from the given reactant mass.
ANSWER: Answer: 0.1 moles of hydrogen gas ($H_2$)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student drops 6.5 g of granulated zinc into an excess of dilute sulfuric acid ($H_2SO_4$) in a conical flask. The gas evolved is collected at standard temperature and pressure. Write the balanced equation, calculate the mass of zinc sulfate formed, and find the volume of hydrogen gas evolved at standard conditions where 1 mole of gas occupies 22.4 liters. (Atomic masses: Zn = 65 g/mol, S = 32 g/mol, O = 16 g/mol, H = 1 g/mol)
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify reactants ($Zn$ and $H_2O$ or $H_2SO_4$) and write the balanced equation: $Zn + H_2SO_4 \rightarrow ZnSO_4 + H_2 \uparrow$.
- step 2: Calculate molar masses: Molar mass of Zn = 65 g/mol; Molar mass of $ZnSO_4 = 65 + 32 + (4 \times 16) = 161$ g/mol.
- step 3: Determine moles of zinc: $\text{Moles of Zn} = \frac{6.5 \text{ g}}{65 \text{ g/mol}} = 0.1 \text{ mol}$.
- step 4: Relate moles of zinc to zinc sulfate: 1 mole of Zn gives 1 mole of $ZnSO_4$. Thus, mass of $ZnSO_4 = 0.1 \text{ mol} \times 161 \text{ g/mol} = 16.1 \text{ g}$.
- step 5: Relate moles of zinc to hydrogen gas: 1 mole of Zn gives 1 mole of $H_2$, so 0.1 mol of $H_2$ is produced.
- step 6: Calculate volume of hydrogen gas: $\text{Volume} = \text{Moles} \times 22.4 \text{ L/mol} = 0.1 \times 22.4 = 2.24 \text{ L}$.
REASONING: Combining stoichiometry, molar mass calculations, and gas molar volume concepts allows us to predict both the solid salt yield and the gaseous product volume from a single metal-acid displacement reaction.
ANSWER: Answer: 16.1 g of zinc sulfate and 2.24 liters of hydrogen gas
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Four metal strips—Copper, Iron, Zinc, and Magnesium—are dipped individually into four separate test tubes containing freshly prepared ferrous sulfate ($FeSO_4$) solution. In which test tubes will you observe a displacement reaction occurring, and why?
COMPETENCY: Predicting displacement reaction feasibility using the metal reactivity series
STEPS:
- step 1: Recall the relative reactivities of the given metals (Magnesium > Zinc > Iron > Copper).
- step 2: Understand the rule of displacement: a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its salt solution.
- step 3: Test each metal against ferrous ($Fe^{2+}$) ions: Copper is less reactive than iron (no reaction); Iron is the same metal (no reaction); Zinc is more reactive than iron (reaction occurs); Magnesium is more reactive than iron (reaction occurs).
- step 4: Conclude that zinc and magnesium will displace iron from ferrous sulfate solution.
ANSWER: Displacement reactions will occur in the test tubes containing Zinc and Magnesium because both are positioned higher than iron in the reactivity series, making them more reactive than iron. Copper is lower than iron and cannot displace it.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A metallurgical student tests three unknown metal samples (A, B, and C) with water and dilute hydrochloric acid. Sample A reacts vigorously with cold water. Sample B does not react with cold water or steam, but reacts slowly with warm dilute hydrochloric acid. Sample C reacts with steam only when heated strongly. Arrange these three metals in increasing order of their chemical reactivity and identify which sample is most likely copper.
COMPETENCY: Analyzing experimental observations to deduce reactivity series and identify unknown metals
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the reaction of Sample A: Reacts vigorously with cold water, indicating it is a highly reactive alkali or alkaline earth metal (like sodium or potassium).
- step 2: Analyze the reaction of Sample C: Reacts with steam only upon strong heating, indicating it is a moderately reactive metal (like iron or magnesium).
- step 3: Analyze the reaction of Sample B: Does not react with water or steam, but reacts with dilute acid, placing it below iron/zinc but above hydrogen (like lead or tin, or copper if acid is oxidizing—here copper is unreactive to dilute HCl, so let's evaluate carefully: copper does NOT react with dilute HCl at all). Wait, if Sample B does not react with water/steam and reacts slowly with warm dilute HCl, it is a less reactive metal like nickel or lead, whereas copper would show no reaction with dilute HCl.
- step 4: Order the metals by increasing reactivity: B < C < A, and state that copper would be even less reactive (unreactive with dilute HCl).
ANSWER: Increasing order of reactivity: Sample B < Sample C < Sample A. Sample A is the most reactive (reacts with cold water), while Sample C is moderately reactive (reacts with steam). Note: Copper is even less reactive than Sample B and would show no reaction with dilute hydrochloric acid.
DIFFICULTY: hard
