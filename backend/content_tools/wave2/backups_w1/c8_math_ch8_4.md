## ELI5
Finding a percentage of a quantity is just fraction-of arithmetic: 'of' means multiply. Fifteen percent of eighty kilograms: write fifteen hundredths, multiply by eighty, and the part appears. The phrase decodes cleanly — 'per cent' contributes the division by hundred, 'of' contributes the multiplication. Supermarkets, mark sheets, mobile batteries: any slice of any whole is this one multiplication wearing different clothes.

## DEFINITION
Finding the percentage of a given quantity means calculating the part of the quantity that corresponds to a given percentage value, using the relationship that "per cent" means "per hundred". Mathematically, it is expressed as: (Percentage/100) × Given Quantity.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can correctly compute the percentage of any given quantity (whole numbers, decimals, fractions) in multi-step real-world problems involving money, measurement, or data interpretation, and can explain why the calculation represents the required part.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers finding a percentage of a quantity where the percentage may be a whole number, fraction, or decimal (e.g., 12.5%, 33⅓%), and the quantity may be in rupees, metres, kilograms, litres, or unitless numbers. Does NOT cover reverse percentage problems (finding the whole from a part) or percentage increase/decrease, which are separate nodes.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you have ₹500 as pocket money and you want to save 20% of it for a new book. How much money will you put aside? Start by writing down the total quantity (₹500) and the percentage (20%). Recall from c8_math_ch8_3 that 20% is the same as the fraction 20/100 or the decimal 0.20. Now think: "of" in mathematics means multiplication, just like when we found 1/3 of 15 in Class 6 fractions. So we multiply 20/100 by 500. The key insight is that percentage is simply a fraction with denominator 100, and finding its value on a quantity is exactly the same as multiplying that fraction by the quantity.

Takeaway: (P/100) × Q is the whole method — percent supplies the ÷100, 'of' supplies the ×; compute in one line with the decimal form when it is clean. Exam format: expect money and measurement contexts, sometimes chained ('discount then tax'); write the percent-of expression before evaluating. Verification beat: a percentage under hundred of a quantity must give less than the whole. Why students miss it: they multiply by the bare percentage and leap a factor of hundred — reading 'per cent' aloud as 'divided by hundred' at the moment of writing prevents it.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the mathematical expression for finding 15% of 80 kg?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The expression is (15/100) × 80 kg or 0.15 × 80 kg. A wrong answer like 15 × 80 or 80/15 reveals the student does not understand that "per cent" means division by 100 and "of" means multiplication. **A wrong answer (15 × 80 or 80 ÷ 15) reveals the student missed that 'per cent' means divide by a hundred and 'of' means multiply — the expression is fifteen hundredths of the quantity, nothing else.** For example, a student in Ludhiana told a sack is fifteen percent lighter than eighty kilograms multiplies fifteen by eighty and reports a loss heavier than the sack itself, an impossible answer that the ÷100 would have prevented.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why does multiplying by 0.25 give the same result as finding 25% of a number?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Because 25% = 25/100 = 0.25, so 25% of a quantity = (25/100) × quantity = 0.25 × quantity. A student who says "it's just a rule" or "decimals are easier" shows they haven't connected the percentage-to-decimal conversion (c8_math_ch8_3) to the meaning of percentage as a fraction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate 12.5% of ₹400.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: 12.5% = 12.5/100 = 0.125. Step 2: 0.125 × 400 = 50. Answer: ₹50. A wrong answer of ₹5000 (from 12.5 × 400) shows the student forgot to divide by 100. An answer of ₹5 (from 0.125 × 40) shows a decimal place error. **A wrong answer (₹5000 from 12.5 × 400, or ₹5 from a misplaced decimal) reveals the student skipped the ÷100 or slipped a decimal place — twelve and a half percent is the fraction 0.125, and 0.125 × 400 = 50.** For example, a student in Kochi billing a customer twelve and a half percent tax on a four-hundred-rupee item who forgets the division by hundred charges a hundred times the fair tax, and the inflated bill exposes the missing ÷100 instantly.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the two methods: (a) finding 30% of 150 by doing (30/100)×150, and (b) finding 10% of 150 and multiplying by 3. Which is more efficient and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Method (b) is often more efficient mentally: 10% of 150 = 15, then 15 × 3 = 45. It uses the fact that 30% = 3 × 10%, leveraging the easy calculation of 10% (shifting decimal). A student who insists both are equally efficient for all numbers may not recognize the power of benchmark percentages (10%, 25%, 50%) for mental math.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student solved "Find 40% of 250" as follows: 40% = 40/100 = 2/5, then 2/5 of 250 = 2 × 50 = 100. Is this correct? Justify.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, correct. 40/100 simplifies to 2/5, and 2/5 of 250 = (250 ÷ 5) × 2 = 50 × 2 = 100. This uses equivalent fractions (Class 6) to simplify before multiplying. A student who says it's wrong because "you must use 40/100" shows inflexibility and misses the connection to fraction simplification.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-life problem where you need to find 33⅓% of a quantity, and solve it using the fraction form of the percentage.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: "A 2-litre bottle of juice is shared. Ravi drinks 33⅓% of it. How many millilitres does he drink?" Solution: 33⅓% = 1/3. 2 L = 2000 mL. 1/3 of 2000 = 2000/3 = 666⅔ mL. A poor answer uses 0.33 × 2000 = 660 mL, showing the student doesn't recognize 33⅓% as exactly 1/3 and loses precision.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often treat the percentage number as a whole number multiplier, e.g., calculating 15% of 200 as 15 × 200 = 3000 instead of (15/100) × 200 = 30. They see "15" and "of" and multiply directly, forgetting that "per cent" means "divided by 100". This is especially common when the percentage is a decimal like 12.5%, where they might compute 12.5 × quantity.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student makes this error, have them rewrite the percentage as a fraction over 100 every single time for a week. Say: "Write 15% as 15/100. Now read it: '15 per 100'. What does 'of' mean? Multiply. So 15/100 × 200." Do 5 problems daily where step 1 is mandatory: "Write percentage as fraction/decimal". Only after consistent success, allow skipping the written step.

## THIS_FORMULA
Percentage of Quantity = (P/100) × Q  where P is the percentage and Q is the given quantity.
## FORMULA_VARIABLES
P = percentage value (can be whole number, fraction, or decimal), Q = given quantity (with units), Result = part of Q corresponding to P% (same units as Q)
## FORMULA_UNITS
The result has the same units as Q (rupees, metres, kg, litres, etc.). Percentage itself is dimensionless.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A school has 800 students. On a particular day, 15% of the students were absent. How many students were present that day?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given quantity (total students = 800) and the percentage to find (15% absent). We need to find 15% of 800 first.
- Step 2: Convert 15% to a fraction: 15% = 15/100. Simplify if possible: 15/100 = 3/20.
- Step 3: Multiply the fraction by the total quantity: Number absent = (3/20) × 800.
- Step 4: Compute: (3 × 800) / 20 = 2400 / 20 = 120. So 120 students were absent.
- Step 5: Find present students: Total - Absent = 800 - 120 = 680. Answer with units: 680 students.
REASONING: We first find the part (absent students) using the percentage formula, then subtract from the whole to get the other part. Using the simplified fraction 3/20 makes the multiplication easier than using 15/100 or 0.15.
ANSWER: 680 students
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A recipe requires 2.5 kg of flour. If you want to make only 40% of the recipe, how many grams of flour do you need?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Given quantity = 2.5 kg. Percentage needed = 40%. We need 40% of 2.5 kg, then convert to grams.
- Step 2: Convert 40% to decimal: 40% = 40/100 = 0.4.
- Step 3: Multiply decimal by quantity in kg: 0.4 × 2.5 = 1.0 kg.
- Step 4: Convert kg to grams: 1 kg = 1000 g, so 1.0 kg = 1000 g.
- Step 5: Verify: 40% is less than half, so answer should be less than half of 2.5 kg (1.25 kg). 1 kg is reasonable. Answer: 1000 grams.
REASONING: Using decimal form (0.4) is efficient for multiplying with a decimal quantity (2.5). Unit conversion is done after the percentage calculation to avoid decimals in grams (2.5 kg = 2500 g, then 0.4 × 2500 = 1000 g also works).
ANSWER: 1000 grams
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: In an election, Candidate A got 45% of the total valid votes. Candidate B got 30% of the total valid votes. The remaining votes went to Candidate C. If the total valid votes were 12,000, by how many votes did Candidate A win over Candidate C?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Total valid votes = 12,000. A = 45%, B = 30%. C's percentage = 100% - (45% + 30%) = 25%.
- Step 2: Find votes for A: 45% of 12,000 = (45/100) × 12,000 = 45 × 120 = 5,400.
- Step 3: Find votes for C: 25% of 12,000 = (25/100) × 12,000 = (1/4) × 12,000 = 3,000.
- Step 4: Calculate winning margin: Votes for A - Votes for C = 5,400 - 3,000 = 2,400.
- Step 5: Check: A (45%) = 5,400, B (30%) = 3,600, C (25%) = 3,000. Sum = 12,000. Correct.
REASONING: This combines finding percentages of a quantity with the concept that percentages of a whole sum to 100%. We first determine C's percentage by subtracting from 100%, then compute both A's and C's votes, and finally find the difference.
ANSWER: 2,400 votes
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A shopkeeper marks up the price of a shirt by 25% above the cost price. If the cost price is ₹400, what is the marked price? Explain why we calculate 25% of ₹400 and then add it to ₹400, rather than just finding 25% of ₹400.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand that "mark up by 25%" means the marked price is cost price plus 25% of cost price.
- Step 2: Calculate 25% of ₹400: (25/100) × 400 = ₹100.
- Step 3: Add this to cost price: ₹400 + ₹100 = ₹500.
- Step 4: Interpret: The marked price is ₹500. Finding only 25% of ₹400 gives the increase amount (₹100), not the final price.
ANSWER: Marked price = ₹500. We add because the marked price includes the original cost plus the extra 25%.
DIFFICULTY: easy
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A water tank holds 5000 litres. On Monday, 12% of the water was used. On Tuesday, 15% of the REMAINING water was used. How many litres of water are left in the tank after Tuesday? Show all steps.
COMPETENCY: Multi-step problem solving with changing base quantities
STEPS:
- Step 1: Monday usage = 12% of 5000 = 0.12 × 5000 = 600 litres. Remaining after Monday = 5000 - 600 = 4400 litres.
- Step 2: Tuesday usage = 15% of remaining (4400) = 0.15 × 4400 = 660 litres.
- Step 3: Remaining after Tuesday = 4400 - 660 = 3740 litres.
- Step 4: Verify: Total used = 600 + 660 = 1260. 5000 - 1260 = 3740. Correct.
ANSWER: 3740 litres
DIFFICULTY: medium
