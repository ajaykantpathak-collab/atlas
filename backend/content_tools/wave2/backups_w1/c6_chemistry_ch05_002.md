## ELI5
Rip a sheet of paper and you hold smaller paper; burn it and you hold ash and smoke — the paper itself is gone. That is the line between the two kinds of change. In a physical change the substance survives, just reshaped: cut wood and every piece is still wood. In a chemical change the original substance transforms into something new with different properties: wood becomes ash and smoke, iron left in the rain becomes rust, and no kitchen trick turns ash back into sheets or rust back into shine. Spot the birth of a new substance that was not there before, and you have caught a chemical change.

## DEFINITION
A chemical change is a process in which one or more substances undergo a transformation to create entirely new substances with different properties. Unlike physical changes, these transformations are often permanent and cannot be easily reversed.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that any change in appearance (like color) or any change involving heat is a chemical change. They fail to realize that ice melting or water boiling (which involves heat) are physical changes because the substance remains the same.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student leaves a shiny iron nail outside in the rain for a week. The nail becomes reddish-brown and flaky. Is this a chemical change? How many steps can you identify in this process?
STEPS:
- 1: Observe the initial state (shiny iron).
- 2: Observe the final state (reddish-brown, flaky substance).
- 3: Determine if the substance is the same (Iron vs. Iron Oxide).
- 4: Conclude that a new substance has formed.
REASONING: The formation of the new reddish layer (rust) indicates that iron has reacted with oxygen and water to form a new compound.
ANSWER: Yes, this is a chemical change.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: You take 50 grams of sugar and dissolve it in 200 ml of water. You then evaporate the water. You get back the 50 grams of sugar. Is this a chemical change?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the ingredients (sugar + water).
- 2: Identify the process (dissolving then evaporating).
- 3: Check the final product (sugar).
- 4: Compare with original substance.
REASONING: Since the sugar properties remain the same after evaporation, no new substance was formed.
ANSWER: No, this is a physical change.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A firework is lit. It produces a bright flash, a loud noise, and grey ash remains on the ground. Classify the changes occurring.
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the inputs (chemicals in firework).
- 2: Identify the visible changes (light, sound, ash).
- 3: Compare inputs and outputs.
- 4: Evaluate if outputs are different substances.
- 5: Combine findings to name the process.
REASONING: The production of light, sound, and ash are markers of a chemical reaction where original chemicals are consumed.
ANSWER: This is a chemical change.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: You have a piece of wax. You melt it, and then you burn it. Explain why one is physical and one is chemical.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying.
STEPS:
- 1: Define physical change (no new substance).
- 2: Define chemical change (new substance formed).
- 3: Analyze melting (state change only).
- 4: Analyze burning (reaction with oxygen).
ANSWER: Melting is physical because the wax remains wax; burning is chemical because it turns into smoke and energy.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: If you have a mixture of iron filings and sand, you can separate them with a magnet. Is this a chemical change? What if you heat the mixture until the iron reacts with the sand?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction.
STEPS:
- 1: Evaluate the mixture (physical separation).
- 2: Evaluate the reaction (new compound).
- 3: Justify the difference.
ANSWER: Separating with a magnet is a physical process. Reacting them with heat is a chemical change because a new substance is formed that cannot be separated by a magnet.
DIFFICULTY: hard
