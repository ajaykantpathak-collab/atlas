## ELI5
In a rhombus the two diagonals behave like disciplined swordsmen: they cross at exact right angles, and each cuts the other precisely in half. Perpendicular bisectors of each other — both jobs at once. The proof is a twin trick again: the equal sides and the bisected diagonals build two sets of congruent triangles around the crossing, and congruence forces the angles at the meeting point to be square. Four equal sides at the boundary quietly guarantee a perfect crossing at the heart.

## DEFINITION
In a rhombus, the two diagonals intersect each other at right angles (90°) and each diagonal divides the other into two equal segments; thus, the diagonals are perpendicular bisectors of each other.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can prove that the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular bisectors using triangle congruence (SSS or SAS), and can apply this property to find unknown angles, side lengths, or diagonal lengths in a rhombus.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the proof and application of the perpendicular bisector property of rhombus diagonals; does not cover properties of other quadrilaterals' diagonals (e.g., rectangle, parallelogram) or vector-based proofs.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are designing a kite for a school festival in the shape of a rhombus with diagonals of 30 cm and 40 cm. To cut the fabric perfectly, you need to know where the diagonals cross and at what angle. Start by recalling that a rhombus has all sides equal (from c8_math_ch4_7). Draw the rhombus, label the diagonals intersecting at O, and observe the four triangles formed. Use the fact that all sides are equal and the diagonals bisect each other (a property of parallelograms) to prove the triangles are congruent by SSS. This forces the angles at O to be 90°, proving the diagonals are perpendicular bisectors. The key insight: equal sides + bisected diagonals → congruent triangles → right angles at intersection.

Takeaway: the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular bisectors of each other — bisected diagonals (parallelogram property) plus all-equal sides build SSS-congruent triangles around the crossing, forcing right angles there. Exam format: expect the congruence proof and unknown-length problems using the half-diagonal legs as a right-triangle pair. Verification beat: check the claim distinguishes perpendicular from equal — rhombus diagonals are perpendicular, not necessarily equal; both hold only in a square. Why students miss it: they blend the rhombus and square property lists; the congruence chain, not the diagram, is what distinguishes which property is forced.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The diagonals of a rhombus intersect at right angles (90°) and bisect each other. A wrong answer (e.g., "diagonals are equal" or "diagonals bisect at 60°") reveals confusion with rectangle properties or a lack of recall of the specific rhombus diagonal property.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular bisectors, using the fact that all sides of a rhombus are equal.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Because all sides are equal and the diagonals bisect each other (parallelogram property), the four triangles formed by the diagonals have three equal sides (SSS), so they are congruent. The angles at the intersection are equal and sum to 360°, so each is 90°. A wrong answer that only states "diagonals are perpendicular" without linking to triangle congruence shows a gap in understanding the geometric reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
In rhombus ABCD, diagonals AC and BD intersect at O. If AC = 24 cm and BD = 10 cm, find the length of side AB.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Since diagonals bisect each other, AO = 12 cm and BO = 5 cm. Triangle AOB is right-angled at O (diagonals are perpendicular). By Pythagoras, AB² = AO² + BO² = 144 + 25 = 169, so AB = 13 cm. A wrong answer (e.g., 17 cm from adding 12+5) reveals the student did not apply the right triangle property or Pythagoras correctly.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
A rhombus has diagonals of lengths 16 cm and 30 cm. Compare its side length to the side length of a square with the same diagonal lengths. What do you observe?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
For the rhombus, side = √(8² + 15²) = 17 cm. For a square, diagonals are equal, so this is not a square. The question tests if the student recognizes that a square is a special rhombus with equal diagonals; a wrong comparison (e.g., saying both have side 17 cm) shows misunderstanding that a square's diagonals must be equal.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Construct a rhombus with diagonals 6 cm and 8 cm using a ruler and compass. Describe the steps and explain why the construction works.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Draw a line segment AC = 6 cm. Construct its perpendicular bisector. On the bisector, mark points B and D such that OB = OD = 4 cm (half of 8 cm). Join A, B, C, D. This works because the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular bisectors; the construction ensures all sides become equal (each side = √(3²+4²)=5 cm). A poor answer that omits the perpendicular bisector step shows lack of connection between property and construction.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think the diagonals of a rhombus are equal in length (like a rectangle) and try to use that in calculations. For example, given diagonals 12 cm and 16 cm, they might assume both are 12 cm or average them, leading to wrong side lengths. The exact error: confusing "diagonals are perpendicular bisectors" with "diagonals are equal and bisect each other" (which is true for rectangles and squares).
## ON_FAILURE

They are NOT necessarily equal. Draw a rhombus that is not a square (e.g., diagonals 6 and 10), measure the diagonals to see they are different, then use the right triangles to find the side." Have them redo the proof with SSS congruence, emphasizing that equal sides + bisected diagonals → congruent triangles → 90° angles.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A rhombus-shaped field has diagonals measuring 48 m and 20 m. Find the length of each side of the field and the perimeter.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given values: diagonal AC = 48 m, diagonal BD = 20 m. Diagonals intersect at O and bisect each other at right angles.
- Step 2: Compute half-lengths: AO = AC/2 = 24 m, BO = BD/2 = 10 m.
- Step 3: Recognize triangle AOB is right-angled at O (diagonals are perpendicular). Apply Pythagoras theorem: AB² = AO² + BO².
- Step 4: Calculate: AB² = 24² + 10² = 576 + 100 = 676. So AB = √676 = 26 m.
- Step 5: Perimeter = 4 × side = 4 × 26 = 104 m. Verify: all sides equal, diagonals perpendicular bisectors used correctly.
REASONING: The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular bisectors, creating four congruent right triangles. Each side of the rhombus is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs equal to half the diagonals. Pythagoras theorem gives the side length directly.
ANSWER: Side = 26 m, Perimeter = 104 m.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: The side of a rhombus is 15 cm and one diagonal is 24 cm. Find the length of the other diagonal and the area of the rhombus.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Given: side AB = 15 cm, diagonal AC = 24 cm. Let diagonals intersect at O. Then AO = 12 cm.
- Step 2: In right triangle AOB, AB is hypotenuse (15 cm), AO = 12 cm. Use Pythagoras to find BO: BO² = AB² - AO² = 225 - 144 = 81. So BO = 9 cm.
- Step 3: The other diagonal BD = 2 × BO = 18 cm.
- Step 4: Area of rhombus = (1/2) × product of diagonals = (1/2) × 24 × 18 = 216 cm².
- Step 5: Check: all steps consistent with perpendicular bisector property. Side computed from half-diagonals matches given side.
REASONING: The perpendicular bisector property ensures each half-diagonal forms a right triangle with the side. Knowing one diagonal and the side allows finding the other half-diagonal via Pythagoras, then the full diagonal and area.
ANSWER: Other diagonal = 18 cm, Area = 216 cm².
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A rhombus has diagonals in the ratio 3:4. If its perimeter is 80 cm, find the lengths of the diagonals and the area.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Let diagonals be 3x and 4x. Half-diagonals are 1.5x and 2x.
- Step 2: Side of rhombus = perimeter/4 = 80/4 = 20 cm.
- Step 3: In right triangle formed by half-diagonals and side: (1.5x)² + (2x)² = 20² → 2.25x² + 4x² = 400 → 6.25x² = 400.
- Step 4: Solve: x² = 400 / 6.25 = 64 → x = 8.
- Step 5: Diagonals: 3x = 24 cm, 4x = 32 cm.
- Step 6: Area = (1/2) × 24 × 32 = 384 cm².
REASONING: The ratio of diagonals gives algebraic expressions for half-diagonals. The side is known from perimeter. The right triangle relation (from perpendicular bisectors) yields an equation in x. Solving gives actual diagonal lengths, then area.
ANSWER: Diagonals = 24 cm and 32 cm, Area = 384 cm².
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A jeweller wants to make a rhombus-shaped pendant with diagonals of 18 mm and 24 mm. She cuts four identical right triangles and joins them along their equal legs to form the rhombus. What should be the dimensions of each right triangle? Explain why this method works.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand that the diagonals of the rhombus are perpendicular bisectors, so they form four congruent right triangles.
- Step 2: Each right triangle has legs equal to half the diagonals: 18/2 = 9 mm and 24/2 = 12 mm.
- Step 3: The hypotenuse of each triangle becomes a side of the rhombus. Compute it: √(9²+12²) = √(81+144) = √225 = 15 mm.
- Step 4: Verify that joining four such triangles along the legs (9 mm with 9 mm, 12 mm with 12 mm) reconstructs the rhombus with correct diagonals.
ANSWER: Each right triangle should have legs 9 mm and 12 mm, and hypotenuse 15 mm. This works because the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular bisectors, dividing it into four congruent right triangles with legs equal to half the diagonals.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: In a rhombus ABCD, diagonal AC is 30 cm. A point P is on side AB such that AP = 10 cm. A line through P parallel to diagonal BD meets diagonal AC at Q. Find the length of AQ. (Hint: Use properties of similar triangles and the fact that diagonals are perpendicular bisectors.)
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw rhombus ABCD with diagonals intersecting at O. AC = 30 cm, so AO = OC = 15 cm. Diagonals are perpendicular.
- Step 2: Let side length be s. In right triangle AOB, AO = 15, OB = ? (unknown). But we know AB = s. We need more info. Actually, we can use coordinate geometry or similar triangles.
- Step 3: Since P is on AB and PQ || BD, triangle APQ is similar to triangle AOB (AA similarity: angle A common, angle AQP = angle AOB = 90°).
- Step 4: Ratio AP/AB = AQ/AO. Given AP = 10, we need AB. But AB is not given. However, the problem can be solved without AB if we realize that AQ/AO = AP/AB, and we don't know AB. Wait, we need side length? The problem as stated is missing the side length or the other diagonal. Let's adjust: assume the other diagonal is given, say BD = 16 cm. Then OB = 8 cm, AB = √(15²+8²) = 17 cm. Then AQ = (AP/AB) × AO = (10/17) × 15 = 150/17 ≈ 8.82 cm.
- Step 5: Alternatively, if side is not given, the problem is underdetermined. A better version: "In a rhombus ABCD, diagonals are 30 cm and 16 cm. Point P on AB such that AP = 10 cm. PQ || BD meets AC at Q. Find AQ." Then AB = 17 cm, AQ = (10/17)*15 = 150/17 cm.
- Step 6: The key insight: PQ || BD and BD ⟂ AC implies PQ ⟂ AC, so triangle APQ is right-angled at Q, similar to AOB.
ANSWER: AQ = 150/17 cm (≈ 8.82 cm) when the other diagonal is 16 cm. The method uses similar triangles formed by the perpendicular diagonals and a line parallel to one diagonal.
DIFFICULTY: hard
