## ELI5
The canteen bill reads 12x + 15y - x samosas, y juice boxes - and a customer orders 4 samosas and 3 juices. Substitution is simply feeding the numbers in: replace x with 4 and y with 3, then let BODMAS finish the job. The letters were placeholders; the customer's order gives them their values. One caution: a negative value goes in wearing brackets, and an exponent attaches only to its letter, not to a coefficient sitting nearby. Substitute first, evaluate second - in that order, every time.

## DEFINITION
Substituting values into an algebraic expression is the process of replacing the letter-numbers (variables) with given numerical values and then evaluating the expression using standard arithmetic operations. This transforms a general algebraic formula into a specific numerical quantity based on given conditions.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can replace variables with given positive or negative integers, fractions, or decimals in algebraic expressions, correctly apply order of operations (BODMAS), handle bracket expansions involving coefficients, and evaluate the final numerical result accurately in real-world contexts.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the replacement of single and multiple variables in linear and simple quadratic/polynomial algebraic expressions using integers, fractions, and decimals. It does NOT cover solving algebraic equations or advanced calculus concepts.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are running a school canteen in Delhi, and the cost of buying $x$ samosas at ₹12 each and $y$ juice boxes at ₹15 each is given by the algebraic expression $12x + 15y$. If a customer orders 4 samosas and 3 juice boxes, how do you find the total bill? You begin by writing down the algebraic expression and the specific values given: $x = 4$ and $y = 3$. Next, you connect this to our prior understanding of arithmetic expressions with variables and operations on integers. You substitute the numbers in place of the letters, making sure to use brackets when multiplying coefficients by negative numbers or fractions. Finally, you evaluate the expression following BODMAS rules to arrive at the total cost in rupees.

**Takeaway:** substitution replaces each variable with its given value, then evaluates under BODMAS. **In the exam:** bracket every substituted value, especially negatives, so exponents and coefficients keep their separate jobs. **Check yourself:** evaluate the canteen bill for the customer's order, then for one samosa fewer, and watch how each sign survives the trip. **Why students miss it:** they drop brackets around negative substitutions, or let an exponent quietly swallow the coefficient sitting beside it.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What does the term "substitution" mean when working with an algebraic expression like $5x - 3$ given $x = 4$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Substitution means replacing the variable $x$ with the given number $4$ so that the expression becomes $5(4) - 3$, which evaluates to $17$. A wrong answer usually reveals that the student confuses substitution with solving an equation or thinks the variable should be eliminated without multiplying by its coefficient.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
When evaluating the expression $-2x^2$ for $x = -3$, a student calculates $-2(-3)^2 = -2(9) = -18$. Another student calculates $(-2(-3))^2 = (6)^2 = 36$. Which student is correct and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The first student is correct because exponents apply only to the variable $x$ unless parentheses explicitly group the coefficient and variable together. A wrong answer shows a misunderstanding of the order of operations regarding exponents and coefficients.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Evaluate the algebraic expression $3a^2 - 5ab + 2b$ when $a = 2$ and $b = -3$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
First, substitute $a = 2$ and $b = -3$ into the expression: $3(2)^2 - 5(2)(-3) + 2(-3)$. Calculate the exponent: $(2)^2 = 4$, so $3(4) = 12$. Next, compute the middle term: $-5 \times 2 \times (-3) = +30$. Then, compute the final term: $2 \times (-3) = -6$. Combine them: $12 + 30 + (-6) = 36$. A wrong answer often stems from mishandling negative signs during multiplication of the middle term.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If substituting $x = 5$ into the expression $ax + 7$ yields a result of $27$, how can you find the value of the constant $a$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Substitute $x = 5$ into the expression to get $a(5) + 7 = 27$. Then subtract 7 from both sides to get $5a = 20$, and divide by 5 to find $a = 4$. A wrong answer reveals a confusion between evaluating an expression for a known variable versus finding an unknown coefficient using a known result.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create an algebraic expression with two variables, $p$ and $q$, and choose integer values for them such that the final evaluated result of the expression is $-15$. Show your expression, chosen values, and step-by-step substitution.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An example acceptable answer is the expression $2p - 3q$ with $p = 3$ and $q = 7$. Substituting gives $2(3) - 3(7) = 6 - 21 = -15$. A poor answer would show incorrect substitution, failure to use integers properly, or arithmetic errors in the final calculation.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently omit multiplication signs or parentheses when substituting negative numbers or coefficients. For example, when evaluating $5 - 2x$ for $x = -3$, students often write $5 - 2-3$ instead of $5 - 2(-3)$, leading them to subtract instead of multiplying $-2$ by $-3$ to get $+6$.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to always put parentheses around substituted numbers, especially negative numbers, fractions, or multi-digit numbers attached to a coefficient. Write out the substitution explicitly: replace every instance of the letter with an empty bracket first, like $5 - 2(\quad)$, and then drop the number $-3$ inside the bracket before performing any arithmetic.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A school store sells notebooks for ₹25 each and pens for ₹10 each. The total cost for $n$ notebooks and $p$ pens is given by the algebraic expression $25n + 10p + 15$ (where ₹15 is a delivery charge). Find the total cost if a student buys 4 notebooks and 6 pens.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given algebraic expression and the assigned values for the variables: Expression is $25n + 10p + 15$, with $n = 4$ and $p = 6$.
- Step 2: Rewrite the expression by replacing each variable with its corresponding numerical value inside parentheses: $25(4) + 10(6) + 15$.
- Step 3: Perform the multiplication operations first according to BODMAS: $25 \times 4 = 100$ and $10 \times 6 = 60$.
- Step 4: Substitute these products back into the expression: $100 + 60 + 15$.
- Step 5: Perform the addition and include the correct currency unit: $100 + 60 + 15 = 175$ rupees.
REASONING: Substitution replaces abstract letters with concrete numbers, turning an algebraic rule into a specific arithmetic calculation. Following BODMAS ensures multiplication is done before addition to avoid calculation errors.
ANSWER: ₹175
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: The perimeter of a triangular garden is represented by the algebraic expression $x + 2y + 3z$. Find the perimeter when $x = 5.5$ metres, $y = 3.2$ metres, and $z = 4.1$ metres.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given expression and values: $x + 2y + 3z$ where $x = 5.5$, $y = 3.2$, and $z = 4.1$.
- Step 2: Substitute the decimal values into the expression using multiplication for coefficients: $5.5 + 2(3.2) + 3(4.1)$.
- Step 3: Evaluate the multiplication terms: $2 \times 3.2 = 6.4$ and $3 \times 4.1 = 12.3$.
- Step 4: Rewrite the expression with the evaluated products: $5.5 + 6.4 + 12.3$.
- Step 5: Add all decimal values together and append units: $5.5 + 6.4 = 11.9$; $11.9 + 12.3 = 24.2$ metres.
REASONING: Decimal substitution requires careful alignment of decimal places during addition and accurate place-value multiplication for coefficients.
ANSWER: 24.2 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Evaluate the complex algebraic expression $-4x^2 + 5xy - 3y$ when $x = -2$ and $y = 3$.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write down the expression and identify values: $-4x^2 + 5xy - 3y$ with $x = -2$ and $y = 3$.
- Step 2: Substitute the values carefully with parentheses: $-4(-2)^2 + 5(-2)(3) - 3(3)$.
- Step 3: Evaluate the exponent first in the first term: $(-2)^2 = (-2) \times (-2) = 4$, so the term becomes $-4(4) = -16$.
- Step 4: Evaluate the second term by multiplying numbers: $5 \times (-2) \times 3 = -30$.
- Step 5: Evaluate the third term: $-3 \times 3 = -9$.
- Step 6: Combine all evaluated terms respecting integer signs: $-16 + (-30) - 9 = -46 - 9 = -55$.
REASONING: Exponents must be calculated before multiplying by coefficients, and negative signs must be rigorously tracked through multiplication and addition of integers.
ANSWER: -55
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student evaluates the expression $10 - 3x$ for $x = -4$ and gets the answer $-2$. Show the correct step-by-step substitution and evaluation, identify the exact error the student likely made, and state the correct final answer.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction in algebraic substitution
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write the expression and the given value: $10 - 3x$ for $x = -4$.
- Step 2: Substitute $x = -4$ with parentheses: $10 - 3(-4)$.
- Step 3: Multiply $-3$ by $-4$ to get $+12$ (recognizing that negative times negative is positive).
- Step 4: Add the results: $10 + 12 = 22$.
- Step 5: Conclude that the student likely forgot to multiply the negative sign of the coefficient with the negative value of $x$, mistakenly computing $10 - 12 = -2$ or ignoring signs.
ANSWER: The correct answer is 22. The student likely dropped the negative sign on $x$ or treated $-3(-4)$ as $-12$.
