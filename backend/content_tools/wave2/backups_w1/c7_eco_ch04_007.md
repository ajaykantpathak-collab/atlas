## ELI5
Borrow a rupee, rent a rupee, lend a rupee - interest is the rent on money. Aarav borrows a small sum for a delivery bicycle and pays for the privilege; his cousin leaves her savings untouched and collects the same rent from the other side. Expressed as a percentage of the principal over time, interest is at once the borrower's cost and the saver's reward - one price, two pockets.

## DEFINITION
Interest is the price paid for borrowing money (the cost of borrowing) and the payment earned for depositing or lending money (the reward for saving), usually expressed as a percentage of the principal amount over a specific time period. It reflects the time value of money and the risk associated with lending.

## WALKTHROUGH
Aarav wants to buy a bicycle costing ₹5,000 for his delivery job, but he only has ₹3,000 in his savings account. He decides to borrow the remaining ₹2,000 from a local bank for one year, while his cousin Riya leaves ₹3,000 untouched in her savings account to earn extra money over the year. To understand what happens next, observe that money has a time value because the bank and savers give up the immediate use of their funds. Building on how banks accept deposits and provide loans, we see that the bank charges Aarav an extra amount for using its money, while it rewards Riya for keeping her money safe with them. This extra percentage payment is known as interest, functioning simultaneously as a cost for the borrower and a reward for the saver.

**Takeaway:** interest is the rent on money: a percentage of the principal over time - cost to the borrower, reward to the saver. **In the exam:** in calculation questions, write principal, rate and time separately, then let the simple-interest formula do one clean job. **Check yourself:** split the walkthrough's siblings - Aarav's borrowed bicycle fund against Riya's untouched savings - and watch one price hit two pockets. **Why students miss it:** students treat interest as a flat fee - it grows with time, and every borrowing is a yes that quietly says no to a slice of future income.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Aarav deposits ₹10,000 in a bank account that offers a simple interest rate of 5% per year. How much total money will Aarav have in his account after 2 years?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The total money is ₹11,000. Step 1: Identify the principal (P = ₹10,000), rate (R = 5% per year), and time (T = 2 years). Step 2: Use the simple interest formula: $SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$. Step 3: Calculate interest: $SI = \frac{10000 \times 5 \times 2}{100} = ₹1,000$. Step 4: Add interest to the principal: Total Amount = Principal + Interest = ₹10,000 + ₹1,000 = ₹11,000. A wrong answer indicates an error in calculating percentages or forgetting to add the interest back to the principal.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that interest is a fixed fee charged once by a bank for the service of handling cash, rather than a percentage fee that grows over time based on how long the money is borrowed or saved. For example, a student might believe borrowing ₹5,000 at 5% means paying exactly ₹5 once, rather than paying ₹250 per year for as long as the loan remains unpaid.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Meera deposits ₹8,000 in a savings account at a local cooperative bank that offers a simple interest rate of 6% per year. How much total interest will Meera earn after 3 years?
STEPS:
- Identify the given variables: Principal $P = ₹8,000$, Rate $R = 6\%$, Time $T = 3 \text{ years}$.
- Recall the simple interest formula: $SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$.
- Substitute the given values into the formula: $SI = \frac{8000 \times 6 \times 3}{100}$.
- Perform the multiplication in the numerator: $8000 \times 6 = 48000$, then $48000 \times 3 = 144000$.
- Divide by 100 to calculate the final interest: $SI = \frac{144000}{100} = ₹1,440$.
REASONING: The formula multiplies the principal by the annual percentage rate and the number of years to find the total reward earned by the saver over time. Dividing by 100 converts the percentage rate into a proper decimal multiplier.
ANSWER: ₹1,440 total interest earned
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Rohan takes a short-term loan of ₹15,000 from a bank to purchase seeds and fertilizers for his farm, at a simple interest rate of 8% per year. He repays the full amount after 2 years. What is the total amount Rohan must pay back to the bank?
STEPS:
- Identify the given variables: Principal $P = ₹15,000$, Rate $R = 8\%$, Time $T = 2 \text{ years}$.
- Recall the simple interest formula: $SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$.
- Substitute the values: $SI = \frac{15000 \times 8 \times 2}{100}$.
- Calculate the interest: $15000 \times 16 = 240000$, then $SI = \frac{240000}{100} = ₹2,400$.
- Calculate the total repayment amount: Total Amount = Principal + Simple Interest = ₹15,000 + ₹2,400 = ₹17,400.
REASONING: When borrowing money, the borrower must return not only the original principal borrowed but also the cost of borrowing (the interest) accumulated over the loan duration.
ANSWER: ₹17,400 total repayment amount
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Priya wants to compare two bank offers for investing ₹20,000 for 3 years. Bank A offers a simple interest rate of 5% per year. Bank B offers a simple interest rate of 6% per year, but charges an annual account maintenance fee of ₹200. Which bank gives Priya a better net return after 3 years?
STEPS:
- Calculate total interest for Bank A: $P = 20000, R = 5, T = 3$. $SI_A = \frac{20000 \times 5 \times 3}{100} = \frac{300000}{100} = ₹3,000$.
- Calculate total interest for Bank B: $P = 20000, R = 6, T = 3$. $SI_B = \frac{20000 \times 6 \times 3}{100} = \frac{360000}{100} = ₹3,600$.
- Calculate total fee for Bank B over 3 years: $\text{Fee} = ₹200 \times 3 = ₹600$.
- Calculate net return for Bank B: Net Return = Total Interest - Total Fee = ₹3,600 - ₹6,000? Wait, ₹3,600 - ₹600 = ₹3,000.
- Compare net returns: Bank A net return = ₹3,000. Bank B net return = ₹3,000. Both options yield the same net financial benefit.
REASONING: Comparing financial products requires looking beyond headline interest rates to account for associated fees and costs that reduce the actual reward earned by the saver.
ANSWER: Both banks give an identical net return of ₹3,000 after 3 years.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Ananya saves ₹10,000 in a fixed deposit yielding 7% simple interest per year for 4 years. Her friend Kabir borrows ₹10,000 from a commercial bank at 10% simple interest per year for 4 years. Explain why there is a difference between the interest Ananya earns and the interest Kabir pays, even though both amounts and time periods are identical.
COMPETENCY: Analyzing financial differences between saving and borrowing
STEPS:
- Understand that Ananya is a saver receiving a reward, while Kabir is a borrower paying a cost.
- Identify that banks pay lower interest rates to depositors to attract funds and charge higher interest rates to borrowers to cover operational expenses and risk.
- Calculate Ananya's earnings: $\frac{10000 \times 7 \times 4}{100} = ₹2,800$.
- Calculate Kabir's payment: $\frac{10000 \times 10 \times 4}{100} = ₹4,000$.
- Conclude that the interest rate spread allows the bank to function profitably as an intermediary.
ANSWER: Ananya earns ₹2,800 while Kabir pays ₹4,000 because banks set deposit rates lower than lending rates to cover their operating costs and earn a profit margin (interest spread).
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A local weaver needs ₹50,000 to buy a new handloom. Bank X offers a loan at 8% simple interest per annum for 3 years. Informal moneylender Y offers a loan of ₹50,000 charging ₹5,000 as fixed interest every year for 3 years. Calculate the total interest paid under both options and advise the weaver on which option is cheaper.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating and comparing credit costs from formal and informal sources
STEPS:
- Calculate total interest for Bank X: $P = 50000, R = 8, T = 3$. $SI_X = \frac{50000 \times 8 \times 3}{100} = \frac{1200000}{100} = ₹12,000$.
- Calculate total interest for Moneylender Y: Fixed interest per year is ₹5,000 for 3 years. Total interest = ₹5,000 × 3 = ₹15,000.
- Compare total costs: Bank X total interest is ₹12,000; Moneylender Y total interest is ₹15,000.
- Evaluate accessibility versus cost: Conclude that Bank X is cheaper by ₹3,000.
ANSWER: Bank X total interest is ₹12,000 ($₹50000 \times 8 \times 3 / 100$), whereas Moneylender Y total interest is ₹15,000 ($₹5,000 \times 3$). Bank X is cheaper and safer for the weaver.
DIFFICULTY: hard
