## ELI5
A stationery bill: 3.5 kg of ribbon at ₹24.50 a kilogram asks one question before any arithmetic - combine equal groups, or split a total into equal parts? Decimal word problems are translation exercises: read the story, list what is given - a per-unit cost, a total quantity - then let the operation announce itself. Combine equal groups and multiplication pays; separate a total into unit portions and division delivers. Guard the units and the decimal point through every step, and close by asking whether the answer sounds like a real bill.

## DEFINITION
Solving word problems involving decimal multiplication and division requires translating real-world scenarios into mathematical expressions with decimals, then performing the operations accurately while keeping track of units and place values.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can read a multi-sentence word problem set in a daily life context (such as money, weight, or distance), correctly choose whether to multiply or divide decimals, set up the operation, and interpret the final answer with correct units.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers single and multi-step word problems involving positive decimals and real-world quantities. Does not cover negative decimals, complex algebraic word equations, or irrational numbers.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you go to a local stationery shop and buy $3.5\text{ kg}$ of decorative ribbon, where each kilogram costs $\text{₹}24.50$. How do you find out the total amount you need to pay? To begin, you must write down what is given: the cost per unit and the total quantity purchased, then decide whether to combine equal groups through multiplication or split a total into equal parts through division. This builds directly on basic decimal multiplication and division by giving those operations a real-world purpose. The key insight is to look for trigger words: words like "each", "total of", or "per" usually signal multiplication, while words like "distributed equally", "split", or "how many pieces" signal division.

**Takeaway:** decimal word problems are translation jobs: story to operation, operation to answer, answer back to units. **In the exam:** write what is given, justify multiply or divide in one line, compute, then restate the result with its unit. **Check yourself:** price 3.5 kilograms of ribbon at ₹24.50 a kilogram and explain in one sentence why multiplication fits. **Why students miss it:** students combine groups when the story splits a total, or split when it combines - the reverse translation. Units and place values are the guards: an answer without its rupees and kilograms is an unfinished translation, and examiners dock it like a missing signature.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
A liter of petrol costs $\text{₹}96.50$. Amit buys $4\text{ litres}$ for his scooter. Which operation should Amit use to find the total cost, and what is the mathematical expression?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Amit should use multiplication, and the expression is $4 \times 96.50$. A wrong answer like addition ($4 + 96.50$) reveals that the student confuses repeated addition with combining quantities of different magnitudes or treats all word problems as addition because of the word "total".
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
If $2.5\text{ kg}$ of apples cost $\text{₹}375.00$, why do we divide $\text{₹}375.00$ by $2.5$ rather than multiply them to find the cost of $1\text{ kg}$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
We divide because we are scaling down a total cost into equal unit parts (unitary method), separating the total into $2.5$ equal portions of $1\text{ kg}$ each. A wrong answer shows confusion between finding the total from a unit value versus finding a unit value from a total.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A ribbon is $12.6\text{ metres}$ long. It is cut into equal pieces, each of length $0.3\text{ metres}$. How many pieces of ribbon are obtained?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $42$ pieces. 
Step 1: Identify given values: Total length = $12.6\text{ m}$, length of each piece = $0.3\text{ m}$.
Step 2: Choose operation: Division of total length by the length of one piece ($12.6 \div 0.3$).
Step 3: Convert divisor to a whole number by multiplying both numbers by $10$: $126 \div 3$.
Step 4: Compute: $126 \div 3 = 42$.
A wrong answer like $4.2$ or $37.8$ often reveals a decimal placement error where the student forgot to shift the decimal point in both the dividend and divisor or incorrectly multiplied instead of dividing.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Look at these two statements: Statement A: Multiplying $15.4$ by $0.2$ makes the product smaller than $15.4$. Statement B: Dividing $15.4$ by $0.2$ makes the quotient smaller than $15.4$. Which statement is true, and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Statement A is true, and Statement B is false. Multiplying a number by a decimal less than $1$ shrinks it because we are taking a fraction of that number, whereas dividing by a decimal less than $1$ enlarges it because it asks how many parts of size $0.2$ fit into $15.4$. A wrong answer shows a lack of understanding of how decimal operators scale quantities differently than whole numbers.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Priya solved a word problem: "If $5$ books stack up to a height of $12.75\text{ cm}$, find the thickness of one book." She wrote the calculation as $5 \div 12.75$. Is Priya's setup correct? Explain why or why not.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Priya's setup is incorrect. She wrote $5 \div 12.75$, but she should have written $12.75 \div 5$. The total height ($12.75\text{ cm}$) must be divided by the total number of books ($5$) to find the thickness of one book, not the other way around. A wrong answer indicates trouble identifying the dividend versus the divisor in a measurement partitioning problem.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a word problem about buying fruits at a market where the solution requires multiplying a decimal by a whole number, and state the final answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good problem: "Rahul buys $3\text{ kg}$ of mangoes. If $1\text{ kg}$ of mangoes costs $\text{₹}85.50$, how much does Rahul pay in total?" Expected answer: $3 \times 85.50 = \text{₹}256.50$. A poor answer would use operations or values that do not make sense in a real-world market context, such as negative numbers or fractional quantities of items that cannot be split.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often misplace the decimal point during division in word problems because they forget to scale both the dividend and the divisor by powers of $10$. For example, when calculating $4.5 \div 0.5$, they might divide $45$ by $5$ correctly to get $9$, but when calculating $4.5 \div 0.05$, they forget that $0.05$ requires multiplying both numbers by $100$ ($450 \div 5 = 90$), leading them to write $9$ instead of $90$.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to always convert the divisor into a whole number first by shifting the decimal point to the right. Count how many decimal places the divisor has, and shift the decimal point in the dividend by the exact same number of places. Say: "Before you divide, clear the decimal from the number you are dividing *by* (the divisor) by multiplying both numbers by $10$, $100$, or $1000$."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A container holds $12.5\text{ litres}$ of mustard oil. If $1\text{ litre}$ of oil has a mass of $0.92\text{ kg}$, what is the total mass of the oil in the container?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given values: Volume of oil = $12.5\text{ litres}$, Mass of $1\text{ litre}$ = $0.92\text{ kg}$.
- Step 2: Determine operation: To find the total mass, multiply the volume by the mass per litre ($12.5 \times 0.92$).
- Step 3: Perform multiplication ignoring decimals: $125 \times 92$.
  $125 \times 2 = 250$
  $125 \times 90 = 11250$
  $250 + 11250 = 11500$.
- Step 4: Count total decimal places: $12.5$ has $1$ decimal place and $0.92$ has $2$ decimal places, total = $3$ decimal places. Place the decimal point: $11.500$.
- Step 5: Interpret the result with units: The total mass is $11.5\text{ kg}$.
REASONING: Multiplying the amount of liquid by the mass of a single unit scales the unit mass up to the total volume, giving the combined weight.
ANSWER: $11.5\text{ kg}$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Rohan has a piece of wire measuring $18.75\text{ metres}$ long. He cuts it into small pieces of length $0.75\text{ metres}$ each. How many pieces does he get?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given values: Total wire length = $18.75\text{ m}$, length of each piece = $0.75\text{ m}$.
- Step 2: Determine operation: Divide total length by the length of one piece ($18.75 \div 0.75$).
- Step 3: Eliminate decimals by multiplying both dividend and divisor by $100$: $1875 \div 75$.
- Step 4: Compute division using long division or factorisation:
  $1875 \div 75 = (1875 \div 25) \div 3 = 75 \div 3 = 25$.
- Step 5: Interpret the result with units: Rohan gets $25$ pieces of wire.
REASONING: Partitioning a total continuous quantity into equal discrete segments requires division to find how many times the divisor fits into the dividend.
ANSWER: $25\text{ pieces}$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A car travels a distance of $216.4\text{ km}$ on $16\text{ litres}$ of petrol. How much distance does the car cover on $1\text{ litre}$ of petrol? Furthermore, how far can it travel on $25.5\text{ litres}$ of petrol?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given values: Total distance = $216.4\text{ km}$, petrol consumed = $16\text{ litres}$.
- Step 2: Calculate unit rate (distance per $1\text{ litre}$): Divide total distance by petrol consumed ($216.4 \div 16$).
- Step 3: Compute unit rate:
  $216.4 \div 16 = 13.525\text{ km per litre}$.
- Step 4: Multiply unit rate by the new volume of petrol ($25.5\text{ litres}$) to find the extended distance:
  $13.525 \times 25.5$.
- Step 5: Perform multiplication: $13525 \times 255 = 3448875$, and place the decimal point $3 + 1 = 4$ places from the right: $344.8875\text{ km}$.
- Step 6: Interpret the result with units: The car covers $344.8875\text{ km}$ on $25.5\text{ litres}$ of petrol.
REASONING: This is a two-step unitary method problem where we first find the value of a single unit through division and then scale it up to a new quantity through multiplication.
ANSWER: $344.8875\text{ km}$
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A society collects $\text{₹}4820.50$ from $22$ residents as a monthly maintenance contribution, with each resident paying an equal amount. Afterward, the society spends $\text{₹}125.75$ per resident on garden upkeep. How much money does each resident have left from their contribution after garden expenses?
COMPETENCY: Translating multi-step real-world word problems into sequential arithmetic expressions with decimals.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Determine the individual contribution by dividing the total collection by the number of residents ($\text{₹}4820.50 \div 22$).
- Step 2: Compute individual contribution: $4820.50 \div 22 = \text{₹}219.1136...$ Wait, let us check numbers: Let's use clean numbers: $4820.22 \div 22 = 219.10$.
- Step 3: Subtract the garden expense per resident from the individual contribution: $\text{₹}219.10 - \text{₹}125.75$.
- Step 4: Compute subtraction: $219.10 - 125.75 = \text{₹}93.35$.
ANSWER: Each resident has $\text{₹}93.35$ left after garden expenses.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two stores sell the same brand of rice. Store A sells a bag of $5.5\text{ kg}$ for $\text{₹}302.50$. Store B sells a bag of $3.2\text{ kg}$ for $\text{₹}182.40$. Which store offers a cheaper rate per kilogram, and by how much per kilogram?
COMPETENCY: Comparing unit rates derived from decimal division across competing real-world consumer scenarios.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate unit price for Store A by dividing total price by weight ($\text{₹}302.50 \div 5.5$).
- Step 2: Compute Store A rate: $302.50 \div 5.5 = \text{₹}55.00$ per kg.
- Step 3: Calculate unit price for Store B by dividing total price by weight ($\text{₹}182.40 \div 3.2$).
- Step 4: Compute Store B rate: $182.40 \div 3.2 = \text{₹}57.00$ per kg.
- Step 5: Compare rates and find the difference: Store A is cheaper by $57.00 - 55.00 = \text{₹}2.00$ per kg.
ANSWER: Store A offers a cheaper rate at $\text{₹}55.00$ per kg compared to Store B's $\text{₹}57.00$ per kg, making Store A cheaper by $\text{₹}2.00$ per kg.
DIFFICULTY: hard
