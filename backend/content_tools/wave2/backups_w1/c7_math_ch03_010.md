## ELI5
The market scale prints 3.75 kg of potatoes; the recipe book asks for three and three-quarter kilograms - the same potatoes wearing two costumes. Translating is mechanical: the digits after the point become a fraction over the matching power of ten - two decimal digits mean over a hundred - then simplify by common factors. Going the other way, a fraction whose bottom is ten, hundred or thousand simply drops its parts after the point. Same value, different dress; pick whichever the question demands.

## DEFINITION
Representing decimals as fractions and vice versa is the mathematical process of converting numbers written in decimal notation (using a decimal point) into fractions (with a numerator and a denominator that is a power of 10) and the reverse, maintaining the exact same numerical value.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can convert any terminating decimal to a simplified fraction in lowest terms, and convert any fraction with a denominator that is a power of 10 (or convertible to one) into a precise decimal number.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers finite/terminating decimals and their equivalent fractions, and fractions with denominators that are powers of 10 (10, 100, 1000, etc.). Does NOT cover infinite or non-terminating recurring decimals (which belong to higher classes).
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at a vegetable market in India, and you buy 3.75 kg of potatoes. The digital weighing scale shows a decimal, but your recipe book writes quantities as fractions, like $3 \frac{3}{4}$ kg. To figure out if you bought the right amount, you need a way to translate between decimals and fractions. We begin by looking at the place value of the digits after the decimal point—tenths, hundredths, and thousandths—which correspond to denominators of 10, 100, and 1000 respectively. Recalling our work with fractions and place value from Class 6, we know that a number like 0.75 is read as seventy-five hundredths. The key insight is that every decimal is simply a fraction in disguise, waiting to be written with a power-of-10 denominator and then simplified!

**Takeaway:** decimals and fractions are two costumes of one value: decimal digits name the power-of-ten denominator, then simplify. **In the exam:** convert by counting decimal places for the denominator; reverse by writing the numerator over the power of ten. **Check yourself:** translate the potato weight into a mixed fraction and back, and confirm nothing changed. **Why students miss it:** they forget to simplify or miscount places, turning hundredths into tenths with one slip.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the decimal representation of the fraction $\frac{45}{100}$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is 0.45. If a student answers 4.5 or 0.045, it reveals a fundamental confusion about how the number of zeros in the denominator of a fraction corresponds to the number of decimal places after the point. They are failing to count the place values correctly from right to left.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why the decimal 0.6 is equivalent to the fraction $\frac{3}{5}$ instead of just $\frac{6}{10}$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct answer is that 0.6 is initially written as $\frac{6}{10}$, but both the numerator and denominator can be divided by their greatest common divisor, which is 2, resulting in the simplified equivalent fraction $\frac{3}{5}$. If a student believes they are different numbers, they do not understand that fractions can be reduced to simplest form while retaining their exact value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Convert the mixed decimal number $4.125$ into a fraction in its simplest form. Show your working.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $4 \frac{1}{8}$ or $\frac{33}{8}$. 
Working: 
1. Separate the whole number and decimal part: $4 + 0.125$.
2. Write the decimal as a fraction: $0.125 = \frac{125}{1000}$.
3. Simplify the fraction by dividing numerator and denominator by 125: $\frac{125 \div 125}{1000 \div 125} = \frac{1}{8}$.
4. Combine with the whole number: $4 \frac{1}{8}$, or convert to an improper fraction $\frac{4 \times 8 + 1}{8} = \frac{33}{8}$. 
If a student writes $\frac{125}{100}$ or fails to simplify, it shows they miscounted the thousandths place or skipped the reduction step.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the fraction $\frac{3}{4}$ and the decimal $0.7$. Without converting both to decimals or both to fractions, explain a method to determine which one is larger.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The correct answer is to convert $\frac{3}{4}$ to a decimal ($0.75$) by multiplying numerator and denominator by 25 to get $\frac{75}{100}$, or convert $0.7$ to a fraction ($\frac{7}{10} = \frac{70}{100}$). Comparing $0.75$ and $0.70$ shows $0.75$ is larger, so $\frac{3}{4} > 0.7$. If a student incorrectly assumes $0.7$ is larger because 7 is greater than 3 or 4, they are ignoring place value positions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student converts $\frac{3}{8}$ to a decimal by writing it as $\frac{3 \times 125}{8 \times 125} = \frac{375}{1000} = 0.375$. Another student claims you can just divide 3 by 8 using long division. Evaluate both methods. Are they both correct?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Both methods are completely correct and mathematically sound. The first method uses equivalent fractions by finding a power of 10 in the denominator ($8 \times 125 = 1000$), while the second method uses fraction-as-division ($3 \div 8 = 0.375$). If a student claims one method is "wrong," they lack flexibility in mathematical approaches and do not see the connection between division and fractions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a decimal number between $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{3}{5}$ and write it both as a decimal and as a simplified fraction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
An acceptable answer is $0.55$ (or $\frac{55}{100} = \frac{11}{20}$), because $\frac{1}{2} = 0.50$ and $\frac{3}{5} = \frac{6}{10} = 0.60$, and $0.55$ lies strictly between them. A poor answer would choose a number outside this range (like $0.45$ or $0.65$), showing a failure to properly compare fractions and decimals on a number line.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often drop trailing zeros or misalign decimal places when converting fractions with powers of 10, or they write the decimal digits directly as the numerator without matching the place value denominator. For example, when converting $0.07$ to a fraction, students frequently write $\frac{7}{10}$ instead of $\frac{7}{100}$ because they ignore the zero placeholder in the tenths place.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student makes this error, guide them back to place value charts. Say: "Let's read the decimal aloud using its place value name. Is 0.07 read as 'seven-tenths' or 'seven-hundredths'?" Once they answer 'seven-hundredths', ask: "What number represents hundredths in the denominator of a fraction?" They will say 100, leading them naturally to write $\frac{7}{100}$.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Rahul bought 2.65 litres of milk for his family. Express this quantity as a mixed fraction in its simplest form.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Separate the whole number part and the decimal part from the given number: $2.65 = 2 + 0.65$.
- Step 2: Write the decimal part $0.65$ as a fraction by looking at its place value. Since the last digit is in the hundredths place, the denominator is 100: $0.65 = \frac{65}{100}$.
- Step 3: Simplify the fraction $\frac{65}{100}$ by finding the greatest common divisor (GCD) of 65 and 100, which is 5. Divide both numerator and denominator by 5: $\frac{65 \div 5}{100 \div 5} = \frac{13}{20}$.
- Step 4: Combine the whole number with the simplified fraction to write the final mixed fraction: $2 \frac{13}{20}$.
REASONING: Separating the whole number preserves its value while we convert only the decimal part. Converting the decimal to a fraction with a power of 10 denominator ($\frac{65}{100}$) and then dividing by the common factor ensures the fraction is in its lowest terms, satisfying standard mathematical convention.
ANSWER: $2 \frac{13}{20}$ litres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Priya has a ribbon of length $\frac{7}{8}$ metres. Convert this fraction into a decimal number using equivalent fractions.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given fraction: $\frac{7}{8}$. We need to find a number to multiply the denominator 8 by to get a power of 10 (10, 100, 1000, etc.).
- Step 2: Test powers of 10. Since 10 and 100 are not divisible by 8, check 1000. Divide 1000 by 8 to find the multiplier: $1000 \div 8 = 125$.
- Step 3: Multiply both the numerator and the denominator by 125 to maintain equivalence: $\frac{7 \times 125}{8 \times 125} = \frac{875}{1000}$.
- Step 4: Convert the fraction with a denominator of 1000 into decimal notation by placing the decimal point three places from the right: $0.875$.
REASONING: Multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same number (125) creates an equivalent fraction with a power-of-10 denominator, which translates directly into decimal place values (thousandths).
ANSWER: $0.875$ metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A running track is divided into segments. Amit ran $1 \frac{3}{5}$ km, and Sneha ran $1.58$ km. Who ran a greater distance, and by how much (expressed as a decimal)?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Convert Amit's distance from a mixed fraction ($1 \frac{3}{5}$) into a decimal. First, convert the fraction $\frac{3}{5}$ to a denominator of 10 by multiplying numerator and denominator by 2: $\frac{3 \times 2}{5 \times 2} = \frac{6}{10} = 0.6$.
- Step 2: Add the whole number to get Amit's total distance in decimal form: $1 + 0.6 = 1.60$ km.
- Step 3: Write down Sneha's distance in decimal form for comparison: $1.58$ km.
- Step 4: Compare the two decimals place by place. Both have 1 as the whole number. In the tenths place, Amit has 6 and Sneha has 5. Since $6 > 5$, Amit ran a greater distance ($1.60 > 1.58$).
- Step 5: Find the difference by subtracting Sneha's distance from Amit's distance: $1.60 - 1.58 = 0.02$ km.
REASONING: To compare fractions and decimals directly, it is easiest to convert all quantities into the same format (decimals). Aligning place values allows us to determine both the larger quantity and the exact difference through standard subtraction.
ANSWER: Amit ran a greater distance by $0.02$ km.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A local artisan needs to cut strips of wood. He has three pieces measuring $\frac{17}{20}$ m, $0.82$ m, and $\frac{4}{5}$ m. Arrange these lengths in ascending order, showing your conversion steps.
COMPETENCY: Translating and comparing mixed representations (fractions and decimals)
STEPS:
- Step 1: Choose a common representation format (convert all to decimals or all to fractions). Let's convert all to decimals.
- Step 2: Convert $\frac{17}{20}$ to a decimal by multiplying numerator and denominator by 5: $\frac{17 \times 5}{20 \times 5} = \frac{85}{100} = 0.85$.
- Step 3: Keep the second measurement as it is: $0.82$ m.
- Step 4: Convert $\frac{4}{5}$ to a decimal by multiplying numerator and denominator by 2: $\frac{4 \times 2}{5 \times 2} = \frac{8}{10} = 0.80$ m.
- Step 5: Compare the decimal values ($0.85, 0.82, 0.80$) and arrange them from smallest to largest: $0.80 < 0.82 < 0.85$. Translate back to original forms: $\frac{4}{5}$ m, $0.82$ m, $\frac{17}{20}$ m.
ANSWER: $\frac{4}{5}$ m, $0.82$ m, $\frac{17}{20}$ m. Explanation: Converting all numbers to decimals with two decimal places ($0.80, 0.82, 0.85$) makes direct comparison straightforward.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students are solving the problem: "Convert $3.045$ into a fraction in lowest terms." 
Student A writes: $3 \frac{45}{100} = 3 \frac{9}{20}$. 
Student B writes: $\frac{3045}{1000} = \frac{609}{200}$. 
Evaluate both students' methods and answers. Who is correct, and what error did the other student make?
COMPETENCY: Error identification, correction, and justification in decimal-fraction conversions
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the given decimal number: $3.045$. Note that the digits after the decimal point are in the tenths (0), hundredths (4), and thousandths (5) places.
- Step 2: Evaluate Student A's work. Student A wrote $\frac{45}{100}$, ignoring the fact that the 5 is in the thousandths place (meaning the fractional part should be $\frac{45}{1000}$). Therefore, Student A's fraction is incorrect.
- Step 3: Evaluate Student B's work. Student B correctly placed 3045 over 1000 because the number extends to the thousandths place ($\frac{3045}{1000}$).
- Step 4: Simplify Student B's fraction by dividing both numerator and denominator by their GCD, which is 5: $3045 \div 5 = 609$ and $1000 \div 5 = 200$, yielding $\frac{609}{200}$ (or $3 \frac{9}{200}$).
- Step 5: Conclude that Student B is completely correct, while Student A made a place value error by treating thousandths as hundredths.
ANSWER: Student B is correct ($\frac{609}{200}$ or $3 \frac{9}{200}$). Student A made a place value error by using 100 as the denominator instead of 1000, failing to account for the zero in the tenths place.
DIFFICULTY: hard
