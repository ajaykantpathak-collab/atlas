## ELI5
Think of three friends sharing a jar of biscuits in the ratio 2:3:5 — that is a multi-term ratio: three or more quantities compared at once. It behaves like a two-term swap: multiply or divide every term by the same number and the sharing stays exactly as fair as before. The deal is tidiest when the terms have no common factor except one — no number can shrink all of them together. Simplify first, then scale up or down, then share: that is the whole game.

## DEFINITION
A multi-term ratio compares three or more quantities of the same kind, written as \(a:b:c:\dots\) where each term represents a relative part. The ratio remains equivalent if all terms are multiplied or divided by the same non-zero number, and it is in simplest form when the terms have no common factor other than 1 (i.e., their HCF is 1).

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine three friends — Anil, Bina, and Chirag — invest ₹12,000, ₹18,000, and ₹30,000 respectively in a small business. To decide how to share profits, we need to compare their investments as a ratio. First, write the amounts in order: 12000:18000:30000. Notice all are multiples of 1000, so divide each by 1000 to get 12:18:30. Now find the HCF of 12, 18, and 30 (which is 6) and divide each term by 6 to obtain the simplest ratio 2:3:5. This simplified ratio tells us that for every 2 parts Anil gets, Bina gets 3 and Chirag gets 5. The key insight is that a multi-term ratio is simplified by dividing **all** terms by their common factor, just like a two-term ratio, and the same scaling factor must apply to every term.

Takeaway: treat a three-term ratio exactly like a two-term one — divide every term by the common factor to simplify, multiply every term by the same number to scale, and never adjust one term alone. Exam format: expect 'simplify a:b:c' and 'divide a total in the given ratio' marks; show the common-factor step explicitly. Verification beat: after simplifying, re-check that every pair inside the ratio (first to second, second to third) still holds its original value. Why students miss it: they simplify one term only — one shrunk term quietly breaks every pair in the ratio at once.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The simplest form is 2:3:5. A wrong answer like 12:18:30 (dividing only by 2) shows the student does not realise that simplification requires dividing by the **highest** common factor of all three terms, not just any common factor. ## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
A student simplifies 15:25:35 to 3:5:7 by dividing each term by 5. Another student says, "You can also divide by 10 to get 1.5:2.5:3.5, so that's also a correct simplified ratio." Is the second student correct? Explain why or why not.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The second student is incorrect. A ratio in simplest form must have **integer** terms with no common factor other than 1. Dividing by 10 produces decimal terms (1.5, 2.5, 3.5), which is not the standard simplified form for ratios of whole numbers. A wrong agreement reveals the misconception that any common divisor yields an equally valid simplified ratio, ignoring the convention that ratios are expressed in whole numbers whenever possible.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
The ratio of red, blue, and yellow paint in a mixture is 2:3:4. If there are 18 litres of blue paint, how many litres of red and yellow paint are there?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Blue corresponds to 3 parts = 18 L → 1 part = 6 L. Red = 2 parts = 12 L, Yellow = 4 parts = 24 L. Answer: Red 12 L, Yellow 24 L. A wrong answer like Red 9 L, Yellow 18 L (using 18/2 and 18/4) shows the student incorrectly applied the unitary method by dividing the given quantity by the other ratio terms instead of first finding the value of one part.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Two multi-term ratios are given: A = 4:6:9 and B = 8:12:18. A student claims they are equivalent because each term of B is double the corresponding term of A. Another student says they are not equivalent because the ratio of the first to the second term in A is 2:3 while in B it is 4:6. Who is right and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The first student is right. Equivalent ratios are obtained by multiplying or dividing **all** terms by the same non-zero number. Doubling each term of A gives B, so they are equivalent. The second student incorrectly compares only the first two terms and notes that 2:3 and 4:6 look different, not recognising that 4:6 simplifies to 2:3. This error reveals a failure to understand that equivalence must be checked across all terms simultaneously, not pairwise.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5

A wrong answer that says "orange runs out first" might come from comparing the available quantities directly (400 vs 600) without scaling to the ratio, showing the student does not use the ratio to determine the limiting ingredient.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example problem: "Three siblings — Priya, Rohan, and Seema — inherit a piece of land worth ₹9,00,000. They agree to divide the value in the ratio of their ages: 20:25:30. How much does each get?" Solution: Simplify ratio to 4:5:6. Total parts = 15. Value per part = ₹9,00,000 ÷ 15 = ₹60,000. Priya = 4 × ₹60,000 = ₹2,40,000; Rohan = ₹3,00,000; Seema = ₹3,60,000. A poor answer would give a problem that only uses a two-term ratio or does not require sharing a total, indicating the student cannot distinguish between part-part and part-whole ratio situations.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often try to simplify a multi-term ratio by cancelling common factors between **pairs** of terms sequentially (e.g., for 12:18:30, they divide 12 and 18 by 6 to get 2:3:30, then divide 3 and 30 by 3 to get 2:1:10). This produces an incorrect ratio because the scaling factor is not applied uniformly to all three terms at the same time. The correct method is to find the HCF of **all** terms (6) and divide every term by that HCF in one step.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student simplifies pairwise, write the three terms vertically and circle the common factors of all three numbers. Ask: "What number divides 12, 18, and 30 exactly?" Guide them to find HCF = 6. Then show: 12÷6=2, 18÷6=3, 30÷6=5 → 2:3:5. Emphasise: "One division step for all terms." Practice with 2-3 more examples where pairwise cancellation gives a different (wrong) result, e.g., 8:12:20 (pairwise might give 2:3:5? Actually 8:12:20 HCF=4 → 2:3:5; pairwise 8:12 by 4 → 2:3:20 then 3:20 no common factor → wrong). Have the student verify by multiplying back: 2×4=8, 3×4=12, 5×4=20.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A school allocates ₹48,000 for prizes in three categories — Academics, Sports, and Arts — in the ratio 5:3:2. How much money does each category receive?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given ratio 5:3:2 and the total amount ₹48,000.
- Step 2: Find the total number of parts by adding the ratio terms: 5 + 3 + 2 = 10 parts.
- Step 3: Calculate the value of one part: ₹48,000 ÷ 10 = ₹4,800.
- Step 4: Multiply each ratio term by the value of one part: Academics = 5 × ₹4,800 = ₹24,000; Sports = 3 × ₹4,800 = ₹14,400; Arts = 2 × ₹4,800 = ₹9,600.
- Step 5: Verify by adding the amounts: ₹24,000 + ₹14,400 + ₹9,600 = ₹48,000. The sum matches the total.
REASONING: The ratio gives the relative share of each category. By finding the value of one part (unitary method), we convert the relative shares into absolute amounts. Adding the parts reconstructs the total, confirming the division is correct.
ANSWER: Academics ₹24,000, Sports ₹14,400, Arts ₹9,600.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2:3:4. Find the measure of each angle.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall that the sum of angles in a triangle is 180°.
- Step 2: Let the angles be 2x, 3x, and 4x (using the ratio terms multiplied by a common variable x).
- Step 3: Write the equation: 2x + 3x + 4x = 180° → 9x = 180°.
- Step 4: Solve for x: x = 180° ÷ 9 = 20°.
- Step 5: Compute each angle: 2x = 40°, 3x = 60°, 4x = 80°.
- Step 6: Verify: 40° + 60° + 80° = 180°.
REASONING: Using a variable x to represent the common multiplier turns the ratio into algebraic expressions. The known sum (180°) provides the equation to find x. This method works for any multi-term ratio where the total is known.
ANSWER: 40°, 60°, 80°.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A metal alloy contains copper, zinc, and nickel in the ratio 7:5:3. If the alloy has 1.4 kg of copper, find the mass of zinc and nickel, and the total mass of the alloy.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write the ratio: Copper : Zinc : Nickel = 7 : 5 : 3.
- Step 2: Copper corresponds to 7 parts = 1.4 kg. Find one part: 1.4 kg ÷ 7 = 0.2 kg.
- Step 3: Calculate zinc mass: 5 parts × 0.2 kg = 1.0 kg.
- Step 4: Calculate nickel mass: 3 parts × 0.2 kg = 0.6 kg.
- Step 5: Find total mass: 1.4 kg + 1.0 kg + 0.6 kg = 3.0 kg. (Alternatively, total parts = 7+5+3 = 15; total mass = 15 × 0.2 kg = 3.0 kg.)
- Step 6: Check consistency: Ratio of masses 1.4 : 1.0 : 0.6 simplifies by dividing by 0.2 → 7:5:3. Correct.
REASONING: When one component's actual quantity is given, we use it to find the value of one part (unitary method). Then all other quantities follow by multiplication. This combines the concepts of equivalent ratios (scaling) and the unitary method.
ANSWER: Zinc = 1.0 kg, Nickel = 0.6 kg, Total alloy = 3.0 kg.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A grandmother divides ₹36,000 among her three grandchildren in the ratio of their ages. The ages are 8 years, 12 years, and 16 years. After receiving the money, the youngest grandchild gives ₹1,000 to the eldest. What is the new ratio of the amounts they hold? Express in simplest form.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and manipulating ratios after a transfer.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write the age ratio 8:12:16 and simplify to 2:3:4 (divide by 4).
- Step 2: Total parts = 2+3+4 = 9. Value per part = ₹36,000 ÷ 9 = ₹4,000.
- Step 3: Initial amounts: Youngest = 2 × ₹4,000 = ₹8,000; Middle = ₹12,000; Eldest = ₹16,000.
- Step 4: After transfer: Youngest = ₹8,000 - ₹1,000 = ₹7,000; Eldest = ₹16,000 + ₹1,000 = ₹17,000; Middle unchanged = ₹12,000.
- Step 5: New ratio = 7000:12000:17000. Simplify by dividing by 1000 → 7:12:17 (HCF of 7,12,17 is 1).
ANSWER: The new ratio is 7:12:17.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A paint mixture requires red, blue, and white in the ratio 3:4:5. A painter has 2.7 L of red, 3.2 L of blue, and 4.5 L of white. What is the maximum volume of paint mixture he can make without changing the ratio? Which colour will be left over, and how much?
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying the limiting component in a multi-term ratio scenario.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Find how many "parts" each available quantity can provide: Red: 2.7 L ÷ 3 = 0.9 L per part; Blue: 3.2 L ÷ 4 = 0.8 L per part; White: 4.5 L ÷ 5 = 0.9 L per part.
- Step 2: The limiting colour is the one that gives the smallest value per part → Blue at 0.8 L/part.
- Step 3: Maximum mixture uses 0.8 L as the value of one part. Total parts = 3+4+5 = 12. Maximum volume = 12 × 0.8 L = 9.6 L.
- Step 4: Amount used: Red = 3 × 0.8 = 2.4 L (left 0.3 L); Blue = 4 × 0.8 = 3.2 L (left 0 L); White = 5 × 0.8 = 4.0 L (left 0.5 L).
- Step 5: Left over: Red 0.3 L, White 0.5 L. Blue is completely used.
ANSWER: Maximum mixture = 9.6 L. Left over: Red 0.3 L, White 0.5 L. Blue is the limiting colour.
DIFFICULTY: hard
