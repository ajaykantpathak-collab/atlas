## ELI5
Hand the shopkeeper a 50.00-rupee note for a 32.75-rupee notebook and the change is a decimal subtraction: line up the points, rupees under rupees, paise under paise. Pad the shorter number with zeros - the note becomes 50.00 so every column holds a digit - then borrow right to left as usual. Skip the padding and the borrowing collapses, which is exactly how wrong change happens in busy markets. The point in the answer again falls straight down; the columns never lie.

## DEFINITION
Subtraction of decimal numbers is the process of finding the difference between two quantities expressed in decimal form by aligning their decimal points and subtracting digits in corresponding place-value columns from right to left, borrowing when necessary.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can correctly align decimal numbers by their decimal points, annex trailing zeros to make number of decimal places equal, perform column-wise subtraction with borrowing, and apply this operation to solve multi-step real-world problems involving money and measurements.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers subtraction of finite decimals including whole numbers, terminating decimals, and numbers with differing decimal places within Class 7 scope. Does not cover recurring decimals or advanced number systems.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you go to a stationary shop in India with ₹50.00 to buy a notebook that costs ₹32.75, and you want to know how much change the shopkeeper will return. To find the difference, you cannot just subtract the digits blindly; you must line up the decimal points so that rupees line up with rupees and paise line up with paise. Just like subtracting whole numbers or fractions, we write the larger number on top and line up the decimal point, adding placeholder zeros if one number has fewer decimal places than the other. Then, we subtract column by column from right to left, borrowing across the decimal point if a top digit is smaller than the bottom digit. The key insight is that aligning the decimal point ensures we are always subtracting like place-value units (tenths from tenths, hundredths from hundredths).

**Takeaway:** decimal subtraction mirrors addition's discipline: points aligned, zeros annexed, borrowing done column by column. **In the exam:** for change questions, subtract from the note, then check by adding the change back to the bill. **Check yourself:** compute the notebook change and verify it by adding the change back to the price. **Why students miss it:** they borrow across the point carelessly or skip the padding, and the change comes out short.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate the value of $42.5 - 7.836$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $34.664$. Working steps: 
1. Write $42.5$ with trailing zeros to match the decimal places of $7.836$, giving $42.500$.
2. Align the decimal points: $42.500 - 7.836$.
3. Subtract column by column from right to left, borrowing across columns: $0$ thousandths becomes $10$, borrowing from hundredths, etc. 
4. Compute: $10 - 6 = 4$ (thousandths); $9 - 3 = 6$ (hundredths); $14 - 8 = 6$ (tenths); place decimal point; $11 - 7 = 4$; $3 - 0 = 3$. 
5. Result is $34.664$. A wrong answer like $35.336$ shows an error in borrowing where the student subtracted smaller digits from larger digits directly without borrowing ($6 - 0$, etc.) or misaligned the place values.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often align decimals by the right edge (like whole numbers) instead of aligning the decimal points, writing $14.2 - 3.56$ as $14.2 - 3.56$ aligned on the right, which incorrectly matches tenths with hundredths. Another common error is failing to add placeholder zeros, leading them to drop digits down directly (e.g., $5.4 - 2.13$ becomes $3.33$ because they drop the $3$ straight down from the hundredths place).
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to always vertical-align the decimal points like buttons on a shirt. Have them draw a straight guideline down through the decimal points before writing any digits. Next, teach them to fill empty trailing spaces with placeholder zeros so every number has the exact same number of decimal places, preventing digits from "falling down" incorrectly.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Aarav went to the vegetable market with ₹100.00. He bought potatoes for ₹37.50 and tomatoes for ₹24.25. How much money does Aarav have left after making both purchases?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the total money Aarav started with (₹100.00) and the individual costs (₹37.50 and ₹24.25).
- Step 2: Find the total amount spent by adding the two costs using decimal addition: $37.50 + 24.25 = 61.75$.
- Step 3: Set up the subtraction problem to find the remaining money: subtract the total spent from the starting amount: $100.00 - 61.75$.
- Step 4: Align decimal points and annex placeholder zeros if needed: write $100.00$ and $61.75$, then perform column-wise subtraction with borrowing across zeros ($0 - 5$, borrowing from left columns through the zeros).
- Step 5: Compute step by step: hundredths: $10 - 5 = 5$; tenths: $9 - 7 = 2$; ones: $9 - 1 = 8$; tens: $9 - 6 = 3$. The result is $38.25$. Verify with addition: $38.25 + 61.75 = 100.00$.
REASONING: Real-world problems involving money require a two-step approach: first combining expenses using addition, and then subtracting that sum from the initial total amount using proper decimal place-value alignment and borrowing.
ANSWER: ₹38.25
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Priya is measuring two wooden strips for a craft project. The longer strip measures $15.3\text{ cm}$ and the shorter strip measures $8.75\text{ cm}$. What is the exact difference in their lengths?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given lengths: longer strip = $15.3\text{ cm}$, shorter strip = $8.75\text{ cm}$.
- Step 2: Set up the subtraction expression: $15.3 - 8.75$.
- Step 3: Annex a trailing zero to the decimal number with fewer digits so both have two decimal places: $15.30 - 8.75$.
- Step 4: Align the decimal points vertically and subtract column by column from right to left with borrowing: hundredths: $10 - 5 = 5$; tenths: $12 - 7 = 5$; ones: $14 - 8 = 6$.
- Step 5: Write the final answer with proper measurement units: $6.55\text{ cm}$.
REASONING: Subtracting measurements with different numbers of decimal places requires equalizing the decimal places with a zero to ensure accurate column subtraction.
ANSWER: $6.55\text{ cm}$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A runner is tracking her daily jogging distance. On Monday she ran $12.0\text{ km}$, but on Tuesday she fell short and ran only $7.34\text{ km}$. On Wednesday, she ran $5.6\text{ km}$ more than Tuesday's distance. Find the difference between Monday's distance and Wednesday's distance.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify Monday's distance ($12.0\text{ km}$) and Tuesday's distance ($7.34\text{ km}$).
- Step 2: Calculate Wednesday's distance by adding $5.6\text{ km}$ to Tuesday's distance: $7.34 + 5.60 = 12.94\text{ km}$.
- Step 3: Set up the final comparison between Monday's distance and Wednesday's distance: Wednesday's distance ($12.94\text{ km}$) is greater than Monday's distance ($12.0\text{ km}$), so find the difference: $12.94 - 12.00$.
- Step 4: Align decimal points and subtract: hundredths: $4 - 0 = 4$; tenths: $9 - 0 = 9$; ones: $12 - 12 = 0$.
- Step 5: State the final difference with units and interpret: Wednesday's distance is greater by $0.94\text{ km}$.
REASONING: Multi-step problems require sequential calculation where the result of an intermediate addition step becomes the operand for a final decimal subtraction comparison.
ANSWER: $0.94\text{ km}$
