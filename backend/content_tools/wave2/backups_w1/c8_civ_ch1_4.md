## ELI5
Turn eighteen and you can drive, sign contracts and pay taxes — yet imagine still waiting three more years before anyone lets you choose your government. Until 1988, Indians had to turn twenty-one to vote. Then the 61st Constitutional Amendment lowered the minimum voting age to eighteen, opening Lok Sabha and Assembly elections to millions of young citizens. Parliament judged the younger generation politically mature — and democracy grew more inclusive, because more of the people it rules now get to rule it back.

## DEFINITION
The lowering of the minimum voting age from 21 to 18 years in 1988 was a major constitutional milestone achieved through the 61st Constitutional Amendment Act, which expanded democratic participation to millions of young Indian citizens.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine turning 18 and realizing you are legally considered an adult who can drive, sign contracts, and pay taxes, but wondering if you have a say in choosing your government. In India before 1988, citizens had to wait until they turned 21 to vote in Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assembly elections. Building on your prior knowledge of universal adult franchise and Article 326 from Class 8 civics, we will explore how the Parliament recognized the political maturity of the younger generation and amended the Constitution to lower the voting age to 18. This change brought millions of young citizens into the active democratic process, proving that democracy grows more inclusive over time.

Takeaway: democracy widened in 1988 — the 61st Amendment cut the voting age from twenty-one to eighteen, inviting millions of young Indians into the electorate as a statement of trust in their maturity. Exam format: expect the amendment named, the before-after ages stated, and the widening justified. Verification beat: pair the number with the amendment — sixty-first, eighteen years. Why students miss it: they know the age but not the amendment — constitutional questions score the pair, and the inclusion argument is the essay half. Remember the pair as one fact: the sixty-first amendment and the eighteenth year — the law's number and the citizen's age, travelling together in every answer.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that the voting age was 18 right from the adoption of the Constitution in 1950, or they confuse the voting age with the minimum age required to contest elections (such as 25 for Lok Sabha).
