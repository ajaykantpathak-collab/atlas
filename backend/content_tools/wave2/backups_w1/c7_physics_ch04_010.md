## ELI5
Speed speaks two dialects: the speedometer's kilometres per hour and the physicist's metres per second. Converting is a fixed exchange rate - to go from km/h to m/s, multiply by the fraction that cancels kilometres into metres and hours into seconds; the reverse ride divides. A village road's milestone markers and your cycle's speedometer both speak one dialect; the exam speaks the other. Convert before comparing, every time.

## DEFINITION
Speed can be expressed in different units such as metres per second ($\text{m/s}$) or kilometres per hour ($\text{km/h}$), where the Standard International (SI) unit is metres per second. Unit conversion involves multiplying or dividing by a conversion factor of $3.6$ to switch between these two standard systems of measurement.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine riding a bicycle down an Indian village road where the speedometer or milestone markers indicate distance in kilometres and time in hours, while a school laboratory stopwatch measures time in seconds. When solving physics problems, we often need to bridge these two worlds by changing units. Notice that $1\text{ kilometre} = 1000\text{ metres}$ and $1\text{ hour} = 3600\text{ seconds}$. By simplifying the fraction $1000/3600$, we get $5/18$, which is our golden key for unit conversions. We will now learn how to use this ratio to convert speeds smoothly without making common arithmetic errors.

**Takeaway:** speed units convert by a fixed rate: km/h to m/s multiplies by five-eighteenths, and the reverse divides - never compare across dialects. **In the exam:** write the conversion factor beside the number, cancel the units on paper, and let the arithmetic finish in front of the examiner. **Check yourself:** ride the walkthrough's village road and ask which unit the milestone speaks and which the physics answer needs. **Why students miss it:** students memorise a magic number without its derivation - the flagged fragility that dissolves under pressure. The factor is just two conversions chained: kilometres to metres, then hours to seconds. Derive it once slowly on paper, and the number can never be forgotten - only reconstructed, which is better.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently multiply by the wrong fraction ($\frac{18}{5}$ instead of $\frac{5}{18}$ when converting from $\text{km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$, or vice versa) because they memorize the numbers without remembering whether the resulting value should get larger or smaller. For instance, converting $36\text{ km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$ should yield a smaller number ($10\text{ m/s}$) because a metre is smaller than a kilometre, meaning it takes more metres to cover the same distance, but a second is a smaller time unit, making speed in $\text{m/s}$ numerically smaller than in $\text{km/h}$ for the same physical motion.

Check whether the final number should look larger or smaller by remembering that $\text{km/h}$ numbers are always larger than their $\text{m/s}$ equivalents (since an hour is much longer than a second).

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A school bus travels from Delhi to a nearby monument at a speed of $54\text{ km/h}$. Express this speed in metres per second ($\text{m/s}$).
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given quantity and target unit: Given speed $= 54\text{ km/h}$, target unit $= \text{m/s}$.
- step 2: Recall the conversion factor from $\text{km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$, which is multiplying by $\frac{5}{18}$.
- step 3: Set up the multiplication expression: $54 \times \frac{5}{18}$.
- step 4: Cancel common factors and compute: $54 \div 18 = 3$, then $3 \times 5 = 15$.
- step 5: Attach the correct unit and verify reasonableness: $15\text{ m/s}$. A school bus travelling at $15\text{ m/s}$ is a physically reasonable speed.
REASONING: Multiplying by the ratio of meters in a kilometer ($1000$) to seconds in an hour ($3600$) converts the compound unit correctly.
ANSWER: 15 m/s
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An athlete runs a sprint training drill at a speed of $8\text{ m/s}$. What is the athlete's speed in kilometres per hour ($\text{km/h}$)?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given quantity and target unit: Given speed $= 8\text{ m/s}$, target unit $= \text{km/h}$.
- step 2: Determine the conversion factor from $\text{m/s}$ to $\text{km/h}$, which is multiplying by $\frac{18}{5}$ (the reciprocal of $\frac{5}{18}$).
- step 3: Set up the multiplication expression: $8 \times \frac{18}{5}$.
- step 4: Compute the numerator and divide by the denominator: $(8 \times 18) \div 5 = 144 \div 5 = 28.8$.
- step 5: Attach the correct unit and check: $28.8\text{ km/h}$. This is a reasonable running speed for a trained athlete.
REASONING: To convert from a smaller unit ($\text{m/s}$) to a larger unit ($\text{km/h}$), we invert the standard conversion factor to scale up the numerical value.
ANSWER: 28.8 km/h
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Two vehicles race on a test track in Mumbai. Vehicle A travels at $25\text{ m/s}$ and Vehicle B travels at $80\text{ km/h}$. Which vehicle has the higher speed, and what is the difference in their speeds in $\text{km/h}$?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given speeds in different units: Vehicle A $= 25\text{ m/s}$, Vehicle B $= 80\text{ km/h}$.
- step 2: Convert Vehicle A's speed to $\text{km/h}$ so both speeds use the same unit for direct comparison.
- step 3: Apply the conversion factor for $\text{m/s}$ to $\text{km/h}$: $25 \times \frac{18}{5}$.
- step 4: Simplify and compute: $25 \div 5 = 5$, then $5 \times 18 = 90\text{ km/h}$.
- step 5: Compare the converted speed ($90\text{ km/h}$) with Vehicle B ($80\text{ km/h}$) to see that Vehicle A is faster.
- step 6: Find the difference: $90\text{ km/h} - 80\text{ km/h} = 10\text{ km/h}$.
REASONING: Comparing physical quantities directly requires them to be expressed in the exact same units. Converting both to $\text{km/h}$ allows an easy subtraction to find the difference.
ANSWER: Vehicle A is faster by 10 km/h

COMPETENCY: Unit analysis and multi-step problem solving
STEPS:
- step 1: Convert the drone's speed from $\text{km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$ to match the distance unit in metres.
- step 2: Calculate converted speed: $72 \times \frac{5}{18} = 20\text{ m/s}$.
- step 3: Use the time formula: $\text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}}$.
- step 4: Substitute values: $\text{Time} = \frac{100\text{ m}}{20\text{ m/s}} = 5\text{ seconds}$.
