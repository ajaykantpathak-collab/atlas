## DEFINITION
Geometric constructions involve drawing precise figures, line segments, angles, and shapes using only two standard mathematical tools: a ungraduated straightedge (ruler) and a pair of compasses, without relying on measurement numbers. This foundational skill ensures geometric exactness based on axioms and Euclidean principles.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are a builder designing a traditional Indian temple or a school playground, and you need to draw a perfectly straight line or split a land boundary into two identical halves without using a measuring tape. How can you be entirely sure your lines are exact? Instead of using numbers or a measuring scale, ancient mathematicians discovered that using a ruler just for drawing straight paths and a compass for keeping a fixed distance allows us to copy lengths and create perfect symmetry. To begin, you must learn how to set your compass to a specific span and swing arcs that intersect to find equidistant points. By building on your earlier understanding of lines and points from Class 6, you will master the art of using these two simple tools to create complex, accurate geometric shapes. The key insight is that a compass guarantees equal distances (radii), transforming simple drawing into precise mathematical creation.

**Takeaway:** construction is logic drawn with two tools: a straightedge for lines, a compass for equal arcs. **In the exam:** set the compass once, mark crossings from two centres, and let the geometry - not a scale - certify the result. **Check yourself:** copy a line segment using only compass arcs, then let the ruler judge the copy, not guide it. **Why students miss it:** students reach for the marked ruler and inherit every printing and parallax error the tool ever carried. Classical construction stays exact because nothing in it is measured; everything is copied.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often change the compass opening inadvertently while drawing arcs because they grip the compass legs too tightly or apply uneven downward pressure, causing the hinge to slip. For example, when trying to transfer a length of 5 cm, a student's hand slips, changing the radius to 5.2 cm, which ruins subsequent intersecting arc steps and results in asymmetrical figures.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to hold the compass only by its insulated or plastic top knob when swinging arcs, keeping fingers away from the movable legs. Show them how to tighten the central hinge screw with a small screwdriver or coin if the legs feel loose, and always re-check the compass span against the ruler before every new arc.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A carpenter in Delhi needs to replicate a decorative wooden beam edge of length 7.4 cm onto a new plank using only a ruler and compass. Describe and calculate the precise steps to construct a line segment $CD$ equal in length to a given line segment $AB = 7.4$ cm.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw a long working line $l$ using the straightedge, and mark a starting point $C$ on it.
- Step 2: Place the metal pointer of the compass at endpoint $A$ and adjust the pencil end so it rests exactly at endpoint $B$ of the given segment $AB$.
- Step 3: Verify the compass span against the ruler to ensure it reads exactly 7.4 cm without slipping.
- Step 4: Without changing the compass width, lift the compass and place the metal pointer at point $C$ on the working line $l$.
- Step 5: Swing an arc that intersects the working line $l$ at a new point, and label this intersection point $D$. The segment $CD$ is now constructed such that $CD = AB = 7.4$ cm.
REASONING: By keeping the compass span fixed at the exact distance between $A$ and $B$, the arc created at point $D$ maintains the identical radius, satisfying the geometric principle of segment congruence.
ANSWER: Line segment $CD$ of length 7.4 cm.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Construct a line segment $XY$ whose length is twice the length of a given segment $PQ = 3.8$ cm, using a straightedge and compass.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw a straight ray or line using the straightedge and mark a starting point $X$.
- Step 2: Measure the given segment $PQ$ by placing the compass pointer at $P$ and the pencil at $Q$, confirming the span is 3.8 cm.
- Step 3: Place the compass pointer at $X$ on the ray and swing an arc intersecting the ray at an intermediate point $M$, so that $XM = 3.8$ cm.
- Step 4: Move the compass pointer to point $M$ without changing the 3.8 cm width, and swing another arc further along the ray to intersect at point $Y$, so that $MY = 3.8$ cm.
- Step 5: Calculate the total length of $XY$ algebraically: $XY = XM + MY = 3.8\text{ cm} + 3.8\text{ cm} = 7.6\text{ cm}$.
REASONING: Consecutive arc markings along a straight path add lengths additively, allowing us to construct multiples of a base segment without doing arithmetic calculations on a scale.
ANSWER: Line segment $XY$ of length 7.6 cm.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A tailor wants to construct a line segment $EF$ that is the difference between two lengths: a given segment $AB = 9.2$ cm and a given segment $CD = 3.5$ cm, using only a compass and straightedge. Find the resulting length and describe the construction steps.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw a long reference line and mark a starting point $E$.
- Step 2: Set the compass to match the longer segment $AB = 9.2$ cm, place the pointer at $E$, and swing an arc on the line to mark a temporary point $T$, making $ET = 9.2$ cm.
- Step 3: Reset the compass to match the shorter segment $CD = 3.5$ cm.
- Step 4: Place the compass pointer at the temporary point $T$ and swing an arc *backwards* (towards $E$) along the line to intersect it at point $F$.
- Step 5: Calculate the remaining length algebraically: $EF = ET - FT = 9.2\text{ cm} - 3.5\text{ cm} = 5.7\text{ cm}$.
REASONING: Subtracting a length geometrically is achieved by measuring the total length forward and then stepping backward by the subtrahend's length using the compass radius.
ANSWER: Line segment $EF$ of length 5.7 cm.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Suppose you are given an unlabelled, unmarked paper with two points, $P$ and $Q$, 5 cm apart. Without using any numbers or numerical markings on your ruler, describe how you would use a compass to construct a triangle $PQR$ where all three sides are exactly equal to 5 cm (an equilateral triangle).
COMPETENCY: Translating geometric properties into multi-step construction plans
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given elements: segment $PQ = 5$ cm represented by the distance between points $P$ and $Q$.
- Step 2: Set the compass width equal to the distance between $P$ and $Q$ by placing the pointer at $P$ and pencil at $Q$.
- Step 3: With the compass width fixed at $PQ$, place the metal pointer at $P$ and swing an arc above the line segment $PQ$.
- Step 4: Without changing the compass width, place the metal pointer at $Q$ and swing another arc above the line segment $PQ$ so that it intersects the first arc at a new point $R$.
- Step 5: Use the straightedge to connect point $P$ to $R$, and point $Q$ to $R$, thereby forming equilateral triangle $PQR$ with all sides equal to 5 cm.
ANSWER: By keeping the compass width equal to $PQ$ and swinging intersecting arcs from both endpoints, both $PR$ and $QR$ are forced to equal $PQ$ by construction, resulting in an equilateral triangle without reading any numbers.
DIFFICULTY: hard
