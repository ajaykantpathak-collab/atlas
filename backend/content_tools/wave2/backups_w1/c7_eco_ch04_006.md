## ELI5
A weaver needs a high-speed loom before the festival orders arrive; the bank holds her neighbours' savings doing nothing. Loans and credit are the handshake between them: the bank lends the pooled deposits, the borrower repays with interest, and idle money becomes a working machine. Credit at a fair rate turns a plan into equipment - and the interest is simply the rent paid for using tomorrow's income today.

## DEFINITION
Loans and credit refer to the economic process where banks lend accumulated deposits to borrowers in exchange for a promise of future repayment along with an added fee called interest. This mechanism transforms idle savings into productive investments that drive economic growth.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine a local weaver in your town who needs ₹50,000 to buy a new high-speed handloom machine to fulfill a large festival order. Because she does not have enough cash saved, she visits a local bank to apply for a business loan. The bank evaluates her application, checks that she can repay it from her future loom earnings, and grants the loan using money deposited by other savers in the community. To begin understanding this process, observe that banks act as financial bridges connecting those who have surplus money with those who need capital. Building on our prior understanding of how banks keep money flowing, we now examine the mechanics of credit creation and interest. The key economic principle is that credit allows production and consumption to happen today using future expected income, fueled by the trust between lender and borrower.

**Takeaway:** loans and credit hand idle deposits to working plans: banks lend, borrowers repay with interest, and savings become equipment. **In the exam:** in repayment questions, compute principal, rate and time separately, then state the total owed, not just the interest. **Check yourself:** walk with the walkthrough's weaver from festival order to sanctioned loan to running loom. **Why students miss it:** students hear loan and think loss - credit used productively repays its own interest, and refusing to borrow can be the costlier no.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the primary source of funds that commercial banks use to provide loans to businesses and individuals?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The primary source of funds is the deposits made by savers into savings, current, and fixed accounts at the bank. If a student answers "government grants" or "bank owner's personal wealth," it reveals a misunderstanding of how financial intermediation works, thinking banks operate purely on external funding rather than pooling community savings.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do banks charge a higher interest rate on loans they give out compared to the interest rate they pay to people who deposit savings?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Banks charge higher interest on loans (lending rate) than they pay on deposits (borrowing/saving rate) to cover their operational costs, administrative expenses, risk of default, and to make a profit. A student who believes banks charge the same interest rate fails to understand the profit model of financial intermediaries, missing how the "interest spread" keeps banks in business.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A farmer takes a crop loan of ₹40,000 from a commercial bank for one year at an annual simple interest rate of 8%. Calculate the total amount the farmer must repay to the bank at the end of the year.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Identify given values: Principal (P) = ₹40,000, Rate (R) = 8% per annum, Time (T) = 1 year.
Step 2: Recall simple interest formula: Interest = $(P \times R \times T) / 100$.
Step 3: Calculate interest: $(40,000 \times 8 \times 1) / 100 = ₹3,200$.
Step 4: Calculate total repayment amount: Principal + Interest = $₹40,000 + ₹3,200 = ₹43,200$.
A student who answers only ₹3,200 has calculated just the interest without adding back the original principal, forgetting that a loan must be repaid in full plus the cost of borrowing.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that banks create money out of thin air without any backing or that the loan money given out belongs entirely to the bank manager's personal vault. They fail to realize that every rupee lent out originates from the collective deposits of community members who trust the bank to safeguard their savings. When a student thinks credit is free money created by banks, they miss the delicate balance of liquidity and risk management required in financial systems.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A local potter in Rajasthan needs ₹25,000 to buy electric pottery wheels. A commercial bank agrees to grant this business loan for 2 years at a simple interest rate of 10% per annum. Calculate the total interest and total repayment amount.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given data: Principal (P) = ₹25,000, Rate (R) = 10% per year, Time (T) = 2 years.
- Step 2: Set up the simple interest formula: Interest ($I$) = $(P \times R \times T) / 100$.
- Step 3: Substitute the numbers into the formula: $I = (25,000 \times 10 \times 2) / 100$.
- Step 4: Calculate the total interest: $I = 500,000 / 100 = ₹5,000$.
- Step 5: Add the interest to the principal to find total repayment: Total Amount = Principal + Interest = ₹25,000 + ₹5,000 = ₹30,000.
REASONING: The potter uses borrowed money to invest in equipment. The bank charges 10% per year for two years, generating ₹5,000 in interest, which compensates the bank for risk and administrative costs while the potter returns the full ₹30,000 over time.
ANSWER: Total interest is ₹5,000 and total repayment is ₹30,000.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A small grocery shop owner borrows ₹60,000 from a cooperative bank to stock festival goods for 6 months (0.5 years) at an annual interest rate of 12%. Calculate the interest and total repayment.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given data: Principal ($P$) = ₹60,000, Rate ($R$) = 12% per year, Time ($T$) = 0.5 years (6 months).
- Step 2: Set up the simple interest formula: Interest ($I$) = $(P \times R \times T) / 100$.
- Step 3: Substitute the numbers into the formula: $I = (60,000 \times 12 \times 0.5) / 100$.
- Step 4: Calculate the total interest: $I = 360,000 / 100 = ₹3,600$.
- Step 5: Calculate total repayment: ₹60,000 + ₹3,600 = ₹63,600.
REASONING: Even though the loan is for less than a full year, the annual rate is adjusted by the time fraction (0.5 years), resulting in a proportional interest charge of ₹3,600.
ANSWER: Total interest is ₹3,600 and total repayment is ₹63,600.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A dairy farmer takes a loan of ₹1,00,000 to buy two cows. The bank charges 9% simple interest per annum. The farmer repays the entire loan amount plus interest in a single lump sum after 3 years. However, before taking the loan, the farmer earned ₹30,000 profit per year; with the cows, profit increases by 50% per year. Calculate the total repayment and determine if the increased profit covers the loan cost over the 3 years.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify loan details: Principal = ₹1,00,000, Rate = 9%, Time = 3 years.
- Step 2: Calculate total interest: $(100,000 \times 9 \times 3) / 100 = ₹27,000$.
- Step 3: Calculate total repayment amount: ₹1,00,000 + ₹27,000 = ₹1,27,000.
- Step 4: Calculate baseline profit over 3 years: ₹30,000 $\times$ 3 = ₹90,000.
- Step 5: Calculate new profit with cows: 50% increase means new profit is ₹45,000 per year $\times$ 3 = ₹135,000.
- Step 6: Compare the extra profit earned due to the investment against the total interest paid: Extra profit = ₹135,000 - ₹90,000 = ₹45,000. Since ₹45,000 is greater than the ₹27,000 interest, the investment is profitable.
REASONING: This multi-step problem connects borrowing costs with business revenue generation. It proves that productive credit creates value exceeding the cost of capital, making the loan economically beneficial.
ANSWER: Total repayment is ₹1,27,000, and the extra profit of ₹45,000 easily covers the ₹27,000 interest cost.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two banks offer loans of ₹50,000 for 1 year. Bank A charges 10% simple interest. Bank B charges 8% simple interest but requires a non-refundable processing fee of ₹1,500 upfront. Calculate the total out-of-pocket cost for both options and explain which bank is cheaper for the borrower.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating financial options and total cost of credit
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate total cost for Bank A: Interest = $(50,000 \times 10 \times 1)/100 = ₹5,000$. Total cost = ₹5,000.
- Step 2: Calculate total cost for Bank B: Interest = $(50,000 \times 8 \times 1)/100 = ₹4,000$. Add processing fee = ₹4,000 + ₹1,500 = ₹5,500.
- Step 3: Compare total costs: Bank A total cost is ₹5,000 while Bank B total cost is ₹5,500.
- Step 4: Conclude economically: Even though Bank B has a lower interest rate, the added processing fee makes Bank A the cheaper option overall.
ANSWER: Bank A total cost is ₹5,000; Bank B total cost is ₹5,500. Bank A is cheaper because hidden fees can outweigh lower nominal interest rates.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A textile weaver needs ₹80,000 for purchasing raw yarn. Informal money lenders in the village charge 3% per month simple interest. A formal commercial bank charges 12% per annum (simple interest). Calculate the interest paid for a 1-month loan under both options on a ₹80,000 principal, and analyze why rural workers sometimes still fall into debt traps with informal lenders despite high rates.
COMPETENCY: Analyzing credit sources and market behavior
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate informal lender monthly interest: Rate = 3% per month, Time = 1 month. Interest = $(80,000 \times 3 \times 1) / 100 = ₹2,400$.
- Step 2: Calculate formal bank monthly interest: Annual rate = 12%, so monthly rate = 12% / 12 = 1% per month. Interest = $(80,000 \times 1 \times 1) / 100 = ₹800$.
- Step 3: Compare the monthly costs: Informal lender costs ₹2,400 per month, whereas the bank costs only ₹800 per month.
- Step 4: Analyze behavioral and institutional reasons: Explain that informal lenders provide instant cash without paperwork or collateral, whereas banks require documentation, formal collateral, and time approval, forcing urgent borrowers into expensive informal credit.
ANSWER: Informal monthly interest is ₹2,400; bank monthly interest is ₹800. Borrowers often use informal lenders due to urgency and lack of bank documentation, leading to severe debt traps.
DIFFICULTY: hard
