## ELI5
Turning a fraction into a percentage is resizing it so its denominator becomes one hundred — then reading the numerator. Multiply the fraction by hundred, attach the symbol, done: eighteen out of twenty-five becomes seventy-two per hundred. That is all the % sign ever does — it declares the scale. Once two scores from different tests, different totals, both stand on the scale of hundred, comparison is a glance instead of an argument.

## DEFINITION
Expressing a fraction as a percentage means converting the fraction to an equivalent value out of 100 by multiplying the fraction by 100 and appending the percent symbol (%). For any fraction \(\frac{a}{b}\) where \(b \neq 0\), the percentage is \(\frac{a}{b} \times 100\%\).
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can convert any given fraction (proper, improper, or mixed) to a percentage by multiplying by 100 and simplifying, and can explain why multiplying by 100 correctly scales the fraction to a "per hundred" value.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers conversion of proper fractions, improper fractions, and mixed numbers to percentages. Does not cover converting percentages back to fractions (node c8_math_ch8_3) or finding a percentage of a quantity (node c8_math_ch8_4).
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you scored 18 out of 25 in a math test, and your friend says she got 72%. Who did better? To compare, you need to express your score as a percentage. Recall from c8_math_ch8_1 that "percent" means "per hundred". So we ask: if 25 marks is the whole, what would your score be out of 100? Write the fraction \(\frac{18}{25}\). To change the denominator to 100, multiply by 100: \(\frac{18}{25} \times 100 = 18 \times 4 = 72\). Add the % sign: 72%. Now you can directly compare: both scored 72%. The key insight is that multiplying by 100 scales the fraction to a denominator of 100, which is exactly what a percentage represents.

Takeaway: percentage = fraction × 100% — multiplication by a hundred rescales any denominator onto the common base of one hundred. Exam format: expect conversions of proper, improper and mixed fractions; show the multiply-by-100 step, then simplify. Verification beat: improper fractions may give percentages above hundred — that is legitimate, not an error. Why students miss it: they divide by a hundred when asked to convert to percentage — the direction matters: fraction to percentage multiplies, percentage to fraction divides.

Final check: improper fractions legally produce percentages above a hundred — an answer of a hundred and twenty percent is correct mathematics, not a mistake to be trimmed.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Write the formula to convert a fraction \(\frac{a}{b}\) into a percentage.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The formula is \(\frac{a}{b} \times 100\%\). A wrong answer (e.g., \(\frac{a}{b} \times 10\) or \(\frac{a \times 100}{b}\) without the % sign) reveals the student has not memorized the exact conversion rule or confuses it with decimal conversion (where you multiply by 10, 100, etc., based on decimal places).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why do we multiply a fraction by 100 to convert it to a percentage? Explain using the meaning of "per cent".
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
"Per cent" means "per hundred". A percentage expresses a number as parts out of 100. Multiplying the fraction by 100 scales it to an equivalent fraction with denominator 100, directly giving the "parts per hundred". A wrong answer (e.g., "because the formula says so" or "to make the number bigger") shows the student lacks conceptual understanding of the definition of percentage and is relying on rote memorization.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Convert \(\frac{3}{8}\) to a percentage.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Write the fraction \(\frac{3}{8}\). Step 2: Multiply by 100: \(\frac{3}{8} \times 100 = \frac{300}{8}\). Step 3: Divide 300 by 8: \(300 \div 8 = 37.5\). Step 4: Add % sign: 37.5%. A wrong answer like 3.75% reveals the student divided by 100 instead of multiplying, or misplaced the decimal. An answer of 37% reveals they truncated instead of completing the division.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the percentages: \(\frac{5}{6}\) and \(\frac{7}{8}\). Which is larger? Explain without calculating the exact percentages.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
\(\frac{7}{8}\) is larger. Reasoning: Both fractions are close to 1. \(\frac{5}{6}\) is \(\frac{1}{6}\) away from 1, while \(\frac{7}{8}\) is \(\frac{1}{8}\) away from 1. Since \(\frac{1}{8} < \frac{1}{6}\), \(\frac{7}{8}\) is closer to 1 (i.e., closer to 100%). A wrong answer choosing \(\frac{5}{6}\) or saying they are equal reveals the student cannot reason about fraction magnitudes relative to the whole and relies solely on computation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student converts \(\frac{2}{5}\) to a percentage by writing \(\frac{2}{5} = \frac{40}{100} = 40\%\). Is this method correct? If yes, explain why it works. If not, correct it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Yes, the method is correct. The student found an equivalent fraction with denominator 100 by multiplying numerator and denominator by 20. Since a percentage is a fraction with denominator 100, \(\frac{40}{100}\) directly reads as 40%. This is equivalent to multiplying by 100 because \(\frac{2}{5} \times 100 = \frac{2}{5} \times \frac{100}{1} = \frac{200}{5} = 40\). A wrong answer saying it's incorrect reveals the student does not recognize that the equivalent-fraction method is mathematically identical to the multiplication method.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-life situation where you need to convert a mixed fraction to a percentage. Write the problem and solve it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example problem: "A recipe requires \(1 \frac{1}{2}\) cups of sugar. You have a 2-cup measuring cup. What percentage of the measuring cup will be filled?" Solution: \(1 \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}\). \(\frac{3}{2} \times 100 = 150\%\). A poor answer (e.g., using a proper fraction only, or solving incorrectly) reveals the student cannot apply the concept to mixed numbers or real-world contexts, indicating a procedural-only understanding.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often forget to multiply by 100 and simply write the numerator as the percentage if the denominator is 100, or they multiply only the numerator by 100 and ignore the denominator. For example, converting \(\frac{3}{4}\): they write 3% (ignoring denominator) or 300% (multiplying numerator by 100 but not dividing by 4). The exact wrong step is treating the fraction \(\frac{a}{b}\) as if \(a\) is already the percentage, or computing \(a \times 100\) without dividing by \(b\).
## ON_FAILURE
Use a 100-grid visual. Draw a rectangle divided into 4 equal columns (representing \(\frac{3}{4}\)). Shade 3 columns. Now divide each column into 25 small squares (total 100 squares). Count shaded squares: 75. Write \(\frac{75}{100} = 75\%\). Connect to calculation: \(\frac{3}{4} \times 100 = \frac{300}{4} = 75\). Practice with \(\frac{1}{3}\) (grid doesn't divide evenly) to force the multiplication method: \(\frac{1}{3} \times 100 = 33.\overline{3}\%\). Emphasize: "Multiply the fraction by 100, then divide numerator by denominator."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: In a school election, 3 out of 5 students voted for the winning candidate. What percentage of students voted for the winner?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the fraction representing the part: \(\frac{3}{5}\).
- Step 2: Recall the conversion rule: multiply the fraction by 100%.
- Step 3: Substitute: \(\frac{3}{5} \times 100\%\).
- Step 4: Simplify: \(\frac{3 \times 100}{5} = \frac{300}{5} = 60\).
- Step 5: Add the percent sign and interpret: 60% of students voted for the winner.
REASONING: Multiplying by 100 converts the fraction to a value out of 100, which is the definition of percentage. Simplifying before multiplying (cancelling 5 with 100) makes computation easier.
ANSWER: 60%
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A recipe calls for \(\frac{2}{3}\) cup of sugar. Express this as a percentage of a full cup.
STEPS:
- Step 1: The fraction is \(\frac{2}{3}\).
- Step 2: Multiply by 100%: \(\frac{2}{3} \times 100\%\).
- Step 3: Compute: \(\frac{200}{3}\%\).
- Step 4: Divide 200 by 3: \(66 \frac{2}{3}\) or 66.666...%
- Step 5: Write the answer as a mixed number percentage: \(66 \frac{2}{3}\%\) (or approximately 66.67%).
REASONING: The fraction \(\frac{2}{3}\) does not convert neatly to a denominator of 100 using equivalent fractions, so we perform the division to get a decimal or mixed number percentage. This shows percentages can be fractional or repeating decimals.
ANSWER: \(66 \frac{2}{3}\%\) (or approximately 66.67%)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: In a class of 40 students, 12 are girls. What percentage of the class are boys?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Find the number of boys: total - girls = 40 - 12 = 28 boys.
- Step 2: Write the fraction of boys: \(\frac{28}{40}\).
- Step 3: Simplify the fraction: divide numerator and denominator by 4 → \(\frac{7}{10}\).
- Step 4: Convert to percentage: \(\frac{7}{10} \times 100\% = 7 \times 10\% = 70\%\).
- Step 5: Verify: 70% of 40 = 28 boys; 30% of 40 = 12 girls. Total 100%.
- Step 6: State answer: 70% of the class are boys.
REASONING: This problem combines finding the complementary part (boys) and then converting that fraction to a percentage. Simplifying the fraction first (\(\frac{28}{40} = \frac{7}{10}\)) makes the multiplication by 100 trivial (just append a zero).
ANSWER: 70%
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student scored 45 out of 60 in Math and 32 out of 40 in Science. In which subject did the student perform better? Justify your answer by converting both scores to percentages.
COMPETENCY: Comparing quantities using percentages.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write the fractions: Math = \(\frac{45}{60}\), Science = \(\frac{32}{40}\).
- Step 2: Simplify each fraction: Math = \(\frac{3}{4}\), Science = \(\frac{4}{5}\).
- Step 3: Convert to percentages: Math = \(\frac{3}{4} \times 100 = 75\%\), Science = \(\frac{4}{5} \times 100 = 80\%\).
- Step 4: Compare: 80% > 75%, so the student performed better in Science.
ANSWER: The student performed better in Science (80% vs 75%).
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A shirt is marked at ₹800. During a sale, it is sold at ₹600. What percentage of the marked price is the selling price? If the shopkeeper wants to express the discount as a percentage of the marked price, what fraction would he use before converting to percentage?
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions (part-to-whole).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Selling price as fraction of marked price = \(\frac{600}{800} = \frac{3}{4}\).
- Step 2: Convert to percentage: \(\frac{3}{4} \times 100 = 75\%\). Selling price is 75% of marked price.
- Step 3: Discount amount = Marked price - Selling price = 800 - 600 = ₹200.
- Step 4: Discount as fraction of marked price = \(\frac{200}{800} = \frac{1}{4}\).
- Step 5: Discount percentage = \(\frac{1}{4} \times 100 = 25\%\).
ANSWER: Selling price is 75% of marked price. The fraction for discount is \(\frac{1}{4}\), giving 25% discount.
DIFFICULTY: hard
