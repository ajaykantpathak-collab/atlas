## ELI5
An equation is a weighing scale in perfect balance: whatever sits on the left weighs exactly as much as the right. To find the hidden weight, you may do anything to the scale — add cargo, remove cargo, multiply both pans — provided you do exactly the same thing to the other pan. Step by step, peeling operations off the variable while keeping both pans equal, the unknown stands alone at last. That is the balancing method: fairness used as a solving technique.

## DEFINITION
The balancing method for solving linear equations states that performing the same arithmetic operation (addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division by a non-zero number) on both sides of an equation preserves the equality, allowing the variable to be isolated systematically.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine Rohan has some money. He spends half of it on a book and then ₹20 on a pen. He has ₹50 left. How much did he start with? This real situation leads to the equation x/2 - 20 = 50. To solve, we "balance" the equation: first add 20 to both sides (x/2 = 70), then multiply both sides by 2 (x = 140). This connects directly to the Class 7 balance model (c7_math_ch15_002) where you learned that an equation is like a weighing scale. The key insight is that whatever operation you do to one side, you must do to the other to keep the scale balanced, working backwards through the operations applied to the variable.

Takeaway: the balancing method solves by symmetric operations — add, subtract, multiply or divide (never by zero) on both sides — until the variable is isolated, then verify by substitution. Exam format: expect equations with integers, fractions, decimals and variables on both sides; show the operation performed on both sides at each line, not just the answer. Verification beat: plug the solution into the original equation; both sides must evaluate equal. Why students miss it: they perform an operation on one side only, or expand a bracket partially — a factor outside a bracket touches every term inside, and one untouched term breaks the balance the whole method depends on.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the first step to solve the equation 3x + 5 = 14 using the balancing method?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Subtract 5 from both sides to get 3x = 9. A wrong answer (e.g., "divide by 3 first") reveals the student doesn't understand the order of inverse operations: we must reverse the order of operations applied to the variable (last operation done to x is +5, so first undo is -5).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why must we perform the same operation on both sides of an equation?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
An equation is a statement of equality; performing the same operation on both sides preserves that equality. If a student says "to get the variable alone," they confuse the goal with the mathematical reason. The equality would break if we only changed one side.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Solve using the balancing method: (2x/3) - 4 = 10. Show all steps.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Add 4 to both sides → 2x/3 = 14.
Step 2: Multiply both sides by 3 → 2x = 42.
Step 3: Divide both sides by 2 → x = 21.
Verification: (2×21)/3 - 4 = 14 - 4 = 10. Correct.
A wrong answer often shows incorrect order (multiplying before adding) or arithmetic errors with fractions (e.g., 14 × 3 = 42 but then 42/2 = 20).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare solving 5x - 3 = 12 and 5x - 3 = 2x + 12. How does the balancing method change when variables appear on both sides?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
For 5x - 3 = 12, we add 3 then divide by 5. For 5x - 3 = 2x + 12, we must first collect variable terms on one side by subtracting 2x from both sides to get 3x - 3 = 12, then proceed. A student who doesn't recognize the need to collect variable terms first will try to divide by 5 prematurely, leaving x on both sides.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student solves 4(x - 2) = 12 as follows:
4x - 2 = 12
4x = 14
x = 3.5
Is this correct? If not, identify the error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Incorrect. The error is in the first step: 4(x - 2) expands to 4x - 8, not 4x - 2. The student forgot to multiply 4 by -2 (distributive property error). Correct solution: 4x - 8 = 12 → 4x = 20 → x = 5. Verification: 4(5-2)=4×3=12. **A wrong answer (the factor outside the bracket multiplied only the first term inside) reveals the student has applied the distributive property halfway — every term inside the bracket must be multiplied.** Its common cousin: dividing only the bracketed term on one side while the other side waits untouched. Practise with a bill from a Srinagar shop — a per-item discount must reach every row of the bill, not merely the first, or the total refuses to balance.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a word problem that leads to the equation (x/5) + 3 = 10, and solve it using the balancing method.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Example: "A number divided by 5, then increased by 3, gives 10. Find the number." Solution: Subtract 3 from both sides → x/5 = 7; multiply both sides by 5 → x = 35. A poor answer might not match the equation structure (e.g., using multiplication first) or have incorrect balancing steps.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often make two specific errors: (1) Distributive property error: writing 3(x+2) = 3x+2 instead of 3x+6, forgetting to multiply the second term. (2) Fraction clearing error: when solving (x/2) + 3 = 5, multiplying only the x/2 term by 2 (getting x + 3 = 5) instead of multiplying every term by 2 (getting x + 6 = 10). Both break the balance because the operation isn't applied to the entire side.
## ON_FAILURE
For distributive errors: use an area model. Draw a rectangle of length 3 and width (x+2). Show area = 3x+6. Have the student physically partition the rectangle. For fraction errors: emphasize "multiply EVERY term by the LCM of denominators." Write each term as a separate fraction first: (x/2) + (3/1) = (5/1). Then multiply each by 2: x + 6 = 10. Practice with 3-4 similar equations until the habit forms.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Riya bought 3 notebooks and a ₹15 pen for a total of ₹75. If each notebook costs the same, find the cost of one notebook using the balancing method.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Let the cost of one notebook be x rupees. The equation is 3x + 15 = 75.
- Step 2: To isolate the term with x, subtract 15 from both sides (balancing): 3x + 15 - 15 = 75 - 15 → 3x = 60.
- Step 3: To isolate x, divide both sides by 3 (balancing): 3x/3 = 60/3 → x = 20.
- Step 4: Verify: 3 × 20 + 15 = 60 + 15 = 75. The solution is correct.
- Step 5: Interpret: Each notebook costs ₹20.
REASONING: We reverse the operations applied to x (multiplication by 3 then addition of 15) by doing the inverse operations in reverse order: first subtract 15, then divide by 3. Performing each step on both sides keeps the equation balanced.
ANSWER: ₹20
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Solve the equation using the balancing method: (2x - 5)/3 = (x + 1)/2
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the equation has fractions with denominators 3 and 2. LCM is 6. Multiply both sides by 6 to clear fractions: 6 × (2x - 5)/3 = 6 × (x + 1)/2.
- Step 2: Simplify each side: 2(2x - 5) = 3(x + 1) → 4x - 10 = 3x + 3.
- Step 3: Collect variable terms on one side: subtract 3x from both sides: 4x - 3x - 10 = 3 → x - 10 = 3.
- Step 4: Isolate x: add 10 to both sides: x = 13.
- Step 5: Verify: LHS = (2×13 - 5)/3 = (26-5)/3 = 21/3 = 7. RHS = (13+1)/2 = 14/2 = 7. LHS = RHS.
REASONING: Clearing fractions first avoids fractional arithmetic. Then we use balancing to collect variable terms on one side and constants on the other, maintaining equality at each step.
ANSWER: x = 13
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: The perimeter of a rectangle is 54 cm. The length is 3 cm more than twice the breadth. Find the length and breadth by forming and solving a linear equation using the balancing method.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Let breadth = b cm. Then length = (2b + 3) cm. Perimeter = 2(length + breadth) = 54.
- Step 2: Form equation: 2((2b + 3) + b) = 54 → 2(3b + 3) = 54.
- Step 3: Divide both sides by 2 (balancing): 3b + 3 = 27.
- Step 4: Subtract 3 from both sides: 3b = 24.
- Step 5: Divide both sides by 3: b = 8.
- Step 6: Find length: 2b + 3 = 2×8 + 3 = 19 cm. Verify perimeter: 2(19+8)=2×27=54 cm.
REASONING: We translate the geometric problem into an algebraic equation. The balancing method is applied stepwise: first simplify the equation by dividing both sides by 2, then isolate the variable term, then solve for b, and finally compute the length.
ANSWER: Breadth = 8 cm, Length = 19 cm

QUESTION: A student solves 2(x - 4) = 10 as follows: 2x - 4 = 10 → 2x = 14 → x = 7. Identify the error, explain why it is wrong, and give the correct solution using the balancing method.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand the problem: check the student's working.
- Step 2: Identify the method: balancing method with distributive property.
- Step 3: Apply: The error is in the first step: 2(x-4) expands to 2x - 8, not 2x - 4. Correct steps: 2x - 8 = 10 → 2x = 18 → x = 9.
- Step 4: Verify: 2(9-4) = 2×5 = 10. Correct.
ANSWER: The student forgot to multiply 2 by -4. Correct solution: x = 9.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: The sum of three consecutive multiples of 7 is 252. Form an equation using the balancing method concept and find the multiples. Explain why your equation correctly represents the problem.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions
STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand: consecutive multiples of 7: let them be 7x, 7(x+1), 7(x+2) OR 7x, 7x+7, 7x+14. Alternatively, let the first multiple be y, then y, y+7, y+14.
- Step 2: Identify method: form equation and solve by balancing.
- Step 3: Apply: Let the multiples be 7x, 7x+7, 7x+14. Sum = 7x + 7x+7 + 7x+14 = 21x + 21 = 252.
- Step 4: Solve: 21x + 21 = 252 → subtract 21 from both sides: 21x = 231 → divide by 21: x = 11.
- Step 5: Find multiples: 7×11=77, 84, 91. Check: 77+84+91=252.
ANSWER: The multiples are 77, 84, and 91. The equation 21x + 21 = 252 correctly represents the sum because we expressed each multiple in terms of x and added them.
DIFFICULTY: medium
