## ELI5
Walk a diagonal corridor across a passage's parallel walls and look at the two angles tucked inside on the same side of the crossing line: co-interior angles, also called consecutive interior angles. Their promise differs from the alternate twins - when the walls are parallel, the pair is supplementary, the two adding to a straight angle like two students sharing one bench. Equal pairs prove parallelism one way; pairs that sum to a half-turn prove it the other. Same side, inside, sum - that is the trio.

## DEFINITION
Interior angles on the same side of a transversal (also known as consecutive interior angles or co-interior angles) are two angles that lie on the interior of two lines and on the same side of the transversal line cutting them. When the two lines intersected by the transversal are parallel, these co-interior angles are supplementary, meaning their sum is exactly $180^\circ$.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are walking straight down a wide school corridor (represented by two parallel walls) and a diagonal corridor crosses right through them, forming a sharp corner where security cameras are mounted. When a line (the transversal) cuts across two other lines, it creates pairs of angles tucked inside the two lines, hugging the exact same side of the crossing road. If the main corridor walls are perfectly parallel, these two inside-corner angles have a special relationship: they always add up to $180^\circ$, just like angles on a straight line! We begin by locating the transversal line, identifying the interior region between the two main lines, and looking for the pair of angles sitting on the same side. Recalling how to solve linear equations from algebra, we set their sum equal to $180^\circ$ and solve for the unknown variable $x$. The key insight is that co-interior angles between parallel lines are supplementary, giving us an equation we can solve instantly.

**Takeaway:** co-interior angles are the same-side interior pair; with parallel lines they are supplementary. **In the exam:** decide first whether the pair is equal-type or sum-type, then write the equation the geometry licenses. **Check yourself:** sketch the corridor crossing, shade the same-side interior pair, and total them aloud. **Why students miss it:** they treat co-interior pairs as equal twins because the alternate rule feels more famous.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. If one interior angle on the same side of the transversal measures $75^\circ$, what is the measure of the other interior angle on that same side?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $105^\circ$. Working steps: 1) Identify that interior angles on the same side of a transversal between parallel lines are supplementary. 2) Write the equation: $\text{Angle}_1 + \text{Angle}_2 = 180^\circ$. 3) Substitute the known value: $75^\circ + \text{Angle}_2 = 180^\circ$. 4) Subtract $75^\circ$ from $180^\circ$ to get $\text{Angle}_2 = 180^\circ - 75^\circ = 105^\circ$. A student who answers $75^\circ$ incorrectly assumes co-interior angles are equal (confusing them with corresponding or alternate interior angles).

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently confuse interior angles on the same side of a transversal with alternate interior angles or corresponding angles. When given two co-interior angles like $(2x)^\circ$ and $(120)^\circ$, they mistakenly set them equal to each other ($2x = 120$) instead of setting their sum to $180^\circ$ ($2x + 120 = 180$), applying the wrong geometric rule.
## ON_FAILURE

Have them physically draw a straight line extended from the transversal to see how the co-interior angles combine to form a linear pair.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: In a school architectural design, two parallel fences, Fence 1 and Fence 2, are crossed by a straight metal rod serving as a transversal. Two interior angles on the same side of the transversal are given as $3x$ and $2x + 30^\circ$. Find the value of $x$ and the measure of both angles in degrees.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that the fences are parallel, which means the interior angles on the same side of the transversal are supplementary. Write down the given expressions: $\text{Angle}_1 = 3x$ and $\text{Angle}_2 = 2x + 30^\circ$.
- Step 2: Set up the equation using the supplementary rule where their sum equals $180^\circ$: $3x + (2x + 30^\circ) = 180^\circ$.
- Step 3: Combine like terms on the left side of the equation: $5x + 30 = 180$.
- Step 4: Isolate the variable term by subtracting 30 from both sides: $5x = 180 - 30$, which simplifies to $5x = 150$. Divide both sides by 5: $x = 150 / 5 = 30$.
- Step 5: Substitute $x = 30$ back into the angle expressions: $\text{Angle}_1 = 3(30) = 90^\circ$ and $\text{Angle}_2 = 2(30) + 30 = 60 + 30 = 90^\circ$. Verify the sum: $90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ$.
REASONING: Because the lines are parallel, co-interior angles must add up to $180^\circ$. We translate this geometric rule into an algebraic equation, combine like terms, and solve for $x$ just like in linear equation problems.
ANSWER: x = 30, Angle 1 = 90 degrees, Angle 2 = 90 degrees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Two parallel railway tracks are intersected by a signaling cable acting as a transversal. One co-interior angle measures $(4x - 10)^\circ$ and the other measures $(5x + 10)^\circ$. Calculate the exact measure of each angle.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given co-interior angles: $(4x - 10)^\circ$ and $(5x + 10)^\circ$ formed by parallel lines and a transversal.
- Step 2: Apply the co-interior angle property for parallel lines: $(4x - 10) + (5x + 10) = 180$.
- Step 3: Group the like terms (combine $4x$ with $5x$ and $-10$ with $+10$): $9x + 0 = 180$, which means $9x = 180$.
- Step 4: Solve for $x$ by dividing $180$ by $9$: $x = 180 / 9 = 20$.
- Step 5: Substitute $x = 20$ into both expressions to find the angle measures: First angle = $4(20) - 10 = 80 - 10 = 70^\circ$. Second angle = $5(20) + 10 = 100 + 10 = 110^\circ$. Verify: $70^\circ + 110^\circ = 180^\circ$.
REASONING: The $-10$ and $+10$ cancel out when adding the expressions, simplifying the algebraic manipulation. The resulting angles correctly sum to $180^\circ$, confirming our solution.
ANSWER: First angle = 70 degrees, Second angle = 110 degrees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A carpenter is cutting wooden beams where two parallel rafters are cut across by a diagonal support beam. The interior angles on the same side of the transversal are given as $(7x - 5)^\circ$ and $(3x + 15)^\circ$. Additionally, the carpenter needs to verify if a third angle adjacent to the first angle forms a linear pair. Find $x$, both co-interior angles, and the measure of the linear pair supplement to the first angle.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write down the equation for the co-interior angles on the same side of the transversal of parallel lines: $(7x - 5) + (3x + 15) = 180$.
- Step 2: Combine the like terms for $x$ ($7x + 3x = 10x$) and numbers ($-5 + 15 = 10$): $10x + 10 = 180$.
- Step 3: Subtract 10 from both sides of the equation: $10x = 180 - 10$, so $10x = 170$.
- Step 4: Divide both sides by 10 to find $x$: $x = 17$, so $x = 17$.
- Step 5: Calculate the first co-interior angle: $7(17) - 5 = 119 - 5 = 114^\circ$. Calculate the second co-interior angle: $3(17) + 15 = 51 + 15 = 66^\circ$. Verify sum: $114^\circ + 66^\circ = 180^\circ$.
- Step 6: Find the linear pair supplement to the first angle: $180^\circ - 114^\circ = 66^\circ$. (Notice this matches the second co-interior angle because of alternate interior / corresponding relationships!).
REASONING: Combining algebraic simplification with multiple angle properties allows us to cross-check our work. The linear pair supplement of the first co-interior angle equals the second co-interior angle, confirming the consistency of our geometry principles.
ANSWER: x = 17, Angle 1 = 114 degrees, Angle 2 = 66 degrees, Linear pair supplement = 66 degrees

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: In a park grid, two parallel walking paths are crossed by a jogging track. Two co-interior angles are represented as $(6x - 12)^\circ$ and $(3x + 30)^\circ$. A surveyor measures the second angle and reports it is an obtuse angle. Calculate $x$, determine the exact numerical values of both angles, and mathematically verify whether the surveyor's observation about the second angle being obtuse is correct.
COMPETENCY: Multi-step algebraic modeling and geometric validation
STEPS:
- Step 1: Set up the equation for co-interior angles on parallel lines: $(6x - 12) + (3x + 30) = 180$.
- Step 2: Simplify and combine like terms: $9x + 18 = 180$.
- Step 3: Isolate the variable term: $9x = 180 - 18$, so $9x = 162$. Solve for $x$: $x = 162 / 9 = 18$.
- Step 4: Substitute $x = 18$ into both angle expressions: Angle 1 = $6(18) - 12 = 108 - 12 = 96^\circ$. Angle 2 = $3(18) + 30 = 54 + 30 = 84^\circ$. Wait, let's re-verify! Ah, Angle 1 = $96^\circ$ and Angle 2 = $84^\circ$. Let's check their sum: $96 + 84 = 180^\circ$.
- Step 5: Re-evaluate the surveyor's observation: Angle 2 is $84^\circ$, which is *acute*, not obtuse! Therefore, the surveyor's observation is incorrect.
ANSWER: x = 18, Angle 1 = 96 degrees, Angle 2 = 84 degrees. The surveyor is incorrect because Angle 2 measures 84 degrees, making it an acute angle, not obtuse.
