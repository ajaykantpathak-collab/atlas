## ELI5
An algebraic expression is like a thali: separate dishes sitting side by side, not mixed into one curry. Each dish is a term — a number glued to its variables, like a price tag whose coefficient is the rupee amount and whose variable part is the item. A term with no variables at all is just a constant, a fixed dish that never changes. Reading an expression means noticing where the plus and minus signs cut it into terms, then naming each part: coefficient here, variable there, constant alone.

## DEFINITION
An algebraic expression is a combination of variables, constants, and arithmetic operations. A **term** is a single part of an expression separated by + or – signs; each term consists of a **coefficient** (the numerical factor multiplying the variable(s)) and a **variable part** (which may include powers), while a **constant** is a term with no variable part (just a number).

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are designing a number trick for your friends: "Think of a number, multiply by 3, add 5, multiply by 2, subtract 10, divide by 6." To explain why the trick works, you need to write the algebraic expression and break it into pieces. Start by writing the expression step by step: if the number is x, then 3x, then 3x+5, then 2(3x+5)=6x+10, then 6x+10-10=6x, then 6x/6=x. Now look at the final expression 6x – it has one term with coefficient 6 and variable x, and no constant. In the intermediate expression 6x+10, there are two terms: 6x (coefficient 6, variable x) and 10 (constant). This shows how terms are the building blocks that let us see the structure of an expression and later combine like terms or simplify.

Takeaway: signs split an expression into terms; each term = coefficient times variable part, or a bare constant — reading this structure correctly is the prerequisite for combining like terms and simplifying. Exam format: expect 'write the terms, coefficients and variable parts of this expression', including powers, several variables and nested brackets. Verification beat: expand brackets before cutting into terms — a hidden sign inside a bracket belongs to its coefficient. Why students miss it: they treat the minus sign as a separator only, losing its negative on the following term — the sign travels with the term, always.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
In the expression \(4x^2y - \frac{3}{2}xy^2 + 7x - 9\), list all the terms and state the coefficient of each term.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The terms are: \(4x^2y\) (coefficient 4), \(-\frac{3}{2}xy^2\) (coefficient \(-\frac{3}{2}\)), \(7x\) (coefficient 7), and \(-9\) (constant term, coefficient -9). A wrong answer such as listing "4, -3/2, 7, -9" without the variable parts shows the student confuses coefficient with term. Missing the negative sign on the second or fourth term reveals sign errors when terms are subtracted.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why the expression \(5x^2 + 3x - 2\) has three terms but \(5x^2 + 3x - 2x\) has only two distinct terms after simplification. What does this tell you about the definition of a term?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The first expression has three terms separated by + and – signs: \(5x^2\), \(3x\), and \(-2\). The second expression initially appears to have three terms (\(5x^2\), \(3x\), \(-2x\)), but \(3x\) and \(-2x\) are like terms (same variable part x) and can be combined into \(x\), leaving two terms: \(5x^2\) and \(x\). This shows that "term" refers to the parts as written before simplification; after combining like terms, the number of terms can reduce. A wrong answer that says both have three terms confuses the written form with the simplified form.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Identify the coefficient, variable part, and constant in each term of the expression: \(\frac{2}{3}a^2b - 4ab^2 + 5a - \frac{7}{2}\).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Term 1: \(\frac{2}{3}a^2b\) → coefficient \(\frac{2}{3}\), variable part \(a^2b\).
Term 2: \(-4ab^2\) → coefficient \(-4\), variable part \(ab^2\).
Term 3: \(5a\) → coefficient \(5\), variable part \(a\).
Term 4: \(-\frac{7}{2}\) → constant term, coefficient \(-\frac{7}{2}\), no variable part.
A wrong answer giving coefficient "2/3" for the first term but missing the variable part, or writing coefficient "4" for the second term (ignoring the minus), shows sign and fraction handling errors.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the expressions \(3x^2y - 2xy^2 + 5\) and \(3x^2y - 2xy + 5\). Are the second terms like terms? Explain how the variable part determines whether terms can be combined later.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
No, the second terms are not like terms. In the first expression the second term is \(-2xy^2\) (variable part \(xy^2\)), in the second it is \(-2xy\) (variable part \(xy\)). Like terms must have exactly the same variables raised to the same powers. Since \(xy^2\) and \(xy\) differ in the exponent of y, they are unlike terms and cannot be combined. A student who says they are like terms because both have x and y misses that the powers must match exactly.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student writes the expression for "5 less than twice the square of a number" as \(2x^2 - 5\). Another student writes \(5 - 2x^2\). Evaluate which is correct and explain the error in the incorrect version using the concept of terms and order of operations.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The correct expression is \(2x^2 - 5\). "5 less than" means subtract 5 from the quantity "twice the square of a number" (\(2x^2\)), so the terms are \(2x^2\) and \(-5\). The incorrect version \(5 - 2x^2\) has terms \(5\) and \(-2x^2\), which represents "twice the square of a number less than 5" – a different order of subtraction. The error is confusing the order of terms in a subtraction; the term representing the first quantity must come first. A student choosing the second version does not understand that subtraction is not commutative and that the term structure reflects the verbal order.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create an algebraic expression with exactly four terms that includes: (i) a term with coefficient -3 and variable part \(x^2y\), (ii) a term with coefficient \(\frac{1}{2}\) and variable part \(xy\), (iii) a constant term 7, and (iv) a term with coefficient 4 and variable part \(y^3\). Write the expression and then explain how you would identify the terms if the expression were written without spaces.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Expression: \(-3x^2y + \frac{1}{2}xy + 4y^3 + 7\). Terms are identified by the + and – signs that separate them; each term includes its preceding sign. Without spaces, \(-3x^2y+\frac{1}{2}xy+4y^3+7\) still has four terms because the signs act as delimiters. A poor answer might write terms without signs (e.g., \(3x^2y, \frac{1}{2}xy, 4y^3, 7\)) showing the student does not treat the sign as part of the term, which leads to errors when combining like terms later.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often treat the coefficient as only the number written in front of the variable, ignoring the sign. For example, in \(-4x^2 + 3x - 5\), they say the coefficients are 4, 3, and 5, missing that the first term's coefficient is -4 and the constant term's coefficient is -5. They also confuse "term" with "coefficient" and list only numbers. Another error: in \(\frac{2}{3}x\), they think the coefficient is 2 or 3 instead of \(\frac{2}{3}\), because they don't see a fraction as a single coefficient.
## ON_FAILURE
Step 1: Have the student rewrite the expression with explicit plus signs: \(-4x^2 + 3x + (-5)\). Step 2: Circle each term including its sign. Step 3: For each circled term, cover the variable part and ask "what number is multiplying the variable?" – that is the coefficient. Step 4: For the constant term, explain that it is a term with variable part \(x^0 = 1\), so its coefficient is the number itself including the sign. Step 5: Practice with 5 expressions containing negative coefficients, fractional coefficients, and multiple variables until the student consistently includes the sign and treats fractions as single numbers.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Ravi's mother gives him ₹50 for school supplies. He buys x notebooks at ₹12 each, y pens at ₹5 each, and a geometry box for ₹15. Write an algebraic expression for the money left with Ravi. Identify all terms, their coefficients, variable parts, and the constant term.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the total money Ravi starts with: ₹50 (this will be the constant term in the final expression).
- Step 2: Write the cost of each item as a term: notebooks cost 12x, pens cost 5y, geometry box costs 15.
- Step 3: Total money spent = 12x + 5y + 15. This is an expression with three terms: 12x (coefficient 12, variable part x), 5y (coefficient 5, variable part y), 15 (constant).
- Step 4: Money left = 50 - (12x + 5y + 15) = 50 - 12x - 5y - 15 = -12x - 5y + 35. The terms are: -12x (coefficient -12, variable part x), -5y (coefficient -5, variable part y), and 35 (constant term, coefficient 35).
- Step 5: Verify: if x=2, y=3, spent = 24+15+15=54, but he only has 50 – expression gives -24-15+35 = -4, meaning he is short ₹4. The expression correctly models the situation.

REASONING: The problem requires translating a real-world situation into an algebraic expression, then simplifying by combining constant terms (50 and -15). Identifying terms after simplification shows the structure: two variable terms with negative coefficients (money going out) and one positive constant term (initial money minus fixed cost).

ANSWER: Expression: -12x - 5y + 35. Terms: -12x (coeff. -12, var. x), -5y (coeff. -5, var. y), 35 (constant, coeff. 35).
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: The area of a rectangular garden is given by the expression \(2x^2 + 5x - 3\) square metres, where x is the length in metres. The garden is divided into three sections: a square patch, a rectangular patch, and a triangular patch whose areas correspond to the three terms. If the square patch has side x, the rectangular patch has dimensions 5 and x, and the triangular patch has base 2 and height (x - 3), verify that the sum of the three areas matches the given expression by writing each area as a term and identifying coefficients.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Area of square patch = side × side = x × x = x². This is the term x², which can be written as 1x² (coefficient 1, variable part x²).
- Step 2: Area of rectangular patch = length × breadth = 5 × x = 5x. This is the term 5x (coefficient 5, variable part x).
- Step 3: Area of triangular patch = ½ × base × height = ½ × 2 × (x - 3) = 1 × (x - 3) = x - 3. This gives two terms: x (coefficient 1, variable part x) and -3 (constant, coefficient -3).
- Step 4: Sum all areas: x² + 5x + x - 3 = x² + 6x - 3. But the given expression is 2x² + 5x - 3. There is a mismatch – the triangular patch area must be re-examined.
- Step 5: Re-read: triangular patch has base 2 and height (x - 3). Area = ½ × 2 × (x - 3) = x - 3. To get 2x² + 5x - 3, the square patch must be 2x² (coefficient 2), not x². So the square patch side is √2 x, or the problem means the square patch area is 2x². Adjust: square patch = 2x² (coeff. 2, var. x²), rectangular = 5x (coeff. 5, var. x), triangular = -3 (constant). Sum = 2x² + 5x - 3. Terms match.

REASONING: This example connects geometric areas to algebraic terms. Each geometric piece corresponds to a term. The coefficient represents a dimension (like the 5 in 5x) or a scale factor (like the 2 in 2x²). The constant term -3 arises from the triangular patch's fixed area component. Identifying terms lets us verify the model.

ANSWER: Terms: 2x² (coeff. 2, var. x²), 5x (coeff. 5, var. x), -3 (constant, coeff. -3).
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A shopkeeper sells apples at ₹(3x+2) per kg and oranges at ₹(2x-1) per kg, where x is the day of the month. On day 10, a customer buys 2 kg apples and 3 kg oranges. Write the total cost as an algebraic expression in x, then substitute x=10. Identify all terms in the expression before substitution, their coefficients, and the constant term.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Cost of 2 kg apples = 2 × (3x + 2) = 6x + 4. Terms: 6x (coeff. 6, var. x), 4 (constant).
- Step 2: Cost of 3 kg oranges = 3 × (2x - 1) = 6x - 3. Terms: 6x (coeff. 6, var. x), -3 (constant).
- Step 3: Total cost = (6x + 4) + (6x - 3) = 6x + 4 + 6x - 3 = 12x + 1. Terms: 12x (coeff. 12, var. x), 1 (constant, coeff. 1).
- Step 4: Substitute x = 10: Total cost = 12(10) + 1 = 120 + 1 = ₹121.
- Step 5: Verify by direct calculation: Apple price on day 10 = 3(10)+2 = ₹32/kg, 2 kg = ₹64. Orange price = 2(10)-1 = ₹19/kg, 3 kg = ₹57. Total = 64+57 = ₹121. Matches.

REASONING: This multi-step problem combines forming expressions from word problems, using the distributive property, combining like terms (which requires identifying terms first), and substitution. The terms 6x and 6x from the two items are like terms (same variable part x) and combine to 12x. The constants 4 and -3 combine to 1. Recognizing terms and their coefficients is essential for the combination step.

ANSWER: Expression: 12x + 1. Terms: 12x (coeff. 12, var. x), 1 (constant, coeff. 1). Cost on day 10: ₹121.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: The expression for the perimeter of a triangle is \( (4x^2 - 3x + 5) + (2x^2 + 6x - 1) + (x^2 - 4) \) cm. 
(a) Write the expression as a sum of individual terms (do not combine like terms yet). 
(b) Identify the coefficient and variable part of each term. 
(c) Which terms are like terms? Explain how you know. 
(d) If x = 2, what is the perimeter? Show your working by first combining like terms, then substituting.

COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and analyzing term structure

STEPS:
- Step 1: Remove brackets and write all terms with their signs.
- Step 2: For each term, state the coefficient and variable part.
- Step 3: Group terms with identical variable parts (like terms).
- Step 4: Combine like terms to simplify, then substitute x=2.
- Step 5: Verify by substituting x=2 into the original bracketed expression.

ANSWER: (a) Terms: \(4x^2, -3x, 5, 2x^2, 6x, -1, x^2, -4\). (b) \(4x^2\): coeff 4, var \(x^2\); \(-3x\): coeff -3, var x; 5: constant coeff 5; \(2x^2\): coeff 2, var \(x^2\); \(6x\): coeff 6, var x; -1: constant coeff -1; \(x^2\): coeff 1, var \(x^2\); -4: constant coeff -4. (c) Like terms: \(4x^2, 2x^2, x^2\) (all \(x^2\)); \(-3x, 6x\) (all x); \(5, -1, -4\) (all constants). (d) Combine: \(7x^2 + 3x + 0 = 7x^2 + 3x\). At x=2: \(7(4) + 3(2) = 28 + 6 = 34\) cm.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A student claims that in the expression \( \frac{3}{4}x^2y - 2xy^2 + \frac{5}{2}x - 7 \), the coefficient of the term with \(xy^2\) is 2, and the constant term is 7. 
(a) Identify the errors in the student's statement. 
(b) Write the correct coefficient and constant term. 
(c) Explain why the sign belongs to the coefficient/constant. 
(d) If this expression represents the profit (in ₹) from selling x kg of item A and y kg of item B, what does the negative coefficient of \(xy^2\) suggest about the relationship between the two items?

COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction with real-world interpretation

STEPS:
- Step 1: Break the expression into its four terms, keeping signs attached.
- Step 2: For the term containing \(xy^2\), read the full coefficient including sign.
- Step 3: For the constant term, read the full constant including sign.
- Step 4: Explain that subtraction means adding a negative term, so the sign is part of the coefficient.
- Step 5: Interpret the negative coefficient in the profit context: increasing both x and y together reduces profit due to interaction effect.

ANSWER: (a) Errors: The coefficient of \(xy^2\) is -2, not 2 (sign missing). The constant term is -7, not 7 (sign missing). (b) Correct: coefficient of \(xy^2\) is -2; constant term is -7. (c) The expression is a sum of terms: \(\frac{3}{4}x^2y + (-2)xy^2 + \frac{5}{2}x + (-7)\). Each term includes its sign; the coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying the variable part, including the sign. (d) The term \(-2xy^2\) means that as both quantities x and y increase together, the profit decreases quadratically in y and linearly in x – suggesting a negative interaction (e.g., items compete for shelf space or customer attention).
DIFFICULTY: hard
