## ELI5
At a vegetable market the vendor loads 2 kg of potatoes on one pan and iron weights on the other until the beam sits level - that level beam is an equation. The equals sign is the pivot: whatever sits on the left must weigh exactly what sits on the right. An expression is a shopping list with no beam; an equation has the beam, and at least one pan hides an unknown waiting to be named.

## DEFINITION
An equation is a mathematical statement that shows two algebraic expressions are equal, connected by an equals sign ($=$), where at least one expression contains an unknown variable. It acts like a balanced weighing scale, meaning the numerical value of the expression on the left-hand side (LHS) must be exactly equal to the numerical value of the right-hand side (RHS) for specific values of the variable.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you go to a local vegetable market in India with your mother, and the vendor uses a traditional beam balance. On one pan, the vendor puts 2 kg of potatoes, and on the other pan, he adds standard iron weights until the beam is completely level and balanced. In mathematics, we represent this balanced state using an equals sign ($=$) between two mathematical expressions. We have already learned how to write algebraic expressions using variables in Class 6, but now we connect two expressions together to form a complete mathematical sentence. If we do not know the exact weight of a certain bundle of vegetables, we assign a variable like $x$ to it, making it part of an equation. The key insight here is that an equation always contains an equals sign, whereas an expression does not.

**Takeaway:** an equation is a balanced beam: the LHS must weigh exactly the RHS. **In the exam:** check a claimed solution by substituting it into both pans and seeing whether the beam stays level. **Check yourself:** test the market scene: potatoes on one pan, iron weights on the other, and the level beam is the equals sign at work. **Why students miss it:** students try to solve expressions or treat the equals sign as 'write the answer here' - the beam model forbids both. Only a statement carrying an equals sign can be solved, and only a value that keeps both pans level satisfies it; anything else tips the vendor's balance and fails the substitution.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which of the following mathematical statements represents an equation?
A) $3x + 5$
B) $7y - 2 = 12$
C) $4 \times 5 - 3$
D) $2a + b > 8$
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is B) $7y - 2 = 12$. An equation is specifically defined as a statement of equality between two expressions, which requires an equals sign ($=$). Option A is an algebraic expression, Option C is a numerical arithmetic expression, and Option D is an inequality, not an equation. A student who chooses Option A confuses expressions with equations, failing to notice the absence of an equals sign.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Consider the equation $5x + 3 = 18$. Explain what the equals sign ($=$) tells us about the relationship between the expression on the left and the expression on the right.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The equals sign indicates that when the correct value for $x$ is substituted, the numerical value computed for the left-hand expression ($5x + 3$) is exactly equal in value to the right-hand expression ($18$). A wrong answer often states that the equals sign just means "write the answer here" or "do the calculation," showing a lack of understanding that an equation represents a permanent balance or equivalence between two quantities.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Substitute $x = 4$ into the equation $3x - 5 = 2x + 1$ to check whether both sides balance. Show the step-by-step evaluation for LHS and RHS.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Evaluate the Left-Hand Side (LHS) by substituting $x = 4$. 
LHS $= 3(4) - 5 = 12 - 5 = 7$.
Step 2: Evaluate the Right-Hand Side (RHS) by substituting $x = 4$. 
RHS $= 2(4) + 1 = 8 + 1 = 9$.
Step 3: Compare LHS and RHS. Since LHS ($7$) $\neq$ RHS ($9$), the value $x = 4$ does not make the equation balanced. 
A student who gets $7 = 7$ has made a substitution error on the RHS by miscalculating $2 \times 4$ or adding incorrectly.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the two statements: Statement 1: $4x + 7$ and Statement 2: $4x + 7 = 23$. Explain why Statement 1 cannot be solved to find a specific numerical value for $x$, while Statement 2 can.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Statement 1 is an expression, which changes its numerical value depending on whatever number we choose to substitute for $x$. Statement 2 is an equation containing a fixed equality condition, constraining $x$ to a single specific value that makes the balance true. A wrong answer suggests that any algebraic string of letters and numbers can be "solved," failing to grasp that finding an unknown requires a boundary condition of equality.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that $x = 6$ is the solution to the equation $2(x - 3) = 12$. Evaluate this claim by testing the solution and identifying any error in the student's reasoning.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The student's claim is incorrect. Let us substitute $x = 6$ into the LHS: $2(6 - 3) = 2(3) = 6$. The RHS is $12$. Since $6 \neq 12$, $x = 6$ is not a solution. The student likely multiplied incorrectly or confused the equation with $2x - 3 = 12$. A wrong answer that accepts the student's claim indicates a failure to properly evaluate brackets using order of operations (BODMAS).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Construct a real-world word problem about buying notebooks and pens at a local stationery shop in rupees that can be represented by the equation $15x + 50 = 200$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse an algebraic expression with an equation. When asked to "solve" an expression like $4x - 5$, they treat it as an equation and try to "find $x$" by setting it equal to zero or inventing a right-hand side, rather than understanding that expressions can only be simplified or evaluated for a given value, whereas equations require an equivalence condition to find an unknown.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to look closely for the equals sign ($=$). Cover up the right side of an equation and show that it consists of two distinct mathematical expressions separated by a balance point. Emphasize: "Expressions are phrases we simplify; equations are complete sentences with an equals sign that we solve."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Priya has a piggy bank. She adds 5 ₹10 coins to her savings box. If the total money currently in the box equals ₹95, write this situation as an algebraic equation where $x$ represents her previous amount of money in rupees, and verify if $x = 45$ is the solution.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the unknown variable. Let Priya's previous money be $x$ rupees.
- Step 2: Form the expression for the addition of money. Adding 5 coins of ₹10 means adding $5 \times 10 = 50$ rupees, so the LHS expression is $x + 50$.
- Step 3: Set up the equation by equating the LHS expression to the given total RHS value of 95. The equation is $x + 50 = 95$.
- Step 4: Verify the solution by substituting $x = 45$ into the LHS: $\text{LHS} = 45 + 50 = 95$.
- Step 5: Compare LHS and RHS. Since $\text{LHS} = 95$ and $\text{RHS} = 95$, both sides balance, confirming $x = 45$ is the correct solution.
REASONING: Setting up an equation requires translating a word problem into an expression for the total quantity and equating it to the known total. Verification ensures that our proposed unknown value maintains the balance of the equation.
ANSWER: Equation is $x + 50 = 95$; $x = 45$ is verified as the solution.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An equilateral triangle has a side length of $(2y + 1)$ cm, and its perimeter is given as 21 cm. Formulate an equation representing this geometric property and test whether $y = 3$ or $y = 4$ is the correct solution.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall that an equilateral triangle has 3 equal sides. The perimeter is calculated as $3 \times \text{side length}$.
- Step 2: Write the algebraic expression for the perimeter: $3(2y + 1)$.
- Step 3: Formulate the equation by setting the perimeter expression equal to the given total perimeter: $3(2y + 1) = 21$.
- Step 4: Test $y = 3$ by substituting into LHS: $3(2(3) + 1) = 3(6 + 1) = 3(7) = 21$. Since $\text{LHS} = 21$ equals $\text{RHS} = 21$, $y = 3$ is a solution.
- Step 5: Test $y = 4$ by substituting into LHS: $3(2(4) + 1) = 3(8 + 1) = 3(9) = 27$. Since $27 \neq 21$, $y = 4$ is not a solution.
REASONING: Geometric properties like perimeter provide the relationship needed to build an equation. Substitution allows us to test candidate values to see which one satisfies the equality condition.
ANSWER: Equation: $3(2y + 1) = 21$; Solution is $y = 3$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Rohan thinks of a number, multiplies it by 4, and subtracts 7. He gets the same result as when he multiplies the same number by 2 and adds 5. Write an equation for this puzzle and verify if $x = 6$ solves it.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Let the unknown number be $x$.
- Step 2: Translate the first part of the sentence into an algebraic expression: "multiplies it by 4, and subtracts 7" gives $4x - 7$.
- Step 3: Translate the second part into an algebraic expression: "multiplies the same number by 2 and adds 5" gives $2x + 5$.
- Step 4: Formulate the equation by setting both expressions equal to each other since they result in the same value: $4x - 7 = 2x + 5$.
- Step 5: Substitute $x = 6$ into the LHS: $4(6) - 7 = 24 - 7 = 17$.
- Step 6: Substitute $x = 6$ into the RHS: $2(6) + 5 = 12 + 5 = 17$. Since $\text{LHS} = \text{RHS} = 17$, $x = 6$ successfully satisfies the equation.
REASONING: Word puzzles often describe two different operations leading to the same outcome, which translates naturally into an equation with expressions on both the left and right sides.
ANSWER: Equation: $4x - 7 = 2x + 5$; $x = 6$ is verified as the correct solution.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A school auditorium has rows of seats. Each row has $r$ seats, and there are 12 rows in total, plus an extra 8 VIP seats placed near the stage, giving a total capacity of 104 seats. 
Part A: Write an equation representing this total capacity.
Part B: Without solving formally, check if $r = 8$ or $r = 9$ is the correct number of seats per row.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and verifying solutions
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify terms—12 rows of $r$ seats equals $12r$, plus 8 VIP seats gives the expression $12r + 8$.
- Step 2: Equate this expression to the total capacity of 104 to form the equation $12r + 8 = 104$.
- Step 3: Test $r = 8$ by evaluating $12(8) + 8 = 96 + 8 = 104$.
- Step 4: Test $r = 9$ by evaluating $12(9) + 8 = 108 + 8 = 116$.
ANSWER: Part A: $12r + 8 = 104$. Part B: $r = 8$ is the correct solution because it balances the equation to 104, whereas $r = 9$ results in 116.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students, Amit and Sneha, are given the statement: "Five times a number decreased by 3 is equal to twice the number increased by 9." 
Amit writes the equation as: $5x - 3 = 2x + 9$.
Sneha writes the equation as: $5(x - 3) = 2(x + 9)$.
Identify who wrote the correct equation, explain the linguistic and mathematical reason for the error in the incorrect equation, and verify the solution for the correct equation using $x = 4$.
COMPETENCY: Error identification, linguistic translation to algebra, and verification
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze Amit's translation: "Five times a number" ($5x$) "decreased by 3" ($-3$) "is equal to" ($=$) "twice the number" ($2x$) "increased by 9" ($+9$). This matches $5x - 3 = 2x + 9$.
- Step 2: Analyze Sneha's translation: Sneha used brackets, which implies subtracting 3 *before* multiplying by 5 ("five times *the quantity* of a number decreased by 3"), which changes the wording meaning.
- Step 3: Conclude that Amit is correct.
- Step 4: Test $x = 4$ in Amit's equation: $\text{LHS} = 5(4) - 3 = 20 - 3 = 17$; $\text{RHS} = 2(4) + 9 = 8 + 9 = 17$. Both sides match.
ANSWER: Amit is correct. Sneha incorrectly grouped the subtraction inside parentheses due to misinterpreting the order of operations in the verbal phrasing. Testing $x = 4$ in Amit's equation shows $17 = 17$, confirming validity.
DIFFICULTY: hard
