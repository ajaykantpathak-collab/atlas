## ELI5
A school bus travels down a residential road past the corner shop while the wall clock crawls. Speed is the exchange rate between ground and time: distance travelled per unit time, metres per second in SI. One number now compares the tortoise, the bus and the monsoon wind. Divide distance by time, keep the units honest, and motion shrinks to a single comparable figure.

## DEFINITION
Speed is the distance traveled by a moving object per unit of time, defined mathematically as $speed = \frac{distance}{time}$. The standard international (SI) unit of speed is meters per second (m/s), and it measures how fast an object changes its position in a given direction.

## WALKTHROUGH
Observe a school bus traveling down an Indian residential road past a landmark, noting how much ground it covers as a wall clock ticks off seconds. To describe this motion precisely rather than just saying "fast" or "slow," we must measure two things: the total length of the path traveled and the duration taken to cover it. Building upon our understanding of how clocks measure intervals of time and how we compare slow and fast motions, we combine these measurements into a single ratio. This relationship is expressed through the fundamental formula: speed equals distance divided by time.

**Takeaway:** speed is distance over time - metres per second in SI - and unit honesty is half the marks. **In the exam:** write the formula, substitute with units shown, and convert km/h to m/s by that famous factor before comparing. **Check yourself:** watch the walkthrough's bus pass the landmark and compute its speed with the clock you can see. **Why students miss it:** students multiply when they should divide - the flagged unit chaos that ruins otherwise perfect arithmetic. Distance per time means divide, always, and the units must divide along with the numbers. Keep units attached through every step, and wrong-unit answers become structurally impossible rather than merely unlucky.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The standard SI unit of speed is meters per second (m/s), derived from the base quantities of length (measured in meters) and time (measured in seconds). A wrong answer often confuses speed with force or energy units, or mistakenly states that kilometers per hour is the official SI unit rather than a convenient everyday unit.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is it incorrect to state that an object traveling a long distance always has a higher speed than an object traveling a short distance? Explain using the formula for speed.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Speed depends on *both* distance and time, not distance alone. If Object A travels 100 meters in 10 seconds (speed = 10 m/s) and Object B travels 50 meters in 2 seconds (speed = 25 m/s), Object B is faster despite covering a shorter distance because it takes much less time. A wrong answer shows a failure to recognize speed as a ratio, focusing only on the numerator (distance) while ignoring the denominator (time).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A cyclist travels a distance of 180 meters on a flat road in 30 seconds. Calculate the speed of the cyclist in meters per second (m/s).
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Given distance $d = 180\text{ m}$ and time $t = 30\text{ s}$. 
Step 1: Identify the formula: $speed = \frac{distance}{time}$. 
Step 2: Substitute values: $speed = \frac{180\text{ m}}{30\text{ s}}$. 
Step 3: Compute: $180 \div 30 = 6$. 
Step 4: Attach units: $6\text{ m/s}$. 
A wrong answer, such as 5400 m/s (from multiplying distance and time) or 0.16 m/s (from dividing time by distance), indicates confusion over formula placement or arithmetic inversion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Two cars, Car X and Car Y, travel between Mumbai and Pune. Car X covers 150 km in 3 hours, while Car Y covers 200 km in 4 hours. Compare their speeds and determine which car traveled faster.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Speed of Car X = $\frac{150\text{ km}}{3\text{ h}} = 50\text{ km/h}$. Speed of Car Y = $\frac{200\text{ km}}{4\text{ h}} = 50\text{ km/h}$. Both cars traveled at the exact same speed. A wrong answer usually assumes Car Y is faster simply because it covered a greater total distance, ignoring the extra time it took.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student calculates the running speed of an ant to be $2500\text{ m/s}$. Evaluate whether this numerical result is physically reasonable, and explain why or why not.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
This result is entirely unreasonable because $2500\text{ m/s}$ is faster than a fighter jet, whereas ants move very slowly over a fraction of a meter in several seconds. A realistic speed for an ant would be a fraction of a meter per second (e.g., $0.02\text{ m/s}$). A wrong answer shows a lack of physical intuition and failure to check if computational outputs make sense in the real world.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple backyard experiment using a rolling marble, a measuring tape, and a stopwatch to determine the average speed of the marble down an inclined wooden plank.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer will state: Set up a wooden plank at an angle, mark a starting point (0 cm) and a finish point (e.g., 200 cm) using the measuring tape. Release the marble and use the stopwatch to record the exact time $t$ in seconds it takes to roll from start to finish. Finally, divide the measured distance by the recorded time to find the average speed in cm/s. A poor answer fails to include measurable parameters, lacks timing tools, or does not mention the formula calculation.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the units of speed and add or multiply distance and time values arbitrarily instead of dividing them. For example, when given distance = 20 m and time = 5 s, a student might add them to get 25 m/s or multiply them to get 100 m/s because they treat numbers in word problems as operations waiting to happen rather than physical dimensions.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this error, guide the student back to the physical meaning of the words: "per" means "divided by" (just like rupees per kg). Have them physically walk a measured 10-meter path in their classroom, time it with a watch, and write down the fraction $\frac{10\text{ meters}}{seconds}$. Emphasize that the fraction bar in $\text{m/s}$ literally means division.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A school bus travels along a straight highway from Delhi to a picnic spot, covering a distance of 1500 meters in 100 seconds. Calculate the speed of the bus in meters per second.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given values: distance $d = 1500\text{ m}$, time $t = 100\text{ s}$. Unknown to find: speed ($v$).
- Step 2: Select the appropriate formula: $v = \frac{d}{t}$. Check that units are in standard SI form (meters and seconds).
- Step 3: Substitute the given values into the formula: $v = \frac{1500\text{ m}}{100\text{ s}}$.
- Step 4: Perform the division and cancel matching units: $1500 \div 100 = 15$, yielding units of m/s.
- Step 5: Verify physical reasonableness: A bus traveling 15 meters every second is a realistic highway speed ($15\text{ m/s}$).
REASONING: By taking the total length of the path and dividing it by the exact seconds elapsed, we find how many meters the bus traverses in a single second, which is the definition of speed.
ANSWER: 15 m/s
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An athlete runs around a running track, covering a distance of 400 meters in 50 seconds. What is the running speed of the athlete?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Extract given parameters: distance $d = 400\text{ m}$, time $t = 50\text{ s}$.
- Step 2: State the formula relating speed, distance, and time: $v = \frac{d}{t}$.
- Step 3: Substitute values with their respective units: $v = \frac{400\text{ m}}{50\text{ s}}$.
- Step 4: Simplify the fraction: $\frac{40}{5} = 8$, maintaining the unit combination m/s.
- Step 5: Check if the answer is physically plausible: 8 meters per second is an elite sprinting pace, confirming our calculation is correct.
REASONING: Dividing distance by time calculates the unit rate of motion, telling us the distance covered per unit interval of time.
ANSWER: 8 m/s
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A toy train moves along a track. In the first part of its journey, it covers 60 meters in 12 seconds. In the second part, it covers 80 meters in 20 seconds. Calculate the speed for both parts and determine which part had the higher speed.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze Part 1 given values: $d_1 = 60\text{ m}$, $t_1 = 12\text{ s}$.
- Step 2: Compute speed for Part 1: $v_1 = \frac{60\text{ m}}{12\text{ s}} = 5\text{ m/s}$.
- Step 3: Analyze Part 2 given values: $d_2 = 80\text{ m}$, $t_2 = 20\text{ s}$.
- Step 4: Compute speed for Part 2: $v_2 = \frac{80\text{ m}}{20\text{ s}} = 4\text{ m/s}$.
- Step 5: Compare both speeds: $5\text{ m/s} > 4\text{ m/s}$.
- Step 6: Conclude that Part 1 had a higher speed than Part 2 because the ratio of distance to time was greater.
REASONING: Comparing multi-stage motion requires calculating individual speeds separately using the ratio formula before making any comparative judgments.
ANSWER: Part 1 speed is 5 m/s; Part 2 speed is 4 m/s; Part 1 is faster.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two objects, A and B, move for the same duration of time. Object A covers twice the distance of Object B. How does the speed of Object A compare to the speed of Object B? Explain your reasoning using the formula.
COMPETENCY: Predicting outcomes from changing variables
STEPS:
- Step 1: Set up the equation for both objects with time $t$ kept constant.
- Step 2: Write $v_A = \frac{2d}{t}$ and $v_B = \frac{d}{t}$.
- Step 3: Compare the two expressions: $v_A = 2 \times (\frac{d}{t}) = 2 \times v_B$.
- Step 4: Conclude that when time is constant, doubling the distance doubles the speed.
ANSWER: Object A's speed is twice that of Object B because speed is directly proportional to distance when time is constant.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A delivery drone flies across a city. It travels 3 km in the first 5 minutes, and then travels another 3 km in the next 3 minutes. Did the drone maintain a constant speed throughout the flight, or did its speed change? Justify your answer with calculations.
COMPETENCY: Interpreting physical situations using formulas
STEPS:
- Step 1: Convert time intervals into consistent units or keep them separate to calculate speeds in km/min.
- Step 2: Calculate Speed 1: $\frac{3\text{ km}}{5\text{ min}} = 0.6\text{ km/min}$.
- Step 3: Calculate Speed 2: $\frac{3\text{ km}}{3\text{ min}} = 1.0\text{ km/min}$.
- Step 4: Compare the two computed speeds ($0.6\text{ km/min} \neq 1.0\text{ km/min}$).
- Step 5: Conclude that the speed changed because equal distances were covered in unequal time intervals.
ANSWER: The drone did not maintain a constant speed; it accelerated during the second part of the trip because its speed increased from 0.6 km/min to 1.0 km/min.
DIFFICULTY: hard
