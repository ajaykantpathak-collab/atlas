## ELI5
Your teacher says 'buy some notebooks at 25 rupees each' - how many? Nobody knows yet, so algebra writes a letter: n notebooks. A variable is exactly that, a letter holding the place of an unknown or changing quantity, while the 25-rupee price stays a constant. Variables turn repeated sentences into one general rule: the total cost becomes n times 25 whatever n turns out to be. Letters are not mystery numbers to fear; they are tidy placeholders that wait patiently for their value.

## DEFINITION
A variable is a letter or symbol used in algebra to represent an unknown or changing quantity that can take various numerical values. It allows us to express general rules, formulas, and relationships in mathematics concisely.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are buying notebooks for your school opening day at the local stationery shop where each notebook costs ₹25, but the total number of notebooks you need depends on how many your teacher asks you to buy. Instead of writing a new sentence every time, we can use a letter like $n$ to stand for the number of notebooks, making our cost calculation $25 \times n$. In Class 6, you learned how to use basic operations with numbers, fractions, and decimals to compute fixed quantities. Now, in Class 7, we extend this by introducing letters to represent quantities that are unknown or can vary. The key insight is that a letter-number (variable) behaves just like a placeholder for numbers, allowing us to write universal rules that work for any value.

**Takeaway:** a variable is a letter standing in for an unknown or changing quantity; constants are the fixed numbers around it. **In the exam:** in word problems, name the variable first - 'let n be the number of...' - then build the expression around it. **Check yourself:** rewrite one shopping sentence with a letter for the unknown count and read it back aloud. **Why students miss it:** they treat the letter as one mysterious fixed number instead of a slot any value may fill.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently confuse variables with constants or labels, treating a letter like $a$ as an abbreviation for an object (e.g., thinking $a$ stands for "apples" rather than "the number of apples"). For example, when asked to write "There are 5 more pens than pencils," where pencils are $p$ and pens are $n$, a student might incorrectly write $p + a = n$ or treat letters as literal units like $5p$ meaning "5 pens" instead of $5 \times p$. Another severe error is assigning an arbitrary fixed value to a variable immediately (e.g., deciding $x$ always equals 1 because it is the first letter).
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that letters in algebra are numbers in disguise, not physical objects. Ask them: "If $p$ is the number of pencils, is $p$ a number or is it a plastic pencil?" Guide them to substitute a small whole number like 2 for the variable to test if their expression makes logical sense (e.g., if there are 2 pencils and 5 more pens, 2 + 5 = 7 pens). Reinforce that the letter stands for an unknown count or amount, and operations (+, -, $\times$, $\div$) apply to it just as they do to regular numbers.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Riya has ₹50 in her piggy bank. Every day, she saves ₹10 from her pocket money. Write an algebraic expression for the total amount of money in her piggy bank after $d$ days, and find the total amount if $d = 7$ days.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the fixed amount already in the piggy bank, which is constant = ₹50.
- Step 2: Identify the daily savings rate and the variable representing the number of days, which is ₹10 per day and $d$ days respectively.
- Step 3: Express the total saved amount through multiplication and addition: Total Money = (Daily savings $\times$ number of days) + Initial amount = $10 \times d + 50$, written algebraically as $10d + 50$.
- Step 4: Substitute the given value $d = 7$ into the expression: $10(7) + 50$.
- Step 5: Compute the arithmetic operations following BODMAS: $70 + 50 = ₹120$.
REASONING: The variable $d$ allows us to represent any number of days dynamically. By multiplying the daily rate by the variable and adding the initial fixed amount, we create an algebraic expression that works for 1 day, 7 days, or 30 days.
ANSWER: Algebraic expression is $10d + 50$; total amount for 7 days is ₹120.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: The length of a rectangular vegetable garden is 5 metres more than twice its breadth. If the breadth is represented by $b$ metres, write the algebraic expression for the length, and calculate the length if the breadth is 4 metres.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given variable representing the breadth as $b$ metres.
- Step 2: Translate "twice its breadth" into algebraic terms: $2 \times b$ or $2b$.
- Step 3: Translate "5 metres more than twice its breadth" into an expression by adding 5: length = $2b + 5$.
- Step 4: Substitute the given breadth value $b = 4$ into the length expression: $2(4) + 5$.
- Step 5: Evaluate the expression: $8 + 5 = 13$ metres.
REASONING: Translating word problems into algebraic expressions requires breaking the sentence into mathematical parts where the unknown breadth is our variable $b$, allowing us to construct the length relationship step by step.
ANSWER: Algebraic expression for length is $2b + 5$; length when $b = 4$ is 13 metres.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A school bus carries students from two residential blocks. Block A sends $x$ students, and Block B sends 3 times the number of students sent by Block A minus 10. If each student's monthly bus pass costs ₹200, write an algebraic expression for the total monthly collection from both blocks combined, and find the collection if $x = 25$.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Define the number of students from Block A as $x$.
- Step 2: Formulate the number of students from Block B in terms of $x$: "3 times $x$ minus 10" translates to $3x - 10$.
- Step 3: Find the total number of students by adding Block A and Block B students: Total students = $x + (3x - 10) = 4x - 10$.
- Step 4: Multiply the total number of students by the cost per pass (₹200) to get the algebraic expression for total collection: $200(4x - 10)$, which simplifies to $800x - 2000$.
- Step 5: Substitute $x = 25$ into the simplified expression: $800(25) - 2000$.
- Step 6: Compute the final value: $20,000 - 2,000 = ₹18,000$.
REASONING: This multi-step problem combines variable definition, combining like terms, and distribution. We first model each block's population using the same variable $x$, sum them to find total students, and then apply the cost per unit to find the final monetary value.
ANSWER: Algebraic expression for total collection is $800x - 2000$; total collection when $x = 25$ is ₹18,000.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A rectangular athletic field has a breadth of $w$ metres and a length that is 3 times its breadth. Due to a running track expansion, the length is increased by 10 metres and the breadth is decreased by 2 metres. 
(a) Write the algebraic expression for the new perimeter of the field.
(b) If the original breadth $w = 15$ metres, calculate the numerical value of the new perimeter.
COMPETENCY: Constructing multi-step algebraic expressions for geometric modifications and evaluating them
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write original breadth ($w$) and original length ($3w$).
- Step 2: Determine new dimensions after modifications: new breadth = $w - 2$, new length = $3w + 10$.
- Step 3: Recall the perimeter formula for a rectangle: Perimeter = $2 \times (\text{Length} + \text{Breadth})$.
- Step 4: Substitute new dimensions into the perimeter formula: $2 \times ((3w + 10) + (w - 2))$ and simplify to $2(4w + 8) = 8w + 16$.
- Step 5: Substitute $w = 15$ into the simplified perimeter expression: $8(15) + 16 = 120 + 16 = 136$ metres.
ANSWER: (a) New perimeter expression is $8w + 16$ metres. (b) When $w = 15$, the new perimeter is $136$ metres.
DIFFICULTY: hard
