## ELI5
At the neighbourhood crossing, an auto-rickshaw and a bicycle start together. Ten seconds later the auto has left the bicycle behind - it covered more ground in the same interval, so it is faster. Comparing speeds is fair racing: same time, compare distance; or same distance, compare time. No stopwatch heroics needed, just honest bookkeeping of who went how far while the clock ticked equally for both.

## DEFINITION
Comparing speeds involves determining which of two or more moving objects covers a greater distance in a given interval of time, or takes less time to cover a fixed distance. It is measured in meters per second (m/s) or kilometers per hour (km/hr) and helps us quantify relative motion in everyday life.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can compare the speeds of two moving objects either by calculating distance per unit time or by keeping one variable (distance or time) constant to determine which object is faster.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers qualitative and basic quantitative comparison of speeds for objects moving along straight lines (linear motion), excluding complex acceleration changes or vector velocity vectors.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine standing near a busy road in your neighborhood and watching an auto-rickshaw and a bicycle start moving from the same traffic signal at the same time. After 10 seconds, you notice the auto-rickshaw has traveled much further down the road than the bicycle. To understand how much faster one is than the other, we must first fix either the time or the distance. We record the distance covered by each vehicle in a standard interval of time, such as one second or one minute. By building on our understanding of time measurement, we use clocks or stopwatches to capture these precise intervals. Finally, we compare these values to declare which object has a higher speed.

**Takeaway:** speed comparison is controlled racing: hold time constant and compare distance, or hold distance and compare time. **In the exam:** state which variable you fixed before declaring a winner - the discipline is the answer's spine. **Check yourself:** watch the walkthrough's crossing for the fixed interval and let the distances argue the verdict. **Why students miss it:** students judge by engine noise - the flagged impressionism. Bigger vehicle, louder engine, no verdict. Same-start, same-clock comparison is the evidence, and it settles every faster-or-slower question without an opinion in sight.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Car A travels a distance of 120 kilometers in 2 hours, and Car B travels 150 kilometers in 3 hours. Calculate the speed of both cars in km/hr and determine which car is faster.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Identify given values for Car A: Distance $d_A = 120\text{ km}$, Time $t_A = 2\text{ hr}$.
Step 2: Identify given values for Car B: Distance $d_B = 150\text{ km}$, Time $t_B = 3\text{ hr}$.
Step 3: Calculate Speed of Car A: $v_A = \frac{120\text{ km}}{2\text{ hr}} = 60\text{ km/hr}$.
Step 4: Calculate Speed of Car B: $v_B = \frac{150\text{ km}}{3\text{ hr}} = 50\text{ km/hr}$.
Step 5: Compare speeds ($60\text{ km/hr} > 50\text{ km/hr}$); therefore, Car A is faster.
A wrong answer shows an error in division or mixing up distance and time values in the formula.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze a race where Runner X runs for 10 seconds and covers 60 meters, while Runner Y runs for 15 seconds and covers 90 meters. How do their speeds compare?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Runner X's speed is $\frac{60\text{ m}}{10\text{ s}} = 6\text{ m/s}$. Runner Y's speed is $\frac{90\text{ m}}{15\text{ s}} = 6\text{ m/s}$. Their speeds are equal. A wrong answer suggests the student blindly looked at the total distance or total time being larger and assumed one runner must be faster without actually calculating the distance covered per unit time.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that if Train X travels twice the distance of Train Y, Train X must be moving at a faster speed regardless of the time taken. Evaluate this claim.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The claim is false. Speed depends on both distance and time. If Train X covers twice the distance but takes three times longer than Train Y, its speed will actually be lower. A wrong answer reveals a failure to realize that speed is a ratio of distance to time, not distance alone.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that the object that travels the greater total distance is always the faster object, completely ignoring the time taken. For example, if a bus travels 200 km in 4 hours and a bullet train travels 150 km in 1 hour, a student might incorrectly say the bus is faster simply because 200 is greater than 150.
## ON_FAILURE

Step 2: Guide them to calculate how much distance each object covers in a single unit of time (such as 1 hour or 1 second).
Step 3: Point out that comparing totals without considering time is like comparing marks scored in exams of different maximum totals without finding percentages.
Step 4: Re-verify using the formula $v = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$ with unit tracking.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Two cyclists, Priya and Rohan, participate in a school race. Priya covers a distance of 300 meters in 50 seconds. Rohan covers a distance of 400 meters in 80 seconds. Who is cycling faster?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify Priya's data: Distance $d_1 = 300\text{ m}$, Time $t_1 = 50\text{ s}$. Identify Rohan's data: Distance $d_2 = 400\text{ m}$, Time $t_2 = 80\text{ s}$.
- Step 2: Recall the concept that speed is distance divided by time ($v = \frac{d}{t}$).
- Step 3: Calculate Priya's speed: $v_1 = \frac{300\text{ m}}{50\text{ s}} = 6\text{ m/s}$.
- Step 4: Calculate Rohan's speed: $v_2 = \frac{400\text{ m}}{80\text{ s}} = 5\text{ m/s}$.
- Step 5: Compare speeds ($6\text{ m/s} > 5\text{ m/s}$); Priya has a higher speed and is cycling faster.
REASONING: By finding the distance covered by each cyclist in a single second (unit rate), we remove the confusion caused by different total distances and times, allowing a direct and fair comparison.
ANSWER: Priya is faster with a speed of $6\text{ m/s}$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A metro train travels 60 kilometers in 45 minutes, while an express bus travels 40 kilometers in 30 minutes. Which vehicle has a greater speed?
STEPS:
- Step 1: List metro train data: $d_1 = 60\text{ km}$, $t_1 = 45\text{ min} = \frac{3}{4}\text{ hr} = 0.75\text{ hr}$.
- Step 2: List express bus data: $d_2 = 40\text{ km}$, $t_2 = 30\text{ min} = \frac{1}{2}\text{ hr} = 0.5\text{ hr}$.
- Step 3: Compute metro train speed in km/hr: $v_1 = \frac{60\text{ km}}{0.75\text{ hr}} = 80\text{ km/hr}$.
- Step 4: Compute express bus speed in km/hr: $v_2 = \frac{40\text{ km}}{0.5\text{ hr}} = 80\text{ km/hr}$.
- Step 5: Compare the two speeds: $80\text{ km/hr} = 80\text{ km/hr}$, meaning both vehicles travel at the exact same speed.
REASONING: Converting time into consistent units (hours) allows us to compute speeds in common units (km/hr) and reveal that different distances and times can yield identical speeds.
ANSWER: Both vehicles travel at the same speed of $80\text{ km/hr}$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A delivery drone flies a distance of 12 kilometers in 15 minutes. A delivery van travels 30 kilometers in 45 minutes to deliver the same package. Which mode of transport is faster, and by how much (in km/hr)?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Convert drone time to hours: $t_1 = 15\text{ min} = \frac{15}{60}\text{ hr} = 0.25\text{ hr}$, with distance $d_1 = 12\text{ km}$.
- Step 2: Convert van time to hours: $t_2 = 45\text{ min} = \frac{45}{60}\text{ hr} = 0.75\text{ hr}$, with distance $d_2 = 30\text{ km}$.
- Step 3: Calculate drone speed: $v_1 = \frac{12\text{ km}}{0.25\text{ hr}} = 48\text{ km/hr}$.
- Step 4: Calculate van speed: $v_2 = \frac{30\text{ km}}{0.75\text{ hr}} = 40\text{ km/hr}$.
- Step 5: Find the difference in speeds: $\Delta v = 48\text{ km/hr} - 40\text{ km/hr} = 8\text{ km/hr}$.
- Step 6: Conclude that the drone is faster by $8\text{ km/hr}$.
REASONING: Converting minutes to hours ensures both speeds are expressed in standard km/hr units, enabling a direct numerical subtraction to find the exact difference in their performance.
ANSWER: The drone is faster by $8\text{ km/hr}$.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two cars move along a straight highway. Car Alpha covers 200 meters in 10 seconds. Car Beta covers 300 meters in 15 seconds. Without doing full division, can you reason whether their speeds are equal or different?
COMPETENCY: Reasoning about proportions and ratios without immediate calculation
STEPS:
- Step 1: Write down the distance-to-time ratio for Car Alpha as $\frac{200}{10}$.
- Step 2: Write down the distance-to-time ratio for Car Beta as $\frac{300}{15}$.
- Step 3: Simplify both fractions: $\frac{200}{10} = 20$ and $\frac{300}{15} = 20$.
- Step 4: Conclude that since both simplify to the same value, their speeds are equal.
ANSWER: Their speeds are equal ($20\text{ m/s}$ each), because simplifying their distance-to-time ratios yields the same unit rate.
DIFFICULTY: medium

QUESTION: A falcon dives down, covering 250 meters in 5 seconds. A bullet train travels 500 meters in 10 seconds. A cheetah runs 100 meters in 2.5 seconds. Rank these three objects from slowest to fastest based on their speeds.

- Step 5: Rank them from slowest to fastest: Cheetah is slowest ($40\text{ m/s}$), while Falcon and Train tie as fastest ($50\text{ m/s}$).
