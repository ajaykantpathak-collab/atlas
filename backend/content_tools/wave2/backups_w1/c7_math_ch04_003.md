## ELI5
Buy x notebooks at 25 rupees and one pen at 15: the bill is 25 times x, plus 15. The moment you know x - say 3 notebooks - you substitute and the algebra melts back into familiar arithmetic: 25 times 3, plus 15. Brackets still obey their distributive duty - the outside number must multiply every term inside - and BODMAS still referees. Algebra has not changed the rules of arithmetic; it has only lent the rules some letters to carry unknowns around.

It formalises the rules of arithmetic properties (like commutativity, associativity, and distributivity) using variables as generalised numbers.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you go to a local stationery shop in your town and buy $x$ notebooks for ₹25 each, plus a single pen for ₹15. How do you write the total cost if you buy 3 notebooks? You substitute 3 for $x$, giving $25 \times 3 + 15$. In Class 6, you learned about basic algebraic expressions, but now we revisit arithmetic expressions containing variables to understand how order of operations (BODMAS) and fundamental properties like the distributive property apply when letters represent unknown quantities. Just as you compute numerical values using brackets, powers, and operations, you must treat variables like numbers to combine like terms and simplify expressions step-by-step. The key insight is that an arithmetic expression with variables obeys the exact same structural rules as pure arithmetic, allowing us to simplify $3x + 2x$ into $5x$ using the distributive property.

**Takeaway:** algebra reuses arithmetic wholesale: substitute values, distribute over brackets, and let BODMAS referee every step. **In the exam:** in evaluate questions, simplify first, substitute at the end, and show one operation per line. **Check yourself:** substitute the notebook count into the bill expression and compute it two different ways. **Why students miss it:** they subtract before multiplying across a bracket, losing the distribution the expression encodes.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct answer requires explaining the distributive property of multiplication over addition, where the factor 4 outside the bracket must multiply every term inside the bracket ($4 \times x$ and $4 \times 5$). ## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Evaluate and simplify the arithmetic expression with a variable: $3(2x + 4) - 5x + 10$ when $x = 3$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $25$. Step 1: Expand the brackets using multiplication: $3(2x) + 3(4) - 5x + 10 = 6x + 12 - 5x + 10$. Step 2: Group like terms together: $(6x - 5x) + (12 + 10) = x + 22$. Step 3: Substitute $x = 3$: $3 + 22 = 25$. A wrong answer like $41$ often occurs if the student multiplies 3 by 4 incorrectly or fails to distribute the negative sign properly, or adds all numbers blindly without grouping.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
No, they are not equivalent. Expression A expands to $5x + 10 + 3 = 5x + 13$, because the 5 multiplies both $x$ and 2. Expression B is $5x + 5$. A wrong answer stating they are equivalent reveals a failure to apply the distributive property to all terms inside the parentheses.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the student's work: A student simplifies $15 - 3(x - 2)$ as $12(x - 2) = 12x - 24$. Identify the error and provide the correct simplification.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The student's error is subtracting $15 - 3$ first, which violates the order of operations (BODMAS/PEMDAS) because multiplication must be performed before subtraction. The correct simplification is: $15 - 3(x - 2) = 15 - 3x + (-3)(-2) = 15 - 3x + 6 = 21 - 3x$. A wrong evaluation accepting the student's work shows a lack of understanding regarding the priority of operations and distribution of negative signs.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often distribute a multiplier to only the first term inside parentheses or fail to distribute a negative sign correctly across all terms. For example, when simplifying $-2(3x - 4)$, students frequently write $-6x - 4$ instead of the correct $-6x + 8$, forgetting that multiplying a negative number by a negative number yields a positive product.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this error, explicitly guide the student to draw arrows from the outer factor to every single term inside the parentheses. Remind them of integer multiplication rules from Class 6: negative times negative is positive. Write out the expansion in two separate mini-multiplications: $(-2) \times (3x)$ and $(-2) \times (-4)$, then combine them.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Simplify the expression $4(3x - 5) + 2(x + 7)$ and evaluate it when $x = 2$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given expression: $4(3x - 5) + 2(x + 7)$ and the value $x = 2$.
- step 2: Apply the distributive property to expand the first bracket: $4 \times 3x + 4 \times (-5) = 12x - 20$.
- step 3: Apply the distributive property to expand the second bracket: $2 \times x + 2 \times 7 = 2x + 14$.
- step 4: Combine the expanded parts and group like terms together: $(12x + 2x) + (-20 + 14) = 14x - 6$.
- step 5: Substitute $x = 2$ into the simplified expression and compute: $14(2) - 6 = 28 - 6 = 22$.
REASONING: The distributive property ensures every term inside the parentheses is multiplied correctly by the outer number. Grouping like terms ($14x$ and constant numbers) simplifies the calculation before substitution, preventing arithmetic errors.
ANSWER: 22
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Riya buys 5 pens at ₹$y$ each and 3 notebooks at ₹$(y + 10)$ each. Write the simplified arithmetic expression for the total cost, and find the cost if $y = ₹15$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Translate the word problem into an algebraic expression: Total Cost = $5 \times y + 3 \times (y + 10)$.
- step 2: Distribute the 3 into the second term: $5y + 3y + 3 \times 10 = 5y + 3y + 30$.
- step 3: Combine like variable terms: $(5y + 3y) + 30 = 8y + 30$.
- step 4: Substitute the given value $y = 15$ into the simplified expression: $8(15) + 30$.
- step 5: Compute the final value: $120 + 30 = ₹150$.
REASONING: Writing algebraic expressions from word problems requires assigning variables correctly, applying distribution, and combining like terms before substitution to make calculations efficient.
ANSWER: ₹150
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Simplify the complex arithmetic expression with variables: $50 - 3[2x - 4(x - 3)]$, and evaluate for $x = 4$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the innermost bracket and apply the distributive property: $-4(x - 3) = -4x + 12$.
- step 2: Substitute this back into the expression inside the square brackets: $2x - 4x + 12 = -2x + 12$.
- step 3: Distribute the outer multiplier $-3$ across the bracket: $-3(-2x + 12) = 6x - 36$.
- step 4: Combine with the remaining constant: $50 + 6x - 36 = 6x + 14$.
- step 5: Substitute $x = 4$ into the final simplified expression: $6(4) + 14 = 24 + 14 = 38$.
REASONING: When dealing with nested brackets, we must always work from the inside out. Distributing negative signs carefully at each step prevents sign errors that frequently occur in multi-step algebraic simplification.
ANSWER: 38
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two students simplified the expression $10 - 2(x - 3)$. Student A got $8(x - 3) = 8x - 24$. Student B got $10 - 2x + 6 = 16 - 2x$. Who is correct? Explain the exact mathematical error made by the incorrect student.
COMPETENCY: Error identification, justification, and application of order of operations
STEPS:
- step 1: Examine Student A's work: subtracted $10 - 2$ first, violating BODMAS/PEMDAS.
- step 2: Examine Student B's work: distributed $-2$ to both $x$ and $-3$, yielding $-2x + 6$, then combined constants $10 + 6 = 16$.
- step 3: Conclude that Student B is correct.
- step 4: Verify by substituting a test value, e.g., $x = 1$: Original expression gives $10 - 2(1 - 3) = 10 - 2(-2) = 14$. Student B gives $16 - 2(1) = 14$.
ANSWER: Student B is correct. Student A violated the order of operations by subtracting 10 and 2 before performing the multiplication indicated by the parentheses. Student B correctly applied the distributive property and handled the negative sign multiplication $(-2 \times -3 = +6)$.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A farmer has a rectangular field whose length is $3x + 5$ metres and breadth is $2x$ metres. Write the arithmetic expression for the perimeter, simplify it completely, and find the perimeter if $x = 4$ metres.
COMPETENCY: Translating geometric dimensions to algebraic expressions and evaluation
STEPS:
- step 1: Recall the perimeter formula for a rectangle: Perimeter $= 2 \times (\text{length} + \text{breadth})$.
- step 2: Substitute the given algebraic expressions for length and breadth: Perimeter $= 2 \times ((3x + 5) + 2x)$.
- step 3: Simplify inside the parentheses by combining like terms: $3x + 2x + 5 = 5x + 5$.
- step 4: Distribute the 2 outside: $2(5x + 5) = 10x + 10$ metres.
- step 5: Substitute $x = 4$ into the simplified expression: $10(4) + 10 = 40 + 10 = 50$ metres.
ANSWER: The simplified expression for the perimeter is $10x + 10$ metres. When $x = 4$, the perimeter evaluates to 50 metres. This is found by adding length and breadth, multiplying by 2, simplifying using the distributive property, and substituting the given numerical value.
DIFFICULTY: hard
