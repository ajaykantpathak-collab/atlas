## ELI5
Uniform motion is the metronome of travel: equal slices of road in equal beats of time, steady as a cyclist pacing an empty straight road. On a graph it draws one honest straight line - and the steeper the line, the faster the beat. One uneven beat breaks the spell and the motion stops being uniform.

## DEFINITION
Uniform motion occurs when an object moves in a straight line at a constant speed, covering equal distances in equal intervals of time. It represents a steady, unchanging rate of travel.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are riding a bicycle on a long, straight, empty road, and you keep your legs moving at the exact same pace the entire time. Because your effort doesn't change, your bicycle covers 10 metres every 2 seconds, no matter which part of the road you are on. Think of this as a "steady rhythm" of movement, which we can visualize as a perfectly straight line on a graph.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse "constant speed" with "constant velocity" or assume that if an object moves, it must be in uniform motion. They might incorrectly label a ball rolling down a ramp (which speeds up) as uniform motion simply because it moves in a straight line.
## ON_FAILURE

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A local train travels between two stations. It covers 10 km in the first 15 minutes, 10 km in the second 15 minutes, and 10 km in the third 15 minutes. Is the motion uniform? Calculate the speed in km/h.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify that the distance is 10 km per 15-minute interval.
- step 2: Confirm intervals are equal (15 mins) and distances are equal (10 km). This is uniform motion.
- step 3: Convert 15 minutes to hours (15/60 = 0.25 hours).
- step 4: Use Speed = Distance / Time = 10 km / 0.25 h.
- step 5: Result = 40 km/h.
REASONING: By confirming the intervals are consistent, we satisfy the condition for uniform motion; converting time units ensures the final speed is in standard units.
ANSWER: 40 km/h
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An athlete runs 400 metres in 80 seconds at a constant rate. What is her speed in m/s?
STEPS:
- step 1: Given distance = 400 m, time = 80 s.
- step 2: Identify the formula: Speed = Distance / Time.
- step 3: Substitute the values: 400 / 80.
- step 4: Divide 400 by 80 to get 5.
- step 5: State the final unit as m/s.
REASONING: Since the rate is constant, the speed calculated for the total trip applies to every second of the race.
ANSWER: 5 m/s
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A runner covers 100m in 20s. If they keep the same speed for 1 minute (60s), how much total distance will they cover?
STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate speed: 100m / 20s = 5 m/s.
- step 2: Identify that 1 minute = 60 seconds.
- step 3: Use the rearranged formula: Distance = Speed × Time.
- step 4: Calculate: 5 m/s × 60 s.
- step 5: Total distance = 300 m.
REASONING: Since uniform motion implies constant speed, we can use the speed calculated from the first 20 seconds to predict the distance covered over a longer duration.
ANSWER: 300 m
