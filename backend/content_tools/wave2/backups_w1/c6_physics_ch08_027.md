## ELI5
A sundial is a clock made of light and shadow: a stiff stick plants its shadow on a marked face, and as the sun strolls across the sky the shadow sweeps the hours like a slow hand. No sun, no clock - clouds pause it and midnight closes it. The stick never moves; the sky does all the walking.

## DEFINITION
A sundial is an ancient device that measures time by using the position of a shadow cast by a stationary object (a gnomon) onto a marked surface, tracking the sun's apparent movement across the sky. It represents an early human attempt to standardize time using natural, repeating celestial cycles.

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever noticed how your own shadow changes length and direction during a sunny day? Early humans used this exact phenomenon by placing a stick in the ground and marking where the shadow fell at different times of the day. As the sun appears to move across the sky, the shadow rotates around the stick, acting like a giant, natural clock hand. Because this method depends entirely on sunlight, early people realized they needed a system that worked even when the sun wasn't visible or during the night. The key insight is that we moved from "watching the sky" to "building tools" to track time more reliably.

**Takeaway:** a sundial reads the sun's apparent stroll - the fixed stick's shadow sweeps the marked face as the sky turns. **In the exam:** in sundial questions, keep the stick still and let the sun's moved position explain every shadow change. **Check yourself:** pace the walkthrough's shadow - morning-long, noon-short, evening-long is the sun's own walk drawn on the ground. **Why students miss it:** students spin the stick instead of the sky - the fixed gnomon is a no to that; only the sun walks. ## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe the sun physically "moves" around the earth, causing the shadow to move. They also frequently confuse the shadow's movement with the stick itself rotating. This occurs because our daily observation (the sun rising and setting) reinforces the geocentric model of the universe rather than the reality of Earth's rotation.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student marks a shadow at 9:00 AM, 10:00 AM, and 11:00 AM. If the shadow moved 15 degrees each hour, what is the total angle of movement by 11:00 AM?
STEPS:
- identify the hourly change: 15 degrees/hour
- identify the time elapsed: 11:00 AM - 9:00 AM = 2 hours
- multiply rate by time: 15 degrees/hour * 2 hours
- compute the result: 30 degrees
- interpret: The shadow has swept through a 30-degree arc.
REASONING: Since the sundial tracks time through angular displacement, multiplying the rate of change by the duration gives the total position shift.
ANSWER: 30 degrees
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: If a sundial shadow is 20 cm long at 9:00 AM and 10 cm long at 12:00 PM, calculate the total change in shadow length.
STEPS:
- identify starting length: 20 cm
- identify final length: 10 cm
- find the difference: 20 cm - 10 cm
- compute result: 10 cm
- interpret: The shadow shortened by 10 cm due to the sun's higher position.
REASONING: As the sun rises higher toward midday, the angle of light becomes steeper, causing the shadow cast by a vertical object to shrink.
ANSWER: 10 cm
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A park needs a sundial that works for 8 hours a day. If the sun rises at 6:00 AM, at what time does the sundial stop working?
STEPS:
- identify start time: 6:00 AM
- identify duration: 8 hours
- add duration to start time: 6 + 8
- compute result: 14:00 (which is 2:00 PM)
- verify: 6 to 12 is 6 hours, plus 2 more hours equals 8 total.
REASONING: Understanding the operational window of a solar device requires adding the active duration to the start time of the light source.
ANSWER: 2:00 PM
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Imagine you are on a planet where the sun never sets. Would a standard Earth sundial work? Explain your reasoning.
COMPETENCY: Critical thinking and applying concepts to new contexts.
STEPS:
- recognize the sundial requires a change in light angle.
- identify that a sun that never sets (or stays in one spot) would not provide the changing shadow needed for timekeeping.
- conclude that the sundial would remain static.
- explain the necessity of the sun's apparent motion.
ANSWER: A standard sundial would not work because it relies on the shadow moving as the sun traverses the sky; if the sun remains stationary, the shadow remains fixed, providing no way to measure the passage of time.

COMPETENCY: Analyzing historical evolution and technological limitations.
STEPS:
- analyze the core dependency of the sundial (sunlight).
- identify the environmental failure points (nighttime, clouds).
