## ELI5
A cube number is a number taken three times in multiplication: n × n × n. The name is a picture too — build a cube with side n from little blocks, and n³ is exactly how many blocks you used. Squares tile floors; cubes fill boxes. The little three in n³, the exponent, is a countdown of how many times the number shakes its own hand. Same number, three factors — volume is where squaring grows up into three dimensions.

## DEFINITION
A cube number is the result of multiplying an integer by itself three times. For any integer \( n \), its cube is denoted as \( n^3 \) and calculated as \( n \times n \times n \). The notation \( n^3 \) is read as "n cubed" and represents the volume of a cube with side length \( n \) units.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can correctly compute the cube of any integer (positive, negative, or zero) using the notation \( n^3 \), explain the geometric meaning of a cube number, and distinguish between \( n^2 \) (square) and \( n^3 \) (cube) in both arithmetic and real-world contexts.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the definition and notation of cube numbers for integers only, including positive, negative, and zero. It does NOT cover cube roots, perfect cubes properties, or operations with cube numbers beyond basic evaluation. Fractions and decimals are excluded at this stage.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are packing small cubic boxes (each 1 cm × 1 cm × 1 cm) into a larger cube-shaped carton. If the carton's side length is 4 cm, how many small boxes fit inside? This is exactly finding \( 4^3 \). Start by recalling that a square number \( n^2 \) comes from a 2D square's area. Now extend to 3D: a cube has length, width, and height all equal to \( n \). Write down the side length \( n \), then multiply it by itself three times: \( n \times n \times n \). The key insight is that the exponent 3 tells you how many times the base appears in the multiplication, just as the exponent 2 does for squares, but now in three dimensions.

Takeaway: n³ = n × n × n, read as 'n cubed', and the geometric meaning is literal — the volume of a cube with side n. Exam format: expect cubes of positive, negative and zero inputs; handle signs by rule — three identical negative factors multiply to a negative result. Verification beat: check magnitude — cubing roughly triples the digits, so a same-size answer signals a squaring slip. Why students miss it: they compute n × 3 or n² × n inconsistently; writing the three-factor line out longhand before multiplying prevents both slips.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the value of \( (-3)^3 \) and how does it differ from \( -3^3 \)?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
\( (-3)^3 = -27 \) because the base is -3, multiplied three times: \( (-3) \times (-3) \times (-3) = 9 \times (-3) = -27 \). For \( -3^3 \), the exponent applies only to 3, so it is \( -(3^3) = -27 \). In this case they are equal, but if the exponent were even they would differ. A wrong answer (e.g., saying \( (-3)^3 = 27 \)) reveals the student does not understand that an odd number of negative factors yields a negative product, or they confuse the placement of parentheses.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why \( 5^3 \) is not equal to \( 3 \times 5 \). Use the geometric meaning of a cube to justify your answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
\( 5^3 = 5 \times 5 \times 5 = 125 \), which is the volume of a cube with side 5 units. \( 3 \times 5 = 15 \) is just three groups of 5, representing a rectangular prism of dimensions 3 × 5 × 1, not a cube. A student who says "both give 15" or "multiplication is the same" shows they interpret the exponent as a multiplier rather than repeated multiplication of the base, missing the dimensional difference between a line (×5), a square (×5×5), and a cube (×5×5×5).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A cubic water tank has a side length of 6 metres. Calculate its capacity in cubic metres. Then find the side length of a cube whose volume is 343 cubic centimetres.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Volume = side³ = 6³ = 6 × 6 × 6 = 216 m³. Step 2: For volume 343 cm³, find n such that n³ = 343. By trial: 7³ = 7×7×7 = 49×7 = 343, so side = 7 cm. A wrong answer like 6³ = 18 (6×3) shows the student multiplies base by exponent. If they cannot reverse the process for 343, they lack the concept of perfect cubes and the inverse relationship.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the growth of \( n^2 \) and \( n^3 \) for n = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. At what value of n does \( n^3 \) first exceed twice of \( n^2 \)? Explain the pattern.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Table: n=1: 1²=1, 1³=1; n=2: 4, 8; n=3: 9, 27; n=4: 16, 64; n=5: 25, 125. Twice n²: 2, 8, 18, 32, 50. n³ exceeds 2n² at n=3 (27 > 18). The pattern shows n³ grows faster because it has an extra factor of n. A student who only computes values without comparing ratios misses the structural reason: n³ / n² = n, so n³ = n × n², which exceeds 2n² when n > 2.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student writes: \( (-2)^3 = -2 \times -2 \times -2 = 4 \times -2 = -8 \). Another writes: \( (-2)^3 = -6 \) because "cubing means multiply by 3". Evaluate both methods and state which is correct and why the other is wrong.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The first method is correct: it follows the definition of exponentiation as repeated multiplication. The second method incorrectly treats the exponent 3 as a multiplier (i.e., -2 × 3 = -6), confusing \( n^3 \) with \( 3n \). This is a classic misconception where the exponent is seen as a factor rather than an instruction for repeated multiplication. A student who defends the second method does not grasp the notation \( n^3 \).

Solution: 8³ = 512 m³; for 1000 m³, side = 10 m because 10³ = 1000. A poor problem would be 2D (area) or use multiplication by 3. The student must demonstrate understanding that cube numbers model volume of cubes.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often interpret \( n^3 \) as \( n \times 3 \) (multiplying the base by the exponent) instead of \( n \times n \times n \). For example, they calculate \( 4^3 = 12 \) (4×3) rather than 64 (4×4×4). This error stems from confusing the exponent notation with a coefficient in algebraic expressions (like 3n). They may also forget that negative bases with odd exponents yield negative results, writing \( (-2)^3 = 8 \) by incorrectly applying the "negative times negative is positive" rule twice and stopping.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student computes \( n^3 \) as \( n \times 3 \), first ask: "What does the little 3 mean in \( 2^3 \)?" Guide them to read it as "2 multiplied by itself 3 times". Use physical cubes (e.g., centimetre cubes) to build a 2×2×2 cube and count the blocks (8). Then build 3×3×3 and count (27). Contrast with 2×3=6 blocks arranged in a rectangle. Emphasize: the exponent tells how many times the base appears in the multiplication, not what to multiply the base by. For negative bases, have them write out the multiplication step by step: \( (-2) \times (-2) \times (-2) \) and apply integer multiplication rules they learned in Class 7.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A cubical gift box has a side length of 12 cm. What is its volume in cubic centimetres? If the box is made of wood 1 cm thick, what is the volume of the wood used? (Assume the box is a solid cube with a hollow cube inside of side 10 cm.)

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the outer side length = 12 cm. Volume of outer cube = side³ = 12³.
- Step 2: Compute 12³ = 12 × 12 × 12. First 12 × 12 = 144. Then 144 × 12 = 144 × (10 + 2) = 1440 + 288 = 1728 cm³.
- Step 3: Inner hollow side length = 12 - 2×1 = 10 cm (1 cm thickness on each side). Volume of hollow part = 10³ = 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000 cm³.
- Step 4: Volume of wood = outer volume - inner volume = 1728 - 1000 = 728 cm³.
- Step 5: Verify: 12³ = 1728, 10³ = 1000, difference = 728. Units are cm³.

REASONING: The problem connects cube numbers to volume of a cube (geometric meaning). Computing 12³ requires multi-digit multiplication, building on Class 6 skills. The subtraction step applies the concept of volume difference for a hollow object.

ANSWER: Outer volume = 1728 cm³, Volume of wood = 728 cm³.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Evaluate \( (-7)^3 \) and \( -7^3 \). Are they equal? Explain using the order of operations and integer multiplication rules.

STEPS:
- Step 1: For \( (-7)^3 \), the base is -7 (including the negative sign). Write as repeated multiplication: (-7) × (-7) × (-7).
- Step 2: Multiply first two: (-7) × (-7) = +49 (negative × negative = positive).
- Step 3: Multiply result by third factor: 49 × (-7) = -343 (positive × negative = negative).
- Step 4: For \( -7^3 \), exponent applies only to 7 (no parentheses). So it is -(7³) = -(7 × 7 × 7).
- Step 5: Compute 7³ = 343, then apply negative: -343. Compare: both equal -343. They are equal because the exponent 3 is odd.

REASONING: This example highlights the critical role of parentheses in exponent notation with negative bases. It reinforces Class 7 integer multiplication rules and order of operations (exponents before negation unless parentheses dictate otherwise). The equality holds only for odd exponents; for even exponents they would differ.

ANSWER: \( (-7)^3 = -343 \), \( -7^3 = -343 \). They are equal because the exponent is odd.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A school stores cubical boxes of chalk. Each box is a cube of side 15 cm. The storage shelf is a cube of side 1.5 m. How many chalk boxes can fit on the shelf if they are packed perfectly without gaps? (Assume boxes cannot be cut.)

STEPS:
- Step 1: Convert all measurements to same unit. Shelf side = 1.5 m = 150 cm. Box side = 15 cm.
- Step 2: Volume of shelf = 150³ cm³. Volume of one box = 15³ cm³.
- Step 3: Number of boxes = shelf volume / box volume = 150³ / 15³.
- Step 4: Use exponent rule (a³ / b³ = (a/b)³) or compute directly: 150³ = 150×150×150 = 3,375,000; 15³ = 3375.
- Step 5: Divide: 3,375,000 ÷ 3375 = 1000. Alternatively, (150/15)³ = 10³ = 1000.
- Step 6: Interpret: 1000 boxes fit exactly (10 along each dimension).

REASONING: This problem combines cube numbers with unit conversion (Class 6 decimals/measurement) and the property that volume scales with the cube of linear scale factor. It shows a practical application of cubes in packing and introduces the algebraic identity (a/b)³ = a³/b³ implicitly.

ANSWER: 1000 boxes.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Ravi says, "If I double the side of a cube, its volume becomes double." Is he correct? Justify your answer with a numerical example and a general explanation using the notation n³.

COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and comparing proportional relationships.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand the claim: doubling side (n → 2n) supposedly doubles volume (n³ → 2n³).
- Step 2: Test with a number: let n = 3. Original volume = 3³ = 27. Doubled side = 6, new volume = 6³ = 216.
- Step 3: Compare: 216 vs 2×27 = 54. 216 ≠ 54. In fact, 216 = 8×27.
- Step 4: Generalize: (2n)³ = 2³ × n³ = 8n³. Volume becomes 8 times, not 2 times.
- Step 5: Conclude: Ravi is incorrect. Volume scales with the cube of the linear scale factor.

ANSWER: Ravi is incorrect. Doubling the side multiplies the volume by 8, not 2. Example: side 3 cm → volume 27 cm³; side 6 cm → volume 216 cm³ = 8 × 27 cm³. General proof: (2n)³ = 8n³.

DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A cubic water tank of side 2 m is full. Water is poured into cubical containers of side 50 cm until they are full. How many such containers can be filled completely? If the remaining water is poured into a cubical container of side 20 cm, how many of those can be filled? Show all steps.

COMPETENCY: Multi-step problem solving with unit conversion, volume calculation, and division interpretation in a real-world context.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Convert all to cm. Tank side = 2 m = 200 cm. Volume = 200³ = 8,000,000 cm³.
- Step 2: Container A side = 50 cm. Volume = 50³ = 125,000 cm³.
- Step 3: Number of container A = 8,000,000 ÷ 125,000 = 64. (Exactly 64, no remainder).
- Step 4: Since no water remains, zero containers of side 20 cm can be filled.
- Step 5: Alternatively, use scale factor: (200/50)³ = 4³ = 64. For 20 cm: (200/20)³ = 10³ = 1000, but only if water remained.

ANSWER: 64 containers of side 50 cm can be filled completely. 0 containers of side 20 cm can be filled from the remainder (since there is no remainder).

DIFFICULTY: hard
