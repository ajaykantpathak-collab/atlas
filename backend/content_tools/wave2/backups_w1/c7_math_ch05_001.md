## ELI5
Two straight roads crossing at a village junction meet in exactly one spot - the point of intersection - and the crossing stamps four angles around it, like four direction boards facing outward. Two straight lines can never cross twice: meeting at two points would force a bend, and straight lines do not bend. Straighten two sticks into an X and the picture is complete: one crossing, four angles around it, and opposite corners that quietly match each other in size.

## DEFINITION
Intersecting lines are two or more straight lines in a plane that meet or cross each other at a single common point called the point of intersection, forming four angles at that junction.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine walking down a street in your neighborhood where two straight roads cross each other at a junction. If you stand right at the middle of the crossing where all four corners meet, you are standing at the exact spot where both roads share space—this is a real-world point of intersection! In geometry, when two straight lines meet, they do not just touch; they cross through one another, creating a unique point and dividing the space around it into four distinct angles. To begin working with intersecting lines, you must first draw or locate the two lines using a ruler, find the single point where they cross, and label it with a capital letter like $P$. Then, use your protractor or geometric rules to examine the four angles spreading out around this point. The key insight is that two intersecting lines always form a pair of lines that share one and only one common point, and this crossing generates four angles whose properties we can measure and calculate.

**Takeaway:** intersecting lines meet at exactly one common point and stamp exactly four angles around it. **In the exam:** for angle-count questions, answer four regardless of how the crossing looks; for crossing-point questions, answer exactly one. **Check yourself:** cross two pencils, count the angles at the junction, and name the crossing point. **Why students miss it:** they confuse intersection points with segment endpoints or imagine two straight lines crossing twice.

Step 3: Conclude that the total number of angles formed at the intersection is always 4, regardless of their individual degrees. A wrong answer like "2" or "8" indicates that the student is only counting the acute angles or counting the rays instead of the complete angles formed between adjacent intersecting rays.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The correct answer is yes, it is possible if all three lines are concurrent (passing through the same single point). ## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the "point of intersection" with the endpoints of line segments or think that intersecting lines can have multiple crossing points without curving. For example, when shown two intersecting lines, a student might point to the ends of the drawn segments instead of the central crossing point, or they might believe two straight lines can loop around and intersect twice like a figure-eight.
## ON_FAILURE
Guide the student by drawing two distinct, infinitely extending straight lines on paper using a sharp pencil and a ruler. Ask them to trace each line with a different colored crayon until their fingers meet at the exact spot where the crayons overlap. Say: "A line goes on forever, so it has no endpoints. The only place where two different lines interact is where they cross over each other. Put your pencil tip right on that crossing spot—that is our point of intersection!"
