## ELI5
An ice cube melts in a steel tumbler and becomes water - different look, same molecule. A physical change is a temporary alteration of shape, size, state or appearance in which no new substance forms and the chemical identity stays untouched. Melt it, freeze it, dissolve it, hammer it: the substance keeps its name. Change the costume, never the actor.

## DEFINITION
A physical change is a temporary alteration in the physical properties of a substance—such as its shape, size, state, or appearance—in which no new chemical substance is formed and the original chemical identity remains completely unchanged.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Students can observe a change in a substance's physical state or appearance, verify that no new chemical substance is produced, explain whether it can be reversed, and distinguish it from a chemical change.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers reversible physical changes involving state transitions (melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation), dissolving solutes in solvents, and mechanical shape alterations where no chemical bonds are broken to form new products. It does NOT cover chemical reactions, combustion, or permanent molecular transformations.
## WALKTHROUGH
Observe how an ice cube left in a steel tumbler melts into liquid water on a warm summer afternoon in Delhi, and how that same water can be placed back in the refrigerator freezer to turn back into ice. Start by examining what changes—the shape and state of the matter—versus what stays the same—it is still water ($H_2O$) in both solid and liquid forms. Connect this back to your Grade 6 understanding of matter and states, where you learned that changing temperature alters particle arrangement without altering the molecules themselves. Finally, recognize that because no new substance is created and the change is easily undone, this is a quintessential physical change.

**Takeaway:** a physical change alters shape, size, state or appearance only - the substance's chemical identity walks out unchanged. **In the exam:** in classification questions, hunt for a new substance; finding none, file the change as physical. **Check yourself:** watch the walkthrough's melting ice cube and confirm the water's identity survived the costume change. **Why students miss it:** students read permanence as chemistry - a hammered nail is bent, not reborn; reversibility is a no to the chemical label here. ## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What happens to the chemical identity of water when it boils and turns into steam?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The chemical identity of water remains completely unchanged ($H_2O$) whether it is liquid water or water vapor (steam); only the distance and energy between the molecules have changed. A student who answers that steam is a new chemical substance has a misconception that any change of state creates a new material.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why dissolving common salt (sodium chloride) in water is classified as a physical change rather than a chemical change.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Dissolving salt in water is a physical change because no new substance is formed; the salt particles simply disperse uniformly among the water molecules. You can recover the original solid salt by evaporating the water through heating. A student who thinks a chemical change occurred likely confuses the disappearance of the solid salt with its permanent destruction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A student takes a glass bottle filled with water, caps it tightly, and leaves it in the freezer overnight. The next morning, the glass bottle cracks because the water freezes and expands. Is this a physical or chemical change, and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
This is a physical change. Step 1: Identify the process as freezing of water ($H_2O_{(l)} \rightarrow H_2O_{(s)}$). Step 2: Note that the substance inside is still water, just in solid form. Step 3: Observe that no new chemical bonds are created or destroyed to form a different substance. Step 4: Conclude that changing shape, volume, or causing a container to break through thermal expansion or phase change is purely physical. A student who claims it is chemical is incorrectly assuming that any structural breakage or destruction of an object signifies a chemical reaction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the melting of wax with the burning of a candle wick. Which part represents a physical change, and how do you know?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The melting of the wax is a physical change because the solid wax turns into liquid wax, which can solidify again upon cooling without changing its chemical composition. In contrast, the burning of the wick and wax vapor is a chemical change producing carbon dioxide and water vapor. A student who fails to distinguish between the melting wax and the burning wick misses the coexistence of physical and chemical processes in everyday phenomena.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that hammering an iron nail flat is a chemical change because the nail undergoes a permanent change in shape and cannot easily be hammered back into its exact original wire form without specialized tools. Evaluate this claim.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The student's claim is incorrect. Hammering an iron nail changes its shape, size, and appearance (mechanical deformation), but the material remains pure iron ($Fe$). No new substance with different chemical properties is produced; therefore, it is a physical change, even if it is difficult to reverse manually. A student who relies solely on the difficulty of reversal to label a change as "chemical" misunderstands that reversibility is a helpful clue, but the ultimate test of a physical change is the absence of new chemical substances.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple kitchen experiment to prove that dissolving sugar in water is a reversible physical change, and explain what observations would confirm your conclusion.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
To prove this, dissolve two spoonfuls of sugar in a glass of warm water until clear. Pour the solution into a shallow pan and heat it gently on a stove until all the water evaporates completely. The white crystalline residue left at the bottom of the pan is sugar, proving that no new substance was formed and the process is reversible. A poor answer would suggest tasting the solution without recovering the solid, failing to demonstrate the recovery of the original substance.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently confuse any change that alters appearance, size, or shape (such as tearing paper, cutting vegetables, or breaking chalk) with a chemical change because the original object cannot be glued or put back together easily. They assume that if an object is permanently altered in form, a new substance must have been created.
## ON_FAILURE
Take a sheet of paper and tear it into small pieces. Ask the student: "What is each piece made of?" (Paper). Ask: "Did paper turn into wood or plastic?" (No, it is still paper). Show that while the shape and size changed, no new chemical substance was formed, and the chemical nature of cellulose fibers remains identical, establishing the rule that altering size or shape is physical.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student takes 50 grams of ice cubes at $0^\circ C$ in a beaker and heats them until all the ice melts into liquid water, and then continues heating until the water boils and completely vaporizes into steam in a closed kitchen vessel. Analyze each stage of this process to determine whether new substances are formed and classify the changes.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the starting material (solid water / ice, $H_2O_{(s)}$) and the changes it undergoes (melting to liquid water $H_2O_{(l)}$ and vaporization to steam $H_2O_{(g)}$).
- Step 2: Check the chemical formula of the substance at each stage: Solid ice is $H_2O$, liquid water is $H_2O$, and steam is $H_2O$.
- Step 3: Determine if any chemical bonds between hydrogen and oxygen atoms are broken to create a new product like hydrogen gas or oxygen gas. No bonds within the $H_2O$ molecules are broken; only intermolecular forces are overcome.
- Step 4: Evaluate reversibility and new substance formation: Since no new chemical species are formed and cooling the steam reverts it back to water and ice, all stages represent physical changes.
REASONING: Phase transitions alter the spatial arrangement and energy of molecules without altering their atomic composition. Because the chemical formula $H_2O$ remains invariant across solid, liquid, and gas phases, these are classic physical changes.
ANSWER: All stages (melting and vaporization) are physical changes because no new substance is formed and the chemical identity ($H_2O$) remains identical throughout.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: In a science laboratory in Mumbai, a student takes 10 grams of solid iodine crystals in an evaporating dish and gently heats them. The dark violet crystals turn directly into purple iodine vapors without melting into a liquid (sublimation). The purple vapors are then made to touch a cold watch glass placed over the dish, where they deposit back into sparkling dark violet iodine crystals. Classify this sublimation and deposition process.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the initial substance (solid iodine, $I_2_{(s)}$) and the phase changes (sublimation to vapor $I_2_{(g)}$, followed by deposition back to solid $I_2_{(s)}$).
- Step 2: Examine whether the chemical formula of iodine changes during sublimation or deposition. The vapor is still composed of iodine molecules ($I_2$).
- Step 3: Check if any chemical reaction occurred or if new chemical compounds were synthesized. No new compounds were formed; it is purely a phase transition driven by thermal energy transfer.
- Step 4: Confirm reversibility: Heating causes sublimation, and cooling causes deposition back into original iodine crystals, proving it is a physical change.
REASONING: Sublimation and deposition are phase changes between solid and gas states. Because the molecular structure of iodine ($I_2$) is preserved and no chemical bonds are permanently ruptured to form a new substance, the entire cycle is a physical change.
ANSWER: Answer: Sublimation and deposition of iodine are reversible physical changes because the chemical composition of iodine ($I_2$) does not change during state transitions.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student prepares a saturated solution of copper sulfate in warm water in a school laboratory. Upon cooling the solution slowly, bright blue, diamond-shaped crystals of copper sulfate pentahydrate ($CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$) separate out from the solution. The student filters out the crystals and evaporates the remaining liquid to recover dry copper sulfate powder. Is this crystallization process a physical change? Break down the chemical and physical aspects.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the process as crystallization, which involves dissolving copper sulfate in water and then precipitating it out as solid crystals through cooling.
- Step 2: Examine the nature of the dissolved state: Copper sulfate dissolves in water by separating into hydrated copper ions ($Cu^{2+}$) and sulfate ions ($SO_4^{2-}$), but it does not react chemically with water to form a fundamentally new type of matter.
- Step 3: Examine the crystals formed: The crystals recovered are copper sulfate pentahydrate ($CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$), which is chemically identical to the original copper sulfate salt combined with water of crystallization.
- Step 4: Test for reversibility and new substance creation: Dissolving and crystallizing physical components does not create an entirely new chemical product; dissolving is a physical dispersion, and crystallization is a physical separation technique based on solubility differences.
- Step 5: Conclude that crystallization is a physical change because the substance retains its chemical identity and can be dissolved and recovered repeatedly.
REASONING: Crystallization relies on changes in solubility with temperature. While crystal geometry changes and water molecules become structurally bound in the crystal lattice, the fundamental chemical identity of the solute remains intact, classifying it as a physical change.
ANSWER: Crystallisation is a physical change because no new chemical substance is created; the solute is simply separated from its solution in crystalline form and retains its original chemical identity.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student cuts a magnesium ribbon into small pieces with scissors and then dissolves one piece in dilute hydrochloric acid, observing fizzing and gas evolution. Another piece is heated strongly in air, where it burns with a dazzling white flame to form a white powder (magnesium oxide). Compare the cutting of the magnesium ribbon with its burning in air in terms of physical versus chemical changes.
COMPETENCY: Distinguishing physical changes from chemical changes in multi-step scenarios
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze cutting the magnesium ribbon: Size and shape are altered, but the pieces are still magnesium metal ($Mg$). No new substance is formed; this is a physical change.
- Step 2: Analyze dissolving in acid and burning in air: Both processes produce entirely new substances (hydrogen gas and magnesium oxide, respectively) through chemical reactions where bonds are broken and formed. These are chemical changes.
- Step 3: Synthesize the comparison by contrasting the preservation of chemical identity in cutting versus the creation of new substances in burning.
ANSWER: Cutting the magnesium ribbon is a physical change because only its size and shape change while its chemical identity as magnesium ($Mg$) remains unaltered. Conversely, burning magnesium in air is a chemical change because it reacts with oxygen to form a completely new substance, magnesium oxide ($MgO$).

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A mixture of iron filings and sulfur powder is prepared in a porcelain dish at room temperature. A magnet is passed through the mixture, attracting the iron filings while leaving the sulfur powder behind. Next, the same mixture of iron filings and sulfur powder is heated strongly in a test tube until a glowing red mass forms, producing a dark grey solid (iron sulfide, $FeS$) that is no longer magnetic and cannot be separated by a magnet. Explain why the first process is physical and the second is chemical, using evidence from properties and composition.
COMPETENCY: Analyzing experimental evidence to differentiate physical mixtures from chemical reactions
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the magnetic separation of iron and sulfur: The iron retains its magnetic property, and sulfur retains its color and solubility properties. No chemical bonds formed between them; they retain their individual identities. Thus, mixing and magnetic separation represent a physical change.
- Step 2: Analyze heating iron and sulfur together: Heating provides activation energy for a chemical reaction to occur, forming iron sulfide ($FeS$), a completely new substance with different properties (non-magnetic, different color and texture).
- Step 3: Compare property retention: Physical changes preserve component properties and allow easy physical separation; chemical changes alter properties permanently and create new chemical substances.
ANSWER: Mixing iron and sulfur is a physical change because the two elements retain their individual properties (iron is magnetic, sulfur is yellow) and can be separated by physical means without forming a new substance. Heating them causes a chemical reaction that forms iron sulfide ($FeS$), a new substance with entirely different properties that cannot be separated magnetically, classifying it as a chemical change.
