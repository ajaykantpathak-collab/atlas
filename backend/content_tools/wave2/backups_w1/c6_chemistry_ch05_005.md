## ELI5
Ice melts into water and the freezer turns it back — but fry an omelet and no machine un-fries the egg. That contrast is the whole chapter. A reversible change can be undone to restore the original: unfold a paper airplane and the flat sheet returns; melt and refreeze water all day if you like. An irreversible change has crossed a line: cut the sheet with scissors and no patience rejoins the pieces, cook the egg and the raw egg is history. The test question is beautifully short — can I get exactly what I started with by undoing what I did? Yes means reversible; no means irreversible.

## DEFINITION
A reversible change is one that can be undone to restore the original substance, whereas an irreversible change creates a new substance or condition that cannot be returned to its previous state.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that if a change involves physical force (like tearing or breaking), it is "physical" and therefore "reversible." They mistake the ease of movement for the ability to reverse the state. For example, they might think breaking a glass is reversible because it's still glass, forgetting that the *form* cannot be perfectly restored.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to perform a "reversibility test" on a piece of paper. First, fold it (reversible) and have them unfold it. Then, tear it (irreversible) and ask them to try to tape it back perfectly. Point out that while the *material* is the same, the *original object* has been permanently altered, which is the definition of an irreversible change.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You have a 500g block of ice. You let it melt in a bowl, then you freeze it again. Finally, you take a 10g piece of paper and burn it. List which actions are reversible and why.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the melting/freezing of ice as a change of state.
- Step 2: Determine if the substance identity changes: No, it remains water.
- Step 3: Conclude that melting/freezing is reversible.
- Step 4: Identify burning as a chemical change producing ash and smoke.
- Step 5: Conclude that burning is irreversible as the original paper cannot be recovered.
REASONING: Melting and freezing are just changes in the state of water, not the substance itself. Burning involves the creation of new substances (ash/gas), which makes reversal impossible.
ANSWER: Ice melting/freezing is reversible; paper burning is irreversible.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: You take a 10cm long metal wire, bend it into a circle, and then straighten it out. Then, you cut a 10cm piece of wood in half. Classify these changes.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the wire bending: the shape changed, but the wire is still the same wire.
- Step 2: Determine if it can be straightened: Yes, it returns to the original form.
- Step 3: Analyze the wood cutting: the wood is physically separated into two smaller pieces.
- Step 4: Determine if they can be joined back into one piece: No, the physical integrity is broken.
- Step 5: Classify accordingly.
REASONING: Reversibility depends on whether the original configuration can be restored without changing the fundamental material properties.
ANSWER: Bending the wire is reversible; cutting the wood is irreversible.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student mixes 20g of sugar into 100ml of water. Later, they heat the solution until all water evaporates, leaving 20g of sugar behind. Is this one change or two? Is the whole process reversible?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the first change (dissolving sugar in water).
- Step 2: Identify the second change (evaporation of water).
- Step 3: Check if the final products (sugar and water) are the same as the start.
- Step 4: Recognize that evaporation allows for the separation of the mixture.
- Step 5: Conclude that the process is reversible.
REASONING: Because the physical components (water and sugar) remain chemically unchanged during the process, they can be separated, returning the system to its initial state.
ANSWER: It is a two-step process that is entirely reversible.
