## ELI5
You walk in with Rs. 100, buy a notebook for Rs. 40, and spend the rest on Rs. 15 pens - how many pens? Setting up the equation is translation: name the unknown x, convert each phrase of the sentence into algebra, and let the total anchor one side. 'Increased by' adds; 'less than' subtracts in reverse - translate the words exactly as they act, and the equation writes itself.

## DEFINITION
Setting up equations from word problems is the process of translating a real-world verbal description into a mathematical statement of equality using an unknown variable. This involves identifying the unknown quantity, assigning a letter (like $x$), and expressing the given relationships as algebraic expressions connected by an equals sign ($=$).
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can read a multi-step word problem, define a variable for the unknown quantity, write a correct linear equation representing the given conditions, and solve it to find the correct value with proper units.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers translating linear word problems involving one variable into equations and solving them (including numbers, ages, perimeter, and money contexts). It does NOT cover simultaneous linear equations with two variables or quadratic equations.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you go to a stationery shop with Rs. 100, buy a notebook for Rs. 40, and spend the rest on pens that cost Rs. 15 each. How do you figure out how many pens you bought? To solve this, we start by naming what we don't know—let the number of pens be $x$. Next, we translate each piece of the sentence into math, connecting the cost of the notebook and the pens to the total money spent. Building on our knowledge of algebraic expressions and balancing equations, we write an equation like $40 + 15x = 100$. The key insight is that words like "is", "equals", or "results in" become the equals sign ($=$), while words like "more than" or "total" guide our addition and multiplication.

**Takeaway:** define the unknown first, translate each phrase literally, and let the total close the equation. **In the exam:** watch reversal phrases - '5 less than a number' is x minus 5, never the other way round. **Check yourself:** the shop trip gives the notebook's price plus 15 times the pens equal to Rs. 100; solve, then state the unit. **Why students miss it:** students flip subtraction phrases - the flagged mix-up - turning 'subtracted from' backwards. English order is not algebra order: the phrase names what happens to the unknown, so write the unknown first and apply the phrase to it. Units at the end turn the number back into a real purchase.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
If a number $x$ is increased by 12 and the result is 45, which of the following statements represents this situation as an equation?
A) $12x = 45$
B) $x - 12 = 45$
C) $x + 12 = 45$
D) $45 + x = 12$
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is C) $x + 12 = 45$. 
If a student chooses A ($12x = 45$), it reveals a misconception that phrases like "increased by" mean multiplication rather than addition. If a student chooses B ($x - 12 = 45$), they are confusing "increased by" with "decreased by".
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Rahul says that "5 less than three times a number $y$" translates to $5 - 3y$. Is Rahul correct? Explain why or why not.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Rahul is incorrect. The correct algebraic expression is $3y - 5$. A student who agrees with Rahul or writes $5 - 3y$ misunderstands the order of operations in language, failing to realize that "less than" means we must subtract 5 *from* the quantity $3y$ that comes before it.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A rectangular garden has a length that is 3 metres more than twice its width. If the perimeter of the garden is 42 metres, set up an equation to find the width ($w$) and determine the width of the garden.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is width $w = 6$ metres. 
Working steps:
1. Let the width be $w$. Then the length is $2w + 3$.
2. The formula for perimeter is $2 \times (\text{length} + \text{width})$.
3. Set up the equation: $2(2w + 3 + w) = 42$.
4. Simplify inside the parentheses: $2(3w + 3) = 42$.
5. Divide both sides by 2: $3w + 3 = 21$.
6. Subtract 3: $3w = 18$, so $w = 6$.
A student who gets $w = 12$ likely forgot to multiply both the length and width by 2 when calculating the perimeter or forgot to divide the entire perimeter equation correctly.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare these two phrases: "Aman has 5 rupees more than twice of what Priya has" and "Twice of what Priya has is 5 rupees more than Aman has". If Priya has $p$ rupees, write the algebraic expressions for Aman's money in both cases and explain how the position of the word "is" changes the equation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
For the first phrase, Aman's money is an expression: $2p + 5$. For the second phrase, the sentence forms a complete comparison equation: $2p = A + 5$ (where $A$ is Aman's money). A student who writes $2p + 5 = A$ for the second phrase fails to recognize that the word "is" acts as the equals sign separating the two distinct sides of the statement.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Arjun solved the word problem: "Sum of three consecutive integers is 48" by writing the equation $x + (x+1) + (x+2) = 48$. His friend Priya wrote $3x = 48$. Evaluate both equations and explain whose equation correctly represents consecutive integers.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Arjun's equation ($x + (x+1) + (x+2) = 48$) is correct. Priya's equation ($3x = 48$) represents three identical numbers added together, not *consecutive* integers. A student who thinks Priya is correct does not understand that consecutive integers must increase by 1 successively ($x, x+1, x+2$).

Create a word problem about buying fruits at a local market where the total cost is Rs. 150, involving two types of fruits (mangoes and apples), such that it requires setting up a two-step linear equation in one variable to solve.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often write subtraction terms in reverse order when translating phrases like "5 subtracted from a number" or "less than". For example, when translating "7 less than a number is 20", students frequently write $7 - x = 20$ instead of the correct equation $x - 7 = 20$, completely ignoring the direction of the subtraction implied by the English phrasing.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student makes this reversal error, ask them to plug in a test number. Say: "If the number is 10, what is 7 less than 10? You do $10 - 7$, right? So if our unknown number is $x$, we must write $x - 7$, not $7 - x$. Always write the base quantity first and then apply the modification." Have them underline words like "less than" or "subtracted from" and draw an arrow pointing backward to show which number comes first in the expression.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM:
Sunita has 15 more marbles than twice the number of marbles Ravi has. If Sunita has 53 marbles, how many marbles does Ravi have? Set up an equation and solve it.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the unknown and assign a variable. Let Ravi's marbles be $x$.
- Step 2: Translate Sunita's marbles into an algebraic expression based on Ravi's: Sunita has $2x + 15$.
- Step 3: Set the expression equal to Sunita's total known marbles to form the equation: $2x + 15 = 53$.
- Step 4: Solve the equation using inverse operations. Subtract 15 from both sides: $2x = 53 - 15 \implies 2x = 38$.
- Step 5: Divide both sides by 2: $x = 19$. Verify: Twice of 19 is 38, and $38 + 15 = 53$, which matches.
REASONING:
Word problems give clues through relational phrases. "Twice the number" means multiplying by 2, and "15 more than" means adding 15. Setting this equal to Sunita's total gives us a balanced equation to solve for $x$.
ANSWER:
19 marbles
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM:
The sum of three consecutive odd numbers is 69. Find the smallest of these numbers by setting up an equation.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define the unknown variable. Let the smallest odd number be $x$.
- Step 2: Express the next two consecutive odd numbers in terms of $x$. Since odd numbers differ by 2, the next two are $x + 2$ and $x + 4$.
- Step 3: Write the equation representing their sum: $x + (x + 2) + (x + 4) = 69$.
- Step 4: Combine like terms on the left side: $3x + 6 = 69$.
- Step 5: Solve step-by-step: $3x = 69 - 6 \implies 3x = 63 \implies x = 21$.
REASONING:
Consecutive odd (or even) numbers always increase by 2 (e.g., 11, 13, 15). By representing them as $x, x+2, x+4$, we create an equation with a single variable that reflects their sum.
ANSWER:
21
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM:
A father's age is 3 years more than three times his son's age. If the sum of their ages is 59 years, find the son's age.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Let the son's age be $y$ years.
- Step 2: Express the father's age using the given relation: Father's age = $3y + 3$.
- Step 3: Set up the equation for the sum of their ages: $\text{Son's age} + \text{Father's age} = 59 \implies y + (3y + 3) = 59$.
- Step 4: Combine like terms: $4y + 3 = 59$.
- Step 5: Isolate the variable term by subtracting 3: $4y = 59 - 3 \implies 4y = 56$.
- Step 6: Divide by 4: $y = 14$. Verify: Son is 14, Father is $3(14) + 3 = 45$, and $14 + 45 = 59$.
REASONING:
Age problems require defining the younger person's age as a variable, expressing the older person's age relative to it, and setting up an equation based on their sum, difference, or product.
ANSWER:
14 years
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION:
A school canteen sells samosas for Rs. 12 each and juice boxes for Rs. 15 each. Rahul spends a total of Rs. 111. He buys 3 juice boxes. 
Part A: Write an equation to find the number of samosas ($s$) Rahul bought.
Part B: Solve the equation to find how many samosas he bought and explain what each term in your equation represents in real life.
COMPETENCY:
Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and interpreting terms
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the cost of juice boxes: 3 juice boxes $\times$ Rs. 15 = Rs. 45.
- Step 2: Let $s$ be the number of samosas. Cost of samosas = $12s$.
- Step 3: Set up the total cost equation: $12s + 45 = 111$.
- Step 4: Solve for $s$: $12s = 111 - 45 \implies 12s = 66 \implies s = 5.5$. Wait, let's re-verify the numbers or interpret the fractional outcome. If $s = 5.5$, can someone buy half a samosa? This tests critical evaluation of real-life constraints!
ANSWER:
Equation: $12s + 45 = 111$. Solving gives $12s = 66 \implies s = 5.5$. Explanation: In this specific scenario, a fractional answer indicates a contradiction or miscalculation in the problem's premise for whole items, showing that real-world equation setup requires checking if solutions make physical sense.
DIFFICULTY:
hard
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION:
Meera thinks of a number, multiplies it by 4, adds 12, and then divides the result by 2 to get 26. 
Part A: Construct a single linear equation representing Meera's puzzle where $n$ is the unknown number.
Part B: Solve the equation step-by-step using inverse operations and justify each step.
COMPETENCY:
Constructing multi-step algebraic equations from sequential operations
STEPS:
- Step 1: Start with number $n$. Multiply by 4: $4n$.
- Step 2: Add 12: $4n + 12$.
- Step 3: Divide the entire expression by 2: $\frac{4n + 12}{2} = 26$.
- Step 4: Multiply both sides by 2 to clear the denominator: $4n + 12 = 52$.
- Step 5: Subtract 12: $4n = 40$.
- Step 6: Divide by 4: $n = 10$.
ANSWER:
The equation is $\frac{4n + 12}{2} = 26$. Solving yields $n = 10$. Justification: Working backward or using inverse operations in reverse order undoes each mathematical operation sequentially until the unknown $n$ is isolated.
DIFFICULTY:
medium
