## ELI5
Is a car alive? It moves and needs petrol — but it never grows from a baby car into an adult car, never breathes, and never makes baby cars, so movement alone proves nothing. Run the checklist of life instead. Nutrition: a cow eats grass, you eat dal, a plant makes its own food. Growth: a puppy becomes a dog, a seed becomes a tree. Respiration: oxygen releases energy from food. Response: a touch-me-not folds at your finger, a cat turns at your voice. Then waste removal, internal movement, and reproduction — a mango tree makes seeds, a dog makes puppies. The verdict rule: a thing is living only when the signs appear together, not one or two.

## DEFINITION
Living things are set apart from non-living things by characteristics they all share: they need food for energy (nutrition), they grow, they respire, they respond to stimuli around them, they remove waste (excretion), they move or show movement inside, and they reproduce to make new organisms. An object showing none of these is non-living.

## WALKTHROUGH
Play detective. Is a car living? It moves and needs petrol — but it does not grow from a baby car into an adult car, it does not breathe, and it never makes baby cars. Movement alone is not enough. Now walk the checklist of life. Sign one — food: every living thing takes in nutrition; a cow eats grass, you eat dal, a plant makes food in its leaves. Sign two — growth: a puppy becomes a dog, a seed becomes a tree; living things grow in a controlled way, not just piling up like a sand heap. Sign three — respiration: living things take in oxygen (or release it during photosynthesis) and use it to release energy from food. Sign four — response: touch a touch-me-not plant and it folds; shout near a cat and it turns; living things react to their surroundings. Signs five, six, seven — waste removal, internal movement, and reproduction: the body throws out waste, leaves open and close, and every living thing can produce more of its own kind — a mango tree makes seeds, a dog makes puppies. The rule that ties it together: a thing is living only if it shows the signs together — not just one or two.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Why is growth alone not enough to call something living?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Because non-living things can also increase in size — a crystal grows, a sand heap piles up, a balloon swells — but they show none of the other life signs like respiration or reproduction. A wrong answer treats size increase as proof of life.

**A wrong answer (crowning growth as the final judge) reveals the student let the crystal pass the interview - crystals and heaps grow too, so growth alone can never swear life in.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
A dried seed lies in a packet for months. Is it living or non-living?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
It is living but dormant — the seed is alive inside in a resting state, and it germinates (grows and breathes) the moment it gets water, air, and warmth. A wrong answer calls it dead because it does not move — dormancy is paused life, not death.

**A wrong answer (planting the seed in the non-living row) reveals the student buried a sleeping traveller among the dead - the dry seed lives, waiting, and germination is its waking.**

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think anything that moves is living — so they call a fan or a car alive. The truth is that living things must show the characteristics together: food, growth, respiration, response, excretion, movement inside or outside, and reproduction. A second error: assuming a still, dormant seed is dead — life can be asleep, not absent.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Decide whether a potted money plant on a windowsill is living, using the characteristics.
STEPS:
- 1: Check nutrition — it takes in water and makes food by photosynthesis.
- 2: Check growth — it puts out new leaves.
- 3: Check respiration — it takes in oxygen and releases energy.
- 4: Check response — its leaves lean toward light; check reproduction — a cutting can sprout a new plant.
REASONING: Every living characteristic is present — the plant qualifies on all counts.
ANSWER: The money plant is living — it satisfies all the characteristics of life.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A robot vacuum cleaner moves, avoids walls, and "eats" electricity to charge. Is it living?
STEPS:
- 1: Test movement — yes, it moves (but a fan moves too).
- 2: Test growth — no, it never grows from small to big.
- 3: Test respiration and reproduction — no breathing, no offspring.
- 4: Test response — it "avoids walls" only through programmed sensors, not a living response.
REASONING: One or two signs are not enough — the checklist must be satisfied together.
ANSWER: Not living — it moves and "charges" but never grows, breathes, or reproduces, so it fails the characteristics of life.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Is fire a living thing? It grows, moves, and "consumes" fuel.
STEPS:
- 1: List the signs fire shows — it grows (spreads), moves (flickers), and consumes fuel.
- 2: Test the deeper signs — fire does not respire as a controlled process, does not respond to touch, and does not reproduce a new fire in an organism-like way.
- 3: Note that sparks spread fire only by igniting new fuel — that is physics, not reproduction.
REASONING: Superficial signs (grow, move, consume) are not the same as the true life characteristics.
ANSWER: Fire is non-living — it mimics a few signs but lacks real respiration, response, and reproduction.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A virus sits on the border between living and non-living — it cannot do anything alone but multiplies inside a living cell. Using the characteristics, explain why it is so hard to classify.
COMPETENCY: Applying a checklist to a genuinely borderline case.
STEPS:
- 1: Test it outside a cell — a virus does not eat, grow, or respire alone → looks non-living.
- 2: Test it inside a cell — it forces the cell to make copies of it → looks like reproduction.
- 3: Note that it borrows life processes instead of running its own.
- 4: Conclude it sits at the border because it fails the checklist alone but hijacks it inside a host.
ANSWER: A virus is borderline because it lacks life signs on its own but can reproduce inside a living cell — it borrows the checklist rather than owning it.
DIFFICULTY: hard

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Design a simple "living or not" test you could run on a strange object found in a garden — what three checks would you run first and why?
COMPETENCY: Constructing a practical test from the characteristics.
STEPS:
- 1: Pick the most decisive signs — does it grow in a controlled way? does it respond to touch or light? does it produce offspring or seeds?
- 2: Run each check — water it and watch for growth; touch it and watch for response; watch for babies or seeds over days.
- 3: Explain why these three are chosen — they separate real life from things that merely move or swell.
- 4: Conclude that passing several signs together proves living.
ANSWER: Check controlled growth, response to a stimulus, and reproduction — an object passing all three is living; one passing none is not.
DIFFICULTY: medium
