## ELI5
The express train slides from New Delhi toward Agra, milestone markers flashing past the window. From speed, the other two quantities are one rearrangement away: distance is speed multiplied by time; time is distance divided by speed. Know any two numbers, and the third falls out - how far to Agra, or when you arrive. The timetable is just the speed formula wearing a uniform.

## DEFINITION
Calculating distance and time from speed involves using rearranged forms of the speed formula ($Distance = Speed \times Time$ and $Time = \frac{Distance}{Speed}$) to determine unknown spatial or temporal values in uniform linear motion, measured in SI units of meters (m) and seconds (s).

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can algebraically rearrange the speed equation to solve for an unknown distance or time given the other two variables, successfully track and cancel units through dimensional analysis, and verify that the final numerical answer is physically reasonable for everyday Indian contexts (such as a train journey or a bicycle commute).
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the calculation of distance and time for uniform and average linear motion using standard units (m, s, km, h), but does not cover accelerated motion involving changing acceleration or calculus-based kinematic derivations.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the mathematical formula used to calculate distance if you know the speed of an object and the time it has traveled, along with its SI unit?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Distance = Speed $\times$ Time, with the SI unit being meters (m). A student who answers "Distance = Speed / Time" is confusing the distance formula with the speed formula, demonstrating a failure to understand how algebraic rearrangement works when isolating variables.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why must we convert time given in minutes or hours into seconds (or speed into m/s) before performing a distance calculation when using standard SI units?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
We must convert units so that the time units in the speed denominator cancel out with the time units multiplied during the calculation, leaving only the desired unit of length. A student who says "units do not matter as long as the numbers are correct" fails to understand dimensional analysis and will end up with nonsensical composite units like meter-hours per second.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A school bus in Bengaluru travels at a uniform speed of $40\text{ m/s}$ for a duration of $15\text{ seconds}$. Calculate the total distance covered by the bus.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Identify given quantities: Speed ($v$) = $40\text{ m/s}$, Time ($t$) = $15\text{ s}$.
Step 2: Identify formula: $Distance = Speed \times Time$.
Step 3: Substitute values: $Distance = 40\text{ m/s} \times 15\text{ s}$.
Step 4: Compute and cancel units: $40 \times 15 = 600$, and the seconds units cancel out ($\text{m/s} \times \text{s} = \text{m}$).
Step 5: Final Answer = $600\text{ m}$. A student who computes $40 / 15$ has used the wrong formula, while one who multiplies $40 \times 15$ and forgets the meter unit has missed dimensional tracking.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Two cyclists start at the same point. Cyclist A travels at $10\text{ m/s}$ and Cyclist B travels at $5\text{ m/s}$. If both travel for a distance of $300\text{ meters}$, analyze who will take less time and calculate the exact difference in their arrival times.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Cyclist A takes less time because higher speed results in a shorter duration for a fixed distance ($Time = Distance / Speed$). Cyclist A's time is $300\text{ m} / 10\text{ m/s} = 30\text{ s}$, while Cyclist B's time is $300\text{ m} / 5\text{ m/s} = 60\text{ s}$. The difference is $60\text{ s} - 30\text{ s} = 30\text{ seconds}$. A student who states that Cyclist B takes less time has inverted the inverse proportionality between speed and time.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student calculates that a bullock cart moving at $2\text{ m/s}$ covers a distance of $10\text{ meters}$ in $20\text{ seconds}$. Evaluate whether this calculation is physically reasonable and identify any mathematical errors.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The calculation is incorrect because $2\text{ m/s} \times 20\text{ s}$ equals $40\text{ meters}$, not $10\text{ meters}$. Alternatively, finding time as $10\text{ m} / 2\text{ m/s}$ yields $5\text{ seconds}$, not $20\text{ seconds}$. A student who accepts $20\text{ seconds}$ as correct fails to check their work using the reverse relationship ($Speed = Distance / Time$: $10/20 = 0.5\text{ m/s} \neq 2\text{ m/s}$).

Release the toy car from rest (ensuring it reaches a uniform speed of $0.5\text{ m/s}$) and start a stopwatch when it crosses the start line, stopping it at the finish line. ## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently confuse which variable to isolate and end up multiplying speed by distance instead of dividing distance by speed when calculating time ($Time = Distance \times Speed$ instead of $Time = Distance / Speed$). For example, if a car travels $100\text{ km}$ at $50\text{ km/h}$, a student might multiply $100 \times 50 = 5000\text{ hours}$ because they associate "finding a missing value" automatically with multiplication.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this error, guide the student back to dimensional analysis using units as a compass. Ask them to write out the units explicitly: if distance is in meters ($\text{m}$) and speed is in meters per second ($\text{m/s}$), multiplying them yields $\text{m} \times (\text{m/s}) = \text{m}^2/\text{s}$, which is clearly not a unit of time. Show them that dividing meters by meters-per-second ($\text{m} / (\text{m/s})$) algebraically inverts the fraction to leave only seconds ($\text{s}$), proving that division is the correct operation.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A delivery scooter in Mumbai travels at a uniform speed of $12\text{ m/s}$ for $5\text{ minutes}$. Calculate the total distance covered by the scooter in meters.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given quantities: Speed ($v$) = $12\text{ m/s}$, Time ($t$) = $5\text{ minutes}$. What to find: Distance ($d$).
- step 2: Check unit consistency: Time is given in minutes, but speed is in meters per second. Convert time to seconds: $t = 5 \times 60\text{ seconds} = 300\text{ s}$.
- step 3: Select the appropriate rearranged formula: $Distance = Speed \times Time$ ($d = v \times t$).
- step 4: Substitute values with units: $d = 12\text{ m/s} \times 300\text{ s}$.
- step 5: Compute arithmetic and track unit cancellation: $12 \times 300 = 3600$, and $\text{m/s} \times \text{s} = \text{m}$.
- step 6: Verify physical reasonableness: $3600\text{ meters}$ ($3.6\text{ km}$) in 5 minutes for a scooter is entirely reasonable.
REASONING: Time must be converted into the same time unit found in the speed denominator (seconds) before multiplying, allowing the time units to cancel out cleanly and leave meters.
ANSWER: 3600 m
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An express train runs between two stations separated by a distance of $180,000\text{ meters}$ at a constant speed of $25\text{ m/s}$. Calculate the time taken by the train to complete the journey, expressing your final answer in seconds and then in minutes.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given quantities: Distance ($d$) = $180,000\text{ m}$, Speed ($v$) = $25\text{ m/s}$. What to find: Time ($t$).
- step 2: Identify the correct formula by rearranging speed: $Time = \frac{Distance}{Speed}$ ($t = \frac{d}{v}$).
- step 3: Substitute values with units: $t = \frac{180,000\text{ m}}{25\text{ m/s}}$.
- step 4: Compute arithmetic and track unit cancellation: $\frac{180000}{25} = 7200$, and $\frac{\text{m}}{\text{m/s}} = \text{m} \times \frac{\text{s}}{\text{m}} = \text{s}$.
- step 5: Convert seconds to minutes for deeper clarity: $7200\text{ s} / 60 = 120\text{ minutes}$ (or $2\text{ hours}$).
- step 6: Verify physical reasonableness: A train traveling $180\text{ km}$ in 2 hours at $25\text{ m/s}$ ($90\text{ km/h}$) is standard for Indian passenger trains.
REASONING: To find time, distance must be divided by speed because speed tells us how many meters are covered in every single second.
ANSWER: 7200 s (or 120 minutes)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A cyclist participating in a village race rides at a steady speed of $8\text{ m/s}$ for the first half of a track, covering $960\text{ meters}$. For the second half of the track, she gets tired and rides at $6\text{ m/s}$ for another $960\text{ meters}$. Calculate the total time taken for the entire $1920\text{ meter}$ journey.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given quantities for Part 1: Distance ($d_1$) = $960\text{ m}$, Speed ($v_1$) = $8\text{ m/s}$.
- step 2: Calculate time for Part 1 using $t_1 = \frac{d_1}{v_1}$: $t_1 = \frac{960\text{ m}}{8\text{ m/s}} = 120\text{ s}$.
- step 3: Identify given quantities for Part 2: Distance ($d_2$) = $960\text{ m}$, Speed ($v_2$) = $6\text{ m/s}$.
- step 4: Calculate time for Part 2 using $t_2 = \frac{d_2}{v_2}$: $t_2 = \frac{960\text{ m}}{6\text{ m/s}} = 160\text{ s}$.
- step 5: Sum the individual times to find the total journey time: $Total\text{ Time} = t_1 + t_2 = 120\text{ s} + 160\text{ s} = 280\text{ s}$.
- step 6: Verify physical reasonableness: Total distance is $1920\text{ m}$, average speed is around $6.85\text{ m/s}$, so $280\text{ seconds}$ is a completely logical duration.
REASONING: When speed changes during a trip, you cannot simply average the speeds; you must calculate the time taken for each distinct segment of uniform motion separately and add them together.
ANSWER: 280 s
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Two cars leave a toll plaza on a straight national highway at the same time. Car X travels at $20\text{ m/s}$ and Car Y travels at $25\text{ m/s}$. If Car Y reaches a destination checkpoint $10,000\text{ meters}$ away, calculate how many seconds Car X will take to reach that exact same checkpoint after Car Y has already finished.
COMPETENCY: Interpreting physical situations using formulas
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand the physical situation: Both cars travel the same distance ($10,000\text{ m}$), but at different speeds.
- step 2: Calculate the travel time for Car Y: $t_Y = \frac{10,000\text{ m}}{25\text{ m/s}} = 400\text{ s}$.
- step 3: Calculate the travel time for Car X: $t_X = \frac{10,000\text{ m}}{20\text{ m/s}} = 500\text{ s}$.
- step 4: Find the time difference between their arrivals: $500\text{ s} - 400\text{ s} = 100\text{ seconds}$.
ANSWER: Car X will take $500\text{ seconds}$, which is $100\text{ seconds}$ after Car Y arrives.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A commuter travels a total distance of $60\text{ km}$ to work. The first $30\text{ km}$ is traveled in a local train at a uniform speed of $20\text{ m/s}$, and the remaining $30\text{ km}$ is traveled in a bus. If the total time taken for the entire $60\text{ km}$ journey is $2500\text{ seconds}$, calculate the uniform speed of the bus in m/s.
COMPETENCY: Unit analysis and dimensional reasoning
STEPS:
- step 1: Convert all distances to meters for SI consistency: Total distance = $60,000\text{ m}$, Part 1 distance = $30,000\text{ m}$, Part 2 distance = $30,000\text{ m}$.
- step 2: Calculate the time taken for Part 1 (train): $t_1 = \frac{30,000\text{ m}}{20\text{ m/s}} = 1500\text{ s}$.
- step 3: Determine the time taken for Part 2 (bus) by subtracting train time from total time: $t_2 = Total\text{ Time} - t_1 = 2500\text{ s} - 1500\text{ s} = 1000\text{ s}$.
- step 4: Use the bus distance and bus time to calculate bus speed: $v_{bus} = \frac{d_2}{t_2} = \frac{30,000\text{ m}}{1000\text{ s}} = 30\text{ m/s}$.
ANSWER: The uniform speed of the bus is $30\text{ m/s}$.
DIFFICULTY: hard
