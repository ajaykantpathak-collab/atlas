## ELI5
Science needed one honest yardstick for 'how concentrated is this push?' and named it the pascal: one newton pressing on one square metre. The number is small — a pascal is a gentle nudge spread wide — so real pressures wear bigger clothes: kilopascals, thousands at a time. Calculating in pascals is a unit discipline: force in newtons, area in square metres, divide, and the answer inherits the proper name. Mixing square centimetres in uninvited garbles the result.

## DEFINITION
The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (symbol Pa), defined as one newton of force acting perpendicularly on one square metre of area (1 Pa = 1 N/m²). It quantifies how concentrated a force is over a surface.

## WALKTHROUGH
Observe a drawing pin (thumbtack) and a coin placed on a wooden table. Press each with the same force using your palm. The drawing pin pierces the wood easily while the coin does not, even though the force is identical. This happens because the pin's tip has a much smaller area, concentrating the force. In the previous node (c8_physics_ch4_1), we defined this concentration as pressure = force/area. To measure this concentration scientifically, we need a standard unit. We introduce the pascal, named after Blaise Pascal, as the SI unit: 1 pascal = 1 newton per square metre. We then practice calculating pressure by converting all areas to square metres before dividing force by area.

Takeaway: one pascal equals one newton per square metre; kilopascals scale by a thousand; every pressure calculation demands newtons over square metres after conversion. Exam format: expect 'state the SI unit and its meaning', conversions between pascals and kilopascals, and computations where the area arrives in inconvenient units. Verification beat: write the unit with the answer — a bare number in a pressure question is an incomplete answer. Why students miss it: they forget the thousand in kilopascals, or leave centimetres unconverted — pressure arithmetic is mostly unit hygiene, and the pascal's definition is the checklist. Write the chain 'newtons over square metres' and the conversion decides itself.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the SI unit of pressure, and how is it defined in terms of the newton and the metre?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa), defined as one newton per square metre (1 Pa = 1 N/m²). A wrong answer such as "newton" or "joule" reveals the student confuses pressure with force or energy, not recognising that pressure is a *distributed* quantity requiring both force and area in its definition.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Why is the pascal considered a *derived* SI unit and not a base unit? Explain using the definition of pressure.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Pressure is defined as force per unit area (P = F/A). Force (newton) is itself a derived unit (kg·m/s²), and area is m². Therefore, 1 Pa = 1 N/m² = (kg·m/s²)/m² = kg·m⁻¹·s⁻², which is a combination of base units (kg, m, s). A student who says "pascal is a base unit" does not understand the hierarchy of SI units or that pressure depends on other defined quantities.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A force of 250 N acts uniformly on a rectangular surface of dimensions 2 m by 1.5 m. Calculate the pressure in pascals.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Area = length × breadth = 2 m × 1.5 m = 3 m².  
Step 2: Pressure = Force / Area = 250 N / 3 m².  
Step 3: 250 ÷ 3 = 83.33... N/m² = 83.33 Pa.  
A wrong answer like 125 Pa (using only one dimension) or 166.67 Pa (using perimeter) reveals the student cannot identify the correct area for the pressure formula or confuses area with linear dimensions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the pressure exerted by a 50 kg girl standing on one heel (area 1 cm²) versus standing on both feet (total area 200 cm²). Use g = 10 m/s². Which situation produces higher pressure and by what factor?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Force = mg = 50 kg × 10 m/s² = 500 N.  
One heel: Area = 1 cm² = 0.0001 m² → Pressure = 500 / 0.0001 = 5,000,000 Pa = 5 MPa.  
Both feet: Area = 200 cm² = 0.02 m² → Pressure = 500 / 0.02 = 25,000 Pa = 25 kPa.  
Pressure on one heel is 200 times higher (area ratio 200:1). A wrong answer often stems from forgetting to convert cm² to m² (1 m² = 10,000 cm²), leading to pressures 10,000 times too small, or not recognising the inverse relationship between pressure and area.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student calculates the pressure as 2000 Pa for a force of 100 N acting on an area of 0.02 m². Is this correct? If not, identify the error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The correct pressure is 100 N / 0.02 m² = 5000 Pa. The student's answer of 2000 Pa is incorrect. They likely multiplied force by area (100 × 0.02 = 2) or divided area by force (0.02 / 100 = 0.0002), showing a fundamental misunderstanding of the formula P = F/A and the inverse relationship between pressure and area.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple experiment using household items to demonstrate that the same force produces different pressures on different areas. List the materials, procedure, and how you would estimate the pressure in pascals.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer: Use a kitchen scale to measure force (e.g., press down until scale reads 5 kg → 50 N). Press a flat coin (area ~2 cm² = 0.0002 m²) and a pin head (area ~0.1 mm² = 1×10⁻⁷ m²) into soft clay or dough. Measure depth of indentation as a proxy for pressure. Calculate: Coin pressure = 50/0.0002 = 250,000 Pa; Pin pressure = 50/1×10⁻⁷ = 5×10⁸ Pa. A poor answer lacks a way to measure force in newtons, uses area in cm² without conversion, or does not connect the observation to the calculated pascal values.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students treat pressure and force as interchangeable, saying "heavy objects exert high pressure" regardless of contact area. They also routinely forget to convert cm² to m², plugging 5 cm² directly into P = F/A and obtaining an answer in N/cm² while labelling it "Pa". For example, a 500 N force on 5 cm² gives 100 N/cm², but the student writes "100 Pa" instead of converting 5 cm² = 0.0005 m² to get 1,000,000 Pa.
## ON_FAILURE
If the student confuses pressure with force, have them press a balloon with an open palm (area ~100 cm²) and then with a fingertip (area ~1 cm²) using a spring scale to apply the *same* force (e.g., 10 N). The fingertip bursts the balloon; the palm does not. Then guide them through the calculation: Palm pressure = 10 N / 0.01 m² = 1000 Pa; Fingertip pressure = 10 N / 0.0001 m² = 100,000 Pa. Emphasise the conversion: 1 m² = 10,000 cm², so divide cm² by 10,000 to get m². Practice three area conversions before attempting pressure problems.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A school bag weighs 150 N and rests on a student's shoulder through a strap of width 5 cm. If the strap is rectangular and 50 cm long, calculate the pressure on the shoulder in pascals.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given quantities: Force F = 150 N (weight of bag). Strap dimensions: width = 5 cm, length = 50 cm. Find pressure P in Pa.
- step 2: Convert dimensions to metres: width = 5 cm = 0.05 m, length = 50 cm = 0.5 m. Area A = width × length = 0.05 m × 0.5 m = 0.025 m². Formula: P = F/A.
- step 3: Substitute values with units: P = 150 N / 0.025 m².
- step 4: Compute: 150 ÷ 0.025 = 6000. Unit cancellation: N/m² = Pa. So P = 6000 Pa.
- step 5: Verify: 6000 Pa = 6 kPa. A typical school bag pressure is a few kPa; the answer is physically reasonable.
REASONING: Pressure depends on both force and contact area. The strap's area must be in square metres to yield pascals. Converting cm to m *before* calculating area avoids the common error of dividing by 100 instead of 10,000.
ANSWER: 6000 Pa (or 6 kPa)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An elephant of mass 4000 kg stands on one foot. The foot is roughly circular with a diameter of 40 cm. Calculate the pressure exerted on the ground in pascals. Take g = 10 m/s².
STEPS:
- step 1: Given: mass m = 4000 kg, g = 10 m/s². Force (weight) F = m × g = 4000 × 10 = 40,000 N. Diameter = 40 cm = 0.4 m, so radius r = 0.2 m.
- step 2: Area of circular foot A = πr² = 3.14 × (0.2 m)² = 3.14 × 0.04 = 0.1256 m². Formula: P = F/A.
- step 3: Substitute: P = 40,000 N / 0.1256 m².
- step 4: Compute: 40,000 ÷ 0.1256 ≈ 318,471.3 Pa ≈ 3.18 × 10⁵ Pa.
- step 5: Verify: Elephant pressure is around 10⁵ Pa (100 kPa), much less than a high heel (MPa), which is reasonable due to larger area.
REASONING: Weight provides the normal force. Circular area uses radius in metres. The result shows that despite the elephant's huge weight, its pressure is moderate because of the large foot area.
ANSWER: 3.18 × 10⁵ Pa (or 318 kPa)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A hydraulic lift in a garage uses a small piston of area 0.005 m² and a large piston of area 0.5 m². If a mechanic applies a force of 200 N on the small piston, what is the pressure in the fluid? What force can the large piston exert? (Assume an ideal hydraulic system where pressure is transmitted equally.)
STEPS:
- step 1: Given: F₁ = 200 N, A₁ = 0.005 m², A₂ = 0.5 m². Find fluid pressure P and output force F₂.
- step 2: Pressure in fluid P = F₁ / A₁ = 200 N / 0.005 m² = 40,000 Pa.
- step 3: By Pascal's principle (pressure transmitted equally), pressure on large piston is same: P = 40,000 Pa.
- step 4: Output force F₂ = P × A₂ = 40,000 Pa × 0.5 m² = 20,000 N.
- step 5: Verify: Area ratio A₂/A₁ = 0.5 / 0.005 = 100. Force multiplication factor = 100, so F₂ = 200 N × 100 = 20,000 N. Consistent.
REASONING: This example connects the pascal to Pascal's principle (a forward concept). The pressure calculated in pascals is the *link* between input and output forces. Consistent SI units (Pa = N/m²) allow direct multiplication by area to get force in newtons.
ANSWER: Pressure = 40,000 Pa (40 kPa); Force on large piston = 20,000 N.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student says, "If I double the force and double the area, the pressure stays the same." Is this statement correct? Explain using the formula and give a numerical example.
COMPETENCY: Predicting outcomes from changing variables
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand the physical situation: pressure depends on the ratio of force to area.
- step 2: Identify the principle: P = F/A. If both F and A are doubled, new pressure P' = (2F)/(2A) = F/A = P.
- step 3: Apply with numbers: Original F = 10 N, A = 2 m² → P = 5 Pa. Doubled: F = 20 N, A = 4 m² → P = 5 Pa.
- step 4: Interpret physically: Pressure is an intensive quantity; scaling both extensive quantities (force and area) proportionally leaves the ratio unchanged.
ANSWER: Yes, the statement is correct. Pressure remains unchanged because it is the ratio of force to area. Doubling both numerator and denominator leaves the ratio unchanged.
DIFFICULTY: easy
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A rectangular brick of dimensions 20 cm × 10 cm × 5 cm and weight 30 N is placed on a table. The brick can be placed on any of its three faces. Calculate the maximum and minimum pressures the brick can exert on the table. Explain why the pressures differ.
COMPETENCY: Interpreting physical situations using formulas and unit analysis
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the three possible face areas: (20×10) cm² = 200 cm², (20×5) cm² = 100 cm², (10×5) cm² = 50 cm².
- step 2: Convert each area to m²: 200 cm² = 0.02 m², 100 cm² = 0.01 m², 50 cm² = 0.005 m².
- step 3: Pressure = Weight / Area. Weight = 30 N (constant).
- step 4: Calculate pressures: P_max = 30 N / 0.005 m² = 6000 Pa (smallest area face). P_min = 30 N / 0.02 m² = 1500 Pa (largest area face).
- step 5: Explain: For a constant force, pressure is inversely proportional to contact area. The 10 cm × 5 cm face gives maximum pressure; the 20 cm × 10 cm face gives minimum pressure.
ANSWER: Maximum pressure = 6000 Pa (on 10 cm × 5 cm face). Minimum pressure = 1500 Pa (on 20 cm × 10 cm face). Pressure differs because the contact area changes while the weight (force) remains the same.
DIFFICULTY: medium
