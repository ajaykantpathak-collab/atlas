## ELI5
Three times some number of notebooks, plus ₹20 for a pen, lands on ₹110 - the shopkeeper's sentence is already an equation. Inverse operations are the un-buttoning: subtract the ₹20 where addition happened, divide by 3 where multiplication happened, each move applied to both sides. Undo the arithmetic in the opposite order it was done, and the number of notebooks steps out of the bill on its own.

## DEFINITION
Solving linear equations using inverse operations involves applying opposite arithmetic operations (such as subtraction for addition, and division for multiplication) to both sides of an equation in a systematic order to isolate the variable and find its unknown value.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can solve one-step and two-step linear equations in one variable using inverse operations, showing each step of isolation clearly, and can verify the solution by substitution.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers linear equations in one variable involving whole numbers, integers, and simple fractions using inverse operations. Does not cover quadratic equations, systems of equations, or equations with variables on both sides.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you went to the local stationery shop in your neighborhood and bought some notebooks, and the shopkeeper tells you that 3 times the number of notebooks you bought, plus ₹20 for a pen, equals ₹110 in total. How do you figure out exactly how many notebooks you bought without guessing? In Class 6, you learned how to balance equations like a see-saw by performing the same operation on both sides. Now in Class 7, we speed up this process by using "inverse operations"—the mathematical opposites like undoing addition with subtraction or undoing multiplication with division. By systematically applying these inverse operations in reverse order of operations, we strip away numbers one by one from the side with the variable. The key insight is that whatever operation you perform to undo a term on one side, you must do the exact same operation to the other side to keep the equality balanced.

**Takeaway:** every operation has an opposite, and the opposite is how the unknown is freed. **In the exam:** addition undoes subtraction, division undoes multiplication - apply the inverse to both sides and show every step of the isolation. **Check yourself:** run the shopkeeper's bill: remove the ₹20 pen first, then divide by 3 to name the notebooks. **Why students miss it:** students subtract 4 from 4x and wonder why nothing isolates - undoing multiplication needs division, not subtraction. Match each inverse to the exact operation it reverses, apply it to both sides, and the variable is left standing by itself, ready for the substitution check that closes the problem.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the inverse operation used to solve the equation $x + 15 = 42$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Subtraction (specifically, subtracting 15 from both sides). A wrong answer like addition or multiplication shows the student confuses the operation present in the equation with its inverse, failing to recognize that to undo adding 15, one must remove it by subtracting.

**A wrong answer (re-solving the equation) reveals the student confused checking with solving - verification is one substitution into both sides, far cheaper than solving the equation a second time from scratch.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
When solving the equation $4x = 36$ using inverse operations, why do we divide both sides by 4 instead of subtracting 4?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
We divide by 4 because $4x$ means 4 multiplied by $x$, and division is the inverse operation of multiplication. Subtracting 4 would change the expression to $4x - 4$, which does not isolate $x$. A wrong answer indicates the student does not distinguish between additive relationships and multiplicative relationships within algebraic terms.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Solve the equation $5x - 7 = 38$ using inverse operations. What is the value of $x$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Add 7 to both sides to undo the subtraction: $5x - 7 + 7 = 38 + 7$, which simplifies to $5x = 45$. Step 2: Divide both sides by 5 to undo the multiplication: $\frac{5x}{5} = \frac{45}{5}$, which gives $x = 9$. A wrong answer often stems from dividing by 5 before undoing the subtraction, or performing incorrect integer arithmetic during transposition.

**A wrong answer (simplifying sloppily) reveals the student let a side collapse carelessly - compute each side completely, following the order of operations, before comparing the two totals.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare solving $x + 8 = 20$ with solving $8x = 20$. Explain why the first uses subtraction while the second uses division, even though both equations involve the number 8.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
In $x + 8 = 20$, 8 is added to the variable, so its inverse is subtraction. In $8x = 20$, 8 is multiplied by the variable, so its inverse is division. The operation used depends on how the number is connected to the variable, not the number itself. A wrong answer shows a misunderstanding of how terms are structured algebraically.

**A wrong answer (calling it close enough) reveals the student an equation has no close enough - 25 and 19 are different numbers, so the tested value is simply not a solution, and the verdict is stated without apology.**

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a two-step linear equation in variable $y$ whose solution is exactly $y = 7$. Show the inverse operations required to solve your equation.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A valid equation is $4y - 6 = 22$. To solve it: Step 1, add 6 to both sides to get $4y = 28$. Step 2, divide both sides by 4 to get $y = 7$. A poor answer creates an expression that does not evaluate to 7 or uses incorrect inverse steps that fail to isolate the variable.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often apply the wrong inverse operation or forget to change the sign when moving terms. For example, when solving $x - 12 = 30$, a student might subtract 12 instead of adding it, or when solving $6x = 48$, they might subtract 6 instead of dividing by 6. This happens because they memorize rules mechanically without understanding that inverse operations literally "undo" what was done to the variable.
## ON_FAILURE
Guide the student by asking: "What operation was originally done to the variable in this equation?" Have them write down the original operation (e.g., "Added 6"). Then ask: "What is the exact opposite (inverse) of that operation?" (e.g., "Subtracted 6"). Remind them to apply that inverse operation to both sides simultaneously to keep the equation balanced.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Riya went to the market and bought some identical notebooks. Each notebook costs ₹25, and she also paid a delivery charge of ₹15. Her total bill came to ₹140. Set up an equation and find the number of notebooks ($n$) she bought using inverse operations.
STEPS:
- step 1: Translate the word problem into a linear equation: $25n + 15 = 140$.
- step 2: Identify the operations acting on $n$: first multiplied by 25, then 15 is added.
- step 3: Apply the inverse of adding 15 (which is subtracting 15) to both sides: $25n + 15 - 15 = 140 - 15$, which simplifies to $25n = 125$.
- step 4: Apply the inverse of multiplying by 25 (which is dividing by 25) to both sides: $\frac{25n}{25} = \frac{125}{25}$, which gives $n = 5$.
- step 5: Verify the result by substituting $n = 5$ back into the original expression: $25(5) + 15 = 125 + 15 = 140$, confirming the answer is correct with 5 notebooks.
REASONING: To isolate the variable $n$, we undo operations in reverse order of PEMDAS/BODMAS. Since 15 was added last, we subtract it first. Since 25 multiplied $n$, we divide by it last, maintaining balance across the equals sign at every step.
ANSWER: 5 notebooks
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Solve the equation $\frac{x}{4} - 6 = 5$ using inverse operations.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the equation: $\frac{x}{4} - 6 = 5$.
- step 2: Identify the operations on $x$: $x$ is divided by 4, and then 6 is subtracted.
- step 3: Apply the inverse of subtracting 6 (which is adding 6) to both sides: $\frac{x}{4} - 6 + 6 = 5 + 6$, which gives $\frac{x}{4} = 11$.
- step 4: Apply the inverse of dividing by 4 (which is multiplying by 4) to both sides: $4 \times \left(\frac{x}{4}\right) = 11 \times 4$, which results in $x = 44$.
- step 5: Verify by substitution: $\frac{44}{4} - 6 = 11 - 6 = 5$, which matches the right-hand side.
REASONING: Subtraction is undone first by addition, leaving the fractional term $\frac{x}{4}$ isolated. Then, division is undone by multiplying both sides by the denominator 4 to completely isolate $x$.
ANSWER: $x = 44$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Amit is thinking of a number. If he multiplies it by 3, subtracts 8, and the result is equal to adding 12 to the number, find the number by setting up an equation and using inverse operations. (Hint: equation is $3x - 8 = x + 12$).
STEPS:
- step 1: Write down the given equation: $3x - 8 = x + 12$.
- step 2: Gather all variable terms on one side by applying inverse operations. Subtract $x$ from both sides: $3x - x - 8 = x - x + 12$, which simplifies to $2x - 8 = 12$.
- step 3: Apply the inverse of subtracting 8 (add 8) to both sides: $2x - 8 + 8 = 12 + 8$, which gives $2x = 20$.
- step 4: Apply the inverse of multiplying by 2 (divide by 2) to both sides: $\frac{2x}{2} = \frac{20}{2}$, which results in $x = 10$.
- step 5: Verify the solution by substituting $n = 10$ into both sides of the original equation: Left side = $3(10) - 8 = 30 - 8 = 22$; Right side = $10 + 12 = 22$. Both sides are equal.
REASONING: When variables appear on both sides, we first use inverse operations to collect them onto a single side. Once simplified into a standard two-step equation, we apply inverse operations for constants and coefficients successively.
ANSWER: $x = 10$
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A farmer has a fenced rectangular vegetable patch where the length is 5 metres more than twice its breadth. If the total perimeter of the patch is 58 metres, set up an equation for the breadth ($b$) and use inverse operations to find the dimensions of the patch.
COMPETENCY: Translating real-world situations to mathematical expressions and solving using inverse operations
STEPS:
- step 1: Let breadth be $b$ metres, so length is $2b + 5$ metres.
- step 2: Use perimeter formula $P = 2(\text{length} + \text{breadth})$ to set up the equation: $2((2b + 5) + b) = 58$.
- step 3: Simplify inside the brackets: $2(3b + 5) = 58$, then divide both sides by 2 to get $3b + 5 = 29$.
- step 4: Apply inverse operations: subtract 5 to get $3b = 24$, then divide by 3 to get $b = 8$. Calculate length: $2(8) + 5 = 21$.
ANSWER: Breadth = 8 metres, Length = 21 metres.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two students, Rahul and Priya, solve the equation $5 - 2x = 13$. 
Rahul says: "Add 5 to both sides first, so $-2x = 18$, then divide by $-2$ to get $x = -9$."
Priya says: "Subtract 5 from both sides first, so $-2x = 8$, then divide by $-2$ to get $x = -4$."
Analyze both methods, identify who is correct, and explain the exact mathematical error made by the incorrect student.
COMPETENCY: Error identification and justification of algebraic methods
STEPS:
- step 1: Examine Rahul's first step: he added 5 to both sides where the original equation had a positive 5 ($+5 - 2x$). Adding 5 makes it $10 - 2x$, not $-2x$.
- step 2: Examine Priya's first step: she subtracted 5 from both sides ($5 - 5 - 2x = 13 - 5$), correctly reducing the left side to $-2x$ and right side to 8.
- step 3: Apply Priya's second step: divide $-2x = 8$ by $-2$, resulting in $x = -4$.
- step 4: Verify Priya's solution by substitution: $5 - 2(-4) = 5 - (-8) = 5 + 8 = 13$, which matches the right side.
ANSWER: Priya is correct. Rahul made a sign error by adding 5 instead of subtracting it, failing to recognize that the positive sign belongs to the constant 5, and its inverse operation requires subtraction.
DIFFICULTY: hard
