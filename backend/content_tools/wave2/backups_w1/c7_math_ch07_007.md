## ELI5
Tear the three corners off a triangular paratha slice and lay them along a ruler's edge: they assemble into a perfect straight line. That is the angle sum property - a triangle's three interior angles always total a straight angle. The consequence list writes itself: the third angle surrenders by subtraction, two obtuse corners can never fit, and ratio questions become algebra - share the straight angle in the parts requested. One tear-and-lay demonstration outlasts any memorised formula.

## DEFINITION
The Angle Sum Property of a triangle states that the interior angles of any triangle always add up to exactly $180^\circ$. This means that if you take any triangle and sum its three interior angles, the total measurement will consistently equal a straight line ($180^\circ$).
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can calculate an unknown interior angle of a triangle given the other two angles, can classify whether a set of three given angle measurements can form a valid triangle, and can apply algebraic equations to solve missing angle problems involving variables.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers interior angles of Euclidean triangles in a plane, including numerical problems and algebraic expressions for angles. It does NOT cover spherical geometry, exterior angle properties (which have their own node), or polygons with more than three sides.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are cutting a triangular slice of a paratha for lunch, and you want to check if the corners fit together on a straight line. If you tear off the three corners of any paper triangle and place them side by side along a ruler's edge, you will notice they form a perfect straight line, which measures $180^\circ$. Building on your prior knowledge of lines, angles, and types of triangles from Class 6, we now investigate how these three interior angles are mathematically locked together. To begin solving problems, write down the fundamental equation: $\text{Angle } A + \text{Angle } B + \text{Angle } C = 180^\circ$. The key insight is that no matter how stretched, skinny, or large a triangle is, its interior angle sum never changes from $180^\circ$.

**Takeaway:** the three interior angles of any triangle total exactly a straight angle. **In the exam:** subtract the two known angles for the third; for ratios, distribute the total across the parts. **Check yourself:** tear a paper triangle's corners and lay them along a ruler edge to see the straight angle. **Why students miss it:** they set the total to a quarter-turn and every answer after inherits the error.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the sum of the three interior angles of any triangle?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The sum of the three interior angles of any triangle is $180^\circ$. A student who answers $360^\circ$ is confusing the interior angle sum of a triangle with that of a quadrilateral or a full circle, while a student who answers $90^\circ$ is confusing it with a right angle or the complementary angle property.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Can a triangle have two obtuse angles (angles greater than $90^\circ$)? Explain why or why not using the angle sum property.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
No, a triangle cannot have two obtuse angles. If a triangle had two obtuse angles, say $95^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, their sum would already be $185^\circ$, which exceeds the total allowable angle sum of $180^\circ$ for a triangle before even adding the third angle. A student who says "yes" fails to comprehend that the total sum is strictly fixed at $180^\circ$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
In $\triangle PQR$, angle $\angle P = 55^\circ$ and angle $\angle Q = 65^\circ$. What is the measure of angle $\angle R$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The measure of angle $\angle R$ is $60^\circ$. Working steps: 1) State the formula: $\angle P + \angle Q + \angle R = 180^\circ$. 2) Substitute known values: $55^\circ + 65^\circ + \angle R = 180^\circ$. 3) Combine like terms: $120^\circ + \angle R = 180^\circ$. 4) Subtract $120^\circ$ from both sides: $\angle R = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$. A student who answers $120^\circ$ stopped after adding the two given angles and forgot to subtract that sum from $180^\circ$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
If the three angles of a triangle are in the ratio $2:3:4$, what is the measure of the largest angle?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The measure of the largest angle is $80^\circ$. Working steps: 1) Let the angles be $2x$, $3x$, and $4x$. 2) Set up the equation using the angle sum property: $2x + 3x + 4x = 180^\circ$. 3) Simplify the expression: $9x = 180^\circ$. 4) Solve for $x$: $x = 180^\circ / 9 = 20^\circ$. 5) Calculate the largest angle: $4 \times 20^\circ = 80^\circ$. A student who answers $4$ forgot to assign a variable multiplier $x$ to the ratio parts and treated them as absolute angle degrees.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student solves for the unknown angles of a triangle where the angles are given as $x$, $2x$, and $3x$. They write the equation $x + 2x + 3x = 90^\circ$ and conclude that $x = 15^\circ$. Identify the error in their reasoning and find the correct value of $x$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The student's error was setting the sum of the angles equal to $90^\circ$ instead of $180^\circ$. The correct equation is $x + 2x + 3x = 180^\circ$, which simplifies to $6x = 180^\circ$, yielding $x = 30^\circ$. A student who agrees with the original working does not remember that the angle sum of a triangle is $180^\circ$, confusing it with complementary angles ($90^\circ$).
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Construct a word problem about a triangular garden plot in a school courtyard where two angles are known algebraically in terms of $x$, and the third angle is a fixed number of degrees, requiring an equation to find all three angles.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently confuse the angle sum property of a triangle ($180^\circ$) with the angle sum of a quadrilateral ($360^\circ$) or a straight line ($180^\circ$), but more commonly, they subtract only one given angle from $180^\circ$ when two angles are provided, or they incorrectly perform arithmetic operations like adding the two angles and setting that equal to the unknown angle without subtracting from $180^\circ$. For example, given angles $50^\circ$ and $70^\circ$, a student might write $\angle C = 50^\circ + 70^\circ = 120^\circ$ as their final answer instead of $180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$.
## ON_FAILURE
If a student struggles, take a physical piece of paper cut into a triangle, tear off the three corners, and physically align them along a flat line drawn on a notebook page to show it forms a straight $180^\circ$ angle. Remind them: "Every single triangle in the universe has three angles that must add up to a straight line. If I give you two angles, your job is first to add those two together, and then find out how much more is needed to reach $180^\circ$ by subtracting from $180^\circ$." Have them write the equation $\text{Angle } 1 + \text{Angle } 2 + \text{Angle } 3 = 180^\circ$ at the top of every problem before substituting any numbers.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: In a triangular park shaped like $\triangle ABC$, angle $\angle A = 48^\circ$ and angle $\angle B = 72^\circ$. A gardener wants to calculate the third angle $\angle C$ to plant a specific flower bed. Find the measure of $\angle C$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given values: $\angle A = 48^\circ$, $\angle B = 72^\circ$, and the total interior angle sum of any triangle is $180^\circ$.
- step 2: Write down the angle sum property equation: $\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ$.
- step 3: Substitute the known values into the equation: $48^\circ + 72^\circ + \angle C = 180^\circ$.
- step 4: Perform addition for the known angles: $120^\circ + \angle C = 180^\circ$.
- step 5: Isolate $\angle C$ by subtracting $120^\circ$ from $180^\circ$: $\angle C = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$.
REASONING: The angle sum property dictates that all three interior angles must combine to form $180^\circ$. By adding the two known angles and finding their deficit from $180^\circ$, we determine the exact size of the remaining angle.
ANSWER: 60^\circ
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: The angles of a triangular banner for a school sports meet are in the algebraic ratio $1 : 2 : 3$. Find the exact measure in degrees of each of the three angles.
STEPS:
- step 1: Let the common multiplier for the ratio be $x$. Therefore, the three angles can be represented as $1x$, $2x$, and $3x$.
- step 2: State the angle sum property equation for the triangle: $1x + 2x + 3x = 180^\circ$.
- step 3: Combine all the like algebraic terms on the left side: $6x = 180^\circ$.
- step 4: Solve for $x$ by dividing both sides by $6$: $x = 180^\circ / 6 = 30^\circ$.
- step 5: Multiply $x$ by each ratio coefficient to find the individual angles: Angle 1 = $1(30^\circ) = 30^\circ$, Angle 2 = $2(30^\circ) = 60^\circ$, Angle 3 = $3(30^\circ) = 90^\circ$.
REASONING: When parts of a whole are given in a ratio, introducing a variable $x$ allows us to express each proportional part algebraically. Setting their sum to $180^\circ$ creates a solvable linear equation.
ANSWER: 30^\circ, 60^\circ, and 90^\circ
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: In $\triangle XYZ$, angle $\angle X$ is $(2x + 15)^\circ$, angle $\angle Y$ is $(3x - 5)^\circ$, and angle $\angle Z$ is $50^\circ$. Find the value of $x$ and then calculate the numerical measure of angle $\angle X$.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify all three interior angle expressions: $\angle X = 2x + 15$, $\angle Y = 3x - 5$, and $\angle Z = 50$.
- step 2: Set up the angle sum equation: $(2x + 15) + (3x - 5) + 50 = 180$.
- step 3: Group the variable terms together and the constant numbers together: $(2x + 3x) + (15 - 5 + 50) = 180$.
- step 4: Simplify the combined terms: $5x + 60 = 180$.
- step 5: Transpose $60$ to the right side and subtract: $5x = 180 - 60 \Rightarrow 5x = 120$.
- step 6: Solve for $x$ by dividing by $5$: $x = 120 / 5 = 24$.
- step 7: Substitute $x = 24$ back into the expression for angle $\angle X$: $\angle X = 2(24) + 15 = 48 + 15 = 63^\circ$.
REASONING: Algebraic expressions for angles obey the exact same geometric rule as numerical angles. We collect like terms, solve the resulting linear equation for the variable $x$, and then substitute that value back in to find the requested angle.
ANSWER: x = 24 and \angle X = 63^\circ
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A triangular signboard has angles measuring $x^\circ$, $(x + 20)^\circ$, and $(2x - 10)^\circ$. A carpenter claims that $x$ can be $30^\circ$. Test this claim by calculating the actual angles produced when $x = 30^\circ$ and determine if a valid triangle is formed.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating mathematical claims and verifying geometric validity through substitution
STEPS:
- step 1: Substitute $x = 30^\circ$ into each of the three angle expressions.
- step 2: Calculate Angle 1: $30^\circ$.
- step 3: Calculate Angle 2: $30^\circ + 20^\circ = 50^\circ$.
- step 4: Calculate Angle 3: $2(30^\circ) - 10^\circ = 60 - 10 = 50^\circ$.
- step 5: Sum the three calculated angles: $30^\circ + 50^\circ + 50^\circ = 130^\circ$.
- step 6: Compare the sum to $180^\circ$ to check if it forms a valid triangle.
ANSWER: The carpenter's claim is false. When $x = 30^\circ$, the angles are $30^\circ$, $50^\circ$, and $50^\circ$, which add up to $130^\circ$, not $180^\circ$. Therefore, these angles cannot form a valid triangle. To form a valid triangle, the angles must sum exactly to $180^\circ$.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two poles are erected in a field, and wire is stretched to form a triangular plot $\triangle ABC$ where angle $\angle A$ is twice angle $\angle B$ ($\angle A = 2 \angle B$), and angle $\angle C$ is $30^\circ$ greater than angle $\angle B$ ($\angle C = \angle B + 30^\circ$). Find the exact measure of all three angles.
COMPETENCY: Translating word descriptions into algebraic relationships and solving multi-variable angle problems
STEPS:
- step 1: Let angle $\angle B = y$. Express the other two angles in terms of $y$: $\angle A = 2y$ and $\angle C = y + 30^\circ$.
- step 2: Write down the angle sum property equation for the triangle: $\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ$.
- step 3: Substitute the expressions into the equation: $2y + y + (y + 30^\circ) = 180^\circ$.
- step 4: Combine all like terms for $y$: $4y + 30^\circ = 180^\circ$.
- step 5: Isolate the term with $y$ by subtracting $30^\circ$ from both sides: $4y = 150^\circ$.
- step 6: Solve for $y$: $y = 150 / 4 = 37.5^\circ$.
- step 7: Calculate each angle: $\angle B = 37.5^\circ$, $\angle A = 2(37.5^\circ) = 75^\circ$, and $\angle C = 37.5^\circ + 30^\circ = 67.5^\circ$.
ANSWER: Angle $\angle B = 37.5^\circ$, Angle $\angle A = 75^\circ$, and Angle $\angle C = 67.5^\circ$. Checking the sum: $75^\circ + 37.5^\circ + 67.5^\circ = 180^\circ$.
DIFFICULTY: hard
