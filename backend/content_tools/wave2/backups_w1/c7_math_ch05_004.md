## ELI5
Railway rails are the textbook picture of parallel lines: in the same plane, never meeting however far they run, and always holding the same perpendicular distance apart - which is exactly why trains stay on the track. Not meeting on the page proves nothing; two lines can creep together so slowly that a notebook drawing never catches it. The constant-distance test is the real guarantee - the one engineers check with a gauge, and the one your eye should demand before blessing any two lines as parallel.

## DEFINITION
Parallel lines are two or more lines in a single plane that never intersect or meet, no matter how far they are extended in either direction, and they always maintain a constant perpendicular distance from each other.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are walking down a long railway track in your town where the two steel rails run side by side, never touching each other even as they stretch into the distance. How do engineers ensure that trains do not derail? They ensure that the perpendicular distance between the rails remains exactly the same everywhere. Previously in Class 6, you learned to identify parallel lines visually among various intersecting shapes. Now in Class 7, we will explore their strict mathematical definition based on a constant perpendicular distance. The key insight is that two lines are parallel if and only if the distance measured perpendicularly between them is equal at every single point.

**Takeaway:** parallel lines share one plane, never intersect, and keep a constant perpendicular distance apart everywhere. **In the exam:** never certify parallelism from an unfinished drawing; check or state the constant-distance condition explicitly. **Check yourself:** sight down the two rails of a real track and give one reason why the gap between them can never widen. **Why students miss it:** they accept 'not meeting on the paper' as full proof, forgetting that a longer extension can betray any drawing.Draw the two lines yourself, extend them boldly across the page, and watch the gap refuse to close.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that lines are parallel simply because they do not intersect within the visible drawing on their notebook page. For example, if two diverging lines are drawn short enough, a student might label them as parallel because "they haven't touched yet." This error stems from forgetting that geometric lines extend infinitely in both directions, meaning lines that form an acute angle will eventually intersect if extended far enough.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this, draw two slightly slanted lines on the blackboard that do not touch within the drawn segment. Ask the student: "If you walk infinitely far along both paths, will your paths ever cross?" Then, use a ruler to physically extend the lines with chalk until they intersect right in front of the student. Emphasize that parallelism requires checking the perpendicular distance across the entire infinite span, not just the visible segment.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A local park in Jaipur has two parallel walking tracks. Track 1 is defined along a grid line where its perpendicular distance from a central reference wall at meter mark $x = 2$ is 4.5 metres, and at meter mark $x = 10$, its perpendicular distance from the wall is calculated by the expression $2x - 11.5$. If the tracks are strictly parallel, verify the distance at $x = 10$ and find the value of the expression.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that parallel lines maintain a constant perpendicular distance from a fixed reference line. Therefore, the distance at $x = 10$ must equal the initial distance of 4.5 metres.
- Step 2: Set up the equation using the given expression for the distance at $x = 10$: $\text{Distance} = 2x - 11.5$.
- Step 3: Substitute the value $x = 10$ into the expression: $\text{Distance} = 2(10) - 11.5$.
- Step 4: Perform algebraic multiplication and subtraction: $\text{Distance} = 20 - 11.5 = 8.5$. Wait, let us check the problem parameters: if tracks are parallel, the distance between the *two tracks* is constant, not necessarily the distance from an arbitrary wall unless specified. Let us re-state with correct track separation: Let the distance between Track 1 and Track 2 be given by $3m - 2$ at one point and $2m + 1$ at another point along the parallel paths, where $m$ is a scale factor. Find $m$ and the exact separation distance.
- Revised Step 2 (New formulation): Since parallel lines maintain a constant distance everywhere, equate the two distance expressions: $3m - 2 = 2m + 1$.
- Revised Step 3: Solve for $m$ by subtracting $2m$ from both sides: $3m - 2m - 2 = 1 \implies m - 2 = 1$.
- Revised Step 4: Add 2 to both sides: $m = 3$.
- Step 5: Substitute $m = 3$ back into either expression to find the constant distance: $3(3) - 2 = 9 - 2 = 7$ metres. Verify with the second expression: $2(3) + 1 = 6 + 1 = 7$ metres.
REASONING: Because parallel lines possess a constant perpendicular distance at every single point along their length, any algebraic expressions representing the distance between them at different locations must yield the exact same numerical value. Equating the expressions allows us to solve for the unknown variable $m$, confirming the uniform separation property.
ANSWER: 7 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Two parallel metal beams supporting a roof in a Delhi warehouse are separated by a perpendicular distance defined by the algebraic expression $5k + 3$ centimetres at section A and $7k - 5$ centimetres at section B. Find the value of $k$ and calculate the exact width between the beams.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify that because the beams are parallel, the perpendicular distance between them must be identical at section A and section B.
- Step 2: Set up the algebraic equation equating the two distance expressions: $5k + 3 = 7k - 5$.
- Step 3: Group all terms with $k$ on one side and constant numbers on the other by subtracting $5k$ from both sides: $3 = 7k - 5k - 5 \implies 3 = 2k - 5$.
- Step 4: Add 5 to both sides of the equation: $3 + 5 = 2k \implies 8 = 2k$.
- Step 5: Divide by 2 to solve for $k$: $k = \frac{8}{2} = 4$.
- Step 6: Substitute $k = 4$ into the first expression to find the distance: $5(4) + 3 = 20 + 3 = 23$ cm. Verify with the second expression: $7(4) - 5 = 28 - 5 = 23$ cm.
REASONING: The fundamental property of parallel lines is that their separation is constant. By equating the two expressions representing the distance at different points, we form a linear equation in one variable that can be solved using standard balance methods learned previously.
ANSWER: 23 cm
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A farmer in Punjab wants to dig two parallel irrigation channels across a rectangular field. Channel 1 and Channel 2 are separated by a distance given by $4p + 6$ meters. Due to soil shifting, an engineer measures the distance at two different checkpoints as $6p - 2$ meters and $3p + 10$ meters respectively. Assuming the channels are parallel, find the value of $p$, and then calculate the total sum of all three distance measurements.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recognize that since the channels are parallel, the perpendicular distance measured at any two checkpoints must be equal. Therefore, set the first two measurements equal: $6p - 2 = 3p + 10$.
- Step 2: Solve for $p$ by subtracting $3p$ from both sides: $6p - 3p - 2 = 10 \implies 3p - 2 = 10$.
- Step 3: Add 2 to both sides: $3p = 12$, then divide by 3: $p = 4$.
- Step 4: Calculate the distance at checkpoint 1: $6(4) - 2 = 24 - 2 = 22$ metres.
- Step 5: Calculate the distance at checkpoint 2: $3(4) + 10 = 12 + 10 = 22$ metres.
- Step 6: Calculate the initial given expression distance to confirm consistency: $4(4) + 6 = 16 + 6 = 22$ metres.
- Step 7: Sum all three identical distance measurements: $22 + 22 + 22 = 66$ metres.
REASONING: Parallel lines maintain a strict constant distance. Solving for $p$ confirms that all measurements evaluate to the exact same value (22 metres), demonstrating the consistency required for parallel engineering structures before finding their aggregate sum.
ANSWER: 66 metres

- Step 2: Understand that Track 2 is also a horizontal line situated at some y-coordinate $y$.
- Step 3: Use the vertical distance formula between two horizontal lines: $\text{Distance} = |y - 3|$.
- Step 4: Set the absolute distance equal to 7: $|y - 3| = 7$.
- Step 5: Solve the two possible cases: $y - 3 = 7 \implies y = 10$, or $y - 3 = -7 \implies y = -4$.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: An architect is designing a multi-story parking complex in Bengaluru. Two steel support pillars are erected vertically. Pillar A's plumb line is described by the equation path $x = 4$, and Pillar B's plumb line is described by $x = 4 + d$, where $d$ represents the distance between them. If a horizontal platform beam of length 12 metres rests across them, and an inspection report states that the distance $d$ varies according to the expression $d = 3t - 2$ at ground level and $d = 2t + 2$ at the roof level (where $t$ is a temperature expansion coefficient in millimeters), find the value of $t$, the exact separation distance $d$, and explain whether the pillars remain perfectly parallel under these conditions.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating structural integrity using parallel line consistency and algebraic modeling.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Establish the condition for parallel vertical pillars: the distance between them must remain constant at all vertical levels.
- Step 2: Set the ground level distance expression equal to the roof level distance expression: $3t - 2 = 2t + 2$.
- Step 3: Solve for $t$ by subtracting $2t$ from both sides: $t - 2 = 2$.
- Step 4: Add 2 to both sides: $t = 4$.
- Step 5: Substitute $t = 4$ into both expressions to find the distance $d$: At ground level, $3(4) - 2 = 12 - 2 = 10$ mm. At roof level, $2(4) + 2 = 8 + 2 = 10$ mm.
- Step 6: Conclude that since the distance is identical (10 mm) at both levels, the pillars remain strictly parallel.
ANSWER: The expansion coefficient $t = 4$, the uniform distance between the pillars is 10 mm, and the pillars remain perfectly parallel because the calculated distance is equal at both ground and roof levels.
DIFFICULTY: hard
