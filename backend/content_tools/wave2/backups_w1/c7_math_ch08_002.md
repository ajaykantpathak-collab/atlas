## ELI5
Three biscuit packets at three-quarter kilo each weigh more than one packet - and multiplication by a whole number is just repeated addition of the fraction, shortcut into one step: multiply the whole number with the numerator, leave the denominator alone. The classic error multiplies the bottom too, which merely renames the same amount. Whole numbers secretly wear denominator one, so the rule is fraction multiplication in disguise, and the answer simplifies like any other fraction.

## DEFINITION
Multiplying a fraction by a whole number means finding a repeated sum or a fractional part of that whole number, calculated by multiplying the whole number by the numerator and keeping the denominator the same. For any whole number $w$ and fraction $\frac{a}{b}$, the product is given by $\frac{w \times a}{b}$.

apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
Can successfully multiply any proper, improper, or mixed fraction by a positive whole number, simplify the resulting fraction to its lowest terms, and interpret the product in real-world contexts involving units such as kilograms, litres, or rupees.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers the multiplication of proper and improper fractions by positive whole numbers and simplification of the resulting fractions. Does NOT cover the multiplication of fractions by fractions or negative integers.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are buying 3 packets of biscuits, and each packet weighs $\frac{3}{4}$ kg. To find the total weight, you need to add $\frac{3}{4}$ three times: $\frac{3}{4} + \frac{3}{4} + \frac{3}{4}$. Just like repeated addition in whole numbers becomes multiplication (e.g., $3 \times 4$), repeated addition of fractions becomes multiplying a fraction by a whole number. To begin, write down the whole number and the fraction as a multiplication expression: $3 \times \frac{3}{4}$. Connect this to what you learned in Class 6 about addition of like fractions. The key insight is that the whole number multiplies only the numerator while the denominator remains unchanged.

**Takeaway:** whole number times fraction: multiply the numerator, keep the denominator, then simplify. **In the exam:** treat the whole number as a fraction over one when in doubt; simplify to lowest terms with units. **Check yourself:** add the three packet weights by hand and confirm the shortcut gives the same total. **Why students miss it:** they scale numerator and denominator together, renaming the fraction instead of growing it. Check the shortcut against the long addition once, and trust the multiplication ever after. One whole number on the numerator, one unchanged denominator below, and the kilograms confirm it.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is the rule for multiplying a whole number by a fraction $\frac{a}{b}$?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct rule is to multiply the whole number by the numerator and keep the denominator the same: $w \times \frac{a}{b} = \frac{w \times a}{b}$. A wrong answer like multiplying both numerator and denominator by the whole number reveals a confusion with creating equivalent fractions, where both terms are multiplied or divided by the same number.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Rahul says that $5 \times \frac{2}{3}$ is equal to $\frac{10}{15}$ because he multiplied both the top and bottom numbers by 5. Is Rahul correct? Explain why or why not.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Rahul is incorrect because multiplying both the numerator and denominator by the same number creates an equivalent fraction (which has a value equal to 1 times the original fraction, not 5 times). To multiply a fraction by a whole number, only the numerator is multiplied by the whole number, giving $\frac{5 \times 2}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$. A wrong answer shows a failure to distinguish between scaling a fraction's value and scaling its form.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Calculate the product of 6 and $\frac{4}{9}$, and express your answer in its simplest form.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $\frac{8}{3}$ (or $2\frac{2}{3}$). Working steps: Step 1: Set up the expression $6 \times \frac{4}{9}$. Step 2: Multiply the whole number by the numerator: $\frac{6 \times 4}{9} = \frac{24}{9}$. Step 3: Simplify by dividing both numerator and denominator by their HCF, which is 3: $\frac{24 \div 3}{9 \div 3} = \frac{8}{3}$. A wrong answer where the student gets $\frac{24}{9}$ but forgets to simplify indicates a gap in reducing fractions to lowest terms using HCF.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the product of $4 \times \frac{3}{5}$ and the sum of $\frac{3}{5} + \frac{3}{5} + \frac{3}{5} + \frac{3}{5}$. What mathematical property does this relationship demonstrate?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Both expressions equal $\frac{12}{5}$. This demonstrates that multiplication is a shorthand notation for repeated addition of the same fraction. A wrong answer shows a lack of conceptual connection between repeated addition and scalar multiplication of fractions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Ananya evaluated $8 \times \frac{5}{8}$ and wrote her answer as $\frac{40}{64}$. Look at her work. What error did she make, and what is the correct answer?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Ananya multiplied both the numerator and denominator by the whole number 8. The correct answer is 5. Working steps: $8 \times \frac{5}{8} = \frac{8 \times 5}{8} = \frac{40}{8} = 5$ (or by cancelling the 8 in the numerator and denominator directly). A wrong answer indicates confusion between fraction multiplication and scaling both terms of a fraction.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-life word problem involving litres of milk where the solution requires multiplying a proper fraction by a whole number, and provide the solved answer.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good problem: "A family consumes $\frac{3}{4}$ litres of milk every day. How many litres of milk do they consume in 5 days?" Solution: $5 \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{15}{4} = 3\frac{3}{4}$ litres. A poor answer uses incorrect units or sets up a division instead of multiplication, showing confusion about scaling a fractional daily amount over multiple days.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently multiply both the numerator AND the denominator of a fraction by the whole number (e.g., calculating $4 \times \frac{2}{3}$ as $\frac{8}{12}$ instead of $\frac{8}{3}$). They confuse this operation with finding equivalent fractions or assume that scaling applies to both parts equally.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student that a whole number can be written as a fraction with a denominator of 1 (e.g., $4 = \frac{4}{1}$). Show them that multiplying fractions involves multiplying numerators together and denominators together: $\frac{4}{1} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{4 \times 2}{1 \times 3} = \frac{8}{3}$. Emphasize that the denominator represents the size of the parts and does not change when counting how many parts you have.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A running track is $\frac{7}{8}$ km long. Amit runs around the track 4 times. What is the total distance covered by Amit in kilometres?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given values: distance of one lap = $\frac{7}{8}$ km, number of laps = 4.
- Step 2: Set up the mathematical expression for total distance: $4 \times \frac{7}{8}$.
- Step 3: Multiply the whole number by the numerator while keeping the denominator unchanged: $\frac{4 \times 7}{8} = \frac{28}{8}$.
- Step 4: Simplify the resulting fraction by finding the HCF of 28 and 8, which is 4, and dividing both numbers: $\frac{28 \div 4}{8 \div 4} = \frac{7}{2}$.
- Step 5: Convert the improper fraction to a mixed number: $\frac{7}{2} = 3\frac{1}{2}$ km.
REASONING: Multiplying the fractional length of one lap by the total number of laps gives the cumulative distance. Simplifying ensures the answer is expressed in lowest terms, and converting to a mixed number makes the physical measurement easy to interpret.
ANSWER: 3\frac{1}{2} km
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Priya has a ribbon of length 12 metres. She uses $\frac{3}{4}$ of it for a craft project. What length of ribbon does she use?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given values: total length = 12 metres, fraction used = $\frac{3}{4}$.
- Step 2: Formulate the multiplication expression representing the fraction of a whole: $12 \times \frac{3}{4}$.
- Step 3: Multiply the whole number by the numerator: $\frac{12 \times 3}{4} = \frac{36}{4}$.
- Step 4: Perform division to simplify the fraction: $\frac{36}{4} = 9$. Alternatively, cancel common factors before multiplying: $\frac{12^3 \times 3}{4^1} = 3 \times 3 = 9$.
- Step 5: Attach the correct unit of measurement to the final result: 9 metres.
REASONING: Finding a fractional part of a whole quantity is mathematically equivalent to multiplying the whole number by that fraction. Using cancellation before multiplication simplifies large numbers efficiently.
ANSWER: 9 metres
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A milk cooperative collects milk from 5 dairy farms. Each farm supplies $14\frac{2}{3}$ litres of milk daily. What is the total quantity of milk collected by the cooperative in one day?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given values: number of farms = 5, milk per farm = $14\frac{2}{3}$ litres.
- Step 2: Convert the mixed fraction into an improper fraction: $14\frac{2}{3} = \frac{(14 \times 3) + 2}{3} = \frac{42 + 2}{3} = \frac{44}{3}$.
- Step 3: Set up the multiplication expression for the total milk: $5 \times \frac{44}{3}$.
- Step 4: Multiply the whole number by the numerator: $\frac{5 \times 44}{3} = \frac{220}{3}$.
- Step 5: Convert the improper fraction back into a mixed number by dividing 220 by 3: $220 \div 3 = 73$ with a remainder of 1, giving $73\frac{1}{3}$ litres.
REASONING: Mixed numbers cannot be multiplied directly by whole numbers without first converting them to improper fractions. Once converted, the standard multiplication rule applies, and the result is converted back for practical reporting.
ANSWER: 73\frac{1}{3} litres

Sneha writes: $8 \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{8 \times 3}{4} = \frac{24}{4} = 6$.
Who is correct? Identify the mathematical error made by the incorrect student and explain why their method fails.

- Step 2: Analyze Sneha's method to verify her steps and final computation.
- Step 3: Identify that Rahul incorrectly multiplied both the numerator and denominator by the whole number.
- Step 4: Conclude that Sneha followed the correct rule and arrived at the correct answer.
ANSWER: Sneha is correct. Rahul incorrectly multiplied both the numerator and the denominator by 8, treating the denominator as if it needed to be scaled by the whole number as well. Sneha correctly multiplied only the numerator by 8 and then simplified $\frac{24}{4}$ to get 6.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A water tank contains 180 litres of water. A gardener uses $\frac{2}{5}$ of the total water on Monday and $\frac{1}{3}$ of the remaining water on Tuesday. Calculate the exact volume of water left in the tank after Tuesday.
COMPETENCY: Translating multi-step real-world situations into fraction operations
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate the water used on Monday by multiplying the total volume by $\frac{2}{5}$: $180 \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{360}{5} = 72$ litres used.
- Step 2: Determine the remaining water after Monday: $180 - 72 = 108$ litres left.
- Step 3: Calculate the water used on Tuesday by multiplying the remaining water by $\frac{1}{3}$: $108 \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{108}{3} = 36$ litres used.
- Step 4: Calculate the final remaining water in the tank: $108 - 36 = 72$ litres.
ANSWER: 72 litres of water are left in the tank.
DIFFICULTY: hard
