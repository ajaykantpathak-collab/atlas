## ELI5
A Delhi parking tower makes integers visible: ground floor zero, three floors up is plus 3, two floors into the basement is minus 2. Integers are the whole numbers plus their negatives - every floor above and below zero on one endless lift line. Zero belongs to neither side; it is the shared reference. The line also ranks them: further left reads smaller - which is why minus fifteen is a deeper cold than minus five, however longer the digit string looks.

## DEFINITION
Integers are a set of numbers that include all whole numbers and their negative counterparts, represented as $\{..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...\}$, where positive integers lie to the right of zero, negative integers lie to the left of zero, and zero is neither positive nor negative.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are standing at ground level in a multi-story parking building in Delhi. Ground level is marked as 0. If you go up 3 floors to park your car, we call that $+3$ (positive 3). If you take the elevator down 2 floors to the basement to check the electricity meter, we call that $-2$ (negative 2). To understand integers fully, we expand our whole numbers by introducing a mirror image of counting numbers to the left of zero. We write positive numbers with a plus sign (or no sign) and negative numbers with a minus sign. Zero acts as the neutral reference point or origin, separating positive and negative quantities.

**Takeaway:** integers extend whole numbers with negatives on one line; zero sits neutral at the centre. **In the exam:** translate lifts, temperatures and bank balances into signed integers, then order them left to right. **Check yourself:** map the parking floors onto a quick number line and mark plus 3 and minus 2. **Why students miss it:** they read the bigger digit as the bigger value, missing that leftward means smaller. Sketch the lift line once, and every storey above and below ground finds its signed address. Say each floor aloud with its sign, and the lift line becomes a habit.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
Which of the following numbers is considered neither a positive integer nor a negative integer?
A) $+1$
B) $0$
C) $-1$
D) Both A and C
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is B) $0$. Zero is the neutral reference integer that separates positive and negative numbers on the number line; it carries no sign. A student who chooses A or C has forgotten that positive and negative integers are strictly greater than or less than zero, respectively. A student who chooses D shows a complete misunderstanding of the definitions of signed numbers.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
An elevator starts at the ground floor ($0$). It goes up $5$ floors, then goes down $8$ floors, and finally goes up $3$ floors. What is the final floor position represented as an integer?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $0$ (ground floor). Working steps: 
1) Start at ground level: $0$. 
2) Going up $5$ floors translates to $0 + 5 = +5$. 
3) Going down $8$ floors translates to $+5 - 8 = -3$. 
4) Going up $3$ floors translates to $-3 + 3 = 0$. 
A student who answers $+3$ or $-3$ likely made an error in tracking directional movements, treating up and down additions incorrectly or miscalculating the arithmetic sequence step-by-step.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student claims that since $-15$ is a larger number than $-5$ in terms of digits ($15 > 5$), $-15$ must represent a higher temperature than $-5^\circ\text{C}$. Evaluate this claim and correct the error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The claim is incorrect. While the whole number $15$ is greater than $5$, when applied to negative integers, $-15$ represents a deficit or depth greater than $-5$, placing $-15$ further to the left on the number line than $-5$. Therefore, $-15^\circ\text{C}$ is colder (lower in value) than $-5^\circ\text{C}$. A student who agrees with the claim fails to understand that for negative numbers, a larger absolute value means a smaller (lower) actual value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a real-world scenario involving a bank account balance, deposits, and withdrawals that results in a final integer value of $-500$ rupees. Explain each step using positive and negative integers.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A valid response should feature a starting balance (e.g., $0$ or a positive amount), a series of withdrawals represented as negative integers, and deposits represented as positive integers, resulting in a net balance of $-500$. For example: Rahul starts with a balance of ₹200 ($+200$). He withdraws ₹800 ($-800$). His current balance is $+200 - 800 = -600$ rupees. Then he deposits ₹100 ($+100$), making his final balance $-600 + 100 = -500$ rupees. A poor response uses operations without assigning correct positive and negative signs to deposits and withdrawals.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the absolute magnitude of digits with the value of negative numbers, assuming that a larger numeral with a negative sign (e.g., $-98$) is greater than a smaller numeral with a negative sign (e.g., $-12$), or they treat zero as a positive number. For instance, when asked to order $\{-12, -5, -98\}$, students frequently write $-98 > -12$ because $98 > 12$.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to visualize a horizontal number line where numbers increase as you move right and decrease as you move left. Have them draw a vertical thermometer or number line: place $-12$ and $-98$ on it. Ask: "If you owe a friend ₹98 versus owing ₹12, which financial situation is worse (smaller value)?" Show them that $-98$ is further left, so it is smaller than $-12$.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: During a winter night in Ladakh, the temperature dropped from $+4^\circ\text{C}$ at sunset to $-6^\circ\text{C}$ by midnight. Represent both temperatures as integers and determine which one represents a colder state.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given temperature at sunset as $+4^\circ\text{C}$ (above zero, positive) and midnight as $-6^\circ\text{C}$ (below zero, negative).
- Step 2: Set up a number line reference where $0$ is the freezing point, positive numbers are to the right, and negative numbers are to the left.
- Step 3: Locate $+4$ and $-6$ on the number line to compare their positions relative to zero.
- Step 4: Analyze position: $-6$ lies to the left of $+4$, meaning $-6$ is smaller than $+4$. A lower temperature on a thermometer means a colder state.
- Step 5: Verify with units: $-6^\circ\text{C}$ is colder than $+4^\circ\text{C}$.
REASONING: Positive integers denote values greater than zero, while negative integers denote values less than zero. Since $-6$ is further left on the number line than $+4$, it represents a lower value and thus a colder temperature.
ANSWER: $+4^\circ\text{C}$ and $-6^\circ\text{C}$; $-6^\circ\text{C}$ is colder.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A scuba diver is exploring coral reefs. She dives to a depth of $45$ metres below sea level, then ascends $15$ metres, and finally dives down another $20$ metres. Express her final position relative to sea level as an integer.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Define sea level as the zero reference point ($0$ metres). Let depths below sea level be represented by negative integers and ascents (upward movements) by positive integers.
- Step 2: Translate the first movement (diving $45$ metres below sea level) into an integer: $-45$.
- Step 3: Translate the second movement (ascending $15$ metres) into an integer addition: $-45 + 15$. Compute: $-45 + 15 = -30$.
- Step 4: Translate the third movement (diving down another $20$ metres) into an integer operation: $-30 - 20$. Compute: $-30 - 20 = -50$.
- Step 5: Interpret the final integer result with units: $-50$ metres means 50 metres below sea level.
REASONING: Real-world contexts like sea level use zero as a baseline. Movements downward decrease the value (negative integers), while movements upward increase the value (positive integers). Step-by-step arithmetic tracks the net position accurately.
ANSWER: $-50$ metres (or 50 metres below sea level)
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: An entrepreneur records financial transactions over three months: Month 1 shows a profit of ₹5000, Month 2 shows a loss of ₹2000, and Month 3 shows neither a profit nor a loss. Represent each month's financial standing as an integer, order them from lowest to highest, and identify what zero signifies in this context.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Assign integer representations: Profit is positive ($+\text{₹5000} = +5000$), Loss is negative ($-\text{₹2000} = -2000$), and break-even (neither profit nor loss) is represented by $0$.
- Step 2: List the integers representing the three months: $+5000$, $-2000$, and $0$.
- Step 3: Order the integers from lowest to highest by examining their positions on the number line (negative numbers are lowest, followed by zero, then positive numbers).
- Step 4: Compare values: $-2000$ is to the left of $0$, and $0$ is to the left of $+5000$. Thus, the ascending order is $-2000, 0, 5000$.
- Step 5: Interpret zero: In this context, zero signifies a break-even point where total revenue equals total expenses, with no net gain or loss.
REASONING: Financial gains and losses map naturally onto positive and negative integers. Ordering requires comparing debts/losses (negative) against break-even points (zero) and profits (positive).
ANSWER: Ordered list: $-2000, 0, 5000$. Zero signifies the break-even financial state.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student states, "Zero is a positive integer because it is greater than all negative numbers." Evaluate this statement mathematically. In your explanation, define the exact classification of zero and why it cannot be classified as positive or negative.
COMPETENCY: Mathematical reasoning and classification justification
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the student's premise that zero is greater than all negative numbers (which is mathematically true: $0 > -5$).
- Step 2: Examine the logical leap: Does being greater than negative numbers make a number positive?
- Step 3: Recall the definition of positive integers (numbers strictly greater than zero: $1, 2, 3...$) and negative integers (numbers strictly less than zero: $-1, -2, -3...$).
- Step 4: Conclude that zero is the neutral boundary separating the two sets and possesses no sign of its own.
ANSWER: The statement is partially flawed. While zero is indeed greater than all negative numbers, it is not a positive integer. Zero is a neutral integer; it has no sign (+ or -) and acts as the origin point on the number line.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Three cities in Northern India record the following midnight temperatures: City A: $-2^\circ\text{C}$, City B: $1^\circ\text{C}$, City C: $-5^\circ\text{C}$. A tourist claims that City A is the coldest because $2$ is a large number. Correct the tourist's error by arranging the temperatures in ascending order (from coldest to warmest) using number line logic, and explain why magnitude alone is misleading for negative numbers.
COMPETENCY: Error identification, ordering, and real-world interpretation
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the temperatures given: City A is $-2$, City B is $+1$, City C is $-5$.
- Step 2: Address the tourist's misconception about the digit magnitude ($2$ vs $5$). Explain that for negative numbers, a larger numerical digit represents a greater debt, deficit, or drop below zero.
- Step 3: Plot $-5$, $-2$, and $+1$ on a mental number line to find their relative positions from left to right.
- Step 4: Order them from left to right (coldest/lowest to warmest/highest): $-5^\circ\text{C}$, $-2^\circ\text{C}$, $+1^\circ\text{C}$.
- Step 5: Summarize why City C is actually the coldest.
ANSWER: The tourist is incorrect. City C ($-5^\circ\text{C}$) is the coldest, followed by City A ($-2^\circ\text{C}$), and City B ($+1^\circ\text{C}$) is the warmest. Ascending order: $-5, -2, 1$. Magnitude is misleading with negative numbers because $-5$ is further to the left on the number line than $-2$, making it smaller (colder).
DIFFICULTY: hard
