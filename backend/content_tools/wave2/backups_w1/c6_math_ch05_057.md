## ELI5
Every equation is a balanced scale, and solving is careful unpacking: strip brackets first, gather like terms, then peel constants and coefficients away with inverse moves on both sides. Whatever presses on one pan must press equally on the other, or the beam tips and the answer lies. Finish with the letter alone, then check it back into the original scales.

## DEFINITION
A linear equation is a mathematical statement asserting that two expressions are equal, where the variable has an exponent of 1. Solving it means finding the specific value for the variable that makes the equality true.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to isolate the variable on one side of the equation and determine its value through logical algebraic steps (simplifying, balancing, and dividing).
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
Covers solving single-variable linear equations using standard isolation methods. Excludes systems of equations, inequalities, and word problems.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you have a balancing scale where one side holds a bag of marbles (your unknown $x$) and some loose marbles, and the other side holds only loose marbles. To find out how many marbles are in the bag, you remove the same number of loose marbles from both sides until the bag is alone, keeping the scale balanced. This is exactly how we solve equations: whatever operation you perform on one side (like subtracting a constant), you must perform on the other to keep the "equation" true. We simplify, isolate the variable, and then calculate. The key insight is that an equation is a balance—never change one side without changing the other.

**Takeaway:** solving means simplifying, then peeling constants and coefficients with inverse operations on both sides until the variable stands alone. **In the exam:** in solving questions, show each balancing move on both sides and finish by substituting the value back. **Check yourself:** unload the walkthrough's scale - remove equal loose weights from both pans before dividing the bags away. **Why students miss it:** students move terms without balancing - the walkthrough's beam is a no to that; an unpaired move tips the scale and breaks the truth. Rehearse the exam move once more - simplify, balance, verify - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often "move" a term to the other side without changing its sign (e.g., changing $3x - 5 = 10$ to $3x = 10 - 5$ instead of $3x = 10 + 5$). This happens because they view the equals sign as a "go" sign rather than a scale that requires inverse operations (adding/subtracting) to maintain balance.
## ON_FAILURE
Ask the student to draw a physical scale with two pans. Tell them: "If you take away 5 loose marbles from the left pan, the scale will tilt unless you also take away 5 from the right." Guide them to perform the inverse operation to "undo" the addition or subtraction, ensuring they write the same operation on both sides of the equation every time.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Solve $3x + 5 = 20$.
STEPS:
- 1: Identify that we need $x$ alone.
- 2: Use inverse of addition (+5) by subtracting 5 from both sides.
- 3: $3x + 5 - 5 = 20 - 5 \implies 3x = 15$.
- 4: Use inverse of multiplication ($\times 3$) by dividing by 3.
- 5: $3x/3 = 15/3 \implies x = 5$.
REASONING: By subtracting 5, we remove the constant. By dividing by 3, we isolate $x$ because $3/3$ equals 1.
ANSWER: $x = 5$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Solve $2(x + 4) = 14$.
STEPS:
- 1: Recognize the parentheses need to be cleared.
- 2: Distribute the 2: $2x + 8 = 14$.
- 3: Subtract 8 from both sides: $2x = 6$.
- 4: Divide by 2: $x = 3$.
REASONING: Distribution simplifies the expression to standard form. The inverse operations then isolate $x$.
ANSWER: $x = 3$
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A notebook costs $x$ rupees. You buy 3 notebooks and pay a delivery fee of 20 rupees. The total is 110 rupees. Solve for $x$.
STEPS:
- 1: Write the equation: $3x + 20 = 110$.
- 2: Subtract 20 from both sides: $3x = 90$.
- 3: Divide by 3: $x = 30$.
- 4: Check: $3(30) + 20 = 90 + 20 = 110$.
REASONING: We translate the word problem into an equation and then apply the same balance steps used in pure math.
ANSWER: 30 rupees
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Why is the solution to $2x = 10$ the same as $2x + 5 = 15$? Explain the relationship between the two equations.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- 1: Solve $2x = 10 \implies x = 5$.
- 2: Solve $2x + 5 = 15 \implies 2x = 10 \implies x = 5$.
- 3: Observe that adding 5 to both sides creates an equivalent equation.
ANSWER: They share the same solution because the second equation is just the first equation with 5 added to both sides, which maintains the balance.
DIFFICULTY: medium
