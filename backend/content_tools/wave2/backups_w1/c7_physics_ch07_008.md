## ELI5
Your day is an acid-base tour: lemon in the morning tea (acid), soap at the bath (base), curd at lunch (acid), baking soda in the evening pakoras (base). Ant stings sting because they inject acid - calamine or baking soda neutralises it. Antacids calm an angry stomach the same way. Acids and bases determine the chemical nature of nearly everything you eat, clean with, and treat yourself with.

## DEFINITION
Acids and bases play a crucial role in our everyday lives, determining the chemical nature of substances we consume, use for cleaning, and encounter in nature. They are measured qualitatively through their chemical behavior or quantitatively using the pH scale, which spans from 0 to 14 in SI-dimensionless units.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that all acids found in everyday life are dangerous and corrosive, similar to laboratory acids like concentrated sulfuric acid. For example, they believe that because hydrochloric acid burns skin, citric acid in lemons or acetic acid in vinegar must also be equally harmful to touch or consume directly.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind students to differentiate between dilute edible acids found in food (like apples, curds, and lemons) and concentrated mineral acids used in laboratories. Have them check nutrition labels of common food items to see weak acids listed safely, and emphasize that concentration and chemical structure determine whether a substance is safe to ingest.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A gardener has soil with an excessively low pH due to acid rain in an industrial area of Delhi. To neutralize 50 kg of acidic soil, the garden store recommends adding a specific quantity of a powdered base. If 2 kg of calcium hydroxide neutralizes a standard acidic patch, how many 2 kg bags are needed for 50 kg of soil proportionally?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify the given quantities: total soil mass = 50 kg, mass of base per standard patch = 2 kg.
- step 2: Set up a proportional relationship based on neutralisation stoichiometry: Bags needed = Total Soil / Soil per bag unit.
- step 3: Substitute values with units: Bags = 50 kg / 2 kg per bag.
- step 4: Compute the numerical value and track unit cancellation: 50 / 2 = 25, and kg cancels out leaving dimensionless count.
- step 5: Verify the answer is physically reasonable: 25 bags is a logical amount for a large scale garden soil treatment.
REASONING: Neutralisation capacity scales linearly with the amount of reactant added, allowing us to use simple proportions to determine how much base is required to correct soil pH.
ANSWER: 25 bags
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: During a science project in Mumbai, a student mixes 100 mL of dilute vinegar (acetic acid) with 100 mL of a mild baking soda solution (base). The initial temperature of the vinegar is 25.0 °C and the resulting mixture temperature is 26.5 °C. Calculate the temperature change (ΔT) of the solution.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given values: Initial temperature ($T_1$) = 25.0 °C, Final temperature ($T_2$) = 26.5 °C.
- step 2: Identify the formula for temperature change: $\Delta T = T_2 - T_1$.
- step 3: Substitute values with units into the formula: $\Delta T = 26.5 \text{ °C} - 25.0 \text{ °C}$.
- step 4: Perform the subtraction: $\Delta T = 1.5 \text{ °C}$ with correct unit tracking.
- step 5: Verify if the result is physically reasonable: A minor temperature rise during a neutralisation reaction is expected as heat is released.
REASONING: Neutralisation reactions are exothermic, releasing thermal energy that can be measured as a small increase in the temperature of the liquid mixture.
ANSWER: 1.5 °C
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A factory produces a waste liquid containing sulfuric acid at a concentration requiring 3 moles of sodium hydroxide per liter for complete neutralisation. If a holding tank contains 500 liters of this waste liquid, calculate the total moles of sodium hydroxide needed to completely neutralize the tank contents.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given variables: Concentration of base needed = 3 moles/liter, Volume of waste liquid = 500 liters.
- step 2: Identify the formula relating moles, concentration, and volume: Moles = Concentration × Volume.
- step 3: Substitute the given values with units: Moles = (3 moles / 1 liter) × 500 liters.
- step 4: Compute the multiplication and cancel out the liter unit: 3 × 500 = 1500 moles.
- step 5: Verify physical reasonableness: 1500 moles is an appropriate scaling for a large 500-liter industrial tank.
REASONING: The total quantity of reactant required for neutralisation depends directly on both the concentration required per unit volume and the total volume of the substance being treated.
ANSWER: 1500 moles

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A farmer notices that crops are failing because the local irrigation water is too basic. The farmer considers adding more chemical fertilizer that leaves an alkaline residue. Evaluate whether this is a good practice and suggest a scientifically sound alternative.
COMPETENCY: Evaluating practical problem-solving strategies in agriculture using acid-base knowledge
STEPS:
- step 1: Analyze the current problem: the soil/water system is already too basic (high pH).
- step 2: Evaluate the proposed action: adding an alkaline fertilizer will increase the basicity further, harming crops.
- step 3: Identify the corrective chemical principle: a basic environment must be neutralized by adding an acidic substance or organic matter that increases acidity.
- step 4: Formulate a sound alternative: add organic compost or a mild acidic amendment to lower the pH toward neutrality.
ANSWER: Adding alkaline fertilizer is a poor practice because it will increase the basicity further and damage crops. Instead, the farmer should add organic matter or acidic amendments to neutralize the excess alkalinity and restore a healthy pH balance.
