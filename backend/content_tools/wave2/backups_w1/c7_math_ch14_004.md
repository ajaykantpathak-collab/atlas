## ELI5
A welder in Jaipur must join two beams at exactly 60 degrees, and the compass delivers where guessing fails: an arc from the ray's start, the same radius stepped onto that arc, and the crossing fixes a perfect 60 - the equilateral triangle's signature. Bisect that sixty for a thirty, stack two for one-twenty, build perpendiculars for 90 and 45; any odd measure like thirty-five still asks for the protractor's honest reading. Special angles come from logic; arbitrary ones come from measurement.

## DEFINITION
Constructing angles of given measures involves using a ruler, compass, and protractor (or standard compass arcs for special angles like 60°, 90°, 45°) to draw an angle that has an exact specified degree measurement from a given ray.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are a builder at a construction site in Jaipur, and you need to weld two metal beams together at an exact angle of 60 degrees to support a decorative arch. How do you draw this precise angle on your blueprint using only a straight ruler and a pencil compass? You start by drawing a straight baseline ray and using your compass to swing an arc of fixed radius. By understanding how equilateral triangles have 60-degree interior angles, you can intersect arcs to find your exact turning point without guessing. Let's explore how to construct both standard geometric angles using a compass and any general angle using a protractor.

**Takeaway:** special angles are built, not measured: 60 from a stepped arc, 30, 45 and 90 by bisection and perpendiculars, and everything else by protractor. **In the exam:** use compass logic for 60, 90, 45 and 30; reserve the protractor for odd measures like 35, and say which you used. **Check yourself:** weld the Jaipur beams on paper: draw the ray, step the arc once, and check the equilateral triangle falls out. **Why students miss it:** students bisect carelessly and inherit a 32 where a 30 was promised - every built angle is only as exact as its arcs. Copy the radius honestly, mark the crossings crisply, and the angle arrives certified by geometry rather than by eyeball. The construction, not the plastic scale, is the authority.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often change the compass radius halfway through constructing a 60-degree angle or a perpendicular line. For example, when drawing an arc of radius 5 cm from the vertex, they might adjust the compass width to 4 cm when making the intersecting arc, which completely distorts the equilateral triangle property and results in an incorrect angle measure.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Construct an angle of 60° at point $A$ on a ray $AB$, and then construct its angle bisector to obtain an angle of 30°. Show all steps.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw a ray $AB$ of any convenient length using a ruler.
- Step 2: With centre $A$ and any suitable radius, draw an arc that cuts ray $AB$ at point $C$.
- Step 3: Keeping the compass radius unchanged, place the needle at point $C$ and draw an arc that intersects the first arc at point $D$.
- Step 4: Join $AD$ and extend it to form ray $AE$. The angle $\angle BAE$ is exactly 60°.
- Step 5: To get 30°, bisect $\angle BAE$ by drawing arcs from $C$ and the point where arc $AD$ intersects the first arc, then drawing the bisector ray $AF$. Angle $\angle BAF$ is 30°.
REASONING: Constructing an arc with a fixed radius from the vertex and then cutting it with the same radius forms an equilateral triangle $\triangle ACD$, where every interior angle is 60°. Bisecting this 60° angle divides it into two equal angles of 30° each.
ANSWER: Angle $\angle BAE = 60^\circ$ and $\angle BAF = 30^\circ$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A carpenter in Delhi needs to cut a wooden joint at a right angle (90°), and then construct a 45° mitre cut by bisecting it. Describe the construction steps using a ruler and compass.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw a horizontal baseline ray $XY$.
- Step 2: With centre $Y$ and any convenient radius, draw a semi-circular arc cutting $XY$ at point $P$.
- Step 3: With centre $P$ and the same radius, cut the semi-circle at point $Q$ (60°), and from $Q$ cut the semi-circle again at point $R$ (120°).
- Step 4: With centres $Q$ and $R$, draw intersecting arcs above the semi-circle meeting at point $S$. Join $YS$ to form a perpendicular line, creating a 90° angle ($\angle SYP$).
- Step 5: Bisect the 90° angle ($\angle SYP$) by drawing arcs from $P$ and the point where $YS$ intersects the semi-circle, intersecting at $T$. Join $YT$ to create the 45° angle.
REASONING: A 90° angle is constructed by finding the perpendicular bisector/intersection of 60° and 120° arcs on a semi-circle. Bisecting that 90° angle yields 45°, which is essential for accurate woodwork mitre joints.
ANSWER: Constructed 90° angle and its 45° bisector.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Construct an angle of 120° at point $O$ on a ray $OP$. Then construct a 150° angle by bisecting the 60° sector between 120° and 180°.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Draw ray $OP$ and extend it backwards slightly to form a full reference line if needed, or keep ray $OP$ as the baseline pointing right.
- Step 2: With centre $O$ and any fixed radius, draw a large semi-circular arc cutting $OP$ at $M$.
- Step 3: With centre $M$ and the same radius, cut the arc at $A$ (60°), from $A$ cut at $B$ (120°), and from $B$ cut at $C$ (180°).
- Step 4: Join $OB$ to form $\angle POB = 120^\circ$.
- Step 5: To get 150°, we need an angle halfway between 120° ($B$) and 180° ($C$). Use a compass to find the bisector of the arc between point $B$ and point $C$.
- Step 6: With centres $B$ and $C$, draw intersecting arcs above the semi-circle meeting at point $D$, and join $OD$. Angle $\angle POD = 150^\circ$.
REASONING: Standard multiples of 60° are easily marked on a semi-circular arc (60°, 120°, 180°). Adding a 30° increment (half of the 60° span between 120° and 180°) via angle bisection gives $120^\circ + 30^\circ = 150^\circ$.
ANSWER: Angle $\angle POB = 120^\circ$ and $\angle POD = 150^\circ$.

STEPS:

- Step 4: Conclude which method is more efficient while confirming both are geometrically correct.
ANSWER: Both methods are geometrically valid and result in an exact 75° angle. Student B's method ($60^\circ + 15^\circ$) is more efficient because 60° is a direct single-arc construction, whereas Student A's method requires constructing a 90° angle first.
