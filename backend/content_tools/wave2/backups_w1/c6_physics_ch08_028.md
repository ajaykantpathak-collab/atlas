## ELI5
Speed is the exchange rate between distance and time: how much road for how much clock. The cyclist who covers more road in the same clock is faster, and doubling the road at the same clock doubles the rate. But numbers wear uniforms - a big number in small units can lose to a small number in big ones, so always check the label before crowning the winner.

## DEFINITION
Speed is the distance an object travels in a specific amount of time. It tells us how fast or slow an object is moving by comparing the total path covered to the time taken.
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL

## MASTERY_CRITERIA

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the definition and calculation of speed for linear motion. It does not cover velocity (direction), acceleration, or relativistic speeds.
## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you and your friend both start walking from the school gate to the park. Even if you start at the same time, one of you might reach the park sooner because you are walking faster. To know who is "faster," we cannot just guess; we look at the distance covered and divide it by the time it took. By using the formula Speed = Distance / Time, we get a single number that lets us compare your movement to your friend's, or even to a car or an aeroplane. The key insight is that speed is a "fair comparison" tool that works regardless of how long the journey was.

**Takeaway:** speed compares path covered to time taken - same road in less time is faster, and units must match before comparing. **In the exam:** in speed questions, divide distance by time and check both travellers wear the same units. **Check yourself:** race the walkthrough's gate-to-park pair - the same start, the same road, and the clock alone separates them. **Why students miss it:** students crown the bigger number blindly - the unit label is a no to that; rates only compare in matching dress. Rehearse the exam move once more - distance, time, units - and the answer arranges itself in exam-ready order with nothing left to chance.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that "fast" means "long distance" regardless of time. They confuse distance with speed, failing to realize that a slow object can cover a huge distance if given enough time (e.g., a snail over a month).

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: An auto-rickshaw travels 30 kilometres in 1.5 hours to reach a market. What is its speed?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify distance = 30 km and time = 1.5 hours.
- step 2: Use the formula Speed = Distance / Time.
- step 3: Substitute values: Speed = 30 / 1.5.
- step 4: Calculate: 30 divided by 1.5 is 20.
- step 5: Result is 20 km/h.
REASONING: We divide the total path by the time to find how many kilometres are covered in a single hour. This standardizes the rate of motion.
ANSWER: 20 km/h
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A student runs 200 metres in 40 seconds. Calculate the speed in m/s.
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify distance = 200 m and time = 40 s.
- step 2: Use the formula Speed = Distance / Time.
- step 3: Substitute values: Speed = 200 / 40.
- step 4: Calculate: 200 divided by 40 equals 5.
- step 5: Result is 5 m/s.
REASONING: Since the distance is in metres and time in seconds, the resulting speed is expressed in metres per second.
ANSWER: 5 m/s
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A cyclist travels 5 km in 30 minutes. What is their speed in km/h? (Hint: 30 minutes = 0.5 hours)
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify distance = 5 km and time = 30 minutes.
- step 2: Convert time to hours: 30 minutes = 0.5 hours.
- step 3: Use formula Speed = Distance / Time.
- step 4: Substitute: 5 / 0.5.
- step 5: Calculate: 5 divided by 0.5 is 10.
REASONING: We must ensure units match the required output (km/h). Converting time to hours is necessary before division.
ANSWER: 10 km/h
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If a tortoise moves 10 metres in 100 seconds and a rabbit moves 10 metres in 5 seconds, explain who is faster using the concept of speed.
COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying
STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate speed of tortoise (10/100 = 0.1 m/s).
- step 2: Calculate speed of rabbit (10/5 = 2 m/s).
- step 3: Compare values (2 > 0.1).
- step 4: Conclude that the rabbit is faster because it covers more distance in the same amount of time.
ANSWER: The rabbit is faster because its speed (2 m/s) is much higher than the tortoise's speed (0.1 m/s), meaning it covers more distance every second.
DIFFICULTY: medium
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Two friends argue. Friend A says, "My car is faster because it reached the destination in 2 hours." Friend B says, "My car is faster because it traveled 200 km." Who is correct?
COMPETENCY: Error identification and correction
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand that speed depends on both distance AND time.
- step 2: Point out that Friend A ignores the distance covered.
- step 3: Point out that Friend B ignores the time taken.
- step 4: Explain that without both pieces of information, we cannot determine who is faster.
ANSWER: Neither is necessarily correct. Speed is a ratio of distance to time; without knowing the distance for Friend A and the time for Friend B, we cannot compare them fairly.
DIFFICULTY: hard
