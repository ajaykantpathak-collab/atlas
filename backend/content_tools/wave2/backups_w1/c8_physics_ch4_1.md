## ELI5
The same push spreads differently depending on the stage it lands on. A drawing pin's broad head takes your thumb's force comfortably; the tiny tip concentrates the identical force into a piercing point. That concentration is pressure — force divided by the area receiving it. Shrink the area, the pressure climbs; spread the force, it fades. Every sharp thing cuts and every broad thing cushions by this single piece of arithmetic.

## DEFINITION
Pressure is the thrust (force acting perpendicular to a surface) applied per unit area of that surface. Its SI unit is the pascal (Pa), where 1 Pa = 1 N/m².
## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
understand
## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student can explain why a drawing pin penetrates a board easily when pushed by its flat head but not by its sharp tip, using the concept of pressure as force distributed over area. The student can also calculate pressure given force and area in SI units, correctly converting cm² to m² when necessary.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the conceptual definition of pressure as a ratio of force to area and its mathematical representation P = F/A. It does NOT cover the SI unit name "pascal" in detail (that is node c8_physics_ch4_2), nor does it cover atmospheric pressure, liquids pressure, or applications like broad straps (those are subsequent nodes).
## WALKTHROUGH
Observe a drawing pin (thumbtack) pressed into a soft notice board in your classroom: the sharp tip goes in smoothly while the flat plastic head remains outside, even though your thumb applies the same push on both ends. Notice that the effect of the force depends on how concentrated it is — the tiny tip area creates a much stronger "pressing effect" than the broad head. Recall from Class 7 (c7_physics_ch04_006) how we defined speed as distance per unit time to compare motion; similarly, we define pressure as force per unit area to compare the intensity of a push on a surface. This leads to the formula P = F/A, where pressure increases if the same force acts on a smaller area.

Takeaway: pressure = thrust (perpendicular force) divided by area, measured in pascals — one newton on one square metre; small area means fierce pressure, large area means gentle pressure. Exam format: expect calculations with area conversions (square centimetres into square metres) and explanations of sharp-versus-blunt designs via the ratio. Verification beat: convert every area to square metres before dividing; the unit is the most common lost mark. Why students miss it: they divide by the number in the area without converting units — a pin's tip in square millimetres multiplies the pressure enormously, which is the whole point of sharpness.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
State the definition of pressure in your own words and write its SI unit with the symbol.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Pressure is the force acting perpendicularly on a surface divided by the area of that surface; SI unit is pascal (Pa). A wrong answer that says "pressure is the same as force" or gives the unit as newton (N) reveals the student is confusing the cause (force) with the effect (pressure) and has not grasped the "per unit area" part of the definition.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
A sharp knife and a blunt knife are made of the same material. You apply the same force on both while cutting a tomato. Why does the sharp knife cut easily while the blunt one crushes the tomato? Explain using the concept of pressure.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The sharp knife has a very small cutting edge area, so the same force produces a large pressure (P = F/A) that exceeds the tomato's skin strength. The blunt knife has a larger edge area, so the pressure is lower and cannot cut cleanly. A wrong answer that focuses only on "sharpness" without mentioning area shows the student has not linked the physical quantity pressure to the geometric property of the contact surface.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A force of 250 N acts perpendicularly on a rectangular surface of dimensions 50 cm × 20 cm. Calculate the pressure exerted on the surface in pascals. Show all unit conversions.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Area = 50 cm × 20 cm = 1000 cm² = 1000 × 10⁻⁴ m² = 0.1 m².
Step 2: P = F/A = 250 N / 0.1 m² = 2500 N/m² = 2500 Pa.
A wrong answer of 2.5 Pa (using cm² directly) or 250000 Pa (incorrect conversion) reveals the student is not converting area to m² before substituting, a very common unit error at this level.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Two identical bricks each weigh 30 N. Brick A rests on its largest face (20 cm × 10 cm). Brick B rests on its smallest face (10 cm × 5 cm). Compare the pressures they exert on the table. Which brick exerts greater pressure and by what factor?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
Area_A = 200 cm² = 0.02 m² → P_A = 30/0.02 = 1500 Pa.
Area_B = 50 cm² = 0.005 m² → P_B = 30/0.005 = 6000 Pa.
Brick B exerts 4 times the pressure of Brick A because its contact area is 1/4th. A wrong answer saying pressures are equal because weights are equal shows the student thinks pressure depends only on force, ignoring the inverse relationship with area.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student writes: "If I double the force on a surface, the pressure doubles. If I double the area, the pressure also doubles because there is more surface to push on." Evaluate this statement and correct the error.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The first part is correct (P ∝ F at constant A). The second part is wrong: doubling area while keeping force constant *halves* the pressure (P ∝ 1/A). The student incorrectly assumes a direct relationship for both variables. This reveals a failure to interpret the formula P = F/A as an inverse proportion with respect to area.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple activity using a balloon, a flat book, and a few drawing pins to demonstrate that pressure depends on area. Describe the steps and what observation would prove the concept.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Place a drawing pin on the table with its sharp tip up. Put the flat book on it, then place an inflated balloon on the book. Press the balloon gently with your palm — it bursts because the pin's tiny area creates huge pressure. Now repeat with a pin lying flat (head down) so its larger area touches the book; the balloon won't burst with the same push. A poor answer that only describes "pushing a pin into a balloon" without comparing two different contact areas shows the student cannot design a controlled comparison.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students believe pressure and force are interchangeable terms — they say "I applied a lot of pressure" when they mean "I pushed hard." They think a larger force *always* means larger pressure, ignoring that spreading the same force over a larger area (like lying on a bed of nails) reduces pressure. For example, they cannot explain why a camel's broad feet prevent sinking in sand while a horse's narrower hooves sink, even if the camel is heavier.
## ON_FAILURE
Have the student press the eraser end of a pencil and then the sharp tip onto their own palm with the *same* push (same force). Ask which hurts more. Guide them to notice the force was the same but the contact area differed. Then write P = F/A on the board and circle the A in the denominator: "Smaller A → larger P for same F." Finally, give two numerical problems where only area changes to cement the inverse relationship.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A woman of mass 60 kg stands on one heel of her high-heeled shoe. The heel is circular with a diameter of 1.0 cm. Calculate the pressure exerted on the floor. (Take g = 10 m/s²)
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given quantities: mass m = 60 kg, diameter d = 1.0 cm = 0.01 m, so radius r = 0.005 m. g = 10 m/s². Find pressure P in Pa.
- step 2: Force (weight) F = m × g = 60 kg × 10 m/s² = 600 N. Area A = πr² = 3.14 × (0.005 m)² = 3.14 × 0.000025 m² = 7.85 × 10⁻⁵ m².
- step 3: Formula P = F/A. Substitute: P = 600 N / (7.85 × 10⁻⁵ m²).
- step 4: Compute: 600 / 7.85e-5 = 7.64 × 10⁶ N/m² = 7.64 × 10⁶ Pa.
- step 5: Verify: A typical high heel pressure is several MPa; 7.6 MPa is reasonable for a thin heel. Answer: 7.6 × 10⁶ Pa (approx).
REASONING: The woman's weight acts as the force. The heel's tiny circular area concentrates this force, producing enormous pressure — which is why high heels damage wooden floors. Unit conversion from cm to m is critical; skipping it gives a wrong answer 10,000 times too small.
ANSWER: 7.6 × 10⁶ Pa
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A rectangular glass slab of dimensions 50 cm × 20 cm × 10 cm weighs 120 N. It is placed on a table first on its largest face, then on its smallest face. Calculate the pressure in each case.
STEPS:
- step 1: Given: Weight F = 120 N. Dimensions: 50 cm, 20 cm, 10 cm. Convert to metres: 0.5 m, 0.2 m, 0.1 m. Find P_large and P_small.
- step 2: Largest face area = 0.5 m × 0.2 m = 0.10 m². Smallest face area = 0.2 m × 0.1 m = 0.02 m².
- step 3: Use P = F/A for each orientation.
- step 4: P_large = 120 N / 0.10 m² = 1200 Pa. P_small = 120 N / 0.02 m² = 6000 Pa.
- step 5: Check: Smallest face has 1/5 the area, so pressure should be 5 times larger (6000/1200 = 5). Correct.
REASONING: The same force (weight) distributed over different areas produces different pressures. This demonstrates the inverse proportionality P ∝ 1/A when F is constant. Converting cm to m before calculating area avoids the common error of using cm² directly.
ANSWER: Largest face: 1200 Pa; Smallest face: 6000 Pa
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A tractor of mass 5000 kg has four tyres. Each tyre makes a rectangular contact patch with the ground of length 40 cm and width 20 cm. Calculate the pressure exerted by the tractor on the ground. (g = 10 m/s²)
STEPS:
- step 1: Given: mass m = 5000 kg, g = 10 m/s². Number of tyres = 4. Each contact patch: 40 cm × 20 cm. Find total pressure on ground.
- step 2: Total weight F_total = m × g = 5000 × 10 = 50,000 N. Weight per tyre = 50,000 N / 4 = 12,500 N (assuming equal load).
- step 3: Area per tyre = 0.40 m × 0.20 m = 0.08 m². Total contact area = 4 × 0.08 = 0.32 m².
- step 4: Pressure P = F_total / A_total = 50,000 N / 0.32 m² = 156,250 Pa. Alternatively, P per tyre = 12,500 / 0.08 = 156,250 Pa (same).
- step 5: Verify: Tractor tyre pressures are typically 100–200 kPa; 156 kPa is realistic.
REASONING: This combines the concept of weight as force (from gravity) with pressure definition. The total pressure on the ground is the total force divided by total contact area, which equals the pressure under each tyre if load is equally shared. This connects to the next node on reducing pressure by increasing area (broad tyres).
ANSWER: 1.56 × 10⁵ Pa (or 156 kPa)
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A block of weight 200 N rests on a table with its base area 0.5 m². A student places an identical block on top of it. What happens to the pressure on the table? Explain using the pressure formula.
COMPETENCY: Predicting outcomes from changing variables
STEPS:
- step 1: Understand the physical situation: force doubles (two blocks), area stays the same (same base).
- step 2: Identify principle: P = F/A. If F becomes 2F and A constant, P becomes 2P.
- step 3: Apply: Initial P = 200/0.5 = 400 Pa. New P = 400/0.5 = 800 Pa.
- step 4: Interpret: Pressure doubles because the force doubled while contact area remained unchanged.
ANSWER: The pressure doubles from 400 Pa to 800 Pa because pressure is directly proportional to force when area is constant.
DIFFICULTY: easy
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A circus elephant of mass 3000 kg stands on one leg during a performance. The foot is a circle of diameter 30 cm. A 50 kg acrobat balances on one heel of diameter 2 cm. Compare the pressures they exert on the ground. Who exerts more pressure and why is this surprising?
COMPETENCY: Interpreting physical situations using formulas and unit analysis
STEPS:
- step 1: Calculate forces: Elephant F_e = 3000 × 10 = 30,000 N. Acrobat F_a = 50 × 10 = 500 N.
- step 2: Calculate areas: Elephant r_e = 0.15 m → A_e = π(0.15)² = 0.0707 m². Acrobat r_a = 0.01 m → A_a = π(0.01)² = 0.000314 m².
- step 3: Calculate pressures: P_e = 30,000 / 0.0707 ≈ 424,000 Pa. P_a = 500 / 0.000314 ≈ 1,592,000 Pa.
- step 4: Compare: Acrobat's heel pressure (~1.6 MPa) is nearly 4 times the elephant's foot pressure (~0.42 MPa).
- step 5: Explain surprise: Despite the elephant being 60 times heavier, its huge foot area reduces pressure. The acrobat's tiny heel concentrates the small force into enormous pressure.
ANSWER: The acrobat exerts about 1.6 MPa, the elephant about 0.42 MPa. The acrobat exerts nearly 4 times more pressure because pressure depends on force/area, not force alone.
DIFFICULTY: hard
