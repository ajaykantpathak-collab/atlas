## ELI5
The vegetable seller's scale reads 1.435 kg of coriander - and every digit after the point is poorer than its left neighbour. Left of the point live the old friends: ones, tens, hundreds. Right of it, the ladder dives: tenths, then hundredths, then thousandths, each seat worth exactly one-tenth of the seat before it. Read a decimal the way you read a big whole number, but continue the chart past the point instead of stopping - the mirror-image rule that makes decimals honest.

## DEFINITION
Decimal place value extends our base-ten number system to the right of the decimal point, representing fractional parts of powers of ten: tenths ($\frac{1}{10}$), hundredths ($\frac{1}{100}$), and thousandths ($\frac{1}{1000}$). Each position to the right is one-tenth the value of the position immediately to its left.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are at the vegetable market buying fresh coriander, and the digital weighing scale reads $1.435\text{ kg}$. What do these digits after the point actually mean? Just like whole numbers use ones, tens, and hundreds going left, decimals use tenths, hundredths, and thousandths going right. To understand this, we start by looking at a whole unit divided into equal pieces, connecting back to fractions like $\frac{1}{10}$, $\frac{1}{100}$, and $\frac{1}{1000}$. The key insight is that moving one step to the right divides the place value by $10$, so the first place after the point is $\frac{1}{10}$ (tenths), the second is $\frac{1}{100}$ (hundredths), and the third is $\frac{1}{1000}$ (thousandths).

**Takeaway:** decimal place value mirrors whole numbers exactly: tenths, hundredths and thousandths march rightward, each seat worth one-tenth of its left neighbour. **In the exam:** for place-value questions, count the seats outward from the decimal point one at a time, then name the fraction the digit holds there. **Check yourself:** expand the coriander reading into ones, tenths, hundredths and thousandths, then read the whole thing aloud once without stopping. **Why students miss it:** they reuse left-side names like 'tens' for right-side seats, misreading hundredths as tenths and losing a whole place in the answer.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
The correct answer is hundredths or $\frac{7}{100}$ (or $0.07$). Students who answer "tens" or $70$ confuse decimal place value with whole number place value on the left of the decimal point. Students who answer "tenths" miscount the position index.

**A wrong answer (reading right-to-left)** walked the places from the wrong door - after the point the first place is tenths, so the 7 lands on hundredths, worth 0.07.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why the digit $5$ in $0.005$ has a much smaller value than the digit $5$ in $0.5$, even though they are both the same face value.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
The correct explanation is that $0.5$ represents $\frac{5}{10}$ (five tenths), whereas $0.005$ represents $\frac{5}{1000}$ (five thousandths), meaning the $5$ in $0.005$ is divided by $100$ relative to $0.5$. Students who incorrectly claim they are equal ignore the position of the digit relative to the decimal point.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
Write the expanded form of the decimal number $304.582$ using fractions for the decimal parts.
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
The correct answer is $300 + 4 + \frac{5}{10} + \frac{8}{100} + \frac{2}{1000}$. 
Steps:
1. Identify the whole number part: $300 + 4$.
2. Identify the tenths digit ($5$) and write its value: $\frac{5}{10}$.
3. Identify the hundredths digit ($8$) and write its value: $\frac{8}{100}$.
4. Identify the thousandths digit ($2$) and write its value: $\frac{2}{1000}$.
5. Combine all terms with plus signs. 
Students who write $300 + 4 + 5 + 8 + 2$ fail to account for the fractional denominators of decimal place values.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Analyze the number $6.444$. How many times larger is the value of the first $4$ (in the tenths place) compared to the second $4$ (in the hundredths place)?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The correct answer is $10$ times larger. 
Steps:
1. Value of first $4$ (tenths) = $0.4 = \frac{4}{10}$.
2. Value of second $4$ (hundredths) = $0.04 = \frac{4}{100}$.
3. Ratio = $\frac{0.4}{0.04} = 10$. 
Students who answer $100$ times or equal fail to realize that each adjacent place value to the right is scaled by a factor of $\frac{1}{10}$, meaning moving left multiplies by $10$.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Evaluate the following statement: "In the number $0.308$, the zero in the hundredths place has no value, so we can completely remove it without changing the number." Is this true or false, and why?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The statement is false. While zero has a face value of zero, holding the hundredths place ensures that the digit $8$ sits in the thousandths place. Removing the zero changes the number to $0.38$, which is $\frac{38}{100}$, whereas $0.308$ is $\frac{308}{1000}$. Students who say it is true confuse place-holding zeros with leading or trailing whole number zeros.
## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Create a decimal number using the digits $2$, $5$, and $9$ exactly once each, such that the digit $5$ has the greatest possible place value among your choices. What is this number, and what is its expanded form?
## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
The number is $5.92$ (or $5.29$). Expanded form: $5 + \frac{9}{10} + \frac{2}{100}$. 
Criteria for a good answer: The digit $5$ must be placed in the ones place (or tenths place if restricted to strictly decimals like $0.592$, but ones gives the greatest possible value). A poor answer places $5$ in the thousandths place ($0.295$), giving it the smallest possible place value.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often assume that numbers with more digits after the decimal point are always larger, or they misread place values by counting from right to left like whole numbers. For example, when comparing $0.45$ and $0.405$, a student might claim $0.405$ is larger because "four hundred five is bigger than forty-five," ignoring that the $4$ in both represents tenths, but the second number has a zero in the hundredths place making its hundredths value smaller.
## ON_FAILURE
To fix this error, guide the student to pad numbers with trailing zeros so they have an equal number of decimal places before comparing. Say: "Let's line up our decimal points vertically and match the number of digits. Compare $0.450$ and $0.405$ column by column from left to right: both have $4$ in the tenths place, but $0.450$ has a $5$ in the hundredths place while $0.405$ has a $0$. Since $5$ hundredths is greater than $0$ hundredths, $0.450$ is larger."

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A science kit contains a tiny copper wire of thickness $0.347\text{ mm}$. Write this number in expanded decimal form and identify the digit in the hundredths place along with its exact numerical value.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given decimal number and separate its whole number and decimal parts: $0.347 = 0 + 0.3 + 0.04 + 0.007$.
- Step 2: Express each decimal place value as a fraction: tenths = $\frac{3}{10}$, hundredths = $\frac{4}{100}$, thousandths = $\frac{7}{1000}$.
- Step 3: Write out the complete expanded form: $0.347 = \frac{3}{10} + \frac{4}{100} + \frac{7}{1000}$ or $0.3 + 0.04 + 0.007$.
- Step 4: Locate the digit in the hundredths place by counting two positions to the right of the decimal point: the digit is $4$.
- Step 5: State the exact numerical value of that digit based on its position: $4 \times \frac{1}{100} = \frac{4}{100} = 0.04$.

REASONING: Breaking a decimal down into its constituent place values reveals the exact fractional contribution of every digit. Moving right from the decimal point, the denominators increase by factors of $10$ ($\frac{1}{10}$, $\frac{1}{100}$, $\frac{1}{1000}$).
ANSWER: Expanded form: $0.3 + 0.04 + 0.007$; Hundredths digit is $4$, with a value of $0.04$ or $\frac{4}{100}$.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Riya writes down a number with $5$ in the ones place, $8$ in the thousandths place, $2$ in the tenths place, and $0$ in the hundredths place. What is the number, and what is its value in words?

STEPS:
- Step 1: Set up the place value skeleton with a decimal point: $\_\_\_ . \_\_\_ \_\_\_ \_\_\_$.
- Step 2: Place the given digits into their respective positions: ones = $5$, tenths = $2$, hundredths = $0$, thousandths = $8$.
- Step 3: Assemble the digits to form the standard decimal number: $5.208$.
- Step 4: Convert the decimal into fractional form to verify: $5 + \frac{2}{10} + \frac{0}{100} + \frac{8}{1000} = 5 \frac{208}{1000}$.
- Step 5: Write the number in words: Five and two hundred eight thousandths.

REASONING: Constructing a number from place-value clues requires mapping each named position (ones, tenths, hundredths, thousandths) directly to its slot relative to the decimal point. A zero in any position acts as a place holder without adding value.
ANSWER: $5.208$; Five and two hundred eight thousandths.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Find the sum of the place value of $6$ in $41.625$ and the place value of $3$ in $9.034$. Express the final result as a single decimal number and as a mixed fraction.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the position and value of $6$ in $41.625$. The $6$ is in the tenths place, so its value is $0.6$ or $\frac{6}{10}$.
- Step 2: Identify the position and value of $3$ in $9.034$. The $3$ is in the hundredths place, so its value is $0.03$ or $\frac{3}{100}$.
- Step 3: Set up the addition of these two place values: $0.6 + 0.03$.
- Step 4: Align decimal places by padding with zeros: $0.60 + 0.03 = 0.63$.
- Step 5: Convert $0.63$ into a fraction: $\frac{63}{100}$.
REASONING: Combining decimal place values requires proper vertical alignment of the decimal point so that tenths are added to tenths and hundredths to hundredths. Padding with trailing zeros prevents column addition errors.
ANSWER: $0.63$ as a decimal; $\frac{63}{100}$ as a fraction.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A student claims that $4.5$ is equal to $4.050$ because "zeros at the end of a decimal number don't change its value." Is this student entirely correct? Justify your answer using place value analysis for each digit after the decimal point.

COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying decimal place value structures
STEPS:
- Step 1: Analyze the number $4.5$ by breaking it into its place value components ($4\text{ ones} + 5\text{ tenths}$).
- Step 2: Analyze the number $4.050$ by breaking it into its place value components ($4\text{ ones} + 0\text{ tenths} + 5\text{ hundredths} + 0\text{ thousandths}$).
- Step 3: Compare corresponding place values: the tenths place has $5$ in $4.5$ but $0$ in $4.050$.
- Step 4: Conclude whether the statement is true and explain the exact role of trailing zeros.
ANSWER: The student is incorrect. While trailing zeros at the very end of a decimal ($4.050 = 4.05$) can be dropped, inserting a zero *after* the decimal point before significant digits ($4.05$ vs $4.5$) shifts the $5$ from the tenths place to the hundredths place, making $4.5$ ($4\frac{5}{10}$) ten times larger than $4.05$ ($4\frac{5}{100}$). However, $4.5$ IS equal to $4.500$, but $4.5$ is NOT equal to $4.050$.
DIFFICULTY: medium
