Aarav's mother pays for groceries by scanning a code - no cash changes hands, yet the payment is completely real. Modern money lives inside bank accounts: deposits, cheques and digital transfers, backed by the banking system and legal authority rather than by paper or metal. A cheque is an instruction telling the bank to move money; a digital payment is the same instruction at lightning speed. Money has mostly become an entry in a ledger, not a thing in your pocket.

## DEFINITION
Modern forms of money include bank deposits, cheques, and digital money, which do not have physical form as coins or paper notes but serve as universally accepted mediums of exchange backed by the banking system and legal authority. These forms allow people to store, transfer, and settle transactions securely without physically moving heavy metal or paper currency.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often believe that digital money and cheques create *new* wealth out of thin air, thinking that an online transfer or cheque payment means magic money appears in an account. In reality, digital money and cheques are simply electronic or paper instructions that move *existing* bank deposits from one person's account to another without creating any new purchasing power.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Priya has a bank deposit of ₹25,000 in her savings account. She pays her school tuition fee of ₹9,000 by writing a cheque. Later, she receives a digital transfer of ₹4,500 from her uncle as a birthday gift into the same account. Calculate her final bank account balance.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify initial bank deposit balance = ₹25,000.
- Step 2: Account for the cheque payment for tuition, which decreases her bank deposit by ₹9,000 (₹25,000 - ₹9,000 = ₹16,000).
- Step 3: Account for the incoming digital transfer from her uncle, which increases her bank deposit by ₹4,500 (₹16,000 + ₹4,500 = ₹20,500).
- Step 4: Verify all transactions are properly recorded against bank deposits without using physical cash.
- Step 5: Conclude that Priya's final bank account balance is ₹20,500.
REASONING: Bank deposits change dynamically based on debits (payments out via cheque or digital pay) and credits (money coming in via digital transfers). By tracking each addition and subtraction sequentially, we determine the correct final account balance.
ANSWER: ₹20,500
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A local bookstore has a digital wallet balance of ₹12,000 at the start of the day. During the day, customers make 15 digital QR-code payments of ₹200 each. The store owner then transfers ₹5,000 from this digital wallet to the publisher via net banking. What is the closing digital balance of the store?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Note the starting digital balance = ₹12,000.
- Step 2: Calculate total incoming digital payments: 15 customers × ₹200/customer = ₹3,000.
- Step 3: Add incoming payments to starting balance: ₹12,000 + ₹3,000 = ₹15,000.
- Step 4: Account for the net banking transfer to the publisher, which subtracts ₹5,000 from the digital wallet: ₹15,000 - ₹5,000 = ₹10,000.
- Step 5: Conclude that the store's closing digital balance is ₹10,000.
REASONING: Digital money operates through continuous additions (inflows from sales) and subtractions (outflows for business expenses) recorded electronically in bank-linked accounts.
ANSWER: ₹10,000
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A farmer sells harvested wheat worth ₹50,000. The buyer offers two payment options: Option A is physical cash notes, and Option B is an immediate digital bank transfer directly into the farmer's savings account, which earns 4% annual interest. If the farmer keeps the money in the bank for one full year without touching it, calculate the total value of his bank deposit at the end of the year under Option B compared to Option H (hoarding physical cash at home earning 0% interest).
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the initial principal amount = ₹50,000.
- Step 2: For Option B (Bank Deposit), calculate annual interest earned: Principal × Rate = ₹50,000 × (4 / 100) = ₹2,000.
- Step 3: Calculate total bank deposit at year-end for Option B: ₹50,000 + ₹2,000 = ₹52,000.
- Step 4: For Option H (Physical Cash hoarding at home), calculate interest earned: ₹50,000 × 0% = ₹0.
- Step 5: Calculate total cash value at year-end for Option H: ₹50,000 + ₹0 = ₹50,000.
- Step 6: Compare both forms of money and conclude that bank deposits under Option B yield ₹52,000, whereas physical cash hoarding yields ₹50,000.
REASONING: Modern bank deposits not only provide secure digital transactions but also reward depositors with interest, making money held in banks more economically productive than physical cash hidden at home.
ANSWER: Option B yields ₹52,000; Option H yields ₹50,000 (Difference of ₹2,000 in favor of bank deposits).
