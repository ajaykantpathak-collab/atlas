## ELI5
A flat map squashes a round Earth — which is why countries near the poles look stretched. A globe keeps the true shapes. Now wrap two families of invisible lines around it. Latitudes run sideways, parallel to the Equator, the 0-degree ring around Earth's middle; they measure how far north or south a place sits, all the way to the poles. Longitudes run pole to pole, measured east or west from the Prime Meridian, the 0-degree line through Greenwich. One latitude alone traces a whole ring, one longitude a whole curve — but where they cross is a single exact address for any spot on the planet.

## DEFINITION
A globe is a three-dimensional spherical model of the Earth showing its true round shape. Latitudes are imaginary lines running east–west, parallel to the Equator, measuring how far north or south a place is — the Equator is 0°, the poles are 90°. Longitudes are imaginary lines running north–south from pole to pole, measuring how far east or west a place is from the Prime Meridian at 0°. A pair of latitude and longitude gives the exact location of any place on Earth.

## WALKTHROUGH
A flat map shrinks a round Earth — and a ball squashed flat always stretches at the edges, which is why countries near the poles look stretched on flat maps. A globe is the Earth in miniature — it keeps the true shapes and distances of the lands and oceans. Now wrap two sets of invisible lines around the globe. First, latitudes — the flat rings running sideways, parallel to the Equator, which is the 0° line around the Earth's middle; count the degrees north or south of the Equator and you know how far toward the poles a place sits (the poles are 90°). Second, longitudes — the long lines running from the North Pole to the South Pole; count the degrees east or west of the Prime Meridian, the 0° line that passes through Greenwich. The crossing point of one latitude and one longitude is an address — like 28°N, 77°E for Delhi. And the reason you need both: a latitude alone is a whole ring around the Earth, a longitude alone is a whole pole-to-pole line — only their crossing gives a single point. Two numbers, one exact spot on the whole planet.

**Takeaway:** the globe keeps Earth's true round shape, and two families of invisible lines turn it into an address system — latitudes east-west from the Equator, longitudes pole-to-pole from the Prime Meridian. **In the exam:** give a place's location as a latitude-longitude pair, never one alone; a single line traces a whole ring or curve, only the crossing is a point. **Check yourself:** name the 0-degree lines: Equator for latitude, Prime Meridian through Greenwich for longitude. **Why students miss it:** they swap the two families, calling the Equator a longitude.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What is a globe?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
What are latitudes?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Flat rings measuring north-south distance. Latitudes are imaginary lines running east–west, parallel to the Equator, measuring how far north or south a place lies — the Equator is 0° and the poles are 90°. A wrong answer confuses them with the pole-to-pole lines — those are longitudes.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
What are longitudes?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Pole-to-pole lines measuring east-west distance. Longitudes are imaginary lines running north–south from pole to pole, measuring how far east or west a place lies, counted from the Prime Meridian at 0°. A wrong answer swaps the two — longitudes run up-and-down, not sideways.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
What are the Equator and the Prime Meridian?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
The Equator is 0 latitude; the Prime Meridian is 0 longitude. The Equator is the 0° latitude — the circle around the Earth's middle from which north–south distance is measured; the Prime Meridian is the 0° longitude — the pole-to-pole line through Greenwich from which east–west distance is measured. A wrong answer confuses which measures which direction.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
Why do you need both latitude and longitude to find a place?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
Only their crossing gives one point. A single latitude is a whole circle around the Earth and a single longitude is a whole pole-to-pole line — only the crossing point of one latitude and one longitude gives a single exact location. A wrong answer thinks one line is enough — it gives a whole ring, not a point.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Why do flat maps stretch sizes near the poles?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Flattening a sphere stretches polar regions. The Earth is round and a map is flat — flattening a sphere stretches the parts near the poles, so places like Greenland look far bigger on flat maps than they really are on a globe. A wrong answer says "the map is wrong" — the distortion is unavoidable when flattening a sphere.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often confuse the two lines — latitude and longitude. Latitude = flat rungs of a ladder measuring north–south position (Equator 0°); longitude = long pole-to-pole lines measuring east–west position (Prime Meridian 0°). A related error: thinking one coordinate is enough — only the pair pins a place.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Delhi is near 28°N latitude and 77°E longitude. Explain what each number tells you.
STEPS:
- 1: Break the first coordinate — 28°N means Delhi sits on the latitude circle 28 degrees north of the Equator.
- 2: Break the second — 77°E means it sits on the longitude line 77 degrees east of the Prime Meridian.
- 3: Combine — the crossing of the two lines is Delhi's exact spot.
REASONING: Latitude gives the north–south position; longitude gives the east–west position; the pair pins the city.
ANSWER: 28°N places Delhi 28° north of the Equator; 77°E places it 77° east of the Prime Meridian — together they locate the city.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: On a flat world map Greenland looks as big as Africa, but on a globe it looks much smaller. Explain.
STEPS:
- 1: Note the shapes — Earth is a sphere; the map is flat.
- 2: Recall the rule — flattening a sphere stretches the parts near the poles.
- 3: Apply it — Greenland is near the pole, so it is stretched huge on the flat map; Africa near the Equator is not.
REASONING: The globe keeps true sizes; the flat map distorts polar regions.
ANSWER: The flat map stretches polar regions, so Greenland looks huge; the globe shows its real, smaller size — Africa is truly far bigger.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A pilot reports "35°N" but forgets the longitude. Why can rescuers still not find the plane?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify what 35°N gives — a whole circle of latitude around the Earth.
- 2: Note the gap — without longitude, every point on that circle is possible.
- 3: Conclude — both coordinates are needed for one spot.
REASONING: A latitude alone is a ring of thousands of kilometres — only the second coordinate narrows it to a point.
ANSWER: 35°N is a whole circle around the Earth — without the longitude the plane could be anywhere on it.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: Your friend says "the Equator and the Prime Meridian are the same kind of line." Correct them and explain what each really measures.
COMPETENCY: Distinguishing two easily-confused references.
STEPS:
- 1: Identify each — the Equator is a latitude; the Prime Meridian is a longitude.
- 2: State what each measures — Equator measures distance north/south; Prime Meridian measures east/west.
- 3: Note their shape difference — the Equator is a full circle; the Meridian is a half-circle pole-to-pole.
- 4: Conclude they are different kinds of reference lines.
ANSWER: They are not the same kind — the Equator is 0° latitude measuring north–south, while the Prime Meridian is 0° longitude measuring east–west.
DIFFICULTY: medium

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: A treasure map says only "40°N." Explain why this is useless alone and what second piece of information would make it pinpoint the treasure.
COMPETENCY: Applying the two-coordinate rule to a practical problem.
STEPS:
- 1: Read what 40°N gives — a whole latitude circle.
- 2: Identify what is missing — the longitude.
- 3: Show why the pair works — the longitude line would cross the latitude at one point.
- 4: Conclude that one coordinate is a circle, two are a point.
ANSWER: 40°N alone is a whole circle of possible spots — adding a longitude (e.g., 74°E) would cross it at one exact point where the treasure lies.
DIFFICULTY: hard
