## ELI5
Your friend speaks one language with his grandparents at home and a completely different one with you at school — two languages before lunch. India hums like this everywhere. The Constitution lists 22 official languages, yet the true count runs far higher, into thousands of dialects, and the street or the cinema mixes regional languages with Hindi and English. Rather than a wall, this becomes a skill: most Indians are multilingual, switching languages depending on where they are and who they are talking to. Many languages, then, are not a barrier to understanding each other — they are the everyday music of the country.

## DEFINITION
Linguistic diversity refers to the existence of a wide variety of languages and dialects within a specific region or society. In India, this means that people across different states communicate using a vast array of distinct mother tongues, often using multiple languages in their daily lives.

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to identify that India has both a set of recognized official languages and thousands of spoken mother tongues, and explain why being "multilingual" is a common trait for Indians.
## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers the count and prevalence of Indian languages and the concept of multilingualism. It excludes formal language policy, the history of scripts, and the classification of language families.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
A good answer would include activities like "phrase swapping," where students teach each other greeting words in their mother tongues, or a multilingual poem recitation. A poor answer would ignore the variety and just pick one language for everyone to use.
## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think that because there are so many languages, India must be "divided" or "confused." They fail to realize that being multilingual is a skill, not a burden, and that these languages coexist peacefully in the daily lives of millions of people.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Identify why a person might know three languages.
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the mother tongue (learned at home).
- 2: Identify the regional language (learned from neighbors/community).
- 3: Identify the link language (like English/Hindi for school or work).
- 4: Analyze how these serve different purposes.
REASONING: Breaking down the "why" shows that each language has a specific function in a person's life.
ANSWER: A person often knows three languages because they need one for family, one for the local community, and one for broader national/global communication.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Why do we say linguistic diversity "enriches" society?
STEPS:
- 1: Think of a specific cultural product (e.g., a folk song or a story).
- 2: Consider how that story changes when told in a different language.
- 3: Compare if the story loses or gains meaning through different words.
- 4: Argue that new languages offer new ways to describe the world.
REASONING: Analyzing cultural products shows that language is not just words, but a way to see the world.
ANSWER: Diversity enriches society because each language carries unique songs, stories, and ways of thinking that, when shared, make the whole culture more vibrant and diverse.
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: If you were to create a map of India showing where different languages are spoken, why would the borders of the colors be "fuzzy" rather than sharp lines?
COMPETENCY: Spatial Reasoning / Understanding Complexity
STEPS:
- 1: Acknowledge that people move (migration).
- 2: Recognize that families from different states live next to each other.
- 3: Conclude that languages overlap in cities and towns.
- 4: State that language is about people, not just land.
ANSWER: The borders are fuzzy because people migrate and live in areas where multiple languages are spoken simultaneously, making it impossible to draw a hard line where one language ends and another begins.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Compare the role of "Official Languages" versus "Mother Tongues." Why do we need both?
COMPETENCY: Analytical Thinking
STEPS:
- 1: Define the purpose of official languages (government/administration).
- 2: Define the purpose of mother tongues (identity/culture/home).
- 3: Explain the conflict/harmony between the two.
- 4: Synthesize why both are necessary for a large nation.
ANSWER: Official languages provide a standard way for the government to communicate with all citizens, while mother tongues preserve our personal identity and connection to our roots; a nation needs both to stay organized while keeping its culture alive.
