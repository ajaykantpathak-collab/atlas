Rahul solved 3(x - 2) = 9 and got x = 1, but substituting 1 back hands the left side -3, not 9 - somewhere a sign slipped. Error analysis is algebra as detective work: substitute first to find where the balance broke, then audit the exact line where the arithmetic went astray. A missed distribution or an unflipped sign is small enough to miss and big enough to sink the whole solution.

## DEFINITION
Most lost marks in linear equations come from a handful of repeating errors: a sign that should flip on transposition but does not, a bracket removed without multiplying every term inside it, a term divided on one side only, or arithmetic slips under the pressure of several steps. The cure is procedure: keep both sides balanced in every move, write each step on its own line, and finish by substituting the answer into the original equation to check that both sides come out equal.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students frequently commit sign-change errors when moving variables or constants across the equals sign, or they fail to distribute a negative multiplier to every term inside parentheses. For example, when solving 5 - 2(x - 3) = 7, students often write 5 - 2x - 3 = 7 instead of 5 - 2x + 6 = 7, forgetting that multiplying -2 by -3 gives a positive +6.
## ON_FAILURE
When a student makes a sign or distribution error, have them circle every bracket and negative sign with a red pen before starting. Remind them: 'Whenever you cross the bridge of the equals sign, your sign must change from positive to negative, and negative to positive.' For brackets, explicitly write out the multiplication of each term separately: -2 × x and -2 × (-3).

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: Amit tried to solve the equation 7 - 3(x + 1) = 4. His final answer was x = 2. Find his mistake, show the correct steps, and verify the correct answer.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Examine Amit's expansion of the brackets. Amit wrote 7 - 3x + 1 = 4, missing the distribution of -3 to +1.
- Step 2: Apply the correct distributive property: -3 × x = -3x and -3 × 1 = -3, so the equation becomes 7 - 3x - 3 = 4.
- Step 3: Combine like terms on the Left Hand Side: (7 - 3) - 3x = 4, which simplifies to 4 - 3x = 4.
- Step 4: Transpose the constant 4 to the Right Hand Side using its inverse operation: -3x = 4 - 4 ⇒ -3x = 0.
- Step 5: Divide by -3 to isolate x: x = (0)/(-3) = 0, and verify by substitution: 7 - 3(0 + 1) = 7 - 3 = 4.
REASONING: Amit failed to distribute the negative multiplier across all terms inside the parentheses and missed the sign change. By systematically expanding each term and combining constants first, we arrive at the correct solution of x = 0.
ANSWER: x = 0
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Divya solved (2x - 3)/(5) = 3 by writing 2x - 3 = 3 × 5, then 2x = 15 - 3, and finally x = 6. Audit her steps, identify the exact error, and solve it correctly.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Check Divya's first step (multiplying both sides by 5): 2x - 3 = 15. This step is correct.
- Step 2: Check Divya's second step (transposing -3): She wrote 2x = 15 - 3, which incorrectly changed -3 to -3 instead of +3.
- Step 3: Apply the correct inverse operation for transposition: moving -3 across the equals sign gives 2x = 15 + 3.
- Step 4: Simplify the Right Hand Side: 2x = 18.
- Step 5: Divide both sides by the coefficient 2: x = 18/2 = 9.
REASONING: Divya committed a sign-flipping error during transposition. When a negative term crosses the equals sign, it must become positive, changing 15 - 3 to 15 + 3.
ANSWER: x = 9
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Rahul was given the problem: "Find the error in solving 5x - 2 = 3x + 8 where a student concluded x = 2." Provide a complete audit, corrected solution, and verification.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Re-evaluate the student's grouping of variable terms. The student likely moved 3x incorrectly or made an arithmetic subtraction error.
- Step 2: Group all variable terms on the Left Hand Side by subtracting 3x from both sides: 5x - 3x - 2 = 8.
- Step 3: Simplify the variable terms: 2x - 2 = 8.
- Step 4: Transpose the constant -2 to the Right Hand Side using its inverse operation (addition): 2x = 8 + 2 ⇒ 2x = 10.
- Step 5: Divide by the coefficient 2: x = 10/2 = 5.
- Step 6: Verify the solution by substituting x = 5 into both original sides: LHS = 5(5) - 2 = 23, RHS = 3(5) + 8 = 23. Since LHS = RHS, x = 5 is correct.
REASONING: The student who got x = 2 likely miscalculated the combination of constants or variables. Proper grouping of variable terms on one side and constants on the other ensures a balanced, error-free result.
ANSWER: x = 5
