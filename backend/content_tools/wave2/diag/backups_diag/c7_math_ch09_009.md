## ELI5
A Jaipur builder holds two roof beams and must prove them twins before cutting expensive wood: name the criterion - three sides, two sides with the caught angle, two angles with the bridging side, or the right-triangle special. Once congruence is certified, CPCTC opens the cupboard - corresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal - so any unknown side or angle in one twin is simply read off the other. In overlapping figures, the shared side counts for both, stated plainly like a borrowed rupee returned.

## DEFINITION
Applying congruence means using the four triangle tests — SSS (three sides), SAS (two sides and the included angle), ASA (two angles and the included side) and RHS (right angle, hypotenuse, side) — to establish that two triangles are exact copies of each other, and then transferring facts between them by CPCTC: corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent. In practice this is the engine of geometric proof: drawing one shared side or one equal pair of angles inside a complex figure, proving the two triangles congruent, and reading off the equal sides, equal angles or midpoint conclusions the figure needs.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine you are a builder at a construction site in Jaipur and you need to verify if two triangular support beams for a roof are exact twins so that one can replace the other. You know their side lengths, but you need to prove whether they match completely before cutting expensive wood. To do this, you start by writing down the given information like side lengths and angles marked on your blueprint diagram. You connect this to what you learned about SSS, SAS, ASA, and RHS criteria for triangle congruence. The key insight is that once two triangles are proven congruent, every single corresponding side and angle must be equal—a principle known as CPCTC (Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent)—allowing you to solve for any unknown measurement.

**Takeaway:** congruence criteria certify twins; CPCTC then transfers any corresponding side or angle. **In the exam:** pick the valid criterion, write the vertex-matched statement, and quote CPCTC for each unknown. **Check yourself:** prove the two beams congruent by SSS, then read off a missing angle by correspondence. **Why students miss it:** they quote CPCTC before establishing congruence, or misread which parts correspond. Write the vertex-matched statement before quoting a single transfer, and the proof reads like a ledger.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often mix up the corresponding order of vertices when applying CPCTC in complex figures. For example, if △ ABC ≅ △ FED, a student will incorrectly equate side BC to side ED instead of side ED matching BC only if the letters align properly, or they will match AB with FD instead of FE.
## ON_FAILURE
Remind the student to trace the triangles on their paper and write the congruence statement letter-by-letter, matching index positions: 1st with 1st, 2nd with 2nd, 3rd with 3rd. Say: "Put your finger on vertex A, then on vertex F. If your congruence statement says △ ABC ≅ △ FED, then AB must pair with FE, not FD." Have them redraw the triangles side-by-side in the exact same orientation before writing CPCTC equations.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: In a local park in Delhi, a triangular flower bed ABC has side AB = 5 m and side AC = 5 m. A path runs from vertex A to the midpoint D on side BC. Prove that △ ABD ≅ △ ACD and find the length of angle ∠ ADB if AD is perpendicular to BC.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the given information in △ ABD and △ ACD: AB = AC = 5 m (given), AD is a common side for both triangles (AD = AD, Reflexive Property), and AD ⊥ BC gives ∠ ADB = ∠ ADC = 90°.
- Step 2: Choose the appropriate congruence criterion. Since we have a right angle, a hypotenuse (AB = AC), and a side (AD = AD), we use the RHS (Right angle-Hypotenuse-Side) congruence criterion.
- Step 3: Write the formal congruence statement matching corresponding vertices: △ ABD ≅ △ ACD by RHS.
- Step 4: Apply CPCTC to determine unknown angle measures. Since ∠ ADB and ∠ ADC form a linear pair along line BC and are equal by CPCTC (∠ ADB = ∠ ADC), we have ∠ ADB + ∠ ADC = 180°, so 2 · ∠ ADB = 180°.
- Step 5: Compute the final angle: ∠ ADB = (180°)/(2) = 90°.
REASONING: We establish congruence using the RHS criterion because the triangles are right-angled with equal hypotenuses and a shared leg. Then, CPCTC ensures that corresponding angles are equal, confirming the perpendicularity condition.
ANSWER: ∠ ADB = 90° and △ ABD ≅ △ ACD
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Two triangular metal plates used in a bridge structure in Mumbai have ∠ B = ∠ E = 90°, hypotenuse AC = DF = 13 cm, and side AB = DE = 5 cm. If side BC is given algebraically as (3x - 1) cm and side EF = 8 cm, find the value of x.

STEPS:
- Step 1: List the given values for △ ABC and △ DEF: ∠ B = ∠ E = 90° (Right angles), hypotenuse AC = DF = 13 cm, and side AB = DE = 5 cm.
- Step 2: Check the congruence condition. We have a right angle, the hypotenuse, and one side equal, so △ ABC ≅ △ DEF by the RHS congruence criterion.
- Step 3: Use CPCTC to equate corresponding sides: side BC corresponds to side EF, therefore BC = EF.
- Step 4: Substitute the algebraic expression and numerical value into the equation: 3x - 1 = 8.
- Step 5: Solve the linear equation: add 1 to both sides giving 3x = 8 + 1 ⇒ 3x = 9, then divide by 3 to get x = 9/3 = 3.
REASONING: After proving triangle congruence via RHS, CPCTC dictates that corresponding legs like BC and EF must be equal. Setting them equal creates a linear equation that we solve using standard algebraic transposition.
ANSWER: x = 3
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A triangular plot of land in Chennai has a fence dividing it from vertex P to a point S on side QR. It is given that PQ = PR and PS bisects angle ∠ QPR. If QS = (2y + 3) m and SR = (3y - 1) m, find the total length of the base QR.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given information for △ PQS and △ PRS: PQ = PR (given), angle ∠ QPS = ∠ RPS (since PS bisects ∠ QPR), and PS = PS (common side, Reflexive Property).
- Step 2: Select the congruence criterion. We have two sides and the included angle equal (PQ = PR, ∠ QPS = ∠ RPS, and PS = PS), so △ PQS ≅ △ PRS by the SAS congruence criterion.
- Step 3: Apply CPCTC to the corresponding base segments: QS = SR.
- Step 4: Substitute the algebraic expressions: 2y + 3 = 3y - 1. Solve for y by subtracting 2y from both sides: 3 = y - 1, then adding 1 gives y = 4.
- Step 5: Substitute y = 4 back into the segment expressions: QS = 2(4) + 3 = 8 + 3 = 11 m, and SR = 3(4) - 1 = 12 - 1 = 11 m. Calculate total base length QR = QS + SR = 11 + 11 = 22 m.
REASONING: SAS congruence proves △ PQS ≅ △ PRS, allowing us to use CPCTC to equate parts QS and SR. Solving the resulting linear equation gives y, which we use to compute individual lengths and sum them for the total base.
ANSWER: Total length QR = 22 m
## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A carpenter builds two triangular wooden roof supports, △ ABC and △ PQR, where AB = PQ, BC = QR, and median AM is equal to median PN. The carpenter claims that because three parts are equal, the triangles must be congruent by SSS. Analyze the carpenter's claim, identify any error in reasoning, and explain the correct approach using congruence.
COMPETENCY: Error identification, reasoning justification, and application of triangle congruence.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Understand the given parts: two sides and a median are equal (AB = PQ, BC = QR, AM = PN).
- Step 2: Analyze the carpenter's claim of SSS: SSS requires all *three full sides* of the triangle to be equal (AB = PQ, BC = QR, and AC = PR). A median is not a full side.
- Step 3: Correct the error: Explain that equality of a median does not directly equal the third side AC and PR without further proof.
- Step 4: Formulate the correct conclusion: To prove congruence, one must first establish congruence of smaller sub-triangles formed by the median (e.g., △ ABM ≅ △ PQN) before applying CPCTC to obtain full side equality.
ANSWER: The carpenter's claim is incorrect because SSS requires all three sides of the triangle to be equal (AC = PR), not a median. The median only helps prove sub-triangle congruence first.
