## ELI5
On Tuesday an empty village square becomes rows of colourful stalls - vegetables, utensils, clothes, spices - and by evening it vanishes. That is a weekly market, the haat: a periodic market set up on a fixed day in a public space, with no permanent shop left behind. Because vendors pay no monthly rent or electricity bills, prices stay low, and villagers get many sellers competing in one place, one day a week.

## DEFINITION
A weekly market, often called a *haat* in rural and semi-urban India, is a traditional periodic market system that is set up on a specific day of the week in a designated public space, bringing together local producers, traders, and rural consumers without permanent brick-and-mortar storefronts.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often assume that because weekly market goods are cheaper, the quality must always be inferior or counterfeit. They fail to understand that lower prices in *haats* result from lower overhead business costs (no permanent rent or electricity) and direct sourcing from local producers, rather than poor product quality.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A clay toy maker attends a weekly *haat* every Thursday. She carries 150 toys in a rented auto-rickshaw. The transport cost is ₹300 for the round trip. She sells each toy for ₹50. Her production cost is ₹20 per toy. Calculate her net profit for that market day.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify the selling price per unit (₹50) and quantity sold (150 toys).
- Step 2: Calculate Gross Revenue by multiplying price and quantity (₹50 × 150 = ₹7,500).
- Step 3: Calculate total production cost by multiplying cost per toy by quantity (₹20 × 150 = ₹3,000) and add transport cost (₹300) to get Total Costs (₹3,000 + ₹300 = ₹3,300).
- Step 4: Subtract Total Costs from Gross Revenue to find Net Profit (₹7,500 - ₹3,300 = ₹4,200).
REASONING: Net profit is the actual financial gain after accounting for all expenses including making the goods and traveling to the periodic market venue.
ANSWER: ₹4,200
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: A spice vendor visits two weekly markets in a week: Tuesday and Friday. At each market, he spends ₹150 on transport and miscellaneous fees. His total revenue across both markets combined is ₹5,000, and his total wholesale cost for the spices sold is ₹3,200. Calculate his total net profit for the week.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify total revenue (₹5,000) and total wholesale cost of goods (₹3,200).
- Step 2: Calculate total transport and fee expenses across both market visits (2 markets × ₹150 = ₹300).
- Step 3: Add wholesale cost of goods and total transport expenses to find Total Expenses (₹3,200 + ₹300 = ₹3,500).
- Step 4: Subtract Total Expenses from total revenue to find weekly net profit (₹5,000 - ₹3,500 = ₹1,500).
REASONING: Operating across multiple weekly markets requires calculating cumulative transport expenses across all market days to find accurate profit.
ANSWER: ₹1,500
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A weaver is deciding whether to sell sarees at a permanent city shop or a weekly rural *haat*. The city shop charges a fixed monthly rent of ₹6,000 and requires daily attendance. The weekly *haat* operates 4 times a month, charging ₹100 per day in ground fees plus ₹200 transport per trip. If the weaver expects to sell 20 sarees a month yielding a net margin (before rent/fees) of ₹400 per saree, which option gives higher monthly profit?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate total profit margin before fixed location fees (20 sarees × ₹400 = ₹8,000).
- Step 2: Calculate total monthly cost for the city shop (Fixed rent = ₹6,000). Net city profit = ₹8,000 - ₹6,000 = ₹2,000.
- Step 3: Calculate total monthly fees for the weekly *haat* (4 trips × [₹100 ground fee + ₹200 transport] = 4 × ₹300 = ₹1,200).
- Step 4: Calculate net *haat* profit (₹8,000 - ₹1,200 = ₹6,800).
- Step 5: Compare both options: ₹6,800 (*haat*) is greater than ₹2,000 (city shop).
REASONING: Low overhead costs in weekly markets make periodic trading much more profitable for small producers selling lower volumes compared to expensive permanent city shops.
ANSWER: Weekly *haat* yields higher profit by ₹4,800.
