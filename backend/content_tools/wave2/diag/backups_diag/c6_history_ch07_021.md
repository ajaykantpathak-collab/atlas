## DEFINITION
The Delhi Sultanate was the first major Islamic empire established in northern India, beginning in 1206 CE when Qutbuddin Aibak declared himself the independent ruler of Delhi. It marked the start of a new political era that would last for over 300 years.

## WALKTHROUGH
Have you ever seen a sports team get a new coach who changes how the whole game is played? Starting in 1206 CE, India experienced a similar change in "leadership" when a former general named Qutbuddin Aibak took charge of Delhi. Think of it like a relay race: Aibak took the baton from his previous leader, Muhammad of Ghur, but instead of continuing to serve someone else, he began his own independent rule. We begin by looking at how a person who was once a slave could rise to become the Sultan, and why this moment is called the "founding" of a new era. By understanding this shift, you realize that history is often shaped by individuals making big decisions at the right moment.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think Qutbuddin Aibak conquered India all by himself as an independent king from the start. They miss the fact that he was a general under Muhammad of Ghur and only became the Sultan *after* his leader's death. This is common because they often simplify history into "one hero" stories rather than seeing the transition of power.
