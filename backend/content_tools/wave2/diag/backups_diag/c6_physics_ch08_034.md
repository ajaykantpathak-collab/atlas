## ELI5
Time is measured in a family tree with the second at the root: minutes and hours are its grown children, and the awkward truth is that the family multiplies by sixty, not ten. Every event takes a slice of time, and picking the right unit is dressing the slice properly - a school walk in hours would be a rumour, not a measurement.

## DEFINITION
Time is a physical quantity used to measure the duration of events or the interval between them, with the 'second' serving as the basic SI unit. Larger and smaller units are derived from the second to allow for convenient measurement of durations ranging from microseconds to centuries.

Just like building a tower with bricks, we stack 60 seconds to make a minute, and 60 of those minutes to make an hour. When you need to measure something very fast, like a camera shutter clicking, we look at smaller parts of a second, such as milliseconds. The key insight is that because time is measured in consistent intervals, we can always use multiplication or division to switch between these units.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often assume that all time conversions use a factor of 10, similar to the metric system for length (10 mm = 1 cm). They might incorrectly calculate 100 seconds in a minute because they are applying the decimal system rules to time, which uses a base-60 system for minutes and hours.
## ON_FAILURE

Ask them to physically count 60 seconds with a stopwatch to see that it takes a "full minute," reinforcing that the conversion factor is 60, not 10.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A student takes 2 hours and 15 minutes to complete their homework. How many minutes did they spend in total?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given time: 2 hours and 15 minutes.
- step 2: Convert hours to minutes using the rule 1 hour = 60 minutes.
- step 3: Calculate: 2 hours × 60 minutes/hour = 120 minutes.
- step 4: Add the extra minutes: 120 minutes + 15 minutes = 135 minutes.
- step 5: Conclude the total time is 135 minutes.
REASONING: We convert all parts of the time into the same unit (minutes) before adding them to ensure the total is accurate.
ANSWER: 135 minutes
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: An express train travels from one city to another in 300 minutes. How many hours is this?
STEPS:
- step 1: Identify given time: 300 minutes.
- step 2: Recall the rule that 60 minutes = 1 hour.
- step 3: Set up division: 300 ÷ 60.
- step 4: Perform the calculation: 300 / 60 = 5.
- step 5: The total duration is 5 hours.
REASONING: Since we are moving from a smaller unit (minutes) to a larger unit (hours), we divide by the conversion factor.
ANSWER: 5 hours
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A light flashes every 2 seconds. How many times will it flash in 10 minutes?
STEPS:
- step 1: Convert the total time (10 minutes) into seconds: 10 × 60 = 600 seconds.
- step 2: Identify the interval: 2 seconds per flash.
- step 3: Divide total time by interval: 600 ÷ 2.
- step 4: Compute: 300 flashes.
- step 5: Verify if the interval logic holds: If it starts at 0, it flashes 301 times, but usually, we calculate intervals in a duration.
REASONING: By converting the duration to the same unit as the interval (seconds), we can easily calculate the frequency of the event.
ANSWER: 300 flashes
