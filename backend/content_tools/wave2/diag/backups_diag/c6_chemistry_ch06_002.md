## ELI5
Leave muddy water standing and watch the mud sink — that quiet settling is sedimentation. Pour the clear water off gently without waking the mud: decantation. Fine dust still floats, so pass the water through a cloth or filter paper that traps the particles and lets clear water drip: filtration. Salt water defeats all three, because the salt is not floating — it is dissolved, and no filter will ever catch it. Only evaporation works: boil the water away and the salt stays behind in the vessel. And churning pulls butter out of curd, beaten until the fat lumps together and floats. The sorting rule: settle the heavy, decant the clear, filter the fine, evaporate the dissolved.

## DEFINITION
Fine or dissolved mixtures need methods beyond the hand. Sedimentation lets heavier insoluble particles settle at the bottom; decantation gently pours off the clear liquid above the sediment; filtration passes the mixture through a filter (cloth or filter paper) that traps solid particles; evaporation boils the liquid away to leave a dissolved solid behind; and churning separates butter from curd by agitation.

## WALKTHROUGH
Take a glass of muddy water and just leave it alone — watch the mud slowly sink and collect at the bottom. That quiet settling is sedimentation. Once the mud has settled, pour the clear water gently into another glass without disturbing the bottom — that careful pour is decantation. Some fine dust is still floating, so pass the water through a clean cloth or a piece of filter paper — the cloth traps the particles and clear water drips through; that is filtration. Now the tricky one — salt water. The salt is not floating in the water, it is dissolved in it, so no settling or filtering will ever catch it. The only way is evaporation: boil the water so it escapes as steam and the salt is left behind in the vessel. And churning is how butter is pulled out of curd — the curd is beaten until the fat lumps together and floats. The rule that sorts them all: visible big pieces → hand or sieve; heavy particles that settle → sediment + decant; fine suspended particles → filter; dissolved solid → evaporate.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think filtration removes everything — even dissolved salt. A filter only catches insoluble particles; anything truly dissolved passes straight through, which is why salt water needs evaporation instead. A related error: thinking decantation is the same as filtering — decanting pours off liquid gently without any barrier.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A pot of rice water holds settled starch grains and dissolved salt. How do you separate both?
STEPS:
- 1: Let it stand — the starch grains settle (sedimentation), then pour off the liquid (decantation).
- 2: The poured liquid still holds dissolved salt — boil it so the water evaporates and the salt stays behind.
REASONING: Settled particles and dissolved solids need different methods — decantation for the settled, evaporation for the dissolved.
ANSWER: Sedimentation and decantation remove the starch grains; evaporation recovers the dissolved salt.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Why does a tea strainer stop the leaves but let the dissolved tea colour through?
STEPS:
- 1: Identify what each part is — leaves are undissolved solids; the tea colour is dissolved in the water.
- 2: Apply the filter rule — a strainer catches only undissolved particles.
- 3: Conclude — leaves are trapped; dissolved colour passes through.
REASONING: Filtration removes insoluble particles only — dissolved substances always pass.
ANSWER: The leaves are insoluble so the strainer traps them; the tea colour is dissolved so it flows through.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: Sea water is left in a shallow open pan under the sun. Describe what happens and what remains.
STEPS:
- 1: Sun heats the water — evaporation begins.
- 2: The water slowly turns to vapour and leaves; the dissolved salt cannot escape.
- 3: What is left is a crust of salt.
REASONING: Evaporation removes the solvent, leaving the dissolved solid behind — this is exactly how sea salt is made.
ANSWER: The water evaporates under the sun and a layer of salt is left behind — the dissolved solid separated from its solvent.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1
QUESTION: A friend wants to "filter the salt out of saltwater." Explain why the filter fails and what method would work, connecting to the difference between dissolved and suspended particles.
COMPETENCY: Choosing between filtration and evaporation by particle state.
STEPS:
- 1: Identify the salt's state — dissolved, not suspended.
- 2: Test filtration — dissolved particles are too small for any mesh, so it passes through.
- 3: Offer the working method — evaporation boils off the water and leaves the salt.
- 4: Conclude the state of the mixture decides the method.
ANSWER: Filtering fails because dissolved salt is too fine to trap — evaporation is needed, because only heat can separate a dissolved solid from its water.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: Design a full clean-up for a jug of muddy salty water so you get clean water and dry salt — list the steps in order and name each method.
COMPETENCY: Sequencing multiple separations to fully resolve a complex mixture.
STEPS:
- 1: Stand the jug — mud settles (sedimentation).
- 2: Pour off the clear salty water (decantation).
- 3: Pass it through filter paper to catch fine particles (filtration) → clean salty water.
- 4: Boil the filtrate — water evaporates, salt remains (evaporation) → clean salt and (if condensed) water.
REASONING: Each stage removes a different component — mud by settling, fine dust by filtering, salt by evaporation.
ANSWER: Sedimentation → decantation → filtration → evaporation — settle the mud, pour off the clear water, filter the fines, then boil to recover the salt.
