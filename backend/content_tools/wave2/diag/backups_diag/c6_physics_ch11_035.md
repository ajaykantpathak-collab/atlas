## ELI5
Light has authors and mirrors: the sun, the stars and the firefly write their own glow, while bulbs, torches and lamps are human-made authors. The moon, however bright, is only a reader - bouncing sunlight for all to see. A mirror is a reader too. The test is simple: does it glow of itself, or borrow?

## DEFINITION
Sources of light are the objects that make light of their own, and they come in two families. Natural sources need no human hand: the Sun — the greatest of all — the stars, fireflies, glow-worms, and the rare glow of some deep-sea fish and fungi. Artificial sources are made by us: the electric bulb and tube-light, the candle and oil lamp, the torch's LED, fireworks. Light itself is a form of energy that travels out from these sources and makes vision possible; objects that do not produce it — the moon, a book, this page — are only visible because they reflect some other source's light into our eyes.

The key insight is that natural sources exist independently of human intervention, whereas artificial sources require human creation or activation.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often categorize the Moon as a "natural source of light." They see it glowing in the night sky and assume it produces its own light like a star. This stems from a confusion between objects that reflect light and objects that emit light.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: You have a list of items: (A) A burning matchstick, (B) A glowing star, (C) A polished metal spoon under a bulb, (D) A firefly. Identify which are sources of light and classify them as natural or artificial.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Check if the object emits its own light.
- Step 2: Matchstick (Yes), Star (Yes), Spoon (No), Firefly (Yes).
- Step 3: Classify: Matchstick (Artificial), Star (Natural), Firefly (Natural).
- Step 4: Spoon is not a source; it is a reflector.
REASONING: A source must generate light. If it is human-made, it is artificial; if it is biological or celestial, it is natural.
ANSWER: Sources are A (Artificial), B (Natural), D (Natural). C is not a source.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Identify if the light coming from a movie projector screen is a natural or artificial source.
STEPS:
- Step 1: Does the screen produce the light?
- Step 2: No, the projector behind or in front of the screen produces the light.
- Step 3: Is the projector human-made? Yes.
- Step 4: Is the projector a source? Yes, an artificial one.
- Step 5: The screen is just a surface for reflection.
REASONING: Distinguishing the source (projector) from the surface (screen) is vital for understanding light emission vs. reflection.
ANSWER: The projector is an artificial source of light.
## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: A student argues that because they can see a book in a dark room using a flashlight, the book is a source of light. How do you correct this?
STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify what is actually emitting light (the flashlight).
- Step 2: Identify what is receiving light (the book).
- Step 3: Define a source as an emitter of light energy.
- Step 4: Apply the definition: The book is not emitting, therefore it is not a source.
- Step 5: Conclude the student is confusing "visibility" with "emission."
REASONING: We see objects because light reflects off them; seeing an object does not make it a source.
ANSWER: The book is not a source; it is a non-luminous object being illuminated by an artificial source (the flashlight).
