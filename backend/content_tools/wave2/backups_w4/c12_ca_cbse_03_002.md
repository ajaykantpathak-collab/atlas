## ELI5
The family firm buys a CNC machine with installation charges — how much of that cost should each of the next ten years absorb? Straight-line depreciation splits it evenly, year after year; written-down value charges more early, less later, on a shrinking base. The spreadsheet functions do the arithmetic; your job is the policy — cost, salvage, life — locked in absolute references so the schedule regenerates itself every March without retyping.

## DEFINITION
Asset Accounting in computerised accounting refers to the systematic process of recording, tracking, and computing depreciation on fixed assets (tangible and intangible) using spreadsheet functions like SLN (Straight Line Method) and DB (Written Down Value Method) to generate depreciation schedules and fixed asset registers for financial reporting.

## COGNITIVE_LEVEL
apply

## MASTERY_CRITERIA
The student must be able to: (1) Design a spreadsheet template for asset depreciation using SLN and DB functions with correct absolute/relative cell references, (2) Compute acquisition cost, salvage value, useful life, and period-specific depreciation for both SLM and WDV methods, (3) Prepare a Schedule of Fixed Assets forming part of the Balance Sheet showing Gross Block, Accumulated Depreciation, and Net Block.

## SCOPE_BOUNDARY
This node covers: depreciation computation using Excel's SLN and DB functions, asset classification per Companies Act Schedule XIV, preparation of depreciation calculation sheets and fixed asset schedules for Balance Sheet reporting. It does NOT cover: impairment testing, revaluation of assets, deferred tax implications, or advanced IFRS/Ind AS treatments beyond Class 12 NCERT scope.

## WALKTHROUGH
Imagine your family's small manufacturing business just purchased a ₹15 lakh CNC machine with ₹2 lakh installation costs — how do you decide how much of this cost to expense each year for the next 10 years, and how would you automate this in Excel so the accountant doesn't recalculate manually every March? Start by listing the asset's acquisition cost (purchase + installation + pre-operative expenses), salvage value, and useful life. Then learn the two depreciation methods: SLM charges equal amounts yearly (use SLN function), while WDV charges declining amounts on reducing book value (use DB function). Build a spreadsheet where changing the asset parameters automatically updates the entire depreciation schedule. The key insight: absolute references ($G$3) lock policy rates (salvage, life) while relative references flow down rows for each asset — this mirrors how real businesses maintain asset registers with hundreds of entries.

Takeaway: mastery means both depreciation methods computed and the fixed-asset schedule assembled from them. Exam moves: template questions place SLN and DB with correct reference locking; parameter questions distinguish acquisition cost, salvage, life, and period per method; schedule questions build the gross-block, accumulated-depreciation, net-block statement. Method: decide the policy first — even or declining — then let the function annualise it; never let the function choose the policy. Drill: one machine, two methods, one register. The accountant's March is automated only as far as the references are correct.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
What are the three components that constitute the 'Acquisition Cost' of a fixed asset for depreciation calculation purposes?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Acquisition Cost = Purchase Price + Installation Expenses + Pre-operating Expenses. A wrong answer (e.g., omitting installation or pre-operative costs) reveals the student treats 'cost' as only the invoice price, ignoring AS-10/Ind AS 16 principle that all costs necessary to bring the asset to working condition must be capitalised. This error propagates into understated depreciation and overstated profits.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why the SLN function in Excel requires an absolute reference for the salvage value cell but a relative reference for the asset cost cell when the formula is copied down a column of multiple assets.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Salvage value and useful life are policy constants (same for all assets of a class), so they must be locked with $ (e.g., $H$5) to prevent shifting when copied. Asset cost varies per row, so it uses relative reference (e.g., G5) to pick up each asset's unique cost. A student who uses relative for salvage will get wrong depreciation for rows 2+ as the formula pulls empty/incorrect cells — this is the #1 spreadsheet error in board practical exams.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A machine was purchased on 15-Oct-2022 for ₹8,00,000 with ₹50,000 installation. Salvage value is ₹50,000, useful life 8 years. Using SLM, compute the depreciation for FY 2022-23 (ending 31-Mar-2023) assuming the company follows the 'half-year convention' for assets installed after 30-Sep.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Step 1: Acquisition Cost = 8,00,000 + 50,000 = ₹8,50,000. Step 2: Depreciable Amount = 8,50,000 - 50,000 = ₹8,00,000. Step 3: Annual SLM Depreciation = 8,00,000 / 8 = ₹1,00,000. Step 4: Half-year convention → 6 months depreciation = 1,00,000 × 6/12 = ₹50,000. A wrong answer (₹1,00,000) shows the student ignored the installation date and pro-rata rule — a frequent board exam trap where 'date of installation' in column C triggers the IF(MONTH>3) logic in the WDV period column.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the depreciation expense pattern over 5 years for an asset costing ₹10,00,000 (salvage ₹1,00,000, life 5 years) under SLM vs WDV. Which method front-loads expense and why does this matter for tax planning?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
SLM: ₹1,80,000 each year (constant). WDV: Year 1 ~₹3,72,000, Year 2 ~₹2,33,000, Year 3 ~₹1,46,000, Year 4 ~₹91,000, Year 5 ~₹57,000 (declining). WDV front-loads expense because it applies a fixed rate to a reducing base. This matters for tax: higher early depreciation → lower taxable profit → deferred tax liability. A student who says 'SLM front-loads' confuses the methods; one who cannot explain 'why' lacks conceptual link to time value of money (cross-subject: finance).

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students believe the DB function's 'Period' argument (column J in Figure 3.7) is simply the current year number (1, 2, 3...). In reality, for assets installed after March, Period = (Current Year - Install Year) + 1 because the first partial year counts as Period 1. Example: Asset installed 15-Jun-2022. For FY 2022-23 (ending 31-Mar-2023), Period = 1 (not 0). For FY 2023-24, Period = 2. The formula IF(MONTH(Inst_Date)>3, YEAR(Year_End)-YEAR(Inst_Date), YEAR(Year_End)-YEAR(Inst_Date)+1) in Figure 3.7 column J implements this. Students who hardcode '1,2,3' get correct Year 1 but wrong Year 2+ depreciation — a silent error that compounds in the fixed asset schedule.

## THIS_FORMULA
SLM Annual Depreciation = (Acquisition Cost - Salvage Value) / Useful Life; WDV Depreciation for Period t = DB(Cost, Salvage, Life, Period, [Month])

## FORMULA_VARIABLES
Acquisition Cost = Purchase Price + Installation Expenses + Pre-operating Expenses (₹); Salvage Value = Estimated realisable value at end of useful life (₹); Useful Life = Expected productive life in years (years); Period = Year number for which depreciation is computed (1, 2, 3...); Month = Months in first year (1-12, defaults to 12)

## FORMULA_UNITS
All monetary values in Indian Rupees (₹); Life and Period in years; Month in months (integer 1-12)

## COMPUTATION_TYPE

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: M/s Precision Tools purchased a CNC Lathe on 20-July-2023 for ₹12,00,000. Transportation cost ₹40,000, installation ₹60,000, pre-operative testing ₹25,000. Estimated salvage value ₹1,25,000 after 10 years useful life. Compute depreciation for FY 2023-24 (ending 31-Mar-2024) using Straight Line Method with pro-rata for partial year.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Calculate Acquisition Cost = Purchase Price + Transportation + Installation + Pre-operative = 12,00,000 + 40,000 + 60,000 + 25,000 = ₹13,25,000.
- Step 2: Calculate Depreciable Amount = Acquisition Cost - Salvage Value = 13,25,000 - 1,25,000 = ₹12,00,000.
- Step 3: Calculate Annual SLM Depreciation = Depreciable Amount / Useful Life = 12,00,000 / 10 = ₹1,20,000 per year.
- Step 4: Determine months of usage in FY 2023-24: Installed 20-Jul-2023 to 31-Mar-2024 = 8.5 months (Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec, Jan, Feb, half Mar). As per convention, round to 9 months.
- Step 5: Compute Pro-rata Depreciation = Annual Depreciation × (Months Used / 12) = 1,20,000 × (9/12) = ₹90,000.
REASONING: SLM allocates depreciable cost equally across each year of useful life. Since the asset was not available for the full year, we proportionally allocate based on months in use — this matches the NOEDP (Number of Effective Days Present) logic from Payroll Accounting, applied to asset life instead of employee attendance.
ANSWER: ₹90,000 depreciation for FY 2023-24.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Using the same CNC Lathe from Example 1 (Acquisition Cost ₹13,25,000, Salvage ₹1,25,000, Life 10 years), compute depreciation for FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 using Written Down Value Method. Assume the WDV rate derived from Schedule XIV is 25.89% (approximate). Show how the DB function parameters map to this calculation.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify DB function parameters: Cost = 13,25,000 (G5), Salvage = 1,25,000 (H5), Life = 10 (I5), Period = 1 for FY 2023-24 (J5), Month = months in first year = 9 (K5, from Jul-Mar).
- Step 2: Compute FY 2023-24 (Period 1) depreciation using DB formula: DB(1325000, 125000, 10, 1, 9). Excel calculates this as: Rate = 1 - (Salvage/Cost)^(1/Life) = 1 - (125000/1325000)^0.1 ≈ 0.2589. First year depreciation = Cost × Rate × (Month/12) = 13,25,000 × 0.2589 × (9/12) ≈ ₹2,57,500.
- Step 3: Book Value at end of FY 2023-24 = 13,25,000 - 2,57,500 = ₹10,67,500.
- Step 4: For FY 2024-25 (Period 2), Month = 12 (full year). DB(1325000, 125000, 10, 2, 12) = (Cost - Accumulated Depreciation) × Rate = 10,67,500 × 0.2589 ≈ ₹2,76,400.
- Step 5: Verify accumulated depreciation after 2 years = 2,57,500 + 2,76,400 = ₹5,33,900. Net Book Value = 13,25,000 - 5,33,900 = ₹7,91,100.
REASONING: WDV applies a constant rate to a declining book value. The DB function automates the rate derivation (1 - (Salvage/Cost)^(1/Life)) and handles the partial first year via the Month parameter. The Period argument simply selects which year's depreciation to return — Period 1 = first year (partial), Period 2 = second year (full), etc. This is why the IF(MONTH>3) logic in Figure 3.7 column J correctly computes Period.
ANSWER: FY 2023-24 Depreciation ≈ ₹2,57,500; FY 2024-25 Depreciation ≈ ₹2,76,400.

| Asset | Opening Gross Block (1-Apr-23) | Additions during year | Deductions during year | Opening Accum. Depreciation (1-Apr-23) | Depreciation for year (from DB/SLN sheet) | Deductions from Depreciation |

Plant & Machinery: 48,00,000 + 18,50,000 - 3,00,000 = 63,50,000. Furniture: 5,40,000 + 1,80,000 - 0 = 7,20,000. Vehicles: 14,00,000 + 5,25,000 - 4,80,000 = 14,45,000.

Plant & Machinery: 1,20,00,000 - 48,00,000 = 72,00,000. Furniture: 12,00,000 - 5,40,000 = 6,60,000. Vehicles: 35,00,000 - 14,00,000 = 21,00,000.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: M/s TechNova Ltd. is deciding between SLM and WDV for a new server farm costing ₹5,00,00,000 (salvage ₹50,00,000, life 10 years). The CFO argues: "WDV gives higher depreciation in early years, reducing taxable profit and saving tax now — so WDV is always better." As the spreadsheet analyst, evaluate this claim. Build a 5-year comparison table showing: Annual Depreciation (SLM vs WDV), Tax Shield @ 25% (Depreciation × 25%), Present Value of Tax Shield at 10% discount rate. Conclude whether WDV is 'always better' and identify the hidden assumption in the CFO's argument.

COMPETENCY: Comparing and justifying financial decisions using spreadsheet analysis
STEPS:
- Step 1: Compute SLM Annual Depreciation = (5,00,00,000 - 50,00,000) / 10 = ₹45,00,000 constant for 10 years.
- Step 2: Compute WDV Rate = 1 - (50,00,000/5,00,00,000)^(1/10) = 1 - 0.1^0.1 ≈ 1 - 0.7943 = 0.2057 ≈ 20.57%. Year 1 Dep = 5,00,00,000 × 0.2057 = ₹1,02,85,000. Year 2 = (5,00,00,000 - 1,02,85,000) × 0.2057 = ₹81,69,000. Year 3 = ₹64,89,000. Year 4 = ₹51,54,000. Year 5 = ₹40,94,000.
- Step 3: Tax Shield each year = Depreciation × 25%. SLM: ₹11,25,000 each year. WDV: Y1 ₹25,71,250, Y2 ₹20,42,250, Y3 ₹16,22,250, Y4 ₹12,88,500, Y5 ₹10,23,500.
- Step 4: PV of Tax Shield at 10%: SLM = 11,25,000 × [1/1.1 + 1/1.1² + ... + 1/1.1⁵] = 11,25,000 × 3.7908 = ₹42,64,650. WDV = 25,71,250/1.1 + 20,42,250/1.1² + 16,22,250/1.1³ + 12,88,500/1.1⁴ + 10,23,500/1.1⁵ = 23,37,500 + 16,87,800 + 12,18,500 + 8,81,200 + 6,35,600 = ₹67,60,600.
- Step 5: WDV PV of tax shield (₹67.6L) > SLM (₹42.6L) — so WDV IS better for tax deferral. BUT the CFO's 'always better' is wrong because: (a) WDV lowers reported profit → may violate debt covenants or affect bonus linked to profit, (b) If tax rates are expected to rise, SLM's later shields become more valuable, (c) WDV never fully depreciates to salvage (residual book value > salvage), creating disposal gain/loss complexity.
ANSWER: WDV provides higher PV of tax shield (₹67.6L vs ₹42.6L) due to front-loading, supporting the CFO's tax deferral argument. However, 'always better' is false — the hidden assumption is that tax deferral is the only objective, ignoring financial reporting impact, covenant compliance, and future tax rate changes. A complete analysis requires multi-criteria evaluation, not just tax PV.
DIFFICULTY: hard
