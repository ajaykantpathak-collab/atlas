## ELI5
The classroom periodic table hides two numbers in every box: an integer counting protons - the atomic number that names the element - and a decimal averaging its isotopes. The mass number tallies all nucleons, neutrons being whatever remains after the protons. Helium stays helium however many neutrons you add; only the proton count christens an element. Isotopes differ in neutrons, isobars in identity, isotones in neither.

## DEFINITION
The atomic number (Z) of an element is the number of protons in its nucleus, which determines the element's chemical identity and its position in the periodic table. The mass number (A) is the total number of nucleons (protons + neutrons) in the nucleus. A nuclide is represented as \(^A_Z X\), where X is the chemical symbol. The neutron number N = A - Z. The atomic mass unit (u) is defined as 1/12th the mass of a carbon-12 atom: 1 u = 1.660539 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.

## WALKTHROUGH
Look at a periodic table on your classroom wall — every box shows an integer (atomic number) and a decimal (average atomic mass). The integer is the proton count, which fixes the element's identity. The decimal is a weighted average of isotopic masses. Now imagine a helium balloon: the helium-4 nucleus has 2 protons and 2 neutrons (A=4). If you could magically add a neutron, you'd get helium-3? No, that would be helium-3 (2 protons, 1 neutron). The key is that changing neutron number creates isotopes of the same element. Start by writing the nuclide symbol for carbon-12: \(^{12}_6 C\). Identify Z=6, A=12, N=6. Then do the same for carbon-13 and carbon-14 to see the pattern. This leads to the general relations A = Z + N and the definitions of isotopes/isobars/isotones.

Takeaway first: Z is the element's name, A its weight class, and N the difference - three integers that sort every nucleus in existence. Exam moves: nuclide-symbol questions reading Z, A and N, arithmetic questions on the sum rule, and distinction questions pairing isotopes, isobars and isotones with examples. Method: rerun the carbons - twelve, thirteen, fourteen - and watch Z hold while A and N climb. Check yourself: isobars share mass number with different Z; isotones share neutron number with different A - keep the three -tones' definitions in separate pockets. Drill: decode three nuclide symbols and classify a given list into isotopes, isobars and isotones.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_1
State the definition of atomic mass unit (u) and give its value in kg. What is the atomic number and mass number of a neutral atom of \(^{23}_{11} Na\)?

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_1
Atomic mass unit (u) = 1/12th the mass of a carbon-12 atom = 1.660539 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. For \(^{23}_{11} Na\): atomic number Z = 11, mass number A = 23. A wrong answer (e.g., confusing u with kg, or swapping Z and A) reveals the student has not internalized the definition of u or the nuclide notation convention (superscript = A, subscript = Z).

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_2
Explain why chlorine has an atomic mass of 35.46 u even though no single chlorine atom has that exact mass. Use the terms isotopes and weighted average in your explanation.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_2
Chlorine exists as two stable isotopes: \(^{35}_{17} Cl\) (75.4% abundance, mass ≈ 34.98 u) and \(^{37}_{17} Cl\) (24.6% abundance, mass ≈ 36.98 u). The quoted atomic mass (35.46 u) is the weighted average: (0.754 × 34.98) + (0.246 × 36.98) ≈ 35.46 u. No individual atom has this mass. A wrong answer (e.g., "chlorine atoms have fractional neutrons") reveals the misconception that atomic mass is the mass of a single atom rather than an average over a natural sample.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_3
A nuclide has 26 protons and 30 neutrons. Write its nuclide symbol. If its atomic mass is 55.934939 u, calculate its mass defect in u using proton mass = 1.00727 u and neutron mass = 1.00866 u. (Do not convert to kg or energy yet.)

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_3
Nuclide symbol: \(^{56}_{26} Fe\). Mass of constituents = 26 × 1.00727 u + 30 × 1.00866 u = 26.18902 u + 30.2598 u = 56.44882 u. Mass defect ΔM = 56.44882 u - 55.934939 u = 0.513881 u. A wrong answer (e.g., using electron mass, or subtracting in reverse order) shows confusion about what constitutes the nucleus (protons + neutrons only) and the definition of mass defect (constituents minus actual nucleus).

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_4
Compare the following pairs and classify each as isotopes, isobars, or isotones: (a) \(^{14}_6 C\) and \(^{14}_7 N\); (b) \(^{12}_6 C\) and \(^{13}_6 C\); (c) \(^{40}_{18} Ar\) and \(^{40}_{20} Ca\); (d) \(^{14}_6 C\) and \(^{15}_7 N\). Explain your reasoning for each.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_4
(1) Pair one — carbon-14 and nitrogen-14 are isobars, sharing the mass number fourteen with different atomic numbers, (2) Pair two — carbon-12 and carbon-13 are isotopes, the same atomic number six with different mass numbers, (3) Pair three — argon-40 and calcium-40 are isobars, again equal mass numbers with different atomic numbers, (4) Pair four — carbon-14 and nitrogen-15 are isotones, each holding eight neutrons, (5) The rule — isotopes share the proton count, isobars share the mass number and isotones share the neutron count, so both parameters must be checked before classifying. A wrong answer such as calling the first pair isotopes matches only one parameter without checking the other.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_5
A student writes: "The mass number of an element is the weighted average of its isotopic masses." Evaluate this statement. Is it correct? If not, correct it and explain the difference between mass number and atomic mass.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_5
The statement is incorrect. Mass number (A) is an integer: the total number of protons plus neutrons in a specific nucleus. Atomic mass (or atomic weight) is the weighted average of the naturally occurring isotopic masses, expressed in u, and is usually not an integer. For example, carbon has mass numbers 12, 13, 14 for its isotopes, but its atomic mass is 12.011 u. Confusing these two is a classic board-exam error that leads to wrong answers in binding energy calculations.

## DIAGNOSTIC_QUESTION_6
Design a simple card-sorting activity for Class 11 students to help them distinguish between isotopes, isobars, and isotones. Specify what information each card should show, how many cards you would make, and the sorting rules.

## DIAGNOSTIC_ANSWER_6
Create cards each showing a nuclide symbol \(^A_Z X\) and its neutron number N. Make 12 cards: 4 isotopes of oxygen (Z=8, A=16,17,18,19), 4 isobars with A=40 (Ar, K, Ca, Sc), 4 isotones with N=20 (e.g., \(^{36}_{16} S\), \(^{38}_{18} Ar\), \(^{40}_{20} Ca\), \(^{42}_{22} Ti\)). Sorting rule: Group by same Z → isotopes; same A → isobars; same N → isotones. A poor activity (e.g., cards missing N, or only showing element names) fails to make the neutron number visible, which is essential for identifying isotones.

## COMMON_MISCONCEPTION
Students often think the atomic number Z equals the number of neutrons, or that the mass number A is the atomic mass in u. They write "Z = number of neutrons" and "A = atomic mass" in board exams. This stems from confusing the subscript (Z) with the neutron count and the superscript (A) with the decimal atomic mass printed on the periodic table. In reality, Z = proton count, N = neutron count, A = Z + N (integer), while atomic mass is a weighted average in u.

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_1
PROBLEM: A sample of naturally occurring magnesium contains three isotopes: Mg-24 (78.99% abundance, mass = 23.98504 u), Mg-25 (10.00% abundance, mass = 24.98584 u), Mg-26 (11.01% abundance, mass = 25.98259 u). Calculate the average atomic mass of magnesium. Verify that the result matches the periodic table value (24.305 u).

STEPS:
- Step 1: Identify given data — three isotopes with mass numbers 24, 25, 26; their isotopic masses in u and fractional abundances (convert % to fraction: 0.7899, 0.1000, 0.1101).
- Step 2: Recall formula for average atomic mass = Σ (fractional abundance × isotopic mass).
- Step 3: Compute contributions: (0.7899 × 23.98504 u) + (0.1000 × 24.98584 u) + (0.1101 × 25.98259 u).
- Step 4: Calculate each term: 18.9466 u + 2.4986 u + 2.8607 u = 24.3059 u.
- Step 5: Round to four significant figures: 24.31 u (periodic table value 24.305 u; difference due to rounding of input abundances/masses). State final answer with unit.

REASONING: The average atomic mass is a weighted average because a macroscopic sample contains a mixture of isotopes in fixed natural proportions. The calculation mirrors the chlorine example in NCERT. The result is not an integer because it is an average of different integer-mass isotopes.

ANSWER: 24.31 u

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_2
PROBLEM: Write the nuclide symbols for the following: (a) An isotope of uranium with 143 neutrons. (b) An isobar of \(^{40}_{18} Ar\) with 19 protons. (c) An isotone of \(^{14}_6 C\) with 8 protons. Identify the element name in each case.

STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall definitions — isotope: same Z, different N; isobar: same A, different Z; isotone: same N, different Z.
- Step 2: (a) Uranium has Z = 92. Given N = 143, so A = Z + N = 92 + 143 = 235. Symbol: \(^{235}_{92} U\). Element: Uranium-235.
- Step 3: (b) Isobar of Ar-40 means A = 40. Given Z = 19, so N = A - Z = 40 - 19 = 21. Element with Z=19 is potassium (K). Symbol: \(^{40}_{19} K\).
- Step 4: (c) Isotone of C-14 means same N. For C-14, N = A - Z = 14 - 6 = 8. Given Z = 8, so A = Z + N = 8 + 8 = 16. Element with Z=8 is oxygen (O). Symbol: \(^{16}_{8} O\).
- Step 5: Verify each symbol follows \(^A_Z X\) format and that A, Z, N are consistent.

REASONING: Each part tests a different classification (isotope, isobar, isotone) but all rely on the fundamental relation A = Z + N and the periodic table to find element symbols from Z. This is a common board exam pattern.

ANSWER: (a) \(^{235}_{92} U\) (Uranium-235); (b) \(^{40}_{19} K\) (Potassium-40); (c) \(^{16}_{8} O\) (Oxygen-16)

## WORKED_EXAMPLE_3
PROBLEM: The mass of a \(^{56}_{26} Fe\) nucleus is 55.934939 u. Calculate its mass defect in u and in kg. Then compute the binding energy in MeV using 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c². (Given: m_p = 1.00727 u, m_n = 1.00866 u)

STEPS:
- Step 1: Determine constituents: Z = 26 protons, N = A - Z = 56 - 26 = 30 neutrons.
- Step 2: Calculate total mass of free nucleons: (26 × 1.00727 u) + (30 × 1.00866 u) = 26.18902 u + 30.25980 u = 56.44882 u.
- Step 3: Mass defect ΔM = mass of constituents - nuclear mass = 56.44882 u - 55.934939 u = 0.513881 u.
- Step 4: Convert ΔM to kg: 0.513881 u × 1.660539 × 10⁻²⁷ kg/u = 8.533 × 10⁻²⁸ kg.
- Step 5: Binding energy E_b = ΔM × 931.5 MeV/c² = 0.513881 × 931.5 MeV = 478.7 MeV.
- Step 6: Binding energy per nucleon = 478.7 MeV / 56 = 8.55 MeV/nucleon. Check reasonableness: for A=56, B.E./nucleon ≈ 8.7 MeV (close to peak of curve).

REASONING: This example bridges to the next node (Binding Energy). It uses the composition (Z, N) from this node to compute mass defect, then applies E=mc² via the conversion factor 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c². The arithmetic is multi-step with careful unit tracking.

ANSWER: Mass defect = 0.5139 u = 8.53 × 10⁻²⁸ kg; Binding energy = 478.7 MeV; Binding energy per nucleon = 8.55 MeV/nucleon.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_1

COMPETENCY: Interpreting physical situations using formulas
STEPS:
- Step 1: Recall definition of isobars: same mass number A, different atomic number Z.
- Step 2: For Ar-40: Z=18, A=40 → N = 40 - 18 = 22 neutrons.
- Step 3: For Ca-40: Z=20, A=40 → N = 40 - 20 = 20 neutrons.
- Step 4: Compare: 22 ≠ 20. The student is incorrect.
- Step 5: State correct relationship: Isobars have same A but different Z, so their neutron numbers differ by exactly the difference in Z (here ΔZ = 2, ΔN = -2).

ANSWER: The student is incorrect. Ar-40 has 22 neutrons, Ca-40 has 20 neutrons. Isobars have the same total nucleons (A) but different proton numbers (Z), so their neutron numbers must differ to keep A constant.

## COMPETENCY_QUESTION_2
QUESTION: In a mass spectrometer, singly ionized atoms of two isotopes of element X are accelerated through the same potential difference V and then enter a uniform magnetic field B. They strike the detector at positions separated by distance Δx. The lighter isotope has mass number A₁ = 35 and the heavier has A₂ = 37. Assuming the ions have the same charge +e and their masses are proportional to their mass numbers, derive an expression for the ratio of their radii of curvature r₂/r₁ in the magnetic field. If Δx = 2.0 cm and r₁ = 10.0 cm, find r₂.

COMPETENCY: Predicting outcomes from changing variables
STEPS:
- Step 1: Kinetic energy gained = qV = (1/2)mv² → v = √(2qV/m).
- Step 2: In magnetic field, centripetal force = magnetic force: mv²/r = qvB → r = mv/(qB).
- Step 3: Substitute v: r = m/qB × √(2qV/m) = √(2mV/(qB²)). So r ∝ √m.
- Step 4: Since mass ∝ mass number A, r₂/r₁ = √(A₂/A₁) = √(37/35) = √1.05714 ≈ 1.0282.
- Step 5: Given r₁ = 10.0 cm, r₂ = 1.0282 × 10.0 cm = 10.28 cm.
- Step 6: Separation Δx = r₂ - r₁ = 0.28 cm. But problem states Δx = 2.0 cm? Contradiction? Wait: Δx is the separation on the detector after a half-circle? Actually, in a mass spectrometer, ions complete a semicircle and hit detector at diameter positions. Separation between impact points = 2(r₂ - r₁) = 2 × 0.28 = 0.56 cm, not 2.0 cm. The problem gives Δx = 2.0 cm as data; we must use it to find r₂. If Δx = 2(r₂ - r₁) = 2.0 cm, then r₂ - r₁ = 1.0 cm. With r₁ = 10.0 cm, r₂ = 11.0 cm. Check consistency: r₂/r₁ = 11/10 = 1.1. Then A₂/A₁ = (1.1)² = 1.21. But A₂/A₁ = 37/35 ≈ 1.057. Inconsistency means either the given Δx or r₁ is for a different setup. However, the question asks: "If Δx = 2.0 cm and r₁ = 10.0 cm, find r₂." So use the geometry: Δx = 2(r₂ - r₁) → r₂ = r₁ + Δx/2 = 10.0 + 1.0 = 11.0 cm. The ratio of mass numbers is extra information or for verification.

ANSWER: r₂ = 11.0 cm. (Using the geometry of the mass spectrometer: the distance between impact points of two isotopes is twice the difference in their radii. The ratio of radii from mass numbers is √(37/35) ≈ 1.028, giving r₂ ≈ 10.28 cm, but the given Δx = 2.0 cm overrides this and yields r₂ = 11.0 cm, implying the experimental conditions (V, B) might not match the simple proportionality or the given numbers are for a different isotope pair.)

DIFFICULTY: hard
