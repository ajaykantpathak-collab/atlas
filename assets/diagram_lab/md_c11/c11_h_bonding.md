## explain
A hydrogen bond forms when a hydrogen stuck to a very electronegative atom (O, N, F) feels the pull of a lone pair on a neighbouring molecule. In water each molecule can hold four H-bonds — that weak glue is why water has an abnormally high boiling point, why ice floats, and why DNA's two strands stay zipped. It is not a true bond — about 20 times weaker than covalent — but it shapes all of biology.
