## explain
Bohr's radical rule: electrons can only occupy specific orbits — n=1, 2, 3... — never the space between. Sitting in an allowed orbit, the electron doesn't radiate; jumping between orbits, it emits or absorbs a photon of exactly the energy difference. One postulate explained hydrogen's spectral lines and founded quantum atomic theory.
