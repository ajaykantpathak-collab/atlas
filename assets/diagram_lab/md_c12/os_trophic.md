## explain
A real food chain bottom-up: green algae (producer) → mollusks (primary consumer) → slimy sculpin (secondary) → Chinook salmon (apex). The gray arrows carry energy up; the coloured boxes mark each trophic rung. Only ~10% crosses each step — which is why Lake Ontario's chain stops at four.

Energy climbs a ladder: producers catch sunlight, primary consumers eat them, then secondary, then top carnivores — each rung keeping only about 10% of the energy below. That tax is why food chains top out at 4-5 levels and why eating plants feeds more people than eating the animals that ate the plants.
